# Story Decision

## Decision

**Revise Part 6 around deliberately networked statecraft.**

The earlier ecosystem framing correctly rejected a universal CIA command theory, but it treated too much of the network’s strategic effect as emergent. The amended Zebra analysis shows that several load-bearing mechanisms were intentionally assembled through complementary roles.

## Governing question

After Congress abolished the Office of Public Safety, which foreign-police, intelligence, influence and security functions merely reappeared through ordinary bureaucratic convergence—and which were deliberately redistributed among agencies, authorities, contractors and foreign partners to produce broader strategic effects?

## Governing thesis

The post-OPS system was not merely a collection of separate bureaucracies that happened to produce strategic effects. In several of its most important components, roles were deliberately divided among agencies, funding authorities, contractors, intelligence relationships and foreign partners.

ICITAP provides the cleanest overt example: AID funded, State guided policy, Justice operated, FBI supplied expertise, contractors implemented, and foreign governments received. CIA–DEA records document deliberate information and liaison architecture without requiring CIA ownership of DEA. Afghanistan deliberately integrated development, counternarcotics, stabilization and counterinsurgency. ZunZuneo added a concealed compartment through contractors, offshore entities and hidden sponsorship.

The entire ecosystem was not universally coordinated. Real public purposes, ordinary outsourcing, institutional rivalry, failed programs and unintended effects remained substantial. But the load-bearing Part 6 mechanisms were deliberately networked.

## Analytical distinctions

### Ordinary or emergent activity

- legitimate investigative and post-conflict needs;
- ordinary agency specialization;
- duplication and weak aggregate coordination;
- unintended Afghanistan outcomes;
- incidental information spillover;
- media/OCCRP and domestic-bleedback propositions not established by this corpus.

### Deliberately networked statecraft

- ICITAP’s divided funding, policy, expertise and operations;
- functional migration through Section 660 exceptions and new agency homes;
- CIA–DEA registries, notification and interagency information centers;
- Afghanistan’s integrated counterinsurgency-development strategy;
- contractor and recipient-government roles assigned inside broader programs.

### Concealed compartments

- OPS/IPS: covered CIA personnel, proprietary training, concealed follow-on instruction and Police Group liaison;
- TWO-FOLD: covert recruitment, proprietary cover and covered personnel;
- ZunZuneo: concealed sponsorship, offshore structures and attribution management.

## Strongest documentary formula

**AID funded. State guided policy. Justice operated. FBI supplied expertise. DOD supplied support. Contractors supplied implementation. Foreign governments supplied recipient institutions. Intelligence agencies gained access, liaison and information where documented.**

The point is not that every actor shared a secret motive. The point is that in documented programs the roles were intentionally complementary.

## What Parts 1–5 establish

The earlier installments prove that this architecture was not merely imaginable. It had already been used:

1. AID served as the overt administrative center of an interagency internal-defense program.
2. Byron Engle retained covered CIA status while directing OPS.
3. IPS provided proprietary and concealed follow-on training.
4. Police Group liaison linked CIA to OPS and IPA.
5. Participant names, biographies and assessments moved through intelligence channels.
6. TWO-FOLD used covert recruitment through BNDD.
7. Selected foreign functions migrated after political and legal restriction.

Those precedents do not prove every later program was covert. They prove deliberate role differentiation and concealed compartments were established instruments of statecraft.

## Claims the article will not make

- Every USAID program belonged to one consciously orchestrated system.
- ICITAP was covert, CIA-controlled or OPS renamed.
- Afghanistan aid intentionally supported heroin production.
- Every contractor or NGO was a cutout.
- Media and anti-corruption reporting was directed story by story by government funders.
- Foreign-facing capabilities were deliberately redeployed against domestic American targets.

## Part 7 boundary

Media and anti-corruption networks, full OTI/civil-society contractor chains, and domestic “bleedback” require award-level and operational evidence not present in this corpus. They remain future investigations, not Part 6 conclusions.

## Final formulation

OPS was easy to see because the functions were concentrated. The post-OPS architecture was harder to see because they were distributed. Distribution did not necessarily make the system accidental. In several documented cases, distribution was the design.

Congress killed the office. The network became the organization.
