# Part 6 Connection Report

## Executive finding

The documentary record supports the strongest structural version of Mike Benz's ecosystem thesis. The Office of Public Safety's closure ended a centralized AID office, but it did not end the functions that office had gathered. Congress retained and multiplied legal exceptions; AID financed Justice's initial administration-of-justice mechanism; State supplied policy guidance and funding; DEA, FBI and DOD operated through their own authorities; intelligence agencies joined information-sharing and narcotics structures; and contractors and grantees supplied distance, personnel and implementation. The surviving functions were connected, but they were not a single chain of command. **The network was the organization.**

## Confirmed high-value connections

### AID → DOJ/ICITAP

- **Connection type:** DIRECT FUNDING / INTERAGENCY AGREEMENT / LEGAL HANDOFF.
- **Evidence:** GAO reports that during FY1986 AID transferred money to DOJ to design and implement projects improving foreign investigative capabilities. DOJ created ICITAP under State oversight.
- **Meaning:** A foreign-aid funding stream helped launch a Justice-run successor function.
- **Limit:** ICITAP was not inside AID and did not initially possess OPS's broad public-order mission.

### Kriskovich → multiple eras

- **Connection type:** PERSONNEL OVERLAP / TRAINING RELATIONSHIP.
- **Evidence:** NDU's official history identifies FBI Inspector-in-Charge David Kriskovich as designer of the Caribbean Police School, trainer of El Salvador's SIU while AID-funded in 1985, founding ICITAP director on FBI loan, and later Bosnia IPTF deputy commissioner.
- **Meaning:** Skills, personal networks and training practices crossed institutional boundaries even when organizational charts changed.
- **Limit:** No OPS or CIA service is documented.

### Narcotics exception → expansion

- **Connection type:** LEGAL HANDOFF / DIRECT FUNDING.
- **Evidence:** Section 660 preserved narcotics and specified DEA/FBI authority. GAO's FY1990 map found $45 million in State narcotics police assistance, $42 million in DOD police support and $20 million in ICITAP, within an incomplete $117 million total.
- **Meaning:** A targeted exception became one of the largest continuing channels for foreign police relationships.
- **Limit:** Counternarcotics missions had distinct statutory purposes; their existence does not prove they were camouflage for OPS.

### Post-conflict exception → whole-force rebuilding

- **Connection type:** LEGAL HANDOFF / JOINT PROGRAM / FUNCTIONAL HANDOFF.
- **Evidence:** Section 660(b)(6) authorized civilian-police reconstitution in nations emerging from instability. ICITAP expanded into Panama, El Salvador, Haiti, Somalia, Bosnia, Kosovo, Afghanistan and Iraq.
- **Meaning:** Activities once associated with public-safety development returned under post-conflict and democratic-policing language.
- **Limit:** Human-rights, civilian-control and rule-of-law objectives were real program constraints, not merely decoration.

### Contractor layer → accountability gap

- **Connection type:** CONTRACT.
- **Evidence:** DynCorp supplied U.S. IPTF personnel and DOD support contractors in Bosnia. HRW cites Army CID records, employee admissions, repatriations, prosecution failures and the Bolkovac tribunal result.
- **Meaning:** Outsourcing added distance between policy authority, employer discipline, host-country jurisdiction and U.S. prosecution.
- **Limit:** The record supports misconduct by named individuals and institutional control failures, not the claim that DynCorp's corporate mission was trafficking.

### CIA/DEA coordination → intelligence interest without ownership

- **Connection type:** INFORMATION SHARING / JOINT PROGRAM.
- **Evidence:** A 1976 CIA/CCPC study describes foreign liaison as vital, an interagency source registry and DEA notification to CIA station chiefs. 1983 Bush remarks describe regional narcotics centers staffed across law enforcement, military and the intelligence community.
- **Meaning:** Intelligence interests could use and support the post-OPS law-enforcement environment without administratively owning every program.
- **Limit:** No located record puts ICITAP, ZunZuneo or the whole distributed architecture under CIA operational control.

### USAID/OTI → Creative → ZunZuneo

- **Connection type:** CONTRACT / SUBAWARD / POLITICAL-INFLUENCE PROGRAM.
- **Evidence:** USAID OIG documents the $15.5 million Creative task order, twelve ZunZuneo grants, concealed sponsorship, offshore structures, acquired phone numbers and attempted political segmentation.
- **Meaning:** A later influence capability used the same distributed grammar: public finance, private implementation, concealed attribution and a foreign audience.
- **Limit:** OIG made no covert-action determination, found no fraud and found little useful political-profile data.

## Connections tested and rejected or narrowed

### USAID ↔ CIA after OPS

The corpus and targeted official records show intelligence interests inside the wider narcotics/law-enforcement environment and both agencies operating in some of the same countries and policy domains. They do not show a post-1975 command relationship, an ICITAP covert annex, or an OPS-style integrated intelligence-screening pipeline. The strongest defensible statement is that post-OPS specialization preserved contact points, information flows and parallel utility while dispersing authority. Anything stronger awaits classified or archival records.

### USAID development ↔ narcotics production

Afghanistan sources describe the collision of crop eradication, rural livelihoods, counterinsurgency and development. Some aid likely produced spillovers in an opium economy, and U.S. policy repeatedly failed to align incentives. That is not evidence of intent to support heroin production.

### Democracy programming ↔ regime change

ZunZuneo proves that USAID/OTI could run politically sensitive information programs through contractors while concealing U.S. sponsorship. The contested IRI Bangladesh document does not prove the broader claim that U.S. aid caused a government overthrow. These are related questions about influence architecture, not interchangeable proof.

### Planning vignette ↔ real operation

The Naruvu story describes a fictional Special Forces vignette. Its migration into a JSOU book under “Nigeria” demonstrates how a hypothetical can acquire false historical weight through citation. It cannot be used as evidence the operation occurred.

## Conventional explanations preserved

- Transnational narcotics, terrorism, trafficking, corruption and post-conflict disorder created genuine demands for foreign law-enforcement cooperation.
- Congress intended many exceptions to constrain missions by crime type, country and human-rights condition.
- DOJ's professional-investigative model was partly designed to avoid the reputation and abuses associated with OPS.
- Fragmentation can result from federal jurisdiction and congressional compromise, not only from a plan to hide continuity.

## Bottom line

What survived OPS was a set of functions: training, investigations, academy building, intelligence exchange, equipment, police management and political-transition support. Those functions migrated into a network with multiple authorities and funders. ZunZuneo shows the same organizational grammar operating in political influence; Afghanistan shows competing missions assembled inside one theater. The network's most important continuity was functional, its most important break was organizational, and its most durable accountability problem was that no single office could be held responsible for the whole. Parts 1–5 documented the centralized version. Part 6 documents the distributed one.
