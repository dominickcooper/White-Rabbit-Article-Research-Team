# Part 6 Entity Network

## Revised governing map

`Congress / legal authorities → State policy → AID and other funding accounts → DOJ / DEA / FBI / DOD operators → contractors and grantees → host-country institutions and audiences`

The CIA and wider intelligence community intersect through liaison, source coordination, information centers and parallel counternarcotics missions. The evidence supports those information-sharing edges; it does not place the intelligence community at the top of every program’s command chain.

Connection labels remain specific: DIRECT FUNDING, LEGAL HANDOFF, INFORMATION SHARING, JOINT PROGRAM, CONTRACT, PERSONNEL OVERLAP and SHARED RECIPIENT are not treated as synonyms for operational control.

## Core agencies

| Entity | Role after OPS | Funding/control relationship | Evidence-backed limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| USAID/AID | Initial AOJ sponsor and funder; later rule-of-law, governance, stabilization and development partner | Transferred funds to DOJ; sometimes received State-appropriated ESF; used contracts/grants | Not the central operator of the full foreign-police system after 1975 |
| DOJ / ICITAP | Police investigations, management, academies, forensics, institutional development and post-conflict rebuilding | Administered program; interagency funds primarily from State/AID early and later State/DOD | Not a wholesale legal successor to OPS; initially narrower |
| State / INM-INL | Policy guidance, funding, narcotics, later broad law-enforcement and contractor missions | Funded ICITAP and major bilateral police programs | Funding/policy guidance does not equal day-to-day implementation |
| State / ATA | Counterterrorism police training | Separate statutory exception and funding | Objectives sometimes overlapped ICITAP but program remained distinct |
| DEA / FBI | Investigative and narcotics training, academy participation, liaison | Original Section 660 exceptions and own authorities | Not proof of CIA operational control |
| DOD | Security assistance, counternarcotics, post-conflict security and contractor support | Own appropriations plus country exceptions | Often prohibited from specific police-development functions absent authority |
| CIA | Intelligence and counternarcotics analysis/operations | Interagency relationships visible in public records | No located primary link showing control of ICITAP or later USAID justice programs |
| ILEA | Multiagency international law-enforcement training | State-led with DOJ/Treasury and partner agencies | Training hub, not sole successor system |

## Programs and recipient nodes

`Section 660` → exceptions → `AOJ / ICITAP`, `INM/INL`, `ATA`, `DEA/FBI`, `DOD counternarcotics`, `post-conflict civilian police`, `ILEA`.

Recurring recipient theaters: El Salvador, Panama, Colombia, Peru, Bolivia, Haiti, Somalia, Bosnia, Kosovo, Afghanistan and Iraq.

## Relationship discipline

- AID → DOJ in 1986: **DIRECT FUNDING / INTERAGENCY AGREEMENT**.
- State → ICITAP: **DIRECT FUNDING + POLICY GUIDANCE**.
- Kriskovich across FBI/AID/ICITAP/IPTF: **PERSONNEL OVERLAP + TRAINING RELATIONSHIP**.
- OPS countries and ICITAP countries: often only **GEOGRAPHIC OVERLAP**.
- CIA and USAID in counternarcotics theaters: **TEMPORAL/GEOGRAPHIC OVERLAP** unless a specific joint record says more.
- DynCorp in Bosnia: **CONTRACT**; employee misconduct is not automatically corporate operational policy.
