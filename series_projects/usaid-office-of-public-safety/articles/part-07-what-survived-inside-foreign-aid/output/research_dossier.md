# Research Dossier — Part 7: The Method That Survived

## WHAT THIS PART ADDS

This finale replaces a narrow institutional-survival story with a 40-country comparative screen and an amended Zebra analysis. It establishes that modern foreign assistance is a Hybrid ecosystem: mostly ordinary programs, with selected cases of deliberately divided strategic roles and a few bounded concealed compartments. It also documents the July 1, 2025 transfer of remaining foreign-assistance administration from USAID to State.

## EARLIER FINDINGS REQUIRED FOR CONTEXT

Parts 1–2 established an overt AID police system with a separately recognized covert compartment. Parts 3–4 showed country effects and functional handoffs. Parts 5–6 established that Section 660 constrained AID but did not erase foreign police/security functions, which operated under other authorities and institutions. The reader needs only this one-sentence callback; the finale does not re-teach the full history.

## NEW FINDINGS

- USAID/State democracy assistance totaled about $14 billion in FY2018–2023, while USAID-funded actors operated across more than 100 countries. Presence is a poor causal signal.
- Syria supplies the clearest modern overt-plus-covert architecture: aid/diplomatic/governance roles and a separate CIA compartment.
- Georgia supplies a clean overt Zebra case through the Countering Malign Kremlin Influence framework.
- Sri Lanka is the strongest Horse/control: major aid presence, strategic overlap and leadership collapse without the predicted event-level signatures.
- OCCRP illustrates intentionally funded actionable information without formal editorial ownership.
- No direct USAID funding path to BLM or FJP was established through Tides.
- Remaining foreign-assistance awards moved to State after USAID stopped administering aid on July 1, 2025.

## RESPONSIBILITY ASSESSMENT

Responsibility must be claim-specific. Funding or building a civic institution creates responsibility for program design, safeguards and foreseeable effects. It does not establish responsibility for every later protest. Diplomatic recognition can shape a succession without causing the assassination that preceded it. Covert armed support creates a different and more direct responsibility than independent media grants. The article keeps funding, operational control, strategic alignment and causal responsibility separate.

## CONTRARY EVIDENCE

- Extensive USAID presence in non-rupture comparison countries.
- Ruptures that harmed U.S.-supported orders, including Myanmar's coup and Afghanistan's collapse.
- Strong domestic economic/constitutional causes in Sri Lanka, Tunisia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan and Pakistan.
- Formal editorial-independence clauses for OCCRP and recipient denials in the stateside funding tests.
- Weak modern personnel continuity and absence of a universal command structure.

## COMPETING EXPLANATIONS

Ordinary foreign policy coordination explains many overlaps. Local political economy explains several ruptures better than external programs. Recipient agency, donor competition, bureaucratic incentives and grant-market path dependence can create convergence without a common secret plan. In selected cases, however, explicit strategies and role allocation make pure emergence inadequate.

## CONNECTIONS TO MASTER DOSSIER

The modern record confirms policy/doctrinal continuity and functional similarity more strongly than institutional or personnel continuity. Black Zebra appears only in bounded cases. The 2025 USAID-to-State transition adds a new, overt instance of function migration after institutional closure.

## QUESTIONS RESOLVED

- USAID presence alone does not predict deliberate political intervention.
- Deliberate networked statecraft is directly documented in selected modern programs.
- Contractors and NGOs can be strategic intermediaries without being covert fronts.
- Information can benefit state objectives without formal ownership.
- Direct USAID funding to BLM/FJP was not established.
