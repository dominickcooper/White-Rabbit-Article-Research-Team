# Part 7 Case Selection

## Method

The screen begins with the fact that USAID or USAID-funded partners worked in more than 100 countries during the relevant era. Presence is therefore a weak signal. The global map is a structured screen, not a prevalence estimate and not a claim that every political rupture in a USAID country was sampled. Cases were selected to maximize variation in outcome, region, strategic interest, documentary visibility and predicted Zebra signatures.

The protocol asks four questions in order: Was the overlap incidental? Was it ordinary interagency coordination? Were roles intentionally divided around a common strategic objective? Was there an additional concealed or deniable compartment? A case cannot become Zebra merely because funding preceded protest, a contractor worked in several countries, or a new leader favored Washington.

## Flagship cases

1. **Syria** is the strongest positive case because public State documents allocate local-governance, opposition, media, diplomatic and stabilization roles while credible reporting and congressional material document a separate CIA arming-and-training compartment.
2. **Ukraine** tests whether long-running democracy support, visible diplomacy, post-2014 security aid and a reported intelligence partnership can be distinguished from proof of operational control over Maidan.
3. **Venezuela** tests an openly assembled transition toolkit: recognition, sanctions, civil-society aid, humanitarian assistance and an explicit transition framework.
4. **Haiti** tests leadership-transition influence after an assassination without turning external recognition into evidence of responsibility for the killing.
5. **Sri Lanka** is a hard control: a large democracy portfolio and strategic alignment are present, yet the strongest causal chain runs through reserves, debt, shortages and domestic protest.
6. **Bangladesh** tests a recent high-salience claim where the underlying democracy network is real but the bridge to the 2024 quota uprising must be proved rather than inferred.
7. **Myanmar** separates the event from the response: the coup opposed U.S. aims, while the post-coup assistance, sanctions, diplomacy and support to democratic actors were deliberately networked.
8. **Georgia** tests overt deliberate statecraft under an explicit Countering Malign Kremlin Influence framework.

## Secondary cases

Bolivia, Nicaragua, Pakistan, Armenia, Sudan, Ethiopia, Belarus, Tunisia, Lebanon, Iraq, Afghanistan, Serbia, Moldova, Kyrgyzstan and Kazakhstan were screened to check whether the flagship pattern traveled. They are reported at lower resolution where the source record did not justify a full causal reconstruction.

## Controls and base rates

The global table includes seventeen comparison countries with USAID presence but no selected leadership rupture in the screen. More important, event-level controls include Sri Lanka, Tunisia, Ethiopia, Sudan's 2021 coup, Myanmar's 2021 coup, Kyrgyzstan and Kazakhstan. Several had extensive foreign-assistance or civil-society ecosystems. Their ruptures nevertheless ran against U.S. preferences or have stronger domestic causes and no evidence of deliberately allocated U.S. roles.

## Selection limits

- The 23 rupture countries were purposefully selected; the sample cannot estimate how often USAID presence predicts upheaval.
- Funding totals are not comparable across all countries because award systems change, subawards are incomplete and democracy assistance is only one part of country portfolios.
- Public records are better at showing overt role allocation than covert tasking. Black Zebra is used only where a concealed compartment is independently documented.
- A strategy can be deliberately networked without causing the rupture it addresses. Myanmar after the coup and Ukraine after Russia's invasion are the clearest examples.
- Absence of evidence is recorded as unresolved when the predicted records might reasonably be classified or unavailable; it is recorded as negative evidence when public documents contradict the proposed mechanism.

## Selection decision

The article should not be organized as eight miniature country histories. It should begin with the global/base-rate problem, prove the architecture in Syria, test it against Ukraine and Venezuela, then use Haiti, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, Myanmar and Georgia to show exactly where the categories change. OCCRP and the stateside funding tests belong after the country cases because they reveal how information and intermediary layers work without converting independence into secret ownership.
