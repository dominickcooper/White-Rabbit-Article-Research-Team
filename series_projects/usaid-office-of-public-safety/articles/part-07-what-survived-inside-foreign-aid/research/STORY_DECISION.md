# Story Decision — Part 7

1. **What did we attempt to prove?** The maximum claim that sensitive U.S. political operations can be distributed across aid, diplomacy, defense, intelligence, law enforcement, democracy institutes, contractors, NGOs, media, and foreign partners as complementary regime-pressure or transition systems.
2. **What did author-supplied sources establish?** The historical OPS/intelligence interfaces, functional migration after Section 660, modern contractor and OTI structures, comparative political-influence theories, and case-specific program records.
3. **What did Parts 1–6 add?** They turn “distributed architecture” from metaphor into documented precedent: CIA status inside OPS, IPS proprietary cover, intelligence access to trainees, TWO-FOLD recruitment, legal breakup, interagency successors, and contractor implementation.
4. **Which books materially changed the investigation?** Wilford most changed the frame; O'Rourke sharpened intermediary and failure predictions; Levin supplied event-level intervention tests; Saunders clarified independence and utility; Robinson clarified network/contractor power but remained interpretive.
5. **Which footnotes produced primary records?** Book trails led back to NSC private-intermediary policy, declassified police/narcotics records, State/USAID country strategies, GAO/SIGAR program records, and public grant evaluations. The O'Rourke NSC item was identified through the quoted text but not separately recovered.
6. **Which extreme claims survived?** Deliberate role division; strategic use of autonomous intermediaries; Syria overt/covert complementarity as strong inference; Venezuela as explicit transition architecture; OCCRP as independent but strategically funded capacity.
7. **Which survived only as strong inference?** Syria track complementarity, pre-Maidan environmental shaping, and the relevance of repeated successor-network cultivation.
8. **Which failed?** Universal USAID=CIA; one hidden command; operational direction of Maidan/Bangladesh; assassination causation in Haiti; Sri Lanka engineering; direct Tides pass-through; OCCRP story tasking.
9. **Strongest connection chain?** Syria.
10. **Strongest modern historical analogue?** ZunZuneo and Syria together: civilian finance/implementation plus concealment or a separate covert compartment.
11. **Which case became more suspicious?** Bangladesh became more consequential because the network and successor affinity are dense, but remained unresolved.
12. **Which case collapsed?** Sri Lanka.
13. **Final thesis?** The distributed method is real and documented in bounded cases; what varies is whether the network is ordinary coordination, openly integrated statecraft, or an architecture containing a concealed compartment.

## What the reader will know that the prior six parts did not establish

The finale does not merely say functions “migrated.” It shows the modern method in operation: how an overt political ecosystem, a military/security track, diplomacy and sanctions, autonomous intermediaries, and sometimes a covert compartment can be fitted together—then shows the controls that stop that insight from becoming a universal conspiracy theory.

## Publication decision

Publish as a finale. The new value is substantial: books change the analytical question, Syria provides the load-bearing modern chain, Venezuela supplies the open-plan comparison, OCCRP resolves the autonomy/utility paradox, and Sri Lanka demonstrates that the method can fail the test.
