# Extreme-Thesis Ledger — Part 7

## Maximum thesis

The CIA and the wider U.S. national-security apparatus learned that politically sensitive foreign operations need not sit inside CIA. Functions can be distributed among USAID, State, DOD, DOJ, law enforcement, NED/IRI/NDI, OTI, contractors, NGOs, media, anti-corruption networks, civil-society organizations, and foreign partners. In some countries those tools operate as complementary parts of deliberate regime pressure, political realignment, stabilization, or leadership-transition strategies.

This is the proposition tested, not the conclusion assumed.

## Strongest confirming observations

1. NSAM 177 explicitly placed overt operating/funding responsibility in AID while excluding covert aspects.
2. Syria combined overt opposition governance, media, public-safety, sanctions, military support, and a separately acknowledged covert arming program around pressure on Assad.
3. Venezuela published the pressure architecture: recognition, sanctions, a designed transitional executive, elections, security terms, and international financial support.
4. Wilford and Saunders show that genuine autonomy and covert strategic utility can coexist inside funded associations.
5. OCCRP records pair editorial independence with state-funded investigative capacity and measured downstream official action.
6. Section 660 ended OPS but did not end the functions; they reappeared under differentiated authorities and implementers.

## Strongest disconfirming observations

1. Sri Lanka's 2022 rupture has a dense domestic economic causal chain and no event-specific U.S. role allocation.
2. Myanmar's military coup opposed U.S. policy; the coordinated American toolkit came after the rupture.
3. Afghanistan's integrated network collapsed against U.S. objectives.
4. No common command record links USAID, CIA, democracy implementers, media grantees, and domestic advocacy across the theory's full breadth.
5. Recipient autonomy, bureaucratic rivalry, monitoring failures, and inconsistent results are pervasive.

## Reduction rule

Retain clauses supported by direct records or independent convergence. Preserve strategic complementarity as inference where common purpose and role differentiation are documented but explicit joint tasking is not. Reject universal proxy, single-command, and event-causation claims that fail their predicted footprints.
