# Part 3 C-123 Airframe Identity Determination

Date: 2026-09-20  
Question: Was the C-123 associated with Barry Seal the same physical airframe as the C-123 shot down over Nicaragua on 5 October 1986?

## Determination

**PROBABLE SAME AIRFRAME**

The available identifiers form a consistent secondary chain around registration N4410F, USAF serial 54-0679, and manufacturer serial 20128. A contemporaneous report also records a DEA spokesman's qualified same-plane assessment. Phase 2A did not acquire the primary FAA registration/transaction file or an official crash-identification packet required to prove physical identity and continuity of ownership/use. Under the Phase 1 gate, only a verified result permits a categorical same-airframe sentence; that gate remains open.

## Evidence supporting the probable finding

1. Aerial Visuals' airframe dossier associates N4410F, USAF 54-0679, manufacturer serial 20128, later HPF821, and the 5 October 1986 loss. Its own notice says imported data have not been reviewed, so it is a discovery/secondary source, not dispositive evidence: https://aerialvisuals.ca/AirframeDossier.php?Serial=25120
2. Geoff Goodall's Fairchild aircraft directory independently compiles the same core serial/registration chain: https://www.goodall.com.au/warbirds-directory-v6/fairchild.pdf
3. A contemporaneous New York Times clipping preserved under CIA Reading Room identifier CIA-RDP90-00965R000402690007-7 reports DEA spokesman Jack Hood as saying, with qualified language, that the downed C-123 was the same one used in a Barry Seal operation. This is a newspaper account of an agency spokesman, not the underlying agency record.
4. A federal legal publication cites an FAA “Fact Sheet on C-123 Shot Down in Nicaragua” and reports N4410F, but Phase 2A did not locate the underlying FAA fact sheet itself.

## Missing proof

The following would be sufficient, alone or in combination, to move the determination toward verified:

- certified FAA registration history for N4410F showing manufacturer serial and each transfer;
- bills of sale or conveyance documents linking the Seal-associated operator/owner chain to the later aircraft;
- an official DEA/Customs inventory or operational record recording the airframe serial during Seal's use;
- an official crash, intelligence, military, or congressional exhibit identifying the recovered/downed aircraft by manufacturer or USAF serial;
- insurer, maintenance, or logbook records that bridge the relevant dates without a registration reuse or aircraft substitution.

## Excluded inferences

- Matching aircraft type alone is not identity.
- Repetition of the same registration in later secondary sources is strong corroboration but does not replace the historical FAA file.
- A spokesman's “best information” assessment is probative but expressly qualified.
- The fact that a CIA archive preserves a newspaper clipping does not convert the clipping into a CIA finding.
- Similar ownership, maintenance, or operating locations cannot independently establish airframe identity.

## Article-use rule

Allowed if needed and accurately sourced: “Available records and compiled airframe histories indicate that it was probably the same C-123, although the primary registration and transfer chain has not been verified.”

Not allowed: “It was the same plane,” “the records prove it was the same aircraft,” or any equivalent categorical formulation.

## Gate status

**OPEN.** Obtain the FAA historical file and an official shootdown identification record before upgrading the determination to verified.
