# Final Alan Hyde Chain

## Narcotics evidence

CIA OIG vol. II, PDF pp. 307–309, records a 1984 USDAO report that Hyde said he made money from cocaine; 1985 reporting that he claimed Customs agents were “in his pocket”; 1986 Coast Guard reports linking his businesses and associates to cocaine movement; 1987 law-enforcement familiarity, vessel watch lists, Honduran reports, and reliable-source descriptions of Hyde as a central Bay Islands criminal actor; and a 1988 CIA assessment alleging cocaine-chemical, gun, and money-laundering activity. Much was allegation or intelligence reporting, not a conviction.

## Operational value and CIA relationship

Mariscos Hybur supplied vessels, storage, docking, aviation fuel, and other logistics to the Contras from mid-1987 into early 1989. Field officers said Hyde's vessels were cheaper, more secure, and faster than aircraft. Later cables called his operation the only Honduran “delivery mechanism” for aviation gasoline.

## Knowledge and continued support

- July 9, 1987: Headquarters warned that Hyde might have drug-trafficker ties, barred new commitments, but allowed ongoing work.
- July 1987: the field said stopping would halt the effort and that no alternative existed.
- August 5–8, 1987: senior officials approved limited use on “operational necessity” grounds after reviewing derogatory information.
- March 1988: DDO Stolz approved an extension despite new smuggling allegations.
- October 1988: another extension was approved; the relationship apparently ended in early 1989.
- A former NOG chief said suspected weapons diversion took priority over verifying whether Hyde smuggled cocaine into the United States (OIG vol. II, PDF p. 316).

## Prosecution exposure

A March 11, 1993 cable discouraged counternarcotics action because Hyde's CIA connection was well documented and could be difficult during prosecution (OIG vol. II, PDF p. 318). This is direct documentary support for an enforcement/reporting anomaly; it is not proof that CIA knew a particular allegation was true.

## Verdict

**DOCUMENTED FUNCTIONAL PATTERN:** serious narcotics allegations + known operational value + senior authorization + continued support + enforcement concern. The evidence establishes knowing risk tolerance and operational priority, not a finding that Hyde was convicted or that CIA authorized narcotics trafficking.
