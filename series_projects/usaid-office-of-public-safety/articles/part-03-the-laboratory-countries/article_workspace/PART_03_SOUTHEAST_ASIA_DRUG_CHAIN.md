## Control questions

- **Who controlled territory?** Hmong and other local forces held important producing zones with decisive CIA military/logistical sponsorship, but local sovereignty and factional authority remained real.
- **Who controlled air transport?** CIA managed or financed the strategic aviation system; Air America controlled formal carrier operations; local passengers/loaders and allied carriers controlled portions of cargo handling.
- **Who controlled roads?** Local armies, officials, and rival factions controlled fragmented road corridors. Airlift mattered precisely because ground control was limited.
- **Who controlled security?** CIA-backed forces and allied Lao officers provided security in key zones; the Royal Lao state and other factions retained authority elsewhere.
- **Who benefited financially?** Farmers, brokers, officials, commanders, refiners, and traffickers. Opium income supported communities and actors important to the proxy war; exact CIA or force-budget receipt is not documented.
- **Who knew?** CIA, State/AID, narcotics agencies, and senior local officials knew of substantial allied narcotics involvement at least in general and sometimes by person.
- **Who had power to intervene?** Local commanders/government, CIA relationship managers, U.S. diplomats, and narcotics agencies each had partial leverage.
- **Who chose not to?** Official accounts expressly say the war took priority and CIA did not prevent civilian use/movement in controlled areas; that is a documented non-suppression choice, not proof of a desire for U.S. addiction.

## Strongest defensible chain

U.S. covert sponsorship sustained an allied territorial and aviation system inside a preexisting opium economy. U.S. officials knew important allies were implicated, chose the war over systematic suppression, and maintained logistics whose access and protection made the surrounding trade more resilient. Heroin from the region reached U.S. troops and the U.S. civilian market. The missing evidentiary bridge is a continuous, knowingly CIA-directed shipment or an institutional design to produce that U.S. result.

**Evidence-ladder result:** ASSOCIATION and KNOWLEDGE—DOCUMENTED; TOLERANCE—STRONGLY SUPPORTED; PROTECTION—STRONG INFERENCE; FACILITATION—STRONG INFERENCE; EXPLOITATION—STRONG INFERENCE; FINANCIAL BENEFIT—LIKELY to allied actors, not proved to CIA; DIRECT PARTICIPATION—INSUFFICIENT; INTENTIONAL SYSTEM—INSUFFICIENT as a trafficking design, PLAUSIBLE/STRONG INFERENCE as knowingly preserved architecture.
