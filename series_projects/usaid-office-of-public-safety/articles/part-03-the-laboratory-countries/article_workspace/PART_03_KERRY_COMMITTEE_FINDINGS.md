# PART 3 KERRY COMMITTEE FINDINGS

Source: U.S. Senate Committee on Foreign Relations, Subcommittee on Terrorism, Narcotics and International Operations, *Drugs, Law Enforcement and Foreign Policy*, S. Prt. 100-165 (report dated Dec. 1988; printed 1989). The local 90-image scan contains the published report through printed p. 169. It does not contain all separately cited deposition/exhibit volumes.

## Evidentiary key

- **Committee finding**: an express finding or conclusion of the subcommittee.
- **Witness allegation/testimony**: a named person's account, not automatically adopted in full.
- **Documentary exhibit/record**: a record described or quoted by the report.
- **Staff inference**: a synthesis supported by the report but not phrased as a formal finding.

## K-01 — Contra-connected trafficking and support network

**CLAIM:** The committee found substantial evidence that individual Contras, suppliers, pilots, mercenaries, and supporters engaged in drug trafficking; parts of the Contra supply network were used by drug organizations, and Contra elements knowingly received material or financial assistance from traffickers.  
**PAGE:** PDF pp. 6-8, 24; printed findings/conclusions around pp. 36-37 and 132-133.  
**EVIDENCE TYPE:** Committee finding.  
**PERSON / NETWORK:** Southern and Northern Front participants; suppliers and pilots.  
**CIA CONNECTION:** These actors operated in or supported the U.S.-backed Contra war; the finding does not make all of them CIA agents.  
**NARCOTICS CONNECTION:** Smuggling, use of supply infrastructure, and trafficker-provided cash, aircraft, weapons, pilots, or other support.  
**U.S. DESTINATION:** The report documents routes and organizations aimed at the United States, but not every transaction's destination.  
**SUPPORTING DOCUMENT:** Testimony, law-enforcement files, contractor records, and the company case studies summarized below.  
**CONTRARY EVIDENCE:** The committee did not find that Contra leaders personally directed drug trafficking.  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** STRONGLY SUPPORTED for network overlap; INSUFFICIENT for a leadership-directed trafficking policy.  
**WHY IT MATTERS:** It defeats the claim that narcotics ties were merely an unsubstantiated rumor while preserving the leadership distinction.

## K-02 — U.S. agencies possessed contemporaneous information

**CLAIM:** U.S. agencies had relevant trafficking information contemporaneously or immediately afterward, while public government statements sometimes denied that significant information existed.  
**PAGE:** PDF pp. 24-25.  
**EVIDENCE TYPE:** Committee finding plus documentary/law-enforcement record.  
**PERSON / NETWORK:** Pastora's circle; Justice Department, FBI, CIA.  
**CIA CONNECTION:** A 1987 CIA Central American Task Force chief described the problem around Pastora as wider than “a couple” of individuals.  
**NARCOTICS CONNECTION:** Information on persons within the Contra orbit implicated in trafficking.  
**U.S. DESTINATION:** Not transaction-specific.  
**SUPPORTING DOCUMENT:** FBI holdings and interagency meeting testimony summarized by the committee.  
**CONTRARY EVIDENCE:** Possessing allegations is not the same as validating each allegation.  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** DOCUMENTED for agency receipt/possession; case-dependent for credibility.  
**WHY IT MATTERS:** Moves the analysis from association to knowledge and raises the adequacy of reporting and response.

## K-03 — Law-enforcement priority yielded to foreign-policy priority

**CLAIM:** In the Bahamas, Honduras, Nicaragua, Panama, and elsewhere, foreign-policy objectives delayed, halted, or interfered with drug-law enforcement; officials working on Central America sometimes avoided drug issues for fear of jeopardizing the anti-Sandinista war.  
**PAGE:** PDF pp. 6-8, 66-74.  
**EVIDENCE TYPE:** Committee finding and conclusion.  
**PERSON / NETWORK:** Noriega; Contra-support networks; State, CIA, DOJ and law-enforcement components.  
**CIA CONNECTION:** CIA was among agencies maintaining relationships with Noriega and Contra actors.  
**NARCOTICS CONNECTION:** Intelligence was withheld or underused; investigations and prosecutions were subordinated.  
**U.S. DESTINATION:** Multiple U.S.-bound cocaine networks.  
**SUPPORTING DOCUMENT:** Noriega chapter; Seal, Bueso-Rosa, Mena, Costa Rica, and contractor cases.  
**CONTRARY EVIDENCE:** National-security equities and source protection can lawfully constrain disclosure; the report does not show a single centralized obstruction order.  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** YES — DOCUMENTED as recurring institutional conflict; case-specific for deliberate protection.  
**WHY IT MATTERS:** This is the report's strongest tolerance/protection finding.

## K-04 — Four State Department contractors had trafficking ties

**CLAIM:** In 1986 the State Department paid four companies with documented trafficking connections or principals under investigation: SETCO ($185,924.25), DIACSA ($41,120.90), Frigorificos de Puntarenas ($261,982), and Vortex ($317,425.17).  
**PAGE:** PDF pp. 27-30.  
**EVIDENCE TYPE:** Committee finding supported by payment and law-enforcement records.  
**PERSON / NETWORK:** Juan Ramón Matta Ballesteros/SETCO; Luis Rodríguez/Frigorificos; DIACSA principals; Michael Palmer/Vortex.  
**CIA CONNECTION:** The companies served a U.S.-organized Contra logistics program; exact CIA control varied and some payments were made through State's humanitarian-aid mechanism.  
**NARCOTICS CONNECTION:** Prior Customs/FBI/DEA intelligence, money-laundering activity, indictments, or principal admissions.  
**U.S. DESTINATION:** SETCO and other associated networks were tied to U.S.-bound cocaine; each flight/payment must still be proved individually.  
**SUPPORTING DOCUMENT:** Payment records and criminal-investigation records cited in the report.  
**CONTRARY EVIDENCE:** A company can perform legitimate freight even when a principal has criminal ties; payment is not proof that the paid cargo contained drugs.  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** DOCUMENTED for payments and known derogatory records; STRONG INFERENCE that vetting/enforcement safeguards failed.  
**WHY IT MATTERS:** It is concrete facilitation of narcotics-connected logistics firms with public funds.

## K-05 — George Morales and the Southern Front

**CLAIM:** George Morales supplied aircraft, pilots, training, and about $100,000 to Southern Front figures in return for access useful to his trafficking organization.  
**PAGE:** PDF pp. 30-32.  
**EVIDENCE TYPE:** Witness testimony corroborated in part by operations and travel/logistics evidence; committee synthesis.  
**PERSON / NETWORK:** George Morales; Adolfo “Popo” Chamorro; Octaviano César; ARDE/FRS personnel.  
**CIA CONNECTION:** Southern Front actors had U.S./CIA relationships; César testified CIA was informed of the Morales relationship.  
**NARCOTICS CONNECTION:** Morales was a convicted trafficker seeking facilities and pilots to move cocaine.  
**U.S. DESTINATION:** His organization trafficked to the United States.  
**SUPPORTING DOCUMENT:** Testimony and documented aircraft/support activity, including repeated Ilopango–Costa Rica/La Penca flights.  
**CONTRARY EVIDENCE:** The exact statement allegedly made to CIA—effectively that the arrangement was acceptable if the Contras did not deal in “powder”—rests on witness recollection and is not a written CIA authorization.  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** CORROBORATED ACCOUNT for the exchange; PLAUSIBLE/STRONG INFERENCE for CIA knowledge depending on the specific participant.  
**WHY IT MATTERS:** It is a concrete example of a trafficker's assets becoming militarily useful while the proxy network became useful to trafficking.

## K-06 — John Hull's airstrips and investigative resistance

**CLAIM:** John Hull, a U.S.-linked Contra supporter with several Costa Rican airstrips, was repeatedly alleged to be connected to cocaine flights; Assistant U.S. Attorney Jeffrey Feldman and an FBI agent encountered resistance and concluded that U.S. officials were protecting him.  
**PAGE:** PDF pp. 33-34.  
**EVIDENCE TYPE:** Witness allegations plus prosecutor/investigator testimony; not a committee finding that every allegation was true.  
**PERSON / NETWORK:** John Hull; Gary Betzner; Feldman; FBI and embassy personnel.  
**CIA CONNECTION:** Hull assisted the Contra effort and the report describes CIA ties/support; he received roughly $10,000 monthly from the FDN at Oliver North's direction.  
**NARCOTICS CONNECTION:** Five witnesses linked Hull to cocaine; Betzner claimed direct observation of guns unloaded and cocaine loaded for Florida.  
**U.S. DESTINATION:** Florida.  
**SUPPORTING DOCUMENT:** Testimony and investigative chronology.  
**CONTRARY EVIDENCE:** Only Betzner claimed direct observation of loading; witness credibility and the absence of flight manifests remain material.  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** CORROBORATED ASSOCIATION; PLAUSIBLE specific trafficking; STRONG INFERENCE of investigative interference based on the prosecutor's account.  
**WHY IT MATTERS:** Combines air logistics, a U.S. destination, intelligence/Contra utility, and an enforcement anomaly.

## K-07 — Seal operation compromised for a political objective

**CLAIM:** Oliver North disclosed information from Barry Seal's sensitive operation to support a Contra-aid vote, jeopardizing an active narcotics case; North also proposed transferring $1.5 million from the sting to the Contras, which DEA rejected.  
**PAGE:** PDF pp. 26, 66-67.  
**EVIDENCE TYPE:** Documentary record and committee finding.  
**PERSON / NETWORK:** Oliver North; Barry Seal; DEA; NSC.  
**CIA CONNECTION:** Part of the interagency covert-support architecture, though the act was attributed to North/NSC rather than proved as a CIA order.  
**NARCOTICS CONNECTION:** Active DEA sting and contemplated use of seized drug funds.  
**U.S. DESTINATION:** United States enforcement target.  
**SUPPORTING DOCUMENT:** North/DEA record summarized in the report.  
**CONTRARY EVIDENCE:** Political disclosure is not proof of participation in trafficking.  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** DOCUMENTED law-enforcement compromise and attempted covert redirection of prospective proceeds.  
**WHY IT MATTERS:** It shows drug enforcement being treated as a resource subordinate to the covert war.

## K-08 — Bueso-Rosa sentencing accommodation

**CLAIM:** Senior officials favored an accommodating resolution for Honduran officer José Bueso-Rosa after a drug-financed assassination plot, partly to prevent disclosure of his Contra support role; he received a five-year minimum-security sentence.  
**PAGE:** PDF pp. 66-67.  
**EVIDENCE TYPE:** Documentary and official-testimony record; committee conclusion.  
**PERSON / NETWORK:** Bueso-Rosa; North; Paul Gorman; Elliott Abrams; Duane Clarridge; DOJ's Mark Richard.  
**CIA CONNECTION:** Clarridge and other national-security officials supported accommodation because of Bueso's operational assistance.  
**NARCOTICS CONNECTION:** The assassination conspiracy was to be financed with a large drug transaction.  
**U.S. DESTINATION:** U.S. prosecution.  
**SUPPORTING DOCUMENT:** Interagency communications/admissions summarized by the committee.  
**CONTRARY EVIDENCE:** Plea and sentencing decisions can reflect cooperation and evidentiary concerns; the report nevertheless identifies the secrecy/Contra rationale.  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** YES — DOCUMENTED protection/leniency linked to operational value.  
**WHY IT MATTERS:** One of the clearest higher-rung protection examples.

## K-09 — Frogman money return

**CLAIM:** U.S. authorities returned $36,020 seized in a case involving drugs, a weapon, and a grenade after Contra-linked letters asserted that the money belonged to the Contra cause.  
**PAGE:** PDF p. 35.  
**EVIDENCE TYPE:** Documentary case record and committee treatment.  
**PERSON / NETWORK:** Frogman defendants; Contra activists.  
**CIA CONNECTION:** Claimed Contra purpose; no proven CIA relationship to defendants.  
**NARCOTICS CONNECTION:** Seizure occurred in a narcotics case.  
**U.S. DESTINATION:** Florida/U.S. case.  
**SUPPORTING DOCUMENT:** Seizure and return record; letters.  
**CONTRARY EVIDENCE:** CIA OIG Vol. I later found no CIA relationship and concluded the money was not actually Contra-owned; the letters may have reflected personal support rather than operational control.  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** DOCUMENTED anomaly; INSUFFICIENT for CIA protection.  
**WHY IT MATTERS:** The action is anomalous, but the broader explanation remains disputed.

## K-10 — Milian Rodríguez's claimed $10 million offer

**CLAIM:** Ramón Milian Rodríguez claimed he offered $10 million in cartel money to the Contras.  
**PAGE:** PDF p. 37.  
**EVIDENCE TYPE:** Witness allegation.  
**PERSON / NETWORK:** Milian Rodríguez; cartel/Noriega-related networks.  
**CIA CONNECTION:** Alleged covert-finance connection, not established acceptance.  
**NARCOTICS CONNECTION:** Drug-money laundering.  
**U.S. DESTINATION:** Covert funding rather than a shipment.  
**SUPPORTING DOCUMENT:** Portions of his testimony about cartel/Noriega/company structures were corroborated.  
**CONTRARY EVIDENCE:** He failed two key polygraph questions and had an inconclusive result on a third; acceptance and disbursement were not proved.  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** ALLEGED; INSUFFICIENT for financial-benefit finding.  
**WHY IT MATTERS:** It is a lead, not a publication-ready proof of Contra receipt.

## K-11 — Noriega: relationship-driven enforcement failure

**CLAIM:** Every U.S. agency maintaining a relationship with Manuel Noriega failed for years to act adequately on corruption and narcotics information; a federal prosecutor said CIA provided only a small fraction of what it knew.  
**PAGE:** PDF pp. 6-8, 66-74.  
**EVIDENCE TYPE:** Committee finding plus prosecutor testimony.  
**PERSON / NETWORK:** Manuel Noriega; CIA, State, Defense, DEA and DOJ personnel.  
**CIA CONNECTION:** Longstanding intelligence relationship.  
**NARCOTICS CONNECTION:** Protection and assistance to trafficking plus withheld/underused intelligence.  
**U.S. DESTINATION:** U.S.-bound cocaine and U.S. prosecution.  
**SUPPORTING DOCUMENT:** Committee's Noriega investigation and agency records.  
**CONTRARY EVIDENCE:** Intelligence compartmentation, source protection, and uneven evidentiary quality complicate the inference of a single protective policy.  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** YES — STRONGLY SUPPORTED for knowledge/tolerance and institutional conflict; case-specific for active protection.  
**WHY IT MATTERS:** Demonstrates how intelligence value could displace enforcement over time.

## K-12 — BCCI, Noriega, and laundering infrastructure

**CLAIM:** The report developed evidence that Manuel Noriega maintained a secretive BCCI account that grew to roughly $20 million through repeated large cash deposits; it also identified BCCI/Panama as an important laundering inquiry but said the subcommittee had not completed that investigation.  
**PAGE:** PDF pp. 12, 46-47, 64.  
**EVIDENCE TYPE:** Bank-official deposition/account evidence plus committee lead; not a completed BCCI adjudication.  
**PERSON / NETWORK:** Manuel Noriega; Amjad Awan; BCCI Panama; Ramón Milian Rodríguez.  
**CIA CONNECTION:** Noriega's long U.S. intelligence relationships form the foreign-policy context; the account evidence does not establish CIA ownership or control of BCCI funds.  
**NARCOTICS CONNECTION:** Panama/BCCI were examined as drug-money laundering mechanisms; Awan denied laundering drug money and was referred for possible perjury concerning his account.  
**U.S. DESTINATION:** Dollar flows and U.S. financial/law-enforcement exposure; not a shipment-specific destination.  
**SUPPORTING DOCUMENT:** Awan deposition, banking information, later money-laundering indictment context.  
**CONTRARY EVIDENCE:** The committee expressly left its BCCI inquiry incomplete; the $20 million account is not itself proved drug proceeds.  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** DOCUMENTED suspicious/secretive account and incomplete inquiry; INSUFFICIENT CIA drug-money chain.  
**WHY IT MATTERS:** BCCI is a serious money-laundering node, but it cannot be used as a shortcut to a CIA-controlled covert-finance conclusion.

## K-13 — Ilopango and reciprocal weapons/drug flight pattern

**CLAIM:** Gary Betzner testified that a 1983 DC-3 delivered grenades and mines to Ilopango, then flew to Colombia for marijuana before returning to the Bahamas; the report places this and other testimony inside a recurring weapons-south/drugs-north aviation pattern.  
**PAGE:** PDF p. 33 and related aviation chronology around pp. 82-83.  
**EVIDENCE TYPE:** Witness testimony plus documentary aviation/customs chronology.  
**PERSON / NETWORK:** Gary Betzner; Richard Healey; George Morales network; Salvadoran personnel.  
**CIA CONNECTION:** Ilopango later became a central Contra-resupply hub managed by U.S.-linked personnel; Betzner assumed one American present worked for Defense, which is not proof of CIA knowledge of the onward drug leg.  
**NARCOTICS CONNECTION:** Claimed marijuana pickup in Colombia after weapons delivery; wider Morales cocaine activity.  
**U.S. DESTINATION:** Bahamas base and wider U.S.-bound network; the described marijuana flight's final U.S. delivery is not established by this passage.  
**SUPPORTING DOCUMENT:** Witness record and flight/aircraft chronology cited by the committee.  
**CONTRARY EVIDENCE:** Criminal-cooperator credibility, assumptions about official identity, and missing manifest/order.  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** CORROBORATED route mechanism; INSUFFICIENT U.S. institutional authorization.  
**WHY IT MATTERS:** Shows how war logistics and drug logistics could share aircraft, crews, and protected military airfields without proving that officials ordered the narcotics leg.

## Requested-term coverage note

The published report also contains country chapters on Colombia, Honduras, Panama, the Bahamas, Cuba/Nicaragua, Haiti, and money laundering. Their most Article-3-relevant conclusions are incorporated above through the contractor, Noriega, Bueso-Rosa, BCCI, Morales, and enforcement-priority findings. “Southern Air Transport,” “Homestead,” and U.S. military-facility references did not yield a distinct committee finding strong enough in the local report body to elevate above the case-specific evidence. Their mention should not be converted into an operational claim without the cited exhibits or another primary record.

## Bottom-line adjudication

The Kerry report does **not** establish that CIA or Contra leadership designed a cocaine pipeline. It does establish a denser and more consequential record: traffickers and implicated companies entered the U.S.-supported logistics environment; agencies possessed derogatory information; public denials outran their internal information; and foreign-policy/operational objectives repeatedly displaced enforcement. That combination supports **knowledge, tolerance, selected protection, facilitation, and exploitation** in specific cases. It does not by itself establish **direct CIA participation** or an **intentional system designed to send drugs into the United States**.
