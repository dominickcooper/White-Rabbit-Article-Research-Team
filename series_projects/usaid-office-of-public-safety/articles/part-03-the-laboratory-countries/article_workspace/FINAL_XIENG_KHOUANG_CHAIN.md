# Final Xieng Khouang Air Transport Chain

## Claim 1: USAID / CIA helped establish the airline

**SOURCE:** Alfred W. McCoy, *The Politics of Heroin in Southeast Asia*, PDF p. 225; Theodore Shackley account as cited independently by Peter Dale Scott, *The American War Machine*, p. 139 n. 48.  
**PAGE:** McCoy PDF 225.  
**UNDERLYING SOURCE:** McCoy's interviews and investigative reporting; Shackley's separate insider account for the carrier's creation. A company-registration, AID grant, loan, or transfer file was not located.  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** **CORROBORATED ACCOUNT** for CIA/USAID assistance, not documentary proof of the funding instrument.

## Claim 2: Vang Pao controlled / benefited from the airline

**SOURCE:** McCoy, PDF p. 225; CIA-held congressional/press material, CIA-RDP73B00296R000300060021-0.  
**PAGE:** McCoy PDF 225.  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** **CORROBORATED ACCOUNT.** Lo Kham Thy was identified as company president, while the carrier functioned in Vang Pao's Long Tieng commercial system. The CIA-hosted document is an archived secondary/congressional item, not an Agency finding.

## Claim 3: The airline served Long Tieng / Vientiane

**SOURCE:** McCoy, PDF p. 225; CIA-held congressional/press material above.  
**PAGE:** McCoy PDF 225.  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** **CORROBORATED ACCOUNT.** The sources agree on two C-47s and a Long Tieng–Vientiane commercial role. Aircraft transfer and daily-flight records were not found.

## Claim 4: USAID officials understood commerce would involve opium

**SOURCE:** McCoy, PDF p. 225.  
**PAGE:** 225.  
**WITNESS:** Former USAID official quoted anonymously by McCoy.  
**IDENTIFIED?** No.  
**FIRSTHAND?** Presented as firsthand knowledge of official expectations.  
**INDEPENDENT?** No independent interview or record located.  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** **PLAUSIBLE CONNECTION / SINGLE-SOURCE FIRSTHAND ACCOUNT.** Do not state as documented institutional knowledge.

## Claim 5: The airline transported opium / heroin

**SOURCE:** McCoy, PDF p. 225.  
**PAGE:** 225.  
**WITNESS:** Hmong sources reported by McCoy; the source names and interview records are not available in the reviewed corpus.  
**FIRSTHAND?** Apparently local firsthand reporting, but the individual basis cannot be audited.  
**INDEPENDENT?** Not demonstrated.  
**CORROBORATION:** General opium movement into Long Tieng and occasional opium carriage aboard the wider Air America system are independently supported. Neither proves that this named carrier carried the reported loads.  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** **PLAUSIBLE CONNECTION**, not a documented cargo finding.

## Source-dependency finding

Later repetitions of the USAID-knowledge and XKAT drug-carriage claims trace back to McCoy. The CIA Reading Room search result repeats congressional or press material derived from the same public controversy; it is not independent CIA validation. The Shackley/Scott chain independently strengthens formation of the carrier, not narcotics carriage.

## Final chain verdict

**PARTIAL.** The carrier's existence, association with Vang Pao's system, Long Tieng–Vientiane route, and U.S. assistance are corroborated. USAID knowledge that commerce would include opium and actual opium/heroin carriage by XKAT remain a single McCoy source chain. The weakness is specific carrier attribution, not the wider Laos logistics or narcotics system.
