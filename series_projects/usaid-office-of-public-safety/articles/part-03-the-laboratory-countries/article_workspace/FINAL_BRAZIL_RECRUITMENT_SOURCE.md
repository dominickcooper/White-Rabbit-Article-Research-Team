# Final Brazil Recruitment Source Check

## Claim tested

Resident U.S./AID police advisers were useful partly because they collected information and created opportunities for CIA recruitment.

## Footnote escalation

Rodrigo Patto Sá Motta, “Modernizing Repression,” PDF p. 7 / printed p. 241, states that resident specialists could monitor local institutions, obtain information, and give the CIA opportunities to recruit collaborators. The precise sentence has **no footnote**.

- Footnote 17 belongs to the preceding weapons discussion and cites NARA RG 286, entry 250-66/3/01, box 20, file 3.
- Nearby notes 10 and 12 cite RG 286, 250-66-3-23, box 12, file 8.
- Notes 13 and 14 cite RG 286, 150-41-23-7/4, box 1.
- Motta PDF p. 10 / printed p. 244 similarly says adviser travel offered “better opportunities for recruitment by information agencies.” That clause is also unfootnoted; note 23 supports Mitrione's English classes, not recruitment.

The NARA Brazil human-rights finding aid confirms that these RG 286 neighborhoods are relevant AID/OPS holdings. It does not identify a document containing Motta's CIA-recruitment wording.

## Classification

**B — Motta's synthesis of several documents** is the best-supported answer. It is not presently traceable to direct language in a U.S. government document, a specific interview, or another named scholar.

## Verdict

**CORROBORATED SCHOLARLY SYNTHESIS**, not primary documentary language. The broader placement-and-access mechanism is consistent with primary OPS records and other theater evidence, but the exact recruitment-purpose wording should be attributed to Motta. No **MAJOR SERIES FINDING** flag is warranted.
