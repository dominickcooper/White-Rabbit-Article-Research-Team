# PART 03 USAID / AID DRUG-PIPELINE EVIDENCE ATLAS

## Reading rule

This is a functional map, not a claim that USAID and CIA were identical. Each entry states whether aid supplied access, capacity, logistics, or cover; it separately states whether a narcotics link is documented.

# AGRICULTURE

**USAID/AID PROGRAM:** IVS/USAID agricultural and refugee work in Hmong Laos  
**COUNTRY:** Laos  
**DATE:** 1960s–1971  
**PERSON:** Edgar “Pop” Buell  
**LOCAL PARTNER:** Hmong villages / Vang Pao's recruitment base  
**CIA CONNECTION:** Work occurred inside the CIA-backed war system.  
**NARCOTICS CONNECTION:** McCoy says Buell improved poppy cultivation; the complete Schanche source directly supports that attribution and also documents an earlier failed substitution effort.  
**SOURCE:** McCoy PDF 215–216 and n.85; Schanche PDF 142 and 267 / printed 131 and 242  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** CORROBORATED ACCOUNT for direct cultivation advice; DOCUMENTED FACT for Buell's agricultural/refugee role.  
**WHAT FUNCTION THE PROGRAM PROVIDED:** Village access, agricultural advice, trusted intermediary.  
**WHY IT MATTERS TO THE PIPELINE:** Links an aid worker's wartime agricultural response to production, while the chronology shows genuine crop-substitution efforts and no institutional narcotics policy.

# REFUGEE RELIEF

**USAID/AID PROGRAM:** Hmong refugee relief and settlement  
**COUNTRY:** Laos  
**DATE:** 1960s–1971  
**PERSON:** Buell; George Cosgrove; USAID personnel  
**LOCAL PARTNER:** Vang Pao's Hmong forces and displaced villages  
**CIA CONNECTION:** Relief supported the population base of Operation Momentum.  
**NARCOTICS CONNECTION:** The same villages produced opium; relocation and food dependence altered production incentives.  
**SOURCE:** McCoy PDF 222–234; notes PDF 346–350; Senate refugee material cited there  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** DOCUMENTED FACT for relief/war integration; STRONG INFERENCE for resilience of the opium economy.  
**WHAT FUNCTION THE PROGRAM PROVIDED:** Food, settlement, schools, medicine, population concentration.  
**WHY IT MATTERS TO THE PIPELINE:** Relief kept the allied population and army viable while opium collection continued.

# FOOD / MEDICINE

**USAID/AID PROGRAM:** Rice drops and medical relief  
**COUNTRY:** Laos  
**DATE:** 1960s–1971  
**PERSON:** Buell; village leaders; USAID official Barnes  
**LOCAL PARTNER:** Hmong villages  
**CIA CONNECTION:** Distributed through the CIA-war air and village network.  
**NARCOTICS CONNECTION:** Rice imports reduced subsistence pressure; McCoy argues medicine reduced local medicinal opium demand.  
**SOURCE:** McCoy PDF 216, 224, 228–235  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** STRONG INFERENCE for rice/opium specialization; PLAUSIBLE CONNECTION for medicine/surplus.  
**WHAT FUNCTION THE PROGRAM PROVIDED:** Subsistence support and public health.  
**WHY IT MATTERS TO THE PIPELINE:** It changed the political economy of producer communities without proving a narcotics intent.

# ROADS

FRUS records document AID's security-oriented ARD road program and Seabee assistance in northern Thai hill areas. Independent 1975 field research confirms a Seabee road into Mae Salong in 1969 and the resulting expansion of stores, markets, and truck traffic. Official records place KMT forces and U.S. opium concerns in the same area; McCoy alone expressly attributes downward opium/morphine/heroin traffic to the road. **EVIDENCE LEVEL: DOCUMENTED FACT for infrastructure; STRONG INFERENCE for drug use.**

# AIR TRANSPORT

**USAID/AID PROGRAM:** Air America charters and relief flights  
**COUNTRY:** Laos / Southeast Asia  
**DATE:** 1960s–1973  
**PERSON:** Air America personnel; USAID mission staff  
**LOCAL PARTNER:** Hmong and Royal Lao forces  
**CIA CONNECTION:** Air America was a CIA proprietary.  
**NARCOTICS CONNECTION:** Official inquiry conceded occasional opium carriage; multiple local accounts report collection/carriage.  
**SOURCE:** Leeker pp. 9–32 and local packet PDF 148; Marchetti/Marks PDF 151–152; Senate Book I PDF 11–17; McCoy PDF 213–235  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** DOCUMENTED FACT for AID funding/CIA ownership and occasional carriage; CORROBORATED ACCOUNT for recurring local carriage; OFFICIAL POSITION against policy/knowing U.S.-pilot carriage.  
**WHAT FUNCTION THE PROGRAM PROVIDED:** The only regular lift for many remote producer/allied areas.  
**WHY IT MATTERS TO THE PIPELINE:** The overt aid customer materially sustained the covert logistics network through which leakage occurred.

# AIRFIELDS

**USAID/AID PROGRAM:** Relief/commercial access to Long Tieng and remote strips  
**COUNTRY:** Laos  
**DATE:** 1960s  
**PERSON:** Buell; Vang Pao; William Young  
**LOCAL PARTNER:** Hmong forces  
**CIA CONNECTION:** Airfields followed CIA military expansion.  
**NARCOTICS CONNECTION:** Producer villages and alleged collection flights used the same nodes.  
**SOURCE:** McCoy PDF 222–225; Scott AWM PDF 122–139  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** DOCUMENTED FACT for covert-war airfields; CORROBORATED ACCOUNT for drug carriage at some nodes.  
**WHAT FUNCTION THE PROGRAM PROVIDED:** Access, resupply, evacuation, market connection.  
**WHY IT MATTERS TO THE PIPELINE:** Air access made remote bulk movement feasible.

# COMMERCIAL AVIATION

**USAID/AID PROGRAM:** Xieng Khouang Air Transport  
**COUNTRY:** Laos  
**DATE:** 1967 onward  
**PERSON:** Vang Pao; Lo Kham Thy; Shackley  
**LOCAL PARTNER:** Vang Pao's organization  
**CIA CONNECTION:** CIA helped establish the carrier; Shackley later acknowledged the assistance.  
**NARCOTICS CONNECTION:** Former USAID and senior Hmong sources told McCoy commerce meant opium and the airline carried opium/heroin.  
**SOURCE:** McCoy PDF 225, nn.118–121; Scott AWM PDF 139, n.48  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** CORROBORATED ACCOUNT for aid/CIA creation; PLAUSIBLE CONNECTION / single-source firsthand chain for narcotics use.  
**WHAT FUNCTION THE PROGRAM PROVIDED:** Local C-47/DC-3 lift and commercial hub.  
**WHY IT MATTERS TO THE PIPELINE:** The formation and route are strong; named-carrier narcotics attribution remains the weakest specific link and must not be overstated.

# LOCAL BUSINESS / COMMERCIAL VENTURES

Long Tieng's planned commercial role and Vang Pao's local airline are documented. A broader list of USAID-created businesses used for narcotics is not. **EVIDENCE LEVEL: PARTIAL / DOCUMENTARY BREAK.**

# POLICE TRAINING

**USAID/AID PROGRAM:** MSU/FOA/ICA/USOM and later Public Safety assistance  
**COUNTRY:** South Vietnam  
**DATE:** 1955–1962  
**PERSON:** Wesley Fishel; MSU advisers  
**LOCAL PARTNER:** Sûreté, municipal police, Civil Guard, VBI  
**CIA CONNECTION:** Ralph Smuckler's MSU oral history says project leadership knowingly accepted at least one seconded CIA officer; Schrader PDF 87–90 and Ramparts report a larger team and cover complaints.  
**NARCOTICS CONNECTION:** Not established.  
**SOURCE:** MSU Final Report PDF 6–8, 51–53; 1956 reorganization report PDF 4–21; Schrader PDF 87–90  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** DOCUMENTED FACT for overt capacity and at least one embedded CIA officer; CORROBORATED ACCOUNT for a larger team; DOCUMENTARY BREAK to narcotics.  
**WHAT FUNCTION THE PROGRAM PROVIDED:** Training, vehicles, arms, tear gas, files, radios, interrogation/detention facilities.  
**WHY IT MATTERS TO THE PIPELINE:** Shows the overt institutional access/capacity layer, not a drug link.

# SECURITY ASSISTANCE

Brazil and South Vietnam records document aid leverage and police/security capacity. They support the access and coercive-capacity thesis. They do not complete a drug chain. **EVIDENCE LEVEL: DOCUMENTED FACT + DOCUMENTARY BREAK.**

# RECORDS / IDENTIFICATION

MSU helped centralize criminal and “subversive” files and convert about 600,000 fingerprint cards (Final Report PDF 53). This is a documented information pipeline; CIA access and narcotics use are not shown in these files.

# COMMUNICATIONS

MSU/USOM built police radio and call-box capacity (Final Report PDF 51–52). Brazil police assistance also emphasized communications in the broader local corpus. These are logistics/intelligence-enabling systems; no named drug movement is traced.

# RURAL DEVELOPMENT

The Laos relief/agriculture complex operated in narcotics-producing highlands. Beyond rice, medicine, village schools, settlement, and air access, a distinct rural-development project tied to a shipment is not documented.

# COUNTERINSURGENCY

Lansdale's proposal for “ICA-type” relief, CIA-backed Hmong mobilization, and the MSU/USOM police system show overt aid nested inside counterinsurgency. **EVIDENCE LEVEL: DOCUMENTED FACT.** Narcotics consequence is strongest in Laos and absent in the MSU primary file.

# MILITARY SUPPORT

In Laos, humanitarian-designated flights carried both refugee supplies and munitions according to the sources McCoy cites (PDF 223). In Vietnam, Civil Guard training/equipment was explicitly paramilitary (MSU Final Report PDF 53). This supports functional military assistance, not a general USAID narcotics policy.

# INTELLIGENCE ACCESS

Police records, active intelligence collection, centralized transmission to the Sûreté, and participant relationships created access opportunities (MSU report PDF 21). Smuckler documents at least one CIA officer inside the project. Agee documents multiple AID Public Safety cover and recruitment cases (PDF 55–56, 268, 405–421). Motta PDF 7 and 10 offers an unfootnoted scholarly synthesis that Brazil adviser access created recruitment opportunities. Direct CIA receipt of a particular MSU file remains a documentary break.

# POLITICAL ACCESS

The 1963 Brazil assistance memorandum explicitly treated aid as leverage to alter Goulart's political-economic orientation (PDF 1–4). This is documented political access; the narcotics arrow is absent.

# FINANCIAL SUPPORT

AID paid Air America more than $83 million in charter services by 1971 according to Marchetti/Marks (PDF 151). CIA/USAID assistance helped create Xieng Khouang Air Transport according to McCoy and Scott. Contract records should be acquired to convert the first from insider account to primary financial evidence.

# COVER

Marchetti/Marks describe CIA officers using AID cover (PDF 69, 251) and covert support programs fronted by AID (PDF 78). Smuckler's primary oral history documents at least one CIA officer inside the ICA/FOA-sponsored MSU police team. Agee supplies multiple named firsthand AID Public Safety cover cases (PDF 55–56, 268, 405–421, 461). Motta's recruitment language is useful synthesis but not traceable to a specific footnote. **EVIDENCE LEVEL: DOCUMENTED AT SYSTEM LEVEL** for cover/access practice; **DOCUMENTARY BREAK** only for a named Article 3 drug actor using a particular cover identity.

# HUMANITARIAN PROGRAMS

Laos refugee relief is the clearest case where a legitimate humanitarian function also sustained a CIA-backed force in an opium economy. Central America “humanitarian assistance” contracts were State-administered; they should not be mislabeled USAID.

# OTHER INFRASTRUCTURE

The corpus documents laboratories, detention/interrogation centers, police academies, vehicles, records, radios, aircraft maintenance, commercial airlines, and bank/payment channels. Only the Laos air/refugee/commercial system presently completes a substantial USAID-to-narcotics intersection.

## Direct answers to the ten USAID-specific questions

1. **Programs physically in producing regions:** Laos Hmong refugee, food, medicine, agricultural, air-transport, airfield, and local-commercial-airline functions.
2. **Personnel working with CIA-backed populations:** Buell and other USAID/refugee personnel with Hmong communities and Vang Pao's support base; MSU police advisers alongside embedded CIA officers; Brazil OPS advisers placed to monitor and create recruitment opportunity. Exact individual cover statuses vary.
3. **Transport infrastructure later used by narcotics actors:** Air America charters and Xieng Khouang Air Transport are the strongest cases; remote strips are corroborated but shipment records are incomplete.
4. **Agricultural/economic effects:** Rice relief and reliable opium markets plausibly increased specialization; Schanche corroborates Buell's direct poppy advice and medicine-related increase in surplus while also documenting crop-substitution efforts.
5. **Commercial/aviation intersections:** Xieng Khouang Air Transport; AID-funded Air America services.
6. **Officials who knew:** McCoy's former USAID source said officials recognized Long Tieng commerce meant opium. Identity and underlying interview notes must be acquired. Schrader/Motta establish intelligence access in other theaters but not narcotics knowledge there.
7. **Cover:** Agee documents specific AID Public Safety cover cases; Smuckler documents at least one embedded CIA officer in MSU; Marchetti/Marks, Ahern, and Motta corroborate the access mechanism. No named Article 3 drug actor is proven to have used that cover.
8. **Actors controlling producer territory:** Relief and aviation materially strengthened Vang Pao's CIA-backed system.
9. **Institutions later appearing in security/narcotics records:** Air America, Hmong/Royal Lao forces, the MSU/ICA police mission, Sûreté/Civil Guard, OPS, and Brazil police/security organs; only the Laos set has a developed USAID-linked narcotics chain.
10. **Incomplete chain:** No independent XKAT carriage record or identified anonymous USAID witness; no continuous named Lao-to-U.S. load; complete CIA IG annexes and interview files remain absent. These limit specific allegations, not the system-level chain.
