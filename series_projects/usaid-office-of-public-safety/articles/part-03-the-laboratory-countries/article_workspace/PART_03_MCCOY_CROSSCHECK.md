# PART 3 MCCOY CROSSCHECK

Source: Alfred W. McCoy with Cathleen B. Read and Leonard P. Adams II, *The Politics of Heroin in Southeast Asia* (1972), full local 369-page copy. McCoy is a secondary investigator with extensive near-contemporaneous field interviews. His footnotes often preserve firsthand accounts, but those accounts remain testimony rather than manifests or agency orders.

## M-01 — CIA intervention changed the political economy of the trade

**McCOY CLAIM:** CIA intervention strengthened political/military clients whose territories and finances were embedded in opium, thereby reshaping rather than creating the existing trade.  
**PAGE:** PDF pp. 201-202 and surrounding chapters.  
**PERSON / NETWORK:** Vang Pao, Hmong forces, Lao generals, CIA paramilitary system.  
**SOURCE CITED:** Field research, interviews, contemporary enforcement and diplomatic reporting.  
**FIRSTHAND?** Mixed.  
**PRIMARY?** Some underlying interviews and BNDD material are primary; McCoy's synthesis is secondary.  
**CORROBORATION:** Official CIA history confirms operational dependence on local allies, knowledge of narcotics concerns, and low suppression priority.  
**OFFICIAL DENIAL:** CIA denies an institutional trafficking policy.  
**LATER DECLASSIFIED RECORD:** Supports extensive CIA air/logistics sponsorship and the war-priority tradeoff, not a narcotics order.  
**CURRENT ASSESSMENT:** STRONG INFERENCE. The intervention altered incentives and capacity; intentional narcotics design is not shown.

## M-02 — Air America carriage from Hmong villages

**McCOY CLAIM:** Villagers, local officials, soldiers, and senior Lao aviation/military figures described Air America aircraft carrying opium from producing areas toward Long Tieng in 1970–71.  
**PAGE:** PDF pp. 213, 225; interview citations around pp. 344 and 346.  
**PERSON / NETWORK:** Hmong farmers; local officials; Ouane Rattikone; Thao Ma; Vang Pao network.  
**SOURCE CITED:** Author interviews, including visits to producing localities.  
**FIRSTHAND?** Yes for several local witnesses; McCoy is the interviewer, not an eyewitness to each flight.  
**PRIMARY?** Oral testimony is primary to the observation claimed.  
**CORROBORATION:** Multiple witness categories; official inquiry concedes opium could occasionally travel aboard carrier aircraft under weak loading controls.  
**OFFICIAL DENIAL:** Official report found no policy and gave pilots a clean bill on knowing carriage.  
**LATER DECLASSIFIED RECORD:** Confirms scale and indispensability of proprietary aviation, not specific drug manifests.  
**CURRENT ASSESSMENT:** CORROBORATED ACCOUNT of carriage; PLAUSIBLE but not proved knowing employee carriage; INSUFFICIENT headquarters authorization.

## M-03 — Vang Pao airline and opium/heroin commerce

**McCOY CLAIM:** CIA/USAID assistance helped Vang Pao obtain private aviation capacity; a former USAID official and Hmong sources said the commerce included opium or heroin.  
**PAGE:** PDF p. 225; notes around p. 346.  
**PERSON / NETWORK:** Vang Pao; former USAID official; Hmong sources.  
**SOURCE CITED:** Interviews.  
**FIRSTHAND?** Partly.  
**PRIMARY?** Oral accounts.  
**CORROBORATION:** Broader record shows U.S. support for Vang Pao logistics and an opium-dependent region.  
**OFFICIAL DENIAL:** Air America institutional denial does not necessarily cover a distinct Vang Pao carrier.  
**LATER DECLASSIFIED RECORD:** Confirms operational support, not specific commercial cargo.  
**CURRENT ASSESSMENT:** CORROBORATED but carrier-specific documentary verification remains needed.

## M-04 — Long Tieng heroin processing

**McCOY CLAIM:** Bureau of Narcotics reporting and witnesses placed heroin processing within Vang Pao's Long Tieng environment.  
**PAGE:** PDF pp. 198-202.  
**PERSON / NETWORK:** Vang Pao; Lao/Chinese processors; BNDD sources.  
**SOURCE CITED:** Contemporary narcotics reporting and interviews.  
**FIRSTHAND?** Mixed.  
**PRIMARY?** Contemporary enforcement intelligence is primary but may be source-reported rather than judicially proved.  
**CORROBORATION:** Multiple descriptions of refining growth and military-protected laboratories.  
**OFFICIAL DENIAL:** CIA disputed direct sponsorship/participation.  
**LATER DECLASSIFIED RECORD:** Confirms CIA knowledge of allied narcotics concerns, without establishing ownership of a refinery.  
**CURRENT ASSESSMENT:** STRONGLY SUPPORTED that refining existed in the allied protected environment; disputed that Vang Pao personally owned/ran the plant.

## M-05 — High-grade heroin moved toward GIs and the U.S.

**McCOY CLAIM:** Golden Triangle high-grade heroin supplied U.S. servicemen in Vietnam and then entered U.S. civilian distribution, including an April 1971 Fort Monmouth shipment.  
**PAGE:** PDF pp. 199-200.  
**PERSON / NETWORK:** Regional exporters, military-market distributors, U.S. recipients.  
**SOURCE CITED:** Contemporary enforcement/military reporting and cases.  
**FIRSTHAND?** Case records rather than a single eyewitness.  
**PRIMARY?** Underlying enforcement records are primary.  
**CORROBORATION:** Contemporary heroin crisis and military investigations.  
**OFFICIAL DENIAL:** No serious denial that Southeast Asian heroin reached GIs/U.S.; dispute concerns CIA responsibility.  
**LATER DECLASSIFIED RECORD:** Supports the war-zone market, not a continuous CIA-controlled route.  
**CURRENT ASSESSMENT:** CORROBORATED U.S. destination; UNKNOWN/INSUFFICIENT continuous chain from a CIA-backed village or flight to a specific U.S. shipment.

## M-06 — Sopsaisana / 60-kilogram case and embassy response

**McCOY CLAIM:** The 60-kilogram heroin episode involving Prince Sopsaisana exposed elite trafficking while the U.S. Embassy did not pursue the political implications; BNDD reporting linked financing/refining to the Vang Pao/Long Tieng environment.  
**PAGE:** PDF p. 198 and surrounding discussion.  
**PERSON / NETWORK:** Prince Sopsaisana; Vang Pao-related allegations; BNDD; U.S. Embassy.  
**SOURCE CITED:** Contemporary public/enforcement reporting.  
**FIRSTHAND?** Documentary/official rather than McCoy eyewitness.  
**PRIMARY?** Underlying official reports are primary.  
**CORROBORATION:** Seizure itself; wider evidence of elite protection.  
**OFFICIAL DENIAL:** Attribution to specific protected patrons remained contested.  
**LATER DECLASSIFIED RECORD:** General knowledge/tolerance supported; exact finance chain unresolved.  
**CURRENT ASSESSMENT:** DOCUMENTED seizure and enforcement anomaly; CORROBORATED but not conclusive patronage claim.

## Scope conclusion

McCoy is strongest where his interviews converge with official admissions: narcotics existed inside the allied political economy; U.S. officials knew; suppression yielded to the war; and U.S.-backed aviation created transport opportunity. He is weaker where the argument requires exact cargo knowledge, individual ownership of laboratories, or a continuous chain to the U.S. civilian market. The source therefore raises the record to **corroborated carriage and strong systemic facilitation**, not to a proved CIA order to traffic.
