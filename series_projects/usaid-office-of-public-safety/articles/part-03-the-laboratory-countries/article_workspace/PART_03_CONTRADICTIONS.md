# PART 3 CONTRADICTIONS

## 1. Air America knowingly carried opium

**CLAIM A:** Air America aircraft carried opium for Vang Pao’s officers and other Lao figures.  
**SOURCE A:** McCoy interviews with Lao generals, village officials, farmers, and soldiers; pp. 213, 225, 235 and notes.  
**CLAIM B:** No evidence substantiated knowing institutional support by CIA or Air America.  
**SOURCE B:** 1972 CIA IG response; official CIA history by William Leary.  
**STRONGER SUPPORT:** Split. Some carriage is credible; institutional authorization is not established.  
**WHY:** Multiple firsthand accounts support occurrence, while official history concedes possible unknowing carriage and CIA knowledge of the setting. The missing evidence is flight-level authorization.  
**UNRESOLVED ISSUE:** Frequency, management knowledge, cargo controls, and specific flights.

## 2. CIA knew nothing versus CIA knew and deprioritized it

**CLAIM A:** Public/institutional denials imply no CIA drug involvement.  
**SOURCE A:** 1972 Helms response and CIA IG cover finding.  
**CLAIM B:** CIA knew about narcotics and did little because the war mission came first.  
**SOURCE B:** Leary official history quoting Colby/Westermeyer; later declassified CIA history.  
**STRONGER SUPPORT:** Claim B on knowledge/tolerance; Claim A on no proved direct policy.  
**WHY:** The two can coexist only if “involvement” is narrowly defined as direct trafficking.  
**UNRESOLVED ISSUE:** Which allegations were known at which level and what actions were considered.

## 3. McCoy’s structural thesis versus preexisting trade

**CLAIM A:** CIA intervention transformed and protected the regional narcotics economy.  
**SOURCE A:** McCoy.  
**CLAIM B:** Opium cultivation and regional trafficking long predated the CIA war.  
**SOURCE B:** historical record and official response.  
**STRONGER SUPPORT:** Both; they address origin versus transformation.  
**WHY:** Preexistence defeats a creation claim, not a facilitation, expansion, or protection claim.  
**UNRESOLVED ISSUE:** counterfactual scale without U.S. logistics and territorial sponsorship.

## 4. Webb’s broad public frame versus official findings

**CLAIM A:** Contra-connected suppliers formed the crucial pipeline behind the crack epidemic and benefited from protection.  
**SOURCE A:** *Dark Alliance*.  
**CLAIM B:** Blandón/Meneses were significant traffickers and made some Contra contributions, but did not cause the crack epidemic and generally were not protected by CIA/law enforcement.  
**SOURCE B:** DOJ OIG 1997; CIA OIG Vols. I–II.  
**STRONGER SUPPORT:** Official findings on national causation and reviewed protection claims; Webb on investigative linkage and anomalies that official reports partly confirm.  
**WHY:** Court and agency records support the narrow chain but not the national causal claim.  
**UNRESOLVED ISSUE:** unredacted asset/referral files and full Kerry evidence.

## 5. “No protection” versus Zavala intervention

**CLAIM A:** No systematic CIA protection of Contra-linked traffickers.  
**SOURCE A:** DOJ/CIA OIG overall conclusions.  
**CLAIM B:** CIA intervened concerning return of seized money in the Zavala case.  
**SOURCE B:** DOJ OIG.  
**STRONGER SUPPORT:** Both at their proper scale.  
**WHY:** A documented episode does not prove a universal policy; the universal denial does not erase the episode.  
**UNRESOLVED ISSUE:** CIA’s stated operational interest and effect on the prosecution.

## 6. Bureaucratic failure versus intelligence-driven accommodation

**CLAIM A:** Meneses/Blandón anomalies arose from ordinary resource, coordination, immigration, and prosecutorial failures.  
**SOURCE A:** DOJ OIG.  
**CLAIM B:** Political connections and intelligence equities preserved traffickers.  
**SOURCE B:** Webb, quoted officials, case chronology.  
**STRONGER SUPPORT:** Bureaucratic explanation for most reviewed episodes; accommodation remains plausible in specific cases.  
**WHY:** Official review found no general intervention but documented unusual outcomes and incomplete records.  
**UNRESOLVED ISSUE:** contemporaneous declination memoranda and CIA traces.

## 7. OPS as professional modernization versus counter-subversion instrument

**CLAIM A:** OPS modernized humane, technical policing and sometimes moderated abuses.  
**SOURCE A:** program self-descriptions and adviser defenses summarized by Motta.  
**CLAIM B:** Program doctrine made police the first line against communism, trained riot/counter-subversion methods, and built institutions that tortured and killed.  
**SOURCE B:** Motta; Brazil diplomatic and truth-commission records.  
**STRONGER SUPPORT:** Claim B for function and outcome; Claim A remains relevant to adviser intent and ordinary-policing content.  
**WHY:** Technical and political functions coexisted; “professional” equipment can increase coercive efficiency.  
**UNRESOLVED ISSUE:** unit-level allocation and adviser responses to named abuses.

## 8. Mitrione as torture instructor versus contested provenance

**CLAIM A:** Mitrione taught torture in Brazil, including on human subjects.  
**SOURCE A:** later accounts including *Brasil: Nunca Mais* tradition.  
**CLAIM B:** Evidence is contested; he left before the acute phase and Brazilian police already possessed torture expertise.  
**SOURCE B:** Motta, pp. 9–10.  
**STRONGER SUPPORT:** Claim B in the acquired record.  
**WHY:** The specific allegation lacks a locally verified contemporary witness/file.  
**UNRESOLVED ISSUE:** earliest source and corroborating testimony.

## 9. OPS–CIA integrated operation versus parallel programs

**CLAIM A:** USAID police missions were CIA fronts.  
**SOURCE A:** Later critics and broad insider accounts; no locally verified country-specific operational file establishes categorical identity.  
**CLAIM B:** AID held the overt role; covert aspects were separate; no acquired Brazil/MSU order makes the whole mission CIA-controlled.  
**SOURCE B:** NSAM 177; program records; Article 2.  
**STRONGER SUPPORT:** Parallel/liaison/access model.  
**WHY:** Direct liaison and proprietary training are documented, but categorical identity is not.  
**UNRESOLVED ISSUE:** named adviser cover and country-level liaison files.

## 10. Brazil program scale

**CLAIM A:** 641 overseas trainees and more than 52,000 trained in country.  
**SOURCE A:** series OPS records.  
**CLAIM B:** around 800 abroad and 100,000 in country.  
**SOURCE B:** Motta, p. 19.  
**STRONGER SUPPORT:** Neither until definitions/date ranges are reconciled; primary program totals should lead.  
**WHY:** “Training,” repeat attendance, program end date, and federal/state inclusion may differ.  
**UNRESOLVED ISSUE:** final project completion report and participant roster.

## 11. U.S. opposition to torture versus continued partnership

**CLAIM A:** U.S. diplomats criticized AI-5 and abuse, reduced aid/staff, and argued advisers promoted humane methods.  
**SOURCE A:** Motta; State records.  
**CLAIM B:** Stronger sanctions were avoided and relationships continued while officials documented systematic torture and killing.  
**SOURCE B:** 1970–73 State records; Motta.  
**STRONGER SUPPORT:** Both; they show internal conflict rather than exoneration or total approval.  
**WHY:** Concern affected tactics and visibility but did not displace strategic partnership.  
**UNRESOLVED ISSUE:** policy deliberations tying abuses to specific assistance decisions.

**WHY:** Brazil/IAPA and later Vietnam assignment are secondary-supported; SOA is not.
