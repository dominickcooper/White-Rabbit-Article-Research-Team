## Chronology verdict

The timelines repeatedly overlap, but overlap has different weights. In Brazil, covert political action, White House/State policy, military contingency planning, and police modernization were **parallel and mutually reinforcing**; overall policy coordination is documented, an OPS–CIA joint operational chain is not. In Laos, CIA war logistics and the opium economy were functionally intertwined; knowledge/tolerance is stronger than direct participation. In the Contra period, named traffickers, proxy politics, reporting gaps, and isolated interventions coexist, but official reviews contest a centrally managed trafficking program.

## Targeted additions

- **Jan.–Mar. 1982:** Attorney General William French Smith and DCI William Casey execute the CIA–DOJ crime-reporting MOU. Narcotics is omitted from mandatory nonemployee categories.
- **1984–89:** Alan Hyde and his company remain relevant to Contra avgas logistics while serious trafficking allegations are assessed but unresolved; use continues into late 1988/early 1989 because alternatives are scarce.
- **1985:** DOJ legal analysis recognizes that the MOU does not require reporting nonemployee narcotics offenses and recommends revision.
- **1985–86:** Morales/Southern Front exchange, Seal operation conflict, Hull allegations, contractor payments, and public/internal information discrepancies accumulate.
- **Oct. 1986–Dec. 1987:** OIG later identifies ten alleged-trafficker cases within the statutory reporting period and incomplete congressional notification.
- **1988/1989:** Kerry report concludes foreign-policy priorities interfered with drug enforcement and Contra-support infrastructure was used by traffickers.
- **Mar. 11, 1993:** CIA cable raises the well-documented CIA relationship as a problem for a contemplated counternarcotics prosecution/initiative involving Hyde.
- **Aug. 3, 1995:** Revised reporting rules broaden coverage to assets/independent contractors and narcotics.
- **1998:** CIA OIG Volumes I–II reject institutional conspiracy while documenting the underlying knowledge/retention/reporting pattern.
