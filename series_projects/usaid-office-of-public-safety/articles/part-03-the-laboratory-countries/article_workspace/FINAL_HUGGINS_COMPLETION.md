## Relevant material extracted

- Huggins says her study uses declassified State, Defense, AID, FBI, and CIA records and nearly 1,000 pages of U.S. and Brazilian government material.
- She describes AID/OPS monthly field reports as a principal primary source for adviser contacts, projects, accomplishments, and observations.
- She argues that public professionalization goals coexisted with covert internal-security purposes and that foreign police became useful to U.S. military and CIA intelligence interests.
- She documents Brazil as the largest Latin American recipient and a counterinsurgency model.
- The accessible excerpt records her interviews with Byron Engle, Jack Goin, Robert Komer, former OPS advisers, and former CIA official Robert Amory.
