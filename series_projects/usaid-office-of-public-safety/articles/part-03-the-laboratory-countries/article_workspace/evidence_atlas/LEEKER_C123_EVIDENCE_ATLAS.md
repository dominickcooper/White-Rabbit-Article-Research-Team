# Joe F. Leeker — Air America C-123 Evidence Atlas

Primary reviewed source: Joe F. Leeker, “Air America: Fairchild C-123 Providers,” UTD History of Aviation Collection; local official Texas Tech Vietnam Center packet.

## LEEKER-001 — AID-439-342 fleet

**PAGE:** Leeker pp. 9, 15, 21–32; local packet PDF p. 148 for operational narrative  
**ARCHIVE REFERENCES:** UTD/Bisson/B5 reels 23, 24, 27, 29  
**AIRCRAFT:** 374, 636, 690, 386, 524, 616, 648 and other C-123s  
**EXACT CLAIM:** Multiple C-123 aircraft served AID-439-342 through implementation orders and modifications from 1969 to 1973.  
**EVIDENCE TYPE:** Archival aircraft/contract history citing surviving orders and records.  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** **DOCUMENTED FACT.**

## LEEKER-002 — Administrative boundaries versus daily use

**PAGE:** Local packet PDF p. 148  
**EXACT CLAIM:** Aircraft nominally assigned to Vientiane/USAID and other customers often flew overlapping mission types, many staging or collecting cargo at Udorn and operating throughout Laos.  
**CARGO / TASKS:** Animals, rice, produce, passengers, troops, refugees, fuel, ammunition, rockets, bombs, aviation gasoline, and T-28 munitions.  
**WHAT THIS ESTABLISHES:** A dual-use operational network whose practical use exceeded clean contract categories.  
**WHAT THIS DOES NOT ESTABLISH:** Narcotics intent or a specific drug load.  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** **DOCUMENTED FACT.**

## LEEKER-003 — Long Tieng and Hmong-war geography

**PAGE:** Leeker pp. 15, 26, 30–31; local packet PDF p. 148  
**EXACT CLAIM:** AID-contracted C-123s repeatedly entered Long Tieng and other Laos bases, while the fleet also supported Peppergrinder and military missions.  
**WHAT THIS ESTABLISHES:** USAID-supported aviation and the CIA/Hmong operational theater shared aircraft types, bases, cargo nodes, and personnel systems.  
**WHAT THIS DOES NOT ESTABLISH:** That a named aircraft carried opium on a named date.  
**EVIDENCE LEVEL:** **DOCUMENTED OVERT INFRASTRUCTURE; STRONG CONVERGENCE FINDING.**
