# CIA Contra Inspector General Evidence Atlas

Primary reviewed sources: CIA OIG *Report of Investigation*, Volumes I and II (local PDFs), including the signed 1982 CIA–DOJ MOU in Vol. II.

## Source-integrity and method note

Each card separates what the cited passage establishes from what it does not. Page numbers are PDF pages unless a printed page is explicitly identified. Short quotations are locator phrases, not substitutes for the surrounding passage. Repeated authors do not count as independent when they share an underlying witness or report.

Official conclusions are classified as OFFICIAL POSITION unless independently corroborated. Admissions and case facts inside the reports are separately weighted.

# Official-denial dossier: institutional Contra-drug conspiracy

**OFFICIAL POSITION:** CIA and its employees did not conspire with or assist drug traffickers to raise funds for the Contras.

**AGENCY:** CIA Office of Inspector General.

**DATE:** 1998 report investigating 1980s conduct.

**EXACT CLAIM DENIED:** An institutional CIA conspiracy or assistance program linking narcotics trafficking to Contra finance.

**SCOPE OF DENIAL:** Institutional conspiracy/assistance and the allegations assigned to Volumes I and II; it is not a finding that no Contra-associated person trafficked, that no CIA contact knew allegations, or that every allegation was reported and acted upon.

**PERSONNEL INCLUDED:** CIA employees and components named in the report; assets, contractors, Contra-related actors, law-enforcement and other witnesses examined case by case.

**PERSONNEL EXCLUDED:** The report does not furnish a single complete universe of all Contra supporters and trafficking witnesses. Availability and investigative depth varied by case; underlying interview files are not local for an independent omission audit.

**TIME PERIOD:** Principally the Contra program and associated 1980s activity, investigated in the late 1990s.

**DEFINITION OF “INVOLVEMENT”:** Conspiracy with or assistance to traffickers is narrower than knowledge, contact, reporting failure, enforcement deference, retention of an operationally useful relationship, or indirect logistical benefit.

**EVIDENCE REVIEWED:** CIA operational and security files, cables, crimes-reporting rules, prior investigations, interviews, and case records described throughout both volumes.

**EVIDENCE NOT REVIEWED:** The local corpus lacks the complete underlying interview/deposition files, all classified annexes or redacted material, the full Kerry exhibit archive, and every law-enforcement prosecution file.

**WITNESSES INTERVIEWED:** Numerous CIA personnel and outside witnesses identified in individual case narratives; the local report, not a complete interview-index archive, is available.

**WITNESSES OMITTED:** Cannot be exhaustively determined from the published local copy; deceased, unavailable, unlocated, or uncooperative case witnesses must be assessed from the underlying files.

**RECORDS AVAILABLE:** Published OIG Volumes I and II, including legal/reporting analysis and the reproduced 1982 CIA–DOJ MOU.

**RECORDS UNAVAILABLE / DESTROYED / UNKNOWN:** Complete interview exhibits, all unredacted operational records, and the full interagency case/prosecution files are not local; their complete preservation status is unknown.

**ADMISSIONS INSIDE THE REPORT:** No agencywide drug-allegation regulation until late 1987; inconsistent practices; a 1982 nonemployee narcotics reporting gap; notification failures; continued Morales contacts; a restricted Hyde relationship; a cable warning that a CIA relationship could complicate prosecution; and Fiers's acknowledgement of significant Southern Front cocaine evidence.

**INDEPENDENT EVIDENCE SUPPORTING THE DENIAL:** No acquired primary record orders CIA personnel to traffic drugs or establishes a centrally administered narcotics-finance program. The Kerry report likewise documents grave case-level problems without proving a headquarters trafficking order.

**INDEPENDENT EVIDENCE CONTRADICTING THE DENIAL:** Kerry's contractor, Morales, Hull, Bueso, Seal, Noriega, and enforcement findings; official court and law-enforcement records in those chains; the OIG's own internal admissions. These contradict blanket exculpation more than the narrow no-conspiracy conclusion.

**WHAT THE DENIAL ACTUALLY RESOLVES:** It weighs strongly against a claim of an institutional CIA–Contra narcotics conspiracy or direct Agency assistance as a general program.

**WHAT IT DOES NOT RESOLVE:** Case-level knowledge, tolerance, reporting discretion, continued relationships, protection decisions, enforcement subordination, use of drug-linked contractors, or indirect operational benefit.

**WEIGHT:** High for the proposition actually tested; **OFFICIAL POSITION** rather than blanket exculpation.

# Official-denial dossier: Ross / Blandón / Meneses and the crack-epidemic claim

**OFFICIAL POSITION:** CIA OIG Volume I found no CIA relationship with Ricky Ross, Oscar Danilo Blandón, or Norwin Meneses, no evidence that their drug dealing was undertaken for the Contras, and no evidence that CIA caused the crack epidemic through them.

**AGENCY:** CIA Office of Inspector General.

**DATE:** 1998 report examining 1980s–1990s allegations.

**EXACT CLAIM DENIED:** Direct CIA relationship, Contra-directed California trafficking, and the broad causal proposition that this network caused the crack epidemic.

**SCOPE OF DENIAL:** These named California/Nicaragua principals and the *Dark Alliance* causal allegations; it does not adjudicate every Contra-drug network in Central or South America.

**PERSONNEL INCLUDED:** Ross, Blandón, Meneses, relevant CIA personnel/components, and individuals discussed in the OIG case reconstruction.

**PERSONNEL EXCLUDED:** Other Southern Front, contractor, Panama, Honduras, and Costa Rica cases belong to different Vol. II investigations and cannot be exculpated by this finding.

**TIME PERIOD:** The principals' relevant 1980s trafficking and later investigative/prosecution history.

**DEFINITION OF “INVOLVEMENT”:** Relationship with CIA, trafficking for the Contra cause, and Agency causal responsibility for crack distribution—not mere Contra sympathy or limited claimed donations.

**EVIDENCE REVIEWED:** CIA record searches and interviews described by OIG, court/prosecution history, the principals' accounts, and Webb's allegations.

**EVIDENCE NOT REVIEWED:** The local corpus does not contain every underlying sealed court record, full interview file, or all source-identifying classified material.

**WITNESSES INTERVIEWED:** Principals and relevant officials are discussed through interviews/statements in the report; a complete underlying interview archive is not local.

**WITNESSES OMITTED:** Cannot be exhaustively audited from the published report alone.

**RECORDS AVAILABLE:** OIG Volume I and local *Dark Alliance*; selected court and official facts reproduced or cited in those sources.

**RECORDS UNAVAILABLE / DESTROYED / UNKNOWN:** Complete underlying OIG interview exhibits and sealed/underlying case files are not local; destruction status is unknown.

**ADMISSIONS INSIDE THE REPORT:** The principals' cocaine trafficking is real; some Contra sympathies and limited claimed support/donations existed; the amount and motivation were not substantiated as Webb's broader thesis required.

**INDEPENDENT EVIDENCE SUPPORTING THE DENIAL:** Court and prosecution records establish drug crimes but do not independently establish a CIA relationship or Agency creation of crack demand.

**INDEPENDENT EVIDENCE CONTRADICTING THE DENIAL:** Webb documents real trafficking and Contra overlap; Cabezas/“Ivan Gómez” evidence belongs to a different, partially corroborated network and does not by itself create a relationship with these three principals.

**WHAT THE DENIAL ACTUALLY RESOLVES:** It is strong contrary evidence against using Ross, Blandón, and Meneses as the completed CIA-to-U.S.-crack bridge.

**WHAT IT DOES NOT RESOLVE:** Other Contra-associated trafficking, CIA handling of other assets/contacts, the Morales/Hyde/Hull cases, or the system-level tolerance/protection thesis.

**WEIGHT:** Very high and appropriately constraining — **OFFICIAL POSITION** with independent court-record consistency on the absence of a proved CIA relationship.
