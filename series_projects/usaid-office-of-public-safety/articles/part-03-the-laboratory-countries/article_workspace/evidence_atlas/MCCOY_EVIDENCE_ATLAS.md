# McCoy Evidence Atlas

Primary reviewed source: Alfred W. McCoy, *The Politics of Heroin in Southeast Asia* (local PDF). This atlas gives special treatment to the Buell chain and its footnotes.

## Source-integrity and method note

Each card separates what the cited passage establishes from what it does not. Page numbers are PDF pages unless a printed page is explicitly identified. Short quotations are locator phrases, not substitutes for the surrounding passage. Repeated authors do not count as independent when they share an underlying witness or report.

The local book is complete enough for the relevant Laos chapter. The deepest remaining gap is not McCoy's pagination but access to several underlying interviews and Schanche/Lewallen materials.

## MC-001

**SOURCE:** Alfred W. McCoy, *The Politics of Heroin in Southeast Asia* (local PDF)

**AUTHOR / AGENCY:** Alfred W. McCoy

**DATE:** 1972 edition in local scan

**PAGE:** PDF 210–211; notes PDF 343

**FOOTNOTE / ARCHIVE ID:** nn.54–56, 61; Ouane Rattikone interview; William Young interview

**COUNTRY / REGION:** Laos / Golden Triangle

**PEOPLE:** Phoumi Nosavan; Ouane Rattikone; William Young

**ORGANIZATIONS:** Royal Lao Army; CIA

**PROGRAM:** CIA-supported Lao forces

**EXACT CLAIM:** After U.S. aid was cut to Phoumi, McCoy describes Phoumi and successor Ouane turning more directly to opium revenue; Ouane headed the semi-official opium administration and imported Burmese opium through a Shan force tied to CIA work.

**SHORT QUOTATION:** “appointed to the administration of the Laos opium trade”

**EVIDENCE TYPE:** investigative secondary source based on named interviews

**USAID / AID CONNECTION:** None established in this passage.

**CIA CONNECTION:** The Shan intermediary and Lao commanders operated in the CIA-supported security environment.

**NARCOTICS CONNECTION:** Opium import, taxation, and administration.

**LOGISTICS CONNECTION:** None established in this passage.

**FINANCIAL CONNECTION:** Opium revenue supported commanders and political power.

**POLITICAL / MILITARY CONNECTION:** Royal Lao command and CIA-backed war.

**U.S. ENDPOINT CONNECTION:** None established in this passage.

**WHAT THIS ESTABLISHES:** Named allied commanders and opium finance were intertwined.

**WHAT THIS DOES NOT ESTABLISH:** CIA headquarters ordered the opium trade.

**CORROBORATING SOURCES:** Scott, *American War Machine*, PDF 131, 138–139; official Senate Laos chapter, PDF 16.

**CONTRADICTING SOURCES:** No official source denies Ouane's general involvement; official materials deny an Agency trafficking policy.

**UNDERLYING SOURCES TO ACQUIRE:** McCoy interview tapes/notes; Royal Lao opium ledgers.

**ARTICLE 3 VALUE:** High: local-ally, narcotics, finance, and CIA-relationship links.
