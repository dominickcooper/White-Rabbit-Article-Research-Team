# Final Thailand ARD / Mae Salong Road Chain

## Overt infrastructure

| Field | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Exact road | Spur/service road into the Mae Salong hill area; an exact project title or number was not located. |
| Project number | **NOT LOCATED** |
| Date | Construction is independently dated to about 1969; McCoy says Seabees worked for roughly a year. |
| Funding source | McCoy attributes the road to USAID's Accelerated Rural Development program. Official FRUS records document substantial AID ARD funding in northern Thailand. |
| Construction organization | U.S. Navy Seabees, corroborated independently. |
| Official purpose | Security/counterinsurgency access and rural development. |
| Area served | Chiang Rai highlands and Mae Salong, a major KMT settlement. |

## Independent corroboration

- FRUS 1964–68, vol. XXVII, doc. 344 describes ARD as a security/counterinsurgency program using pioneer/service roads and records Seabee assistance to Thai Border Patrol Police construction in hill areas.
- FRUS 1969–76, vol. XX, doc. 50 records continued ARD road funding and security purposes.
- FRUS doc. 173 places KMT/Chinese Irregular Force resettlement in Chiang Rai/Mae Salong, describes road building in newly opened areas, and records U.S. monitoring of promises by Li and Tuan to stay out of opium.
- A 1975 *Journal of the Siam Society* field study says Seabees bulldozed a road into the Mae Salong hills in 1969, after which Chinese stores, markets, and daily truck traffic expanded.
- Contemporary congressional and U.S. intelligence material identified Mae Salong as a narcotics transshipment/refining center.

## Narcotics layer

McCoy, PDF p. 282, reports that the road lowered KMT transport costs and that supplies moved uphill while opium, morphine base, and heroin moved down. This is not footnoted. The independent field study corroborates the road's commercial/logistical effect but does not identify drug cargo.

## Verdict

**STRONG INFERENCE.** AID-backed counterinsurgency road building, Seabee construction, Mae Salong/KMT presence, U.S. knowledge of KMT narcotics connections, and the road's commercial transformation are independently documented. Actual drug movement on that road remains McCoy's field attribution, not a manifest or separate firsthand account. The missing project number is a document-quality gap, not a break in the functional infrastructure chain.
