## Network-level finding

Leeker's narrative says official distinctions were often unclear: aircraft assigned under USAID and other contracts flew multiple mission types, many staging from Udorn, while most cargo was picked up there. Loads included animals, rice, passengers, troops, refugees, fuel, ammunition, rockets, bombs, aviation gasoline, and T-28 munitions. Long Tieng received major military loads. By 1971 the fleet was nominally Vientiane/USAID; in 1973 it shifted to Air Force control.

## Verdict

**DOCUMENTED OVERT INFRASTRUCTURE AND DUAL-USE OPERATIONAL NETWORK.** The same aircraft family, bases, crews, and administrative system served USAID relief/agriculture, CIA-backed territory, and military resupply. This does not prove a specific narcotics load. It materially strengthens the capability, access, and convergence arrows without requiring a heroin manifest.
