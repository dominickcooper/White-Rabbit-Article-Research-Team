# Outline — The Laboratory Countries

1. Open with the 1955 South Vietnam national-police blueprint.
2. Establish chronology: FOA/ICA–USOM–MSU preceded OPS.
3. Translate communications, files, fingerprints, laboratory and equipment into operational capacity.
4. Use Hilsman's 1961 memorandum to establish the explicit intelligence/counterinsurgency purpose.
5. Follow MSU personnel and Smuckler's bounded account of CIA secondees and the transition.
6. Move to Brazil as the mature OPS program: quantify advisers, trainees and hardware.
7. Trace data, radio coordination and riot-control capability into political policing.
8. Separate documented torture from unproven OPS direction or OBAN control.
9. Test the ordinary professionalization rationale against the internal-defense purpose.
10. Transition to the individual adviser/responsibility problem reserved for Part 4.

## Country selection
Vietnam contributes the pre-OPS prototype and an explicit intelligence architecture. Brazil contributes quantifiable OPS capacity under authoritarian consolidation and the sharpest capability-to-repression attribution test. Dominican Republic and Chile were excluded because the reviewed evidence did not add a comparably distinct mechanism.

## Guardrails
- Never call the 1955 MSU project OPS.
- Treat Smuckler as a retrospective participant witness.
- Distinguish political crowd-control links from torture allegations.
- Do not imply American operational command.
- Keep Uruguay and the Mitrione allegations out of Part 3.
