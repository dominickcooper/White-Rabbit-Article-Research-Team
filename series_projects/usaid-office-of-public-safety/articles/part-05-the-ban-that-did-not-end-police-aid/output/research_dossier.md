# Research dossier: The Ban That Did Not End Police Aid

## WHAT THIS PART ADDS

Readers learn that the operative AID cutoff came in December 1973, that OPS's 17 programs and 95 overseas advisers phased out by August 1974, and that original Section 660 took effect July 1, 1975 with deliberate Justice/FBI/DEA/narcotics and contract exceptions.

## EARLIER FINDINGS REQUIRED FOR CONTEXT

Parts 1–4 established the AID-led internal-defense architecture, CIA interfaces, country capacity and the Uruguay controversy. These explain the backlash without requiring repetition.

## NEW FINDINGS

- Abourezk's 1972 restriction failed but supplied the earliest serious floor challenge located.
- His 1973 language reached appropriations under any law; the final rule was FAA-fund specific.
- The Senate knowingly protected limited Justice, FBI, LEAA and DEA activity after questioning its scope.
- AID generally completed the phaseout; five advisers changed to narcotics work.
- GAO found narcotics equipment grew from $2.2m in FY1973 to $12.5m in FY1974.
- GAO found indirect military benefits through shared facilities, personnel rotations, dual-role units and martial-law policing.
- Alleged program renaming and an en bloc Justice transfer were not proven.
- Separately funded CIA coverage remained unclear.

## EVIDENCE-WEIGHTED CONCLUSIONS

Congress successfully ended the classic AID/OPS model. It did not abolish all federal foreign-police cooperation. Immediate continuations comprised explicit exceptions, indirect military benefits and ambiguous boundaries rather than proven wholesale evasion.

## RESPONSIBILITY ASSESSMENT

Congress bears responsibility for both the prohibition and deliberately retained exceptions. AID generally complied. Questionable procurement and category decisions require case-level inquiry; available evidence does not establish organized circumvention.

## CONTRARY EVIDENCE

The restriction materially ended programs, advisers and academy operations. Later similar functions do not prove institutional succession. Narcotics assistance was expressly authorized.

## COMPETING EXPLANATIONS

Successful reform; bureaucratic adaptation; deliberate functional preservation; evasion. Evidence supports successful institutional termination plus authorized/ambiguous survival, not the strongest evasion claim.
