# Research dossier — Part 2: The Academy and the Agency

## WHAT THIS PART ADDS
It replaces a vague CIA/USAID association with a sourced mechanism map covering the proprietary, course sequence, knowledge compartments, assessment channel, liaison and specialist instruction.

## EARLIER FINDINGS REQUIRED FOR CONTEXT
Part 1 established that NSAM 177 assigned AID the overt police mission while excluding covert aspects. No other Part 1 chronology is needed.

## NEW FINDINGS
IPS proprietary status; the 14+4-week sequence; operational distribution of trainee information; daily Police Group liaison; a long-term redacted CIA personnel detail at OPS leadership; TIC #7's dates, content, cohort and funding.

## NEW SOURCES
Rockefeller Commission printed p. 235; the Pike reproduction's foreign-police section; Family Jewels PDFs 105–106, 218 and 605–612; visually checked CIA doctrine-file PDFs 1, 5–6 and 55–56.

## NEW PEOPLE / ORGANIZATIONS
James Angleton; CIA Police Group; IPS; CIA Technical Services Division; Dade County Police Bomb Squad; unnamed CIA OPS-director detailee.

## Bottom finding
The reviewed official record supports a bounded pipeline: OPS ran overt training; IPS, a CIA proprietary, supplied a concealed four-week follow-on and assessed students; OPS cooperated in transferring lists and biographies to CIA components; the CIA Police Group maintained daily liaison with OPS/IPA and arranged participation and follow-on activity. The record supports spotting and operational referral, while actual later recruitment remains uncounted.

## Provenance findings

### CIA-RDP80B01083A000100120014-7
- **Container:** CIA Reading Room release, approved 2005-04-22; originally SECRET.
- **PDF p. 1:** May 7, 1962 CIA memorandum to the Deputy Director (Intelligence), through the Assistant Director for National Estimates, from James A. Brammell for the Assistant Director, Research and Reports. Subject: “Military Geography as Essential Intelligence for Counterinsurgency.” Refers to USIB-D-63.6/1 and an enclosure.
- **PDF p. 6 onward:** separately headed SECRET “DRAFT S/P: 5/8/62,” *General US Doctrine on Counterinsurgency (Internal Defense) Operations*. It delineates roles of State, Defense/JCS, AID, CIA and USIA.
- **PDF pp. 55–56 / printed pp. 50–51:** identifies unresolved operational concepts and says roles/missions remain to be supplied from the “Kitchen-Moose paper.”
- **Judgment:** a released file package containing a CIA cover memo and incomplete interagency draft doctrine. It is not safely described as final U.S. policy or a single authored CIA doctrine. “S/P” suggests a policy-planning origin, but the reviewed pages do not establish a named author.

### Pike material
- **Container:** CIA-RDP03-01541R000200420004-8, a CIA-held press reproduction of the suppressed House Select Committee report.
- **Status:** contemporaneous congressional investigative work; not a formally published/adopted House report.
- **Core propositions:** CIA operated IPS as a Washington-area proprietary until late 1973; OPS's 14-week course was followed by four weeks at IPS under an AID contract; students were unaware; a handful of AID officials including the OPS director knew; instructors identified pro-American students; lists and biographies went to CIA components; no apparent recruitment occurred in the United States; as many as 5,000 officers from 100+ countries received CIA training.

### Rockefeller Commission
- **Container:** June 1975 presidential commission report, chapter on CIA relationships with other agencies.
- **Printed p. 235 / local PDF p. 12:** CIA school began in 1952; Panama school opened in 1960 with CIA faculty and State/OPS costs; activities moved to the United States in 1963 under a commercial contract with a domestic corporation that was a CIA front; AID administrator did not know, OPS chief apparently did; school and corporation ended in 1973; about 5,000 graduates; firearms/equipment sales ranged about $6,000–$48,000 annually.
- **Judgment:** independent official confirmation of the proprietary arrangement and scale, though this passage does not name IPS.

## Claim judgments

### IPS was a CIA proprietary — high confidence
Pike names it; Rockefeller independently describes the contracted CIA front. Exact corporate registration, named officers and full funding remain unresolved.

### CIA-linked follow-on training — high confidence
Pike documents the 14+4-week sequence. Rockefeller supplies corroborating chronology. It is not established that every IPA graduate passed through IPS.

### Trainee knowledge — high confidence for general concealment
Pike says students were not informed. Scale estimate also characterizes recipients as unwitting. Individual exceptions cannot be excluded.

### AID knowledge — high confidence for compartmentation
OPS director plus a handful reportedly knew; AID administrator reportedly did not. Family Jewels adds a redacted CIA personnel detail at OPS leadership level.

### Assessment/recruitment — high for assessment, unresolved for completed recruitment
Instructor notes, lists, biographies and operational distribution support spotting. Pike explicitly says no apparent recruitment in the United States. Later outcomes remain unavailable.

### Scale — medium-high
Pike: as many as 5,000 from 100+ countries. Rockefeller: about 5,000 across the school and special courses over 20+ years. Approximation and population definition must accompany the figure.

### Explosives instruction — high for TIC #7, rejected as universal claim
Angleton memorandum directly documents a 26-person specialist course with practical explosives, incendiaries, sabotage and booby-trap work, framed as counterterrorist investigation.

## Conventional explanation and contrary evidence
Communist insurgency and terrorism were real policy concerns; local police capability could reduce direct U.S. military involvement; liaison with foreign services is ordinary intelligence practice; concealment could protect officers and relationships. Rockefeller found the foreign-police education itself within CIA authority. These points do not erase the concealed contractor, the assessment system or undisclosed transfer of trainee information.

## Unresolved gaps
- IPS certificate of incorporation, nominal directors, facility addresses and detailed financial records.
- Identity of the redacted OPS-director detailee.
- Complete student roster and selection criteria.
- Evidence of later recruitment approaches and successful assets.
- USAID and CIA end reports for TIC #7.

## EVIDENCE-WEIGHTED CONCLUSIONS
IPS proprietary status, the training handoff, Police Group liaison and TIC #7 are documented at high confidence. The circa-5,000 total is an official approximation. Completed recruitment remains unresolved.

## RESPONSIBILITY ASSESSMENT
AID/OPS owned the overt program and contract interface; IPA hosted public training; IPS was CIA-controlled; Police Group coordinated operational exploitation; CIA technical staff conducted the second specialist phase.

## CONTRARY EVIDENCE
Rockefeller found the education itself legally proper; Pike found no apparent recruitment in the United States; ordinary police subjects dominated the general-school description; Family Jewels says Police Group did not direct all field liaison.

## COMPETING EXPLANATIONS
The conventional account is allied police professionalization and counterterrorism under Cold War pressure. The critical account is that the overt academy supplied concealed intelligence access. The records support elements of both.
