# FACT AUDIT V2

This report records only substantive red flags and consequential inferential boundaries from the sentence-level audit. Routine documentary statements that verified cleanly are omitted.

## TWO-FOLD NAME INTERPRETATION

**CLAIM:** The existence of two components explained the cryptonym TWO-FOLD.

**CURRENT WORDING:** V1: “That was the ‘two-fold’ design.”

**EVIDENCE:** The May 25, 1973 memorandum and Rockefeller Commission document two components. No naming memorandum or cryptonym explanation was recovered.

**ASSESSMENT:** Unsupported etymological inference.

**ACTION TAKEN:** Replaced with: “The surviving record gives us two distinct halves of the operation. It does not tell us why the cryptonym TWO-FOLD was chosen.”

## MAY–JULY 1973 FOREIGN ENDPOINT

**CLAIM:** TWO-FOLD’s foreign component continued after the domestic component ended.

**CURRENT WORDING:** V1 treated the May 1973 continuation instruction as proof that the foreign component survived beyond Rockefeller’s July endpoint.

**EVIDENCE:** Osborn’s May memorandum records approval to preserve foreign narcotics collection; Rockefeller says the two covered-agent arrangements ended in July 1973.

**ASSESSMENT:** Documented authorization, unresolved implementation.

**ACTION TAKEN:** Made the conflict explicit and listed the three live possibilities: July termination, different personnel, or another channel.

## PARALLEL CIA ACCESS

**CLAIM:** CIA had covert access into two parallel policing systems.

**CURRENT WORDING:** “This project sat between two policing systems to which CIA had secured covert access.”

**EVIDENCE:** CIA identifies OPS director Byron Engle as its employee under cover; CIA and Rockefeller document proprietary recruitment and covered administration for BNDD.

**ASSESSMENT:** Strong inference directly grounded in separate primary records. “Access” is supported; institutional identity is not.

**ACTION TAKEN:** Retained the claim and immediately named the two documented mechanisms.

## BYRON ENGLE PLACEMENT

**CLAIM:** CIA placed one of its officers at the top of OPS under cover.

**CURRENT WORDING:** “CIA had one of its own officers at the top of the Office of Public Safety, serving under cover.”

**EVIDENCE:** CIA’s September 29, 1976 damage assessment says Engle was “in actual fact a CIA employee” and “retired under cover”; his OPS directorship is independently established.

**ASSESSMENT:** Documented fact and strong direct inference. The covert command/reporting arrangement remains unknown.

**ACTION TAKEN:** Retained strong wording; compressed the limitation into the next paragraph.

## PORTABLE FUNCTIONS / FUNCTIONAL MIGRATION

**CLAIM:** Foreign-police and intelligence functions migrated through the narcotics channel as OPS contracted.

**CURRENT WORDING:** V1 described a set of “portable functions” and later called narcotics a “politically protected” channel.

**EVIDENCE:** BNDD already performed foreign liaison/training; CIA supplied clandestine instruction; Section 660 preserved international narcotics activities; Vientiane A-102 assigned an OPS narcotics-advisory role to DEA; CIA-experienced personnel entered DEA intelligence through separate arrangements.

**ASSESSMENT:** Strong inference, with Laos as the documented transfer and broader migration supported by converging chronology rather than a single transfer order.

**ACTION TAKEN:** Replaced “politically protected” with “statutorily preserved,” made Laos the explicit case, and stated that no wholesale OPS-to-DEA transfer is documented.

## PATRICK FULLER

## JOSEPH DIGENNARO

**CLAIM:** DiGennaro participated in an overseas rendition and had authority to kill resisting targets.

**CURRENT WORDING:** V1 paraphrased an “overseas seizure and forcible return” and said he alleged lethal authority.

**EVIDENCE:** Valentine attributes to DiGennaro a specific Colombia account involving seizure, drugging, handcuffing and return to the United States. Valentine’s surrounding narrative, footnoted to the interview, characterizes the assignment as kidnapping and killing if targets resisted. Official records establish only the existence of two commercially covered foreign agents targeting international traffickers.

**ASSESSMENT:** Disputed firsthand testimony with partial contextual corroboration, but no operational corroboration.

**ACTION TAKEN:** Used the specific Colombia account, attributed the broader lethal description to Valentine’s account, and stated the official floor and unresolved elements in the same passage.

## ANTHONY TRIPONI

**CLAIM:** A covert Fuller reporting channel persisted into 1974.

**CURRENT WORDING:** V1 called the statement evidence of a “contested wind-down.”

**EVIDENCE:** A contemporaneous inspection memorandum proves Triponi made the allegation. Inspectors also documented acute illness, difficulty judging reality and incoherence.

**ASSESSMENT:** Documented allegation, medium-low confidence as to truth.

**ACTION TAKEN:** Narrowed “wind-down” to “disputed residue” and explicitly said the record does not prove intact domestic continuation.

## SEVEN CIA-TO-DEA TRANSFERS

**CLAIM:** CIA experience and relationships moved into DEA intelligence in 1973–74.

**CURRENT WORDING:** V1 said personnel and expertise continued to move after TWO-FOLD.

**EVIDENCE:** Seven John Does administrative records document recruitment of seven former CIA special agents to build DEA intelligence; some testimony describes continued foreign liaison or informant relationships.

**ASSESSMENT:** Documented personnel transfer; relationship continuity is documented testimony/strong inference. No proof the seven were the 19 TWO-FOLD recruits.

**ACTION TAKEN:** Preserved the bridge, stated the later arrangement was separate, and specified what moved without treating it as continuation of TWO-FOLD.

## LAOS HANDOFF

**CLAIM:** An OPS narcotics-advisory function was handed to DEA.

**CURRENT WORDING:** “OPS was leaving the narcotics-advisory role; DEA was scheduled to take it over.”

**EVIDENCE:** Vientiane A-102 states OPS narcotics assistance was to terminate after FY1975 and assigns DEA advisory responsibility involving Lao police and intelligence bodies.

**ASSESSMENT:** Documented country-specific transfer. It cannot establish a global institutional succession.

**ACTION TAKEN:** Retained strong wording, added the official public link and repeated the geographic limit once.

## CASEY IMMUNITY WARNING

**CLAIM:** CIA surveillance could make traffickers effectively immune from U.S. prosecution.

**CURRENT WORDING:** Casey said targets could become “virtually immune from prosecution in the United States” and receive “de facto immunity.”

**EVIDENCE:** Original internal DEA memorandum P5-0141. The public CounterPunch page reproduces the wording but is not the evidentiary basis.

**ASSESSMENT:** Documented internal DEA assessment, not an adjudicated legal finding about every target.

**ACTION TAKEN:** Identified Casey as the author, retained the exact language, and disclosed the public-reproduction/primary-record distinction.

## STRICKLER CASE EXAMPLES

**CLAIM:** CIA surveillance or recruitment caused release, non-indictment and blocked prosecution in specific cases.

**CURRENT WORDING:** V1 narrated the examples as events.

**EVIDENCE:** Strickler’s February 11, 1977 DEA memorandum P5-0142 describes the redacted cases. The underlying surveillance, arrest and prosecution files are absent.

**ASSESSMENT:** Documented DEA characterization of events; underlying case outcomes are not independently reconstructed.

**ACTION TAKEN:** Added an explicit source boundary before the examples and used “Strickler wrote,” “characterized,” and “in DEA’s assessment” where appropriate.
