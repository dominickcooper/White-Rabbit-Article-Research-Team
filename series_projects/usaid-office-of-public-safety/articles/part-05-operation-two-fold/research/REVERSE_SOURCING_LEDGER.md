# Part 5 reverse-sourcing ledger

Claims by Douglas Valentine, Peter Dale Scott and other secondary authors will be mapped
to the exact cited or implied primary record where possible. Outcomes: `VERIFIED FROM
PRIMARY`, `PARTIALLY VERIFIED`, `PRIMARY RECORD CONTRADICTS`, `CITATION MISSING`, or
`PRIMARY RECORD NOT YET LOCATED`.

| Secondary claim / map | Secondary source | Cited or implied record | Result | Assessment / remaining gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CIA helped BNDD create a covert internal inspection/counterintelligence unit beginning in 1970. | Valentine, *Strength of the Pack*, ch. 10 (P5-0146) | Rockefeller Commission ch. 17, pp. 232–35; CIA 25 May 1973 memo, MORI 1451843 | **VERIFIED FROM PRIMARY** | Official records establish the request, recruitment/training mechanism and mission. |
| The program recruited 19 or “twenty-odd” people through a proprietary. | Valentine, P5-0146 | Rockefeller Commission ch. 17 | **PARTIALLY VERIFIED** | Commission states 19 recruits between Dec. 1970 and July 1973. It does not supply a roster or validate every name/interview. |
| TWO-FOLD had a second, covert operational facet against international traffickers. | Valentine, P5-0146; *CIA as Organized Crime* (P5-0144/145) | CIA 25 May 1973 memo; Rockefeller Commission ch. 17 | **PARTIALLY VERIFIED** | CIA record says activity was “extended” to covert acquisition of nonofficial-cover BNDD Staff Agents against principal international traffickers; Commission documents two covered agents. Kidnapping/assassination claims are not established by these records. |
| CIA maintained control/contact with inspection recruits after BNDD entry through covert reporting. | Valentine interviews with Fuller/Gutensohn/Burke; P5-0146 | Rockefeller Commission ch. 17 says CIA relinquished all control/contact; P5-0147 Triponi allegation | **PRIMARY RECORD CONFLICT / NOT RESOLVED** | Published official account conflicts with interview-derived allegations. Fuller's files, participant testimony and coded communications are required. |
| TWO-FOLD continued after the 1973 termination decision. | Valentine, P5-0144/146 | CIA Family Jewels memo/action notation, MORI 1451843; Rockefeller Commission; later program records | **PARTIALLY VERIFIED FROM PRIMARY** | The May 1973 written disposition terminated paragraph 2 but expressly continued paragraph 3 for foreign narcotics-intelligence assignments. This proves selective continuation, but not Valentine's full account, roster, duration beyond that authorization, or claim that the domestic inspection activity persisted. The Commission's July termination date requires reconciliation. |
| The seven MSPB appellants were TWO-FOLD personnel. | Possible inference from Valentine narrative and corpus juxtaposition | P5-0001/02 | **CITATION MISSING / UNSUPPORTED** | MSPB records identify a 1973–74 NIO transfer cohort with different stated purpose/timing; no local primary record assigns them to TWO-FOLD. |
| Thomas C. Tripodi/Tripodi was an early CIA–narcotics bridge and corruption investigator. | Valentine, P5-0146 | P5-0002; FBN roster P5-0077 | **VERIFIED FROM PRIMARY IN PART** | Career sequence and 1960 FBN employment are documented. Exact CIA assignments and any formal TWO-FOLD membership remain unresolved. |
| Anthony “Tony” Triponi reported covertly to Pat Fuller. | Valentine, P5-0144/146 | P5-0147 contemporary inspection interview | **PARTIALLY VERIFIED AS ALLEGATION** | The claim exists in a 1974 government record, but Triponi was acutely ill/incoherent. Corroboration is mandatory; he is not Thomas Tripodi. |
| CIA/DEA intelligence methods impaired prosecutions and protected CIA interests/sources. | Valentine, P5-0146 and later polemic P5-0144/145 | P5-0141/42 Casey/Strickler memoranda; P5-0007 Bolten interview | **VERIFIED FROM PRIMARY IN MECHANISM, NOT EVERY CASE** | DEA leaders explicitly described discovery, immunity and source-conflict harms; underlying redacted case files are needed for particulars. |
| CIA used narcotics agencies as cutouts before TWO-FOLD. | Valentine institutional prehistory, P5-0140/146 | P5-0108–0123 CIA–FBN safehouse files | **VERIFIED FROM PRIMARY** | Safehouses, unwitting testing and efforts to conceal CIA's role are documented; precedent does not prove identical purpose in TWO-FOLD. |
| TWO-FOLD was the vehicle by which USAID/OPS functions moved into DEA. | Series-level hypothesis; Valentine adjacency | Rockefeller Commission ch. 17 places CIA–BNDD and CIA–OPS relationships side by side; P5-0050/51 | **PRIMARY RECORD NOT YET LOCATED** | Functional overlap and contemporaneous CIA proprietary methods are documented, but no transfer order or personnel mapping establishes an en bloc migration. |
| The CIA–OPS police school relationship was hidden from AID leadership. | Earlier series/secondary accounts | Rockefeller Commission ch. 17, pp. 235–36 | **VERIFIED FROM PRIMARY, WITH QUALIFICATION** | Commission says AID Administrator did not know the contractor was a CIA front, while the person in charge of OPS apparently did. |
| Byron Engle was a CIA employee serving under cover as AID/OPS director. | Valentine and other secondary histories identify Engle as CIA | CIA damage assessment, NARA 104-10428-10019; redacted CIA personnel memorandum in MORI 1451843 | **VERIFIED FROM PRIMARY** | The 1976 CIA record names Engle, calls him an actual CIA employee, says he directed OPS for more than ten years and retired under cover. This establishes Engle's status but not a role in TWO-FOLD. |
| CIA clandestine-tradecraft assistance to BNDD began with TWO-FOLD. | Potential chronological inference from secondary TWO-FOLD narratives | CIA Office of Training FY1972 annual report, CIA-RDP78-06207A000100060002-9 | **PRIMARY RECORD CONTRADICTS AS AN ORIGIN CLAIM** | BNDD requested CIA guidance on clandestine agent/informant operations abroad in November 1969, and CIA surveyed its needs in March 1970. This separate foreign-training channel predates TWO-FOLD's late-1970 domestic request. |
