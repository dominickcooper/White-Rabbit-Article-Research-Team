1. Reference is made to my memorandum report dated June 9, 1967 concerning 
interviews with l/ivienne NAGELBERG during May 1967. During these interviews, 
NAGELBERG made numerous allegations about several Agents and Ex-Agents in this 
office. Immediately upon my return from Canada I reported these allegations 
to my immediate supervisor, Mr. Leonard Schrier. Due to the sensitive nature 
of the information and the lack of any substantiation, it was then decided not 
to make them the subjaqt of an official written report. 

2. Upon learning of the recant arrests of Jack Ghode. and Gleophus Robinson, 
inasmuch as they were the main subjects of NAGELBERG' s accusations, I was 
obliged to re-evaluate her information. Accordingly I went to Hr. Arthur 
Fluor, Assistant to the Supervisor (Operations) anci told him what I had 
previously reported to Mr. Schrier. 

* 

3. After discussing this situation with District Supervisor George M. 

Beik and carefully reviewing the information it was decided that this 
information should be put into a written report so that it could be properly 
utilized by any agents assigned to continue this investigation. On 
January 29, 1968 I was instructed to turn over the NAGELBERG investigation 
over to Group Leaner Ivan LJurms. 


4. Lihon Vivienne NAGELBERG was brought into the RCMP office at Darval 
Airport after the arrest of FRUNTIERI et al she immediately remembered me us 
one of the Agents who had arrested her husband several years ago. She felt 
that she was in a great deal of trouble and it was not difficult to get her 
to confide in me. She first denied any connection with the FRGNTIERI case 
(she later changed her story) and stated that she was at Dorvul on orders 
from "Agent Ghode". When I insisted that Ghode was no longer an Agent and 
that his new position would not have any basis for her to be in Canada, she 
implored me tu contact Ghode and he "would clear everything up." 

5. A good portion of this conversation was again repeated before RCMP 
Staff Sergeant Plante. He requested that I immediately contact my office and

1. Reference is made to my memorandum report dated August 14, 1968, 
case file number as above, subject: Interview of Jean NEBBIA conducted 
on August 7, 1968, at the Federal Penitentiary , Atlanta, Georgia. 


2. On August 15, 1968, I received an urgent written request for 

an interview from Jean NEBBIA. Based on the prior agreement that such 
a request would only be for the purpose of supplying additional infor- 
mation on the smuggling venture allegedly organized by Raphael SANTANA 
and Joseph LUCCAROTTI, Customs Agent Albert W. Seeley and I proceeded 
to the Federal Penitentiary, Atlanta, Georgia on August 19, 1968. 

3. NEBBIA was interviewed on August 19, 1968, between 12:15 PM and 
2:00 PM. He stated that he had requested the interview because he 
had learned that SANTANA f s unidentified Puerto Rican friend was 
scheduled to return to Europe, this time in the company of his wife. 
NEBBIA added, however, that he now has learned that the trip had been 
cancelled . 




4. It was immediately obvious that NEBBIA * s "information" was but 

a pretext for the interviews and that the real subject was to be his 
eventual cooperation with the Government. NEBBIA had extensive notes, 
which I read subsequently, but which he refused to turn over to me. 
These notes served as a basis for the conversation. 


5. NEEBIA started by asking what chances he had to win his appeal. 

I told him that no one could make an estimate. He then stated that he 
thought he would win his appeal but felt that the Government would 
prosecute him again and that it would be a long time before he would 
gain his freedom. I agreed with NEBBIA on that point. 


6. NEBBIA then proposed the following scheme: After he has won 

his appeal, the Government would decline any further prosecution and 
would expel him to France where he would cooperate with District #17. 
NEBBIA insisted that he would be much more useful as an Informant in 
France than as an inmate in a U.S. Penitentiary.

7. In turn, I pointed out to NEBBIA that he could have been useful 
even in a Penitentiary had he revealed the activity of Jean BOUSQUET 
and Victor MERZ at the time of bis arrest. I stressed that such a 
timely revelation would probably have prevented the importation into 
the United States of some 1,000 kilos of heroin. To this NEBBIA stated 
that, at the time of his arrest, he was not ready to cooperate. 

8 . I then asked NEBBIA how he proposed to guarantee his continued 
cooperation after his return to Prance. NEBBIA was very vague on that 
subject, suggesting that he could be controlled by the French Police, 
inasmuch as he intended to again operate clandestine houses of pro- 
stitution and that he could do so only with the tacit agreement of the 
Police . 

9. I told NEBBIA that a first step in his proposed cooperation would 
be that he would reveal everything he knows about the narcotics traffic. 
NEBBIA refused, stating that if he were to "talk" he would jeopardize 
his "future activity as an Informant in France”. I told NEBBIA that 
his whole scheme appeared unrealistic, but upon his assistance, I agreed 
to submit his proposal to United States Attorney, William Tendy. 

10. It would seem that NEBBIA will eventiially offer his full and un- 
restricted cooperation to the Government. It is doubtful that his co- 
operation will be of any value at the time of his offer. It should be 
noted that NEBBIA’ s attitude and the fact that he is looking for ex- 
cuses to request interviews reflects unfavorably on his credibility in
