STATEMENT OF THE CASE 


A. Introduction 

This is a case of first impression. It presents the ques- 
tion whether, in the unique circumstances of this case. Central 
Intelligence Agency (CIA) service (to which a special early 
retirement system applies) will be credited in the same way 
as law enforcement officer service (to which a similar special 
early retirement system applies) for retirement purposes ■ when 
the number of years in both services, if served only in one, 
would unquestionably entitle the officer to the special retire- 
ment system. Here a small group of CIA officers transferred 
to the Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) (formerly called 
Bureau of Narcotics and Dangerous Drugs [BNDD] ) to become law en- 
forcement officers when the government's narcotics intelligence 
function was transferred from CIA to DEA. They did so with 
the encouragement of both DEA and CIA. DEA and CIA officials 
expressly promised them, as an inducement to transfer, 
that they would receive law enforcement officer retirement 
benefits for their unique and hazardous service as CIA officers. 
The Office of Personnel Management (OPM) denied these benefits 
to the former CIA officers and this appeal followed. 

Appellant A put the essence of the case when he appeared 

on the witness stand: 

For my 20 years of Government service I 
have always been in a special retirement 
program in a small group or population 
within the larger population. At CIA the 
special retirement I was in was not for 
everybody in CIA. In DEA the special 
retirement is not for everybody in DEA. 

I have never been out of this type of 
special retirement system. I have met 
all the requirements of the CIA system 
for retirement except, the age. I have 
met all of the requirements of the DEA 
system, as far as I Know, except for the 
age. I have paid into both systems, at 
the special rate in the DEA system. I'm 
still paying at a higher deduction from 
my salary for retirement that I've been 


told I can't have. I don't understand 
why I shouldn't have the retirement that 
I have paid for and earned — not paid 
for just in money, but paid for in a lot 
of personal ways: long hours, family 
strain, very difficult things to put a 
price on. 

(Tr. Ill 39) . 

During a five day evidentiary hearing appellants proved 
by a preponderance of the evidence that the CIA service 
they performed meets the definition of law enforcement officer 
duties set forth in statute and regulation, as normally inter- 
preted by OPM and the predecessor Civil Service Commission 
(CSC) (part II, infra) ; that high agency officials, acting within 
their authority, promised appellants law enforcement officer 
credit for their prior CIA service, and that appellants reasonably 
relied on the promises made to their detriment (part III, infra ) . 
Appellants are therefore entitled to relief under applicable 
statute and regulations and under the equitable doctrine of 
promissory estoppel. 

Moreover, the public interest strongly favors the retire- 
ment credit appellants seek here. They have earned the promised 
benefits by their uncommon federal service and have paid for 
it by higher retirement contributions. There is no counter- 
vailing harm to the government; a ruling for appellants will 
apply essentially only to them and will have limited applica- 
tion, posing no threat to the civil service retirement fund 
(part IV, infra ) .

PURPOSES — 

The special law enforcement officer retirement program 
established pursuant to 5 U.S.C. §§ 8336(c) provides that the 
officer may retire at the age of^50 after completing 20 years 
of law enforcement officer work. "Law enforcement officer 
is defined for retirement purposes in 5 U.S.C. § 8331 (20) (1974) 
as "an employee/ the duties of whose position are primarily the 
investigation/ apprehension/ or detention of individuals sus 
pected or convicted of offenses against the criminal laws of 
the United States ..." and "an employee engaged in this activity 
who is transferred to a supervisory or administrative position." 
Before amendment on July 12, 1974 r the statutory scheme included 
an additional consideration pertinent to retirement: that the 
law enforcement officer's duties should have involved a "degree

of hazard" as determined by the agency head and the CSC.

their transfer to DEA before July 12, 1974. (Simon Tr. I 24,

EVIDENCE OF PLANNING 


As stated In the body cf the Memorandum, the 
coming together of more than 60 persons from all over 
the United States and Cuba to a small village in upstate 
New York on a working day {Thursday, November M ) is 
by itself persuasive evidence that the Apalachin meeting 
was planned and not coincide rice, as claimed. indeed, 
the location of the Barbara heme in a r. out of rhe way 
and hard to reach area indicates at least that road 
instructions must hove been given. 

There is much additional specific evidence of 
planning as follows: 

On November 5, Barbara Sr. ordered over 
$400 worth of meat from Armour & Company for 
delivery November i3, including 20 boxes o? 
special cut steaks. This is the first known 
time an order was given for delivery at a 
later date. 

On November 6 , Bufalino came to Apalachin 
for a meeting with Barbara. 

On Monday, November II, the Barbara maid 
was instructed to clean the house for what 
appeared to her to be an obvious special event. 

Also on Novemver II, DeSimone and Scozzari 
flew from Los Angeles to New York and checked 
in at the Hotel New Yorker. 


(Attachment C ) 


On Tuesday, November 12, Barbara made 
reservations at the Arlington Hotel, to be 
charged to Canada Dry. These were later 
occupied by Larocca and M. Genovese. 

Also on November 12, Colletti from Colorado 
and Zito from Illinois checked in at the Riela- 
Bonanrto Airport Motel in Newark. 

On Wednesday morning November 13, 

Barbara, Sr. went to Binghamton Airport with 
another man and checked the TWA schedule. 

He also reconfirmed the reservations of 7 or 8 
other men at the Sawtelle Travel Service, 
bring ing the men with him. 

Also on November 13, Barbara, Jr. made 
reservations at the Parkway Motelfor a Canada 
Dry convention, faking the keys and refusing 
to identify the expected guests. Six men (who 
must first have seen Barbara, Jr. and obtained 
the keys) came, two with an Ohio car who 
refused to register saying "Joe would take care 
of it. " 

Beginning Wednesday afternoon November 
13, people began congregating in the area 
from all over. At least seven different motel 
and hotel groups have been identified 
(Attachment H). 

A preliminary meeting was held Wednesday 
afternoon and evening November 13, at the 
Barbara home. The Turrigiano, LaDuca, 
and Evola cars were there, and at least the 
following persons probably participated, in 
addition to the Barbaras: Carl i si, Cucciara, 
DeMarco, Evola, LcDucq, Lombardozzi, 
Riccobcno, Scalish and Turrigiano 

Another group met the evening of November 
13 at the Del Motel, joining Chiri and possibly 
Cucciara, Lombardozzi and Riccobono there.

Some guests remained overnight at the 
Barbara home, November 13, the maid making 
up four extra beds the following morning 
(November M) and observing six strangers 
at breakfast. 

The rest of the guests began arriving early 
Thursday, November 14. 5-6 men arrived on 

Mohawk Airlines (using aliases) and were picked 
up by Barbara, Jr. and driven to the house. 

The others arrived by cor or taxi, many in 
groups. (Attachment H). 

After learning of the roadblock, many of 
the attendants left by different means, either 
with other groups by car, on foot, or through 
the woods in an attempt to escape detection. 
While this is evidence of consciousness of 
some kind of wrongdoing, it also is evidence 
that these persons did not come on a social 
call with the original groups just to visit a 
sick friend or after making a wrong turn on 
the road. 

Many of the attendants had travel in- 
structions, business cards and telephone 
numbers of other conferees, or had given the 
Barbara telephone number as an airline flight 
contact, also indicating that the meeting had 
been planned in advance. 

Four of the co-conspirators allege they 
were invited to a party for a sick man. At 
least these alibis are evidence that a meeting 
had been arranged in advance, which most 
attendants deny.
