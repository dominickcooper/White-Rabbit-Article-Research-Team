1. Reference is made to my memorandum report dated June 9, 1967 concerning 
interviews with l/ivienne NAGELBERG during May 1967. During these interviews, 
NAGELBERG made numerous allegations about several Agents and Ex-Agents in this 
office. Immediately upon my return from Canada I reported these allegations 
to my immediate supervisor, Mr. Leonard Schrier. Due to the sensitive nature 
of the information and the lack of any substantiation, it was then decided not 
to make them the subjaqt of an official written report. 

2. Upon learning of the recant arrests of Jack Ghode. and Gleophus Robinson, 
inasmuch as they were the main subjects of NAGELBERG' s accusations, I was 
obliged to re-evaluate her information. Accordingly I went to Hr. Arthur 
Fluor, Assistant to the Supervisor (Operations) anci told him what I had 
previously reported to Mr. Schrier.

6. When I told VIVIENNE what GHODE had said she appeared shocked

Robinson was related to me?" Indeed I was surprised. She stated 
that Robinson used to spend more time in her apartment than she did 
and that he could not be trusted. (She later in New York told me 

that ROBINSON had told "the wrong" people in Harlem about her arrest in 
Montreal.) She also said that farmer Agent Frank 8ISHGPP was also a

repaid. She said that she had done a lot for the Agents in New York 
and turned to me and said, "I could tell you some things about your 
good friend Lenny SCHRIER." I replied, (in front of Sergeant Plante) 
"does it have anything to do with narcotics?" She answered "no". I 
said, "Then I'm not interested." She then tried to impress Sgt. 

Plante by saying that she had met with "even your District Supervisor 
Mr. Belk", and "he owes me a favor too." She did not further explain 
these remarks, in fact all her conversation was apparently vague and 
insinuating.

explanation for this request. It seems hard for me to believe that
