# Part 5 open leads

External rabbit holes will be logged here only when grounded in a source-corpus name,
event, citation, document identifier or unresolved contradiction.

## Complete Seven John Does administrative record

- **Grounding:** P5-0001 and P5-0002 are truncated OCR extracts from MSPB dockets
  DA08318110337 and DC08318110596.
- **Need:** full closing briefs, hearing transcript, exhibits, ALJ disposition, Board review
  and any federal appellate record.
- **Why:** separates sworn testimony, ALJ findings and party argument; may expose Project
  TWO-FOLD names, transfer documentation and CIA/DEA witnesses omitted from local OCR.

## Resolve the NIO cohort against Project TWO-FOLD

- **Grounding:** the Seven John Does files name seven 1973–74 CIA transferees plus the
  earlier Thomas Tripodi path; the Part 4 corpus describes TWO-FOLD generically.
- **Need:** TWO-FOLD memoranda, annexes, personnel rosters, transfer dates, NIO staffing
  lists and sanitized CIA/DEA personnel files.
- **Question:** Were Simon, Baldwin, Chavez, Davis, Murray, Dyckman and Appellant A the
  project recruits, later transferees through a related channel, or a mixed cohort?

## Authenticate and expand P5-0010's Office of Intelligence roster

- **Grounding:** P5-0010 lists former CIA personnel with service/EOD dates; P5-0009 adds
  field names but lacks a cover page.
- **Need:** original scan, cover/routing pages, creation date, office organization charts,
  position descriptions and individual personnel/FOIA files.
- **Why:** the roster is the best current map from generic project description to named
  people, but employment sequence cannot substitute for operational evidence.

## Find contemporaneous CIA-to-DEA dissemination rules

- **Grounding:** Seymour Bolten says CIA reports circulated to DEA were held by DEA
  Intelligence rather than in operational files because of court-use problems.
- **Need:** CCINC Intelligence Subcommittee files, CIA Narcotics Control Group directives,
  DEA intelligence manuals and case examples.
- **Why:** may explain how intelligence relationships remained compartmented and why later
  litigation/oversight records show incomplete operational trails.

## Recover the CIA Office of Training–BNDD foreign-operations file

- **Grounding:** CIA-RDP78-06207A000100060002-9 records BNDD's November 1969 request, a
  three-week CIA survey in March 1970, a withdrawn SIO request in November 1971 and training
  for 18 Regional Intelligence Officers in April 1972.
- **Need:** the November 1969 BNDD request; March 1970 survey/report; November and December
  1971 SIO/BNDD correspondence; attendee roster and lesson plan for 24–25 April 1972; DDP
  approval; and any follow-on requests for “purely covert training.”
- **Why:** the file should define a pre-TWO-FOLD foreign-clandestine channel and test whether
  it later intersected with the covered Staff Agent component, SIO or the NIO program.
- **Boundary:** shared training, dates or foreign assignments do not establish TWO-FOLD
  membership without a project, personnel or funding link.

## Recover Byron Engle's CIA cover/detail records

- **Grounding:** CIA damage assessment 104-10428-10019 identifies Engle as an actual CIA
  employee who directed AID/OPS for more than ten years and retired under cover; a Family
  Jewels personnel memorandum independently describes the same redacted career fact.
- **Need:** CIA personnel/detail and cover files; AID appointment and reporting records;
  OPS director correspondence; budget/reimbursement records; Inspector General and State
  Department reviews; and Pike/Rockefeller source submissions behind the damage assessment.
- **Why:** these records should define the legal and administrative mechanism, Engle's CIA
  reporting chain and the limits of his tasking. They may also distinguish institutional CIA
  control from liaison or selective compartmentation within OPS.
- **Boundary:** do not infer that Engle ran TWO-FOLD or that OPS personnel migrated into BNDD
  unless a personnel, project, funding or command record supplies that bridge.

## Obtain the original TWO-FOLD approval and implementation records for the May 1973 disposition

- **Grounding:** CIA's 25 May 1973 memorandum says DCI approved TWO-FOLD on 12 February
  1971. Its action notation records Colby's recommendation and DCI concurrence to terminate
  paragraph 2 while continuing paragraph 3 only for foreign assignments collecting narcotics
  intelligence abroad. The Rockefeller Commission later describes a July 1973 termination.
- **Need:** original 1971 approval package; implementing cables/orders after the May 1973
  decision; Office of Security, NARCOG and BNDD assignment files; July cutoff documentation;
  and reimbursement ledgers.
- **Why:** these records should establish formal scope, authorized components, command chain,
  legal advice, what actually continued abroad, and whether the July date marks a later end,
  reassignment, or only the end of the original domestic/two-agent configuration.
- **FOIA trail:** CIA's released FY2007 case log records request **F-2007-01866**, subject
  “PROJECT TWOFOLD,” received 14 August 2007. Locate its final response/released-document
  package or re-request by case number rather than beginning with an unbounded search.

## Identify the 19 internal-security recruits and two covered agents

- **Grounding:** Rockefeller Commission gives a count of 19 recruits (December 1970–July
  1973) and two covered agents (January 1972–July 1973), while the CIA memorandum distinguishes
  internal-security personnel from nonofficial-cover BNDD Staff Agents.
- **Need:** proprietary applicant files, training rosters, BNDD appointment/EOD records,
  Office of Inspections assignments, reimbursement vouchers and CIA administrative files.
- **Test:** match names proposed by Valentine—Gutensohn brothers, Terry Burke and others—one
  by one. Do not infer membership from later CIA/DEA employment alone.

## Resolve whether the “two folds” were official program components

- **Grounding:** the direct CIA memorandum describes an original internal-security mission
  and a later extension acquiring covered Staff Agents against international traffickers.
  Valentine describes a public inspection rationale and a concealed operational facet.
- **Need:** project charter/amendments and operational annexes showing whether the second
  component was formally inside TWO-FOLD, an extension administered under the same account,
  or a later retrospective conflation.

## Test the CIA's claimed relinquishment of control

- **Grounding:** Rockefeller Commission says CIA ended all control/contact after recruits
  entered BNDD; Valentine's interview-derived account alleges coded reporting to Pat Fuller
  and hidden agendas; P5-0147 records Anthony Triponi's unstable but specific claim of a
  covert Fuller channel.
- **Need:** handler communications, inspection reports, coded-name indexes, travel/training
  files, Fuller papers and participant testimony given to the Rockefeller Commission.
- **Standard:** the commission statement is an official finding, not proof that no covert
  contact ever occurred; participant allegations are leads, not rebuttal without records.

## Obtain Rockefeller Commission TWO-FOLD testimony and exhibits

- **Grounding:** published chapter 17 reports the program but does not name the CIA field
  officer, proprietary, recruits or covered agents.
- **Need:** Commission witness transcripts, staff memoranda, CIA submissions and exhibits.
  The Library of Congress finding aid narrows the search to the **Baker–Weidner File, boxes
  CL 1–CL 3**, which contains the federal-agency portion of chapter 17, and **box TS 5**, which
  lists Howard J. Osborn's 17 February 1975 testimony. Also inspect Colby material in CL 5,
  CL 9, CL 22, CL 26 and testimony appendices in CL 29. Prioritize Ingersoll, Fuller and the
  unnamed field-office officer if separately indexed.
- **Repository:** Library of Congress finding aid confirms testimony and staff-interview
  files exist; National Archives/Church Committee holdings may contain transferred copies.
