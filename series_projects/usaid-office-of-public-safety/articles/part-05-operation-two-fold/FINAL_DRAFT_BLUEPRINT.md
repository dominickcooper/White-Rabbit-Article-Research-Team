The article’s new value is not simply proof that TWO-FOLD existed. The reader should finish Part 5 understanding how a covert CIA personnel project inside BNDD fits into the larger contraction of the AID/OPS police apparatus, the preservation of foreign narcotics authorities, the movement of people and capabilities into DEA, and the later collision between intelligence secrecy and criminal prosecution.

# FINAL THESIS

> **By the early 1970s, the CIA had covert access to two parallel American policing systems: foreign police assistance through AID’s Office of Public Safety, directed for more than a decade by CIA employee Byron Engle under cover, and federal narcotics enforcement, where CIA was helping BNDD build clandestine overseas capacity and secretly recruiting personnel through Operation TWO-FOLD. When political and legal pressure dismantled OPS and ended TWO-FOLD’s unlawful domestic mission, selected intelligence, liaison, training, foreign-police, and narcotics functions did not disappear. The foreign component was expressly preserved; separate CIA-supported personnel transfers helped build DEA’s intelligence capability; section 660 protected the narcotics channel; and an OPS-to-DEA advisory handoff is documented in Laos. Named participants describe both the project’s recruitment machinery and a less orderly practical wind-down than the official July 1973 date suggests. By 1976–77, DEA itself warned that CIA secrecy could make major narcotics targets effectively immune from prosecution. TWO-FOLD is the documentary window through which this functional and personnel migration becomes visible.**

This thesis means:

- **Functional migration**, not a claim that OPS became DEA.
- **Personnel movement across distinct institutions**, not proof that every former CIA officer belonged to TWO-FOLD.
- **Two parallel CIA-accessed systems**, not a single hidden super-agency or unified chain of command.
- **Formal termination plus bounded survival and disputed residue**, not an assertion that TWO-FOLD continued intact.
- **A documented Laos handoff**, not a universal global OPS-to-DEA transfer.
- **CIA-created access and capacity inside narcotics enforcement**, not proof that CIA controlled DEA.

The testimonial record strengthens the thesis by supplying mechanism, participants, and friction. It does not replace the documentary chain on which the thesis rests.

## 1. THE ORDER TO END TWO-FOLD—AND KEEP PART OF IT

### The memorandum with two different endings

Open on the May 25, 1973 CIA memorandum, not on a generalized history of CIA–DEA relations. Establish immediately:

- the project name;
- DCI approval on February 12, 1971;
- BNDD director John Ingersoll’s request;
- the internal-security recruitment component;
- the later nonofficial-cover foreign component;
- the disposition that terminated one paragraph and continued the other only for foreign narcotics intelligence.

The opening mystery is embedded in the same document: why did the CIA decide one form of police work had crossed the line while another remained worth preserving?

### The official account of termination

Bring in the Rockefeller Commission early enough to establish the official count and legal finding: 19 recruits, two covered agents, July 1973 termination, and the conclusion that CIA participation violated the National Security Act’s law-enforcement restriction.

Do not resolve the apparent tension prematurely. State the formal end and the CIA’s selective continuation together. The article will return to practical wind-down in H2 4.

### Narrative function

This section supplies the documentary hook and the article’s organizing question: **what stopped, what survived, and where did the surviving capability go?** It must not become a chronology dump or disclose every later answer.

### Visual opportunity

Primary-document crop of the memorandum’s two components and disposition, ideally showing the language “terminate paragraph 2 activity and continue paragraph 3 only.” The caption identifies the document and date; it does not claim the full project survived.

## 2. TWO PARALLEL DOORS INTO POLICE POWER

### The AID door

Briefly recall the series architecture rather than reteaching Parts 1–4:

- NSAM 177 assigned overt foreign police assistance to AID while excluding covert aspects from that assignment.
- Byron Engle directed OPS and chaired the interagency Police Group.
- CIA’s 1976 damage assessment identifies Engle as “in actual fact a CIA employee” who “retired under cover” after more than ten years directing OPS.
- IPS, the CIA Police Group, and CIA–IPA liaison supplied bounded proprietary, training, assessment, and information channels.

The Engle reveal must remain prominent and clean. His CIA identity and covered status are documented. “CIA placement at the top of OPS” is the high-confidence inference. Do not bury the reveal under an extended evidentiary disclaimer or imply that CIA directed every OPS activity.

### The narcotics door

Establish that TWO-FOLD was not the beginning of CIA–narcotics cooperation:

- CIA–FBN intelligence exchange and liaison predated BNDD.
- A March 1969 record documents CIA–BNDD scientific/intelligence liaison.
- BNDD asked CIA in November 1969 for help developing clandestine agents and informants abroad.
- CIA surveyed BNDD’s needs in 1970 and briefed 18 Regional Intelligence Officers in 1972.

Keep these lines distinct from TWO-FOLD unless a source connects them. Their point is institutional preparation: CIA already had a foreign-police channel inside AID and a growing intelligence relationship with federal narcotics enforcement.

### A human intersection, held for later

End with a brief teaser, not the full case study: Charles Gutensohn later told Douglas Valentine that after CIA service in Laos he was offered a choice between returning there as a narcotics liaison or entering TWO-FOLD. State here that official records confirm his later senior DEA career; reserve the rest of his testimony for H2 3.

This is a human personnel bridge, not proof of an OPS-to-DEA organizational transfer.

### The fair conventional explanation

State the legitimate operational case without caricature: international trafficking crossed borders; BNDD faced real corruption risk; CIA had foreign access and tradecraft BNDD lacked; a new narcotics bureau needed overseas informants and liaison relationships. That explanation accounts for why cooperation began. The next section shows why the means chosen cannot be reduced to ordinary interagency assistance.

### Visual opportunity

Parallel-channel diagram with CIA linked separately to AID/OPS–IPA–foreign police and FBN/BNDD–foreign offices–informants. No direct OPS→TWO-FOLD arrow. Any overlap must be sourced and labeled by type: personnel, liaison, training, or function.

## 3. WHAT TWO-FOLD ACTUALLY BUILT

### The proprietary and the 19

Build the section’s strongest convergence in this order:

1. CIA memorandum: the Agency used a proprietary to spot, assess, and recruit personnel for BNDD internal security.
2. Rockefeller Commission: 19 were recruited; CIA gave short tradecraft instruction; BNDD controlled assignments.
3. Patrick Fuller: direct participant testimony describing how the proprietary recruitment worked.

Use Fuller’s quotation in a short, verified form:

> “We recruited the CIA officers for BNDD through a proprietary company.”

If the longer sentence about three officers posing as private businessmen is used, quote it exactly from Valentine’s text and attribute it directly to Fuller. Official records corroborate the proprietary mechanism; they do not independently confirm the precise number or identities of the covered interviewers.

Fuller is central because he turns an institutional abstraction into machinery. Do not make his statements about missing files, unknown participants, or other compartments carry the same weight as the corroborated recruitment mechanism.

### What recruitment looked like

Use Charles Gutensohn as the primary human-scale case study:

- **Documented:** his later senior DEA career.
- **Attributed to Gutensohn through Valentine:** CIA service, Pakse assignment, the choice between Laos liaison work and TWO-FOLD, Jerry Soul and Fuller at the recruitment meeting, and code-name details.
- **Unrecovered:** CIA personnel file, Pakse record, TWO-FOLD roster entry, proprietary record.

Article form:

> Charles Gutensohn later told Douglas Valentine that, after CIA service in Laos, he was offered a choice between returning there as a narcotics liaison or entering TWO-FOLD. Public records confirm Gutensohn’s later senior DEA career, but the government roster that could independently confirm his CIA and TWO-FOLD account has not surfaced.

Use Terry Burke as a short contrast. Burke told Valentine that BNDD recruited him from a CIA/paramilitary background and assigned him to examine agents, but he said he proved himself through undercover cases rather than spying on colleagues. His testimony shows that an assignment and an agent’s account of what he actually did need not be identical.

Omit William Logay from the main narrative unless a single sentence is needed to show recruitment was not always accepted. His claim that he was invited and refused adds little beyond that point.

### The second, deeper component

Return first to the official record:

- CIA “extended this activity” to acquire nonofficial-cover BNDD staff agents against principal international traffickers.
- CIA administered them.
- The memorandum says the agents were unaware of Agency involvement.
- Rockefeller counted two and described BNDD as directing them while CIA supplied cover/administration.

Then use John Ingersoll’s named testimony. He described the component to Valentine as tightly held, foreign-focused, and kept from regional directors. His senior proximity makes him useful; his interest in presenting the arrangement as lawful and foreign-facing should remain visible through the conflict with other accounts, not through editorial psychologizing.

#### The disputed operational account

Joseph DiGennaro earns **one bounded passage or sidebar**, not a controlling narrative role. Use only because his account offers a possible view inside the otherwise anonymous two-agent component.

Required sequence:

1. DiGennaro told Valentine that Fuller recruited him under the code name “Novo Yardley” into a separately trained foreign operation.
2. Official records independently confirm that a two-agent, commercially covered foreign component existed.
3. Joe Quarequio recalled that DiGennaro was rarely or never in the office; Gerry Carey separately described unusual safehouse detention practices, but neither witness connects those observations to the official two-agent component.
4. DiGennaro’s claim that he participated in an overseas seizure/rendition remains uncorroborated by an operational, court, immigration, or foreign record.
5. His allegation that personnel could kill targets who resisted has no recovered order or second direct witness and must remain explicitly his allegation.
6. His description of CIA knowledge/control conflicts with the CIA memorandum’s claim that the covered agents were unaware of Agency involvement and with Rockefeller’s account of BNDD direction.

Do not identify DiGennaro as one of the official two agents. Do not use his Bureau of Mines funding assertion, proposed roster, or killing allegation in a headline, deck, pull quote, visual caption, or section conclusion.

### The line CIA crossed

Close H2 3 with the Rockefeller Commission’s legal conclusion. A real corruption problem explains the request. It does not erase the proprietary, the covert recruitment of domestic law-enforcement personnel, or the Commission’s finding that CIA participation violated the 1947 Act’s law-enforcement restriction.

The reader should now understand why TWO-FOLD was more than liaison or training—and why the distinction between its domestic and foreign components mattered in 1973.

Strictly sourced two-lane process diagram:

- CIA → proprietary → 19 recruits → BNDD chief inspector/internal security.
- CIA administration/cover → two BNDD staff agents → foreign trafficker intelligence.

Use count badges and a clear control/assignment legend. Do not attach disputed names to either official count.

## 4. THE PROJECT ENDS. THE RELATIONSHIP DOESN’T.

### Formal termination

Restate only what is needed from H2 1:

- the CIA disposition terminated the internal-security paragraph;
- Rockefeller reports July 1973 termination;
- the foreign narcotics-intelligence function was expressly allowed to continue abroad.

Do not say “TWO-FOLD continued.” Say the formal project ended while its own disposition preserved the foreign function.

### A messier wind-down

Use George Brosan as the principal witness. He later told Valentine that Fuller described the program as having fallen into disuse, that Brosan pressed for formal closure, and that residual funds allegedly remained in late 1974. No recovered account ledger confirms the funds.

Use the Anthony Triponi memoranda only as contemporaneous evidence of possible residue:

- A 1974 internal inspection memorandum records Triponi alleging that Fuller selected him during agent school as a covert regional source with a direct reporting code and special file.
- The document proves he made the allegation; it does not prove the channel existed or that it was TWO-FOLD.
- Triponi was hospitalized, acutely ill, and at points incoherent. If his allegation enters the article, that condition must appear in the same compact passage.

The conclusion is bounded: testimony and a contemporaneous inspection record make the practical wind-down look less clean than the official date, but they do not establish continuation of the intact domestic project.

### Seven CIA officers move into DEA

Make a clean documentary break before introducing the 1973–74 NIO transfers. The *John Does v. Civiletti* record establishes:

- seven former CIA officers entered BNDD/DEA intelligence;
- CIA assisted the recruitment and William Colby supported it;
- the stated purpose was to build DEA’s own sophisticated narcotics-intelligence capability and relieve CIA of that obligation;
- at least some comparable skills, sources, functions, or relationships crossed with them.

State explicitly that the seven are **not established as the 19 TWO-FOLD recruits**. Organizational independence and capacity migration can coexist.

### The people who carried capability

Use Thomas C. Tripodi as the named institutional-personnel bridge:

- **Documented:** FBN service, roughly five and a half years as a CIA investigator/security officer, and transfer to BNDD to help resolve an internal-corruption problem.
- **Attributed:** Tripodi’s claim that he helped create the arrangement and brought CIA officers into narcotics enforcement.

Article form:

> Tripodi later described himself as one of the men who helped create the arrangement. Official personnel records do not establish that creator role, but they do confirm the career path beneath it: FBN, then CIA, then BNDD internal-corruption work.

Tripodi demonstrates personnel movement without proving a unified project lineage. Do not confuse him with Anthony Triponi.

- 1973–74 separate seven-officer transfer;
- Tripodi’s individual FBN→CIA→BNDD path.

## 5. THE NARCOTICS EXCEPTION BECOMES A BRIDGE

### What section 660 closed—and left open

Explain the statutory transition precisely:

- Congress ended broad Foreign Assistance Act-funded police support.
- The restriction became effective July 1, 1975.
- International narcotics control and specified federal law-enforcement activities remained outside the broad ban.

Do not claim Congress designed a covert loophole. The stronger conclusion is structural: as general foreign-police aid became legally and politically untenable, narcotics remained an authorized channel capable of carrying selected training, liaison, intelligence, and advisory work.

### Laos: the handoff in writing

Use the August 28, 1974 Vientiane airgram as the decisive cross-series case:

- OPS assistance was ending.
- DEA would assume the narcotics-advisory role.

This is the documented OPS-to-DEA handoff. It proves one country-level transfer, not a universal pattern.

Gutensohn’s testimony can echo here in one callback: his claimed Pakse-to-TWO-FOLD path supplies a human parallel to the institutional handoff, but it does not prove he was part of that handoff or that OPS employed him.

### Functions already waiting in the narcotics channel

Show that the receiving channel already existed:

- FBN personnel taught at the International Police Academy.
- OPS/IPA maintained narcotics capacity.
- BNDD’s 1972 Mexico roster included a Police Liaison and Training Officer.
- CIA and BNDD had already developed foreign tradecraft and intelligence relationships.

The inference is selective functional migration. Avoid compressing the causal chain into “OPS closed, therefore DEA inherited it.” The bridge consists of preserved law, existing narcotics capacity, an explicit Laos handoff, separate personnel movement, and overlapping foreign-police functions.

### The conventional explanation—and where it stops

Return to the legitimate explanation from H2 2. Transnational drugs, corruption, informants, and foreign liaison explain why the narcotics exception existed and why DEA needed these capabilities. They do not adequately explain:

- Engle’s covered CIA placement at the top of OPS;
- proprietary recruiting into domestic BNDD internal security;
- CIA administration concealed from the two covered personnel;
- multiple compartmented channels;
- the evidence of practical residue after formal termination;
- an organized CIA-supported personnel transfer into DEA intelligence;
- later prosecution conflicts created by intelligence secrecy.

The article’s judgment should arise from the mismatch, not from accusing every official rationale of bad faith.

### Visual opportunity

Two-track 1973–75 timeline: OPS phaseout/section 660 on one track; TWO-FOLD disposition/DEA formation and intelligence transfers/Laos handoff on the other. Different symbols for law, personnel, and function. Adjacency must not be illustrated as causation unless a record supplies the link.

## 6. WHEN INTELLIGENCE MADE PROSECUTION IMPOSSIBLE

### The same target in two systems

Open the final section by making the project break explicit: the 1976–77 dispute is not established as TWO-FOLD. It is a later conflict inside the wider CIA–DEA relationship that TWO-FOLD exposes.

Explain the mechanism from the DEA memoranda:

- CIA collected intelligence through channels outside ordinary DEA case files.
- DEA assistance could help select surveillance targets.
- a later independent criminal case might encounter the same information or target;
- CIA disclosure resistance could make evidence unusable or force dismissal/non-prosecution.

### “De facto immunity”

Use the primary documents’ language. DEA officials warned that subjects could become “virtually immune from prosecution in the United States” or receive “de facto immunity.” Their proposed remedy—suspending support for unilateral CIA collection and limiting CIA to strategic intelligence—shows that they understood the problem as structural, not theoretical.

This is the payoff: the intelligence-enforcement hybrid could undermine the public prosecution it was supposedly designed to strengthen.

### What TWO-FOLD reveals

Conclude the investigative narrative with the bounded synthesis:

- A covert CIA officer had led the AID police program.
- CIA helped build clandestine and personnel capacity inside federal narcotics enforcement.
- the unlawful domestic TWO-FOLD mission ended formally;
- foreign narcotics collection, legal authority, selected personnel, and functions survived through distinct mechanisms;
- the resulting overlap created consequences DEA later documented in its own files.

Do not claim a single continuous project. Do not end on the missing roster or on DiGennaro’s allegation. End on the documented institutional consequence.

### Part 6 bridge

The evidence-led next question is not whether DEA “became OPS.” It is which later institutions rebuilt, redistributed, or newly authorized foreign police training and assistance after section 660—and what safeguards accompanied them. Tease the authorities, organizations, and country relationships Part 6 will follow without disclosing unresearched conclusions.

### Visual opportunity

Primary-document crop centered on “de facto immunity,” or a sourced evidence-flow diagram: CIA collection → undisclosed overlap → discovery/disclosure conflict → dismissal or non-prosecution. Avoid generic espionage imagery.

# FINAL NARRATIVE SEQUENCE

### Movement 1 — Begin at the split

The reader encounters a CIA document that does not simply shut a project down. It sorts the project into a domestic activity that must end and a foreign narcotics function that may continue. This creates the central question before the reader knows the full institutional history.

### Movement 2 — Reveal the two systems already in place

The story rewinds. One door runs through AID/OPS, with Engle’s covered CIA identity at its top. The other runs through FBN/BNDD, where liaison, foreign informants, tradecraft, and intelligence training are already expanding. The fair conventional explanation—real corruption and a borderless drug trade—is introduced here.

### Movement 3 — Enter the machinery

The article moves from architecture to mechanics. The CIA memorandum supplies the proprietary; Rockefeller supplies the 19; Fuller explains how the recruiting process worked. Gutensohn and Burke show how alleged recruits understood the assignment. Ingersoll helps explain the second component. DiGennaro appears only as a disputed glimpse into the blank space left by official files.

### Movement 4 — Cross the legal line, then watch the closure blur

Rockefeller’s legal conclusion changes the meaning of “assistance.” The official domestic project ends. The foreign function does not. Brosan and the Triponi inspection memo complicate the practical endpoint without erasing it.

### Movement 5 — Follow people and capacity, not a cryptonym

The narrative makes a deliberate break: seven CIA officers enter DEA intelligence through a separate transfer. Tripodi’s documented FBN→CIA→BNDD path embodies personnel movement. The reader learns to follow functions, skills, sources, and relationships without pretending every transfer was TWO-FOLD.

### Movement 6 — Follow the lawful channel

Congress closes broad police aid but preserves narcotics authority. Laos provides the direct country handoff from OPS advising to DEA advising. Earlier FBN/IPA and BNDD foreign-police functions show that DEA did not receive an empty mandate; a working narcotics channel already existed.

### Movement 7 — Test the conventional explanation

Legitimate need explains much of the cooperation, but not the covered leadership, proprietary domestic recruiting, inward concealment, compartmented residue, organized personnel transfer, or later disclosure crisis. The article judges the institutional means and consequences without claiming the drug threat itself was fabricated.

### Movement 8 — End with consequence

The 1976–77 memoranda reveal the trap: intelligence collection demanded secrecy while prosecution demanded disclosure. DEA officials warned that the overlap could make traffickers practically immune. TWO-FOLD ends as the window into a transformation, not as the name of every program that followed.
