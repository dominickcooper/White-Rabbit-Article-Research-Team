# OPS to DEA continuity

## Personnel continuity

- **Verified programmatic overlap:** FBN/BNDD instructors taught narcotics-control courses through AID's International Police Academy in at least 1966–68.
- **Verified intelligence penetration mechanism:** Project TWO-FOLD allowed CIA to spot, assess and recruit BNDD internal-security personnel and covert staff agents. Recruits joined BNDD without knowing CIA had arranged the process.
- **Unresolved:** no acquired roster yet identifies an OPS/IPA employee who transferred into DEA or a specific foreign graduate who became a DEA asset/contact.

## Institutional continuity

- FBN became BNDD in 1968; Reorganization Plan No. 2 of 1973 created DEA by consolidating federal drug-enforcement functions.
- OPS remained in AID until the Section 660 phaseout.
- These are distinct institutional lineages. Continuity arises through collaboration and reassigned functions, not a merger.

## Functional continuity

- IPA/OPS built foreign investigative, intelligence, laboratory, records and whole-force narcotics capacity.
- DEA possessed an international narcotics mandate expressly preserved by Section 660's original exceptions.
- A 1974 Embassy Vientiane airgram states the transition directly: OPS aid would end and DEA would assume narcotics advising.

## Foreign liaison continuity

- DOJ's FY1971 report described IPA alumni as useful contacts for U.S. law-enforcement agencies.
- Laos's General Service of Investigations combined police, military police and customs personnel, giving DEA an inherited multi-service counterpart.
- Named liaison continuity in other countries remains open.

## Training continuity

- **Verified:** FBN narcotics modules at IPA; OPS/IPA multinational courses; DEA successor advising in Laos.
- **Strong inference:** a training/contact network built under AID supplied institutional access useful to later U.S. narcotics work.
- **Limit:** similar course content does not prove unchanged doctrine, command or covert purpose.

## Operational continuity

- TWO-FOLD demonstrates CIA/BNDD operational interpenetration separate from OPS.
- Section 660 preserved narcotics assistance while closing broad police support.
- The surviving function was narcotics enforcement, not the entire OPS internal-security mission.

## Evidence-weighted judgment

**VERIFIED:** Laos country-level advisory handoff; FBN/BNDD participation in IPA; statutory DEA/narcotics exceptions; CIA recruitment inside BNDD.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** police contacts, training relationships and foreign enforcement capacity built under OPS made the narcotics channel a practical carrier of some post-OPS functions.

**UNRESOLVED:** a multi-country personnel chain demonstrating that former OPS advisers or IPA graduates systematically became DEA nodes.
