# Article brief

## Topic
The 1962 Kennedy directives that placed AID at the center of a global police-assistance system.

## Central mystery
Why did a civilian economic-development agency receive a mission framed in terms of internal security, counterinsurgency, subversion and mass violence?

## Preferred title
The Aid Memo That Built a Global Police System

## Scope
February–December 1962, with only the minimum prehistory and aftermath needed. Global architecture rather than country abuses, CIA operations, Mitrione, Section 660 or modern continuity, which belong later.

## Key questions
- What changed from NSAM 132 to the July report and NSAM 177?
- What authority did AID receive?
- What did “except for their covert aspects” establish and leave unresolved?
- How quickly was OPS implemented?

## Important people and agencies
John F. Kennedy, Fowler Hamilton, Alexis Johnson, Robert Komer, Byron Engle, AID, State, Defense, CIA, Justice and the Special Group (Counter-Insurgency).

## Source priorities
JFKNSF-338-004 original scans/searchable PDF; FRUS documents 72, 91 and 99; AID's December implementation report and General Notice. Use exact archival page verification for quotations.

## Conventional explanation
Stable and professional police institutions were necessary for development and offered a civilian alternative to military repression.

## Unresolved questions
Who handled covert aspects, what CIA/IPS access existed, and how the system worked in country programs.

## Modern implications
Reserve for the finale except a brief warning that later similarity must not be confused with descent.
