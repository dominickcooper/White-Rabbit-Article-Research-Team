# Part 1 research dossier

## WHAT THIS PART ADDS

A primary-document reconstruction of how Kennedy administration decisions in 1962 transformed dispersed police aid into a centralized AID-led internal-defense program. The reader learns that AID's responsibility was explicit, that covert aspects were explicitly outside that assignment, and that OPS followed within twelve weeks.

## EARLIER FINDINGS REQUIRED FOR CONTEXT

None. This is the first installment. It briefly notes preexisting police programs because NSAM 132 was a directive to review and strengthen them, not create police aid from nothing.

## NEW FINDINGS

1. **NSAM 132, February 19, 1962 (FRUS VIII, doc. 72):** Kennedy distinguished police support from economic-growth criteria and justified it through internal security and resistance to Communist-supported insurgency. Evidence: DOCUMENTED FACT; confidence high.
2. **NSAM 162, June 19, 1962 (FRUS VIII, doc. 91):** the administration treated indigenous police, paramilitary and military capabilities as elements of internal defense. Evidence: DOCUMENTED FACT; confidence high.
3. **July 20 committee memorandum/report (FRUS VIII, doc. 99; JFKNSF-338-004):** recommended AID coordination, academy planning, a professional cadre and funding insulation. Evidence: DOCUMENTED FACT. The three-page FRUS memorandum is not mislabeled as the complete annex.
4. **NSAM 177, August 7, 1962 (JFKNSF-338-004 pp. 1–3):** AID received coordination/leadership and operating/funding responsibility, excluding covert aspects and selected DOD programs. It ordered an office whose functions included a technical repository on controlling subversion and mass violence. Exact wording verified visually against JFKNSF-338-004-p0002.jpg.
5. **Implementation report, December 1, 1962:** says reserved FY1963 police funds were virtually doubled after field review. It records an interagency group chaired by Byron Engle and formal establishment of OPS on November 1. Evidence: DOCUMENTED FACT; confidence high.

## EVIDENCE-WEIGHTED CONCLUSIONS

The civilian-agency placement was deliberate, not accidental. The program's overt mission combined development language with a primary internal-defense rationale. NSAM 177 documents an interagency division of labor and a covert carve-out; it does not state who performed every covert activity or prove that all OPS work was CIA work.

## RESPONSIBILITY ASSESSMENT

AID has DOCUMENTED INSTITUTIONAL RESPONSIBILITY for coordinating and operating/funding the overt police-assistance system described by NSAM 177. State retained general policy guidance; DOD retained specified programs. The directive identifies covert aspects but does not assign them in the quoted paragraph, so agency-specific operational responsibility remains for Part 2.

## CONTRARY EVIDENCE

The documents present police capacity as supporting viable states and development. Police professionalization can be a civilian alternative to military control and can improve ordinary public safety. These explanations fit part of the record but do not erase the directives' repeated internal-security, subversion and insurgency language.

## COMPETING EXPLANATIONS

Administrative rationalization explains centralization; Cold War strategy explains the content and urgency. The strongest judgment combines them: policymakers rationalized scattered programs because they considered police a central instrument of internal defense.

## CONNECTIONS TO MASTER DOSSIER
