# White Rabbit Network Summary

The most important cross-series connection is a recurring **indirect-capacity model**. Washington pursues security or political effects through institutions that remain formally foreign, civilian or private: local police in OPS, indigenous networks in Gladio, intelligence-linked local services in the Vietnam/Phoenix environment, grantees and media organizations in transition programs, and contractors in modern USAID delivery.

This is a network pattern, not proof of one master organization. The documentary strength changes by edge. OPS-to-DEA narcotics work has a statute, retained personnel and functional continuity. IPA-to-IPS has a documented training and assessment channel. OPS-to-Afghanistan is doctrinal recurrence without a proven transmission line. OPS-to-Gladio is a comparison in anti-communist internal-security method, with no located command, funding or personnel bridge.

The delivery mechanism changed over time. The early system depended heavily on American government advisers, academies and an institutional contract such as Michigan State's Vietnam project. Later USAID programs more often placed private implementers and grantees between the agency and field activity. The *False Profits* connection matters here: contractor scale and weak oversight can obscure responsibility even when no intelligence role exists.

The information function also changed form. OPS helped foreign states build communications, biographic records, fingerprints and intelligence capacity. OTI-era programs funded polling, media and communications networks, including ZunZuneo. Both treat information as an instrument of political order, but the recipients, law and techniques differ.

The archive's strongest correction concerns the International Police Academy. A prior Jonestown article called IPA a CIA front. The evidence assembled for this series supports a more structurally revealing model: IPA was a real OPS/AID academy whose normal operations gave CIA recurring access to foreign police personnel, participant information, liaison and pathways into further activity. International Police Services—not IPA—was the separate CIA proprietary. IPA was not the proprietary; it was the access point.

## Five research leads

1. **Jones–Mitrione provenance:** establish contemporaneous records for the claimed Indianapolis relationship and Brazil overlap.
2. **Personnel after OPS:** identify the five retained narcotics advisers and trace later assignments.
3. **Contractor genealogy:** map MSU, Creative Associates, Chemonics and other implementers by contracts, personnel and task orders without assuming intelligence affiliation.
4. **Information-capability evolution:** compare OPS records doctrine with later OTI media, polling and communications program design.
5. **Gladio comparison:** test country by country for actual liaison, shared personnel or funding rather than relying on doctrinal resemblance.
