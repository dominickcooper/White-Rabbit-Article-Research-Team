# Series Canonical Facts

These are the controlling formulations for publication of the seven-part series.

| Topic | Canonical fact | Evidence level | Prohibited drift |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founding | NSAM 177 assigned AID operating and funding responsibility for foreign-police programs in August 1962, except their covert aspects, and ordered a central police office. | Documented | Do not say AID invented all U.S. foreign-police assistance. |
| OPS lifespan | OPS was organized in 1962; its overseas programs and International Police Academy closed in 1974. | Documented | Do not imply a single worldwide program began from nothing in 1962. |
| AID and CIA | AID/OPS and CIA were distinct institutions with documented liaison, contracts, personnel relationships, information transfer and selected training channels. | Documented | Do not say USAID or the public IPA “was the CIA.” |
| International Police Academy | IPA was an overt OPS/AID institution with a genuine training mission and documented CIA operational integration and access through liaison, participant information, Agency-sponsored students and pathways to further activity. | Documented facts; “backdoor” is analytical shorthand | Do not classify IPA itself as a proprietary, fake academy or wholly CIA-controlled institution. |
| International Police Services | Selected officers could move from OPS/IPA training to IPS, a documented CIA proprietary, without being told its ownership. | Documented | Do not collapse IPS into IPA. |
| White Rabbit shorthand | IPA was not literally the CIA proprietary/front; it functioned as a CIA backdoor into an overt U.S. foreign-police program. | Strong inference from documented mechanisms | “Backdoor” is an analytical description, not an official CIA label. |
| Vietnam | U.S. police-system planning in South Vietnam predated OPS and included communications, records, laboratories, intelligence and mobility. | Documented | Do not attribute every Vietnamese security abuse directly to OPS inputs. |
| Brazil | U.S. police assistance continued after the 1964 coup and strengthened institutional capacity within a repressive state. | Documented | Do not claim the reviewed record proves U.S. advisers directed particular torture acts. |
| Mitrione | Mitrione led USAID's Uruguay public-safety mission; personal instruction in torture is plausible but unproven. | Mixed | Do not present the “precise pain” quotation or live-victim story as verified fact. |
| Uruguay chronology | Police torture was documented before Mitrione arrived in 1969. He was kidnapped July 31, 1970, and killed August 1970. | Documented | Do not say he introduced torture to Uruguay. |
| Sendic cable | Washington instructed its ambassador to raise a threat to kill Raúl Sendic and others if Mitrione were killed; the record does not establish that the threat was carried out. | Documented | Do not convert contemplated coercion into an execution claim. |
| Section 660 | Congress enacted a real restriction on foreign-police assistance in 1974 with exceptions; later amendments and notwithstanding authorities widened permissible activity. | Documented | Do not call it a universal or permanent ban. |
| Post-OPS system | Later police aid was distributed across mission-specific programs and agencies. No reviewed record shows an intact OPS transfer or renaming. | Documented absence / synthesis | Do not call ICITAP, ILEA, INL or OTI “OPS 2.0” as fact. |
| Narcotics bridge | The statutory narcotics exception, retained advisers and later DEA/State activity form the strongest legal, personnel and functional bridge. | Strong | Do not claim DEA inherited OPS as an organization. |
| Afghanistan | USAID programs were explicitly integrated with counterinsurgency and PRT stabilization. | Documented | Describe this as doctrinal/functional recurrence, not proven transmission from OPS. |
| ZunZuneo | OTI funded a politically purposed communications network with concealed U.S. sponsorship through Creative Associates. | Documented | CIA direction or legal covert-action status was not established by the reviewed record. |
| NED/Freedom House | Public funding documents establish grants and democracy programming. | Documented | Funding alone does not prove CIA control or OPS descent. |
| Series verdict | OPS died institutionally. Its strongest legacy is legal reopening plus recurring use of civilian aid for security, legitimacy, information and political objectives. | Series synthesis | Do not collapse similarity, recurrence and institutional descent. |
