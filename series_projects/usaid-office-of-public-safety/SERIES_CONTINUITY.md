## PART 1 — The Aid Memo That Built a Global Police System

- Core findings already established: NSAM 132 gave police aid a distinct internal-security/counterinsurgency rationale; the July committee designed an AID-led interagency structure; NSAM 177 approved it, excepted covert aspects and ordered a central office; AID created OPS November 1, 1962.
- People introduced: Kennedy, Fowler Hamilton, Alexis Johnson, Robert Komer and Byron Engle.
- Institutions explained: AID, OPS, State, DOD, CIA and Special Group (Counter-Insurgency) at architecture level.
- Dates/events explained: 1962-02-19, 1962-06-19, 1962-07-20, 1962-08-07, 1962-11-01 and 1962-12-01.
- Claims understood: AID's overt role was explicit; covert aspects were separately recognized; this does not prove AID and CIA were identical.
- Evidence-weighted conclusion: OPS was the overt administrative center of an interagency internal-defense police project.
- Open questions: who handled covert aspects and what occurred through CIA Police Group/IPS/IPA channels?
- Rabbit holes deferred: country cases, Mitrione, Section 660, post-OPS programs and modern USAID.
- Last transition: the founding memorandum leads to records about CIA liaison, IPA and International Police Services.
- NEXT ARTICLE QUESTION: Did intelligence coordination become trainee evaluation, follow-on training and recruitment?
- NEXT ARTICLE CONNECTION: Part 2 tests the covert exception against operational records.
- REVEALS SAFE TO TEASE: Police Group, IPA and IPS existed in the same documentary field.
- REVEALS TO WITHHOLD: scope, mechanisms and final judgment on the CIA-front allegation.

- People already introduced: James Angleton; an unnamed/redacted CIA employee recorded as serving more than ten years as OPS director. Byron Engle remains introduced from Part 1 but is not equated with the redacted person.
- Institutions already explained: IPA as overt OPS school; IPS as CIA proprietary; CIA Police Group as operations coordination/liaison mechanism; Dade County Bomb Squad's limited TIC #7 role.

## PART 5 — The Exception

- Core findings already established: Abourezk's 1972 attempt failed; the 1973 Senate proposal was broader than final law; Congress knowingly preserved specified Justice/FBI/DEA/narcotics functions; the December 1973 cutoff drove AID's phaseout; original §660 took effect July 1, 1975; GAO found general AID compliance plus authorized narcotics growth and indirect military boundaries.
- People introduced: James Abourezk, John McClellan and Roman Hruska.
- Institutions explained: GAO, DEA and LEAA at the legislative-boundary level.
- Dates/events explained: Feb. 4, 1972 debate; Oct. 2, 1973 debate; Dec. 17, 1973 cutoff; Feb.–Aug. 1974 phaseout; Dec. 30, 1974 enactment; July 1, 1975 effective date; Feb. 19, 1976 GAO report.
- Claims understood: §660 barred FAA-funded training, advice and financial support to police, prisons, law enforcement, internal intelligence and surveillance; it was not an all-authority federal ban.
- Evidence-weighted conclusions: OPS was genuinely terminated; narcotics and limited federal law-enforcement channels were explicit exceptions; DOD produced indirect benefits and classification disputes; wholesale evasion or an en bloc Justice transfer is unproven.
- Open questions: CIA coverage, later amendments, Thailand legality and actual descent into successor organizations.
- Rabbit holes deferred: full DOJ/ICITAP, FBI, DEA, State, DOD and ILEA architecture.
- Last transition: the office ended, but demand and several legal pathways remained.

- NEXT ARTICLE CONNECTION: Part 6 maps agencies and authorities after OPS.
- REVEALS SAFE TO TEASE: a patchwork of later authorities emerged.
- REVEALS TO WITHHOLD: which institutions represent descent, reauthorization or similarity.

## PART 7 — What Survived Inside Foreign Aid (Finale)

- Established: 2024 USAID guidance expressly remembers OPS/abuse as §660's background while describing later police authorities and Leahy vetting; OTI is overtly political transition programming without OPS ancestry; Afghanistan explicitly integrated USAID development with counterinsurgency/PRTs; ZunZuneo concealed sponsorship and pursued political-information objectives without proven CIA control/covert-action status.
- Final claims rejected: USAID was/is CIA; OPS survived intact; OPS became OTI/ICITAP; ILEA inherited IPA; NED is an OPS successor; ZunZuneo proves intelligence control.
- Partially supported: Mitrione personal torture instruction remains plausible/unproven; modern programs display legal, doctrinal and functional recurrence without direct descent.
- Strongest primary records: NSAM 177; CIA Family Jewels/IPS records; country evaluations; Aug. 9, 1970 Sendic cable; P.L. 93-559 §660; GAO 1976/1992; USAID 2024 police guidance; Afghanistan budget/OIG; Cuba OIG.
