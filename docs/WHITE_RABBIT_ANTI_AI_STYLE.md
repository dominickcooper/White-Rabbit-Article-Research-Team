# White Rabbit anti AI style review

This authority detects cumulative machine-like cadence. It is not a banned-phrase list
and must not be used to make prose sloppy, slangy or artificially quirky.

## Review the pattern, not the isolated phrase

Any one of these moves can be useful. Repetition is the warning:

- announcing that a distinction matters instead of showing the consequence;
- “This matters because,” “In other words,” “Seen in that context,” “The pattern is
  clear,” or “Taken together” as routine connective tissue;
- “not X but Y,” “not merely X; Y,” “both X and Y,” and perfectly parallel contrasts;
- a conventional explanation followed by an equally sized caveat in every section;
- exactly three items whenever emphasis is needed;
- every section opening with recap and ending with a summary;
- repeated “This,” “That” or “The” paragraph openings;
- abstract nouns replacing the people and offices doing the work;
- an explanation after the document, quotation or list has already made the point;
- uniform paragraph length and polished transitions that erase the path of discovery.

Machine-like prose has two common forms. Type A is the polished essay scaffold already
described above: “Taken together,” “The pattern is clear” and “That distinction matters.”
Type B is over-performed humanity or responsibility: “I can't prove,” “I won't pretend,”
“I am not going to fill in,” “The responsible conclusion is” and “Here's where things
get complicated.” Both become formulas when repeated.

## Performative responsibility and lawyer voice

Flag passages that demonstrate how careful, nuanced or responsible the writer is instead
of stating the accurate sentence. Common signals include:

- “I am not going to fill a black bar with a guess”;
- “The file needs a warning label”;
- “What the document proves is narrower”;
- “The most defensible conclusion” or “the evidence allows/supports”;
- “It would be irresponsible,” “we should be careful” or “to be precise”;
- “This distinction is important” or “that limit matters.”

These phrases are not forbidden. Multiple instances trigger a review for caution
inflation. Ask: can this caution be expressed as a short factual sentence instead? If
yes, rewrite it.

Also flag publication prose that sounds like a legal brief, peer-review response,
intelligence assessment, congressional staff report or fact-check note. Internal analysis
may say that a claim outruns the evidence or that a defensible map stops at a particular
point. Publication prose should normally say what the documents show and where they stop.

Caution density is a contextual diagnostic: a short factual claim followed by several
sentences whose main job is limiting that claim should be reviewed for compression. Do
not attempt to decide this with phrase counts alone. The editorial pass makes the final
judgment and preserves every material qualification.

Ask repeatedly: would the reader understand this without being told? Cut the sentence
when the answer is yes. Do not cut the evidentiary bridge, a material qualification,
chronology, or the strongest competing explanation.

## Red team procedure

The Anti AI Style Red Team reads the near-final article without the drafting prompt.
For each flag it quotes or identifies the passage and explains the pattern. It does not
rewrite the whole article and does not judge whether evidence is sufficient. The Author
Voice Editor resolves the critique; the Evidence Integrity Editor then checks that the
resolution did not alter claim status.

Diagnostics may count repeated stems, opening words, paragraph-length variance,
symmetric contrasts, recap density, isolated punch lines and emphasis density. These
are prompts for judgment, never target scores. A warning may be waived in audit.md when
the repetition serves evidence, quotation fidelity or deliberate pacing.

The red team separately reports Type A polished scaffolding and Type B performed caution.
It looks for inflated source provenance, symmetrical disclaimer constructions, repeated
self-policing, manufactured punch lines and explanations that continue after the evidence
has made the point. It recommends local compression, not a wholesale voice rewrite.

## Do not overcorrect

Never add fake typos, forced slang, profanity, anecdotes, uncertainty, outrage, jokes,
contrarianism or fragments. Do not scatter rhetorical questions or em dashes to simulate
personality. The target is documentary precision plus an identifiable investigator,
irregular rhythm and strong evidence.
