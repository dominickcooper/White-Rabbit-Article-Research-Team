# White Rabbit format and visual style

Formatting and visuals are part of the argument. Apply this authority after structure
and main prose are stable, then reconcile it again after the final voice revision.

## Emphasis

Bold answers: what should a skimming reader remember? Use it for central names when
introduced, agencies or programs at the point they become important, dates and amounts
that change the story, discoveries, decisive quoted language and concise conclusions.
Do not bold every proper noun, every recurrence or whole evidence paragraphs.

Italics answers: what should the reader hear the author saying? Use it for selective
questions, asides, reactions, skepticism, irony and tonal pivots. Do not italicize every
question or use italics as generic decoration.

Bold italic is rare. Reserve it for an earned authorial or argumentative punch. An
isolated one-line paragraph may deliver a reveal, pause, reaction or quote setup. Do not
merge an intentional punch into surrounding prose, and do not manufacture enough of
them to create a metronome.

## Lists

Use bullets when at least three parallel items matter individually: divisions of labor,
policy categories, funding options, chronology, stages or stacked evidence. Use prose
when relationships and causality matter more than enumeration. Avoid consecutive lists
that turn an investigation into notes.

## Visual classes

Classify every planned visual internally as one of:

1. documentary evidence;
2. archival or historical context;
3. explanatory;
4. relationship or map;
5. atmospheric;
6. analogy or humor;
7. hero or promotional.

Prefer primary-source excerpts, declassified files, official photographs, government
and congressional records, court filings, patent diagrams, archival clippings, original
maps, historical photographs and sourced charts before generated art. Generated imagery
is suitable for a hero, conceptual diagram, relationship map, analogy, or a scene with
no surviving photograph. It must never masquerade as evidence.

## Placement

Put a document crop immediately after the first material claim or quotation it supports.
Introduce an archival portrait when the person enters the story. Put a source excerpt at
the reveal it verifies. Place a conceptual image where an abstract idea needs a memory
hook, while making clear that it is analogy rather than proof. Avoid delaying evidence
for several paragraphs, clustering unrelated images or interrupting a causal passage.

Do not enforce an image quota. Ask whether the reader has gone too long without a useful
visual break, whether a real document is available, whether an archival face would orient
a biography, and whether the graphic adds something prose cannot.

## Visual plan metadata

For every proposed visual, keep this complete record in research_dossier.md:

```text
[IMAGE: exact description]
PURPOSE: documentary evidence | archival context | explanatory | relationship/map |
         atmospheric | analogy/humor | hero/promotional
SOURCE: stable source, local provenance and page/location
PLACEMENT: the exact paragraph or claim after which it appears
CAPTION: concise identification and relevance without unsupported interpretation
ALT TEXT: visible content, document/date when material, and visible highlighted language
EVIDENCE STATUS: primary evidence | secondary evidence | context | conceptual
```

Publication Markdown may use the compact `[IMAGE: description | ALT: alt text]` marker.
Alt text describes what is visible; it does not argue that the image “proves” a theory.
Captions identify people, date, place, document and highlighted language without adding
unsupported interpretation. Audit document identity, quote, date, crop context and
caption accuracy. Prefer one strong crop to redundant screenshots.

## Calls to action

Place subscription, share, paid/free messaging and series teasers at earned pauses: the
completion of a major movement, a cliffhanger, a transition into paid material or the
final evidence payoff. Do not insert them at fixed word counts. Use the workflow's
explicit markers and keep platform-specific copy distinct from article evidence.
