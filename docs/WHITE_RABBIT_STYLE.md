# THE WHITE RABBIT REPORT — WRITING STANDARDS

## Voice
Write like an intelligent investigative newsletter/Substack, not a generic SEO blog post and not a sterile academic paper.

The voice should be:
- conversational
- skeptical
- curious
- evidence-driven
- confident when documentation is strong
- willing to ask uncomfortable questions
- clear about uncertainty

Make the reader feel as if they are following the investigation while it unfolds.

Use active voice, personal pronouns naturally, rhetorical questions strategically, useful analogies, strong transitions, and occasional surprising pivots.

## Paragraphs
Most paragraphs should be approximately 3–5 sentences.

Do not write the article as one sentence per line.

Use a one-line paragraph only for a major reveal, pivot, or rhetorical punch.

## Formatting
- Use `#` for the article title only.
- Main article sections should generally use `##` headings in ALL CAPS.
- Use `###` for subordinate sections when needed.
- Use **bold** for important names, agencies, companies, numbers, documents, programs, discoveries, claims, and memorable phrases.
- Use *italics* for rhetorical emphasis, pivots, and questions.
- Use blockquotes for real quotations.
- Use bullets when they materially improve readability.

## Opening
Never begin with generic SEO filler such as:
- "In today's world..."
- "Throughout history..."
- "X has long been a topic of debate..."

Open with something concrete and strange:
- a quote
- a document
- a contradiction
- an unexplained event
- a peculiar transaction
- a surprising patent
- a government memo
- an unresolved question

The primary keyword should still appear naturally within the first ~100 words when practical.

## Narrative engine
Use this White Rabbit formula organically:

THE MYSTERY
↓
THE DOCUMENTS
↓
THE CONNECTIONS
↓
THE ORDINARY EXPLANATION
↓
THE PROBLEM WITH THAT EXPLANATION
↓
THE BIGGER PATTERN
↓
THE NEXT RABBIT HOLE

Do not mechanically label the sections this way.

## Fact → Question → Judgment → Implication
When a document raises a larger issue, move naturally through:

**FACT:** what the record establishes.

**QUESTION:** what a reasonable reader would ask next.

**JUDGMENT:** what the evidence most likely means after testing competing explanations.

**IMPLICATION:** why the answer could matter.

The implication is not proof unless the evidence makes it proof.

## Specificity
Use exact records when available.

Prefer:
- names
- dates
- dollar values
- document titles
- patent numbers
- contract numbers
- FOIA numbers
- program names
- agencies
- corporate entities
- direct quotations

Specificity gives the investigation weight.

## Handling uncertainty
Do not drown the article in disclaimers.

Avoid repeating "this does not prove" every other paragraph.

Use natural language such as:
- "From what I can tell..."
- "The public record doesn't show..."
- "By all appearances..."
- "The unresolved question is..."
- "What we can document is..."

## Alternative explanations
Present the strongest credible conventional explanation fairly.

Explain what it accounts for before explaining what remains unresolved.

Uncertainty should identify the boundary of knowledge, not replace judgment. After presenting competing explanations, tell the reader which one best fits the total record and why. Use confident evidence-weighted language when multiple independent facts converge, while continuing to label inference as inference.

## Length
Default target: approximately 2,000–3,500 words.

Go longer when the evidence warrants it. Never pad to hit a target.

## Internal links
Use approximately 3–6 genuinely relevant White Rabbit links in a normal long article when available. Do not force them.

## FAQ
Include exactly five useful FAQs after the article conclusion unless the user explicitly requests otherwise.

Good FAQ themes:
1. the obvious objection;
2. the conventional explanation;
3. whether the underlying event/technology/program is real;
4. what is documented versus inferred;
5. broader significance.

The FAQ should add clarity, not merely repeat the article.

## Related articles
After the FAQ include:

`## YOU MAY BE INTERESTED IN THESE ARTICLES`

List approximately 3–6 genuinely relevant White Rabbit articles. For each:
- use the actual article title;
- hyperlink the title to the canonical article URL;
- give one short explanation of the connection.

## Conclusion
Do not end with a generic summary.

Move from:

specific mystery → broader pattern → implication → memorable final line

The last paragraph should leave the reader thinking about the investigation differently than when they began.
