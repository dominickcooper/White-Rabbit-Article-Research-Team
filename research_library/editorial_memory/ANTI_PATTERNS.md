# White Rabbit editorial anti-patterns

These are review triggers, not absolute lexical bans.

- Repeated “This matters because,” “That distinction matters,” “At minimum,” “This does not prove,” “The record does not show,” or “There is no evidence that.”
- Repetitive “not X, but Y” symmetry; mechanical “conventional explanation / however” framing.
- Repeated “the stronger conclusion,” “the better question,” “the evidence stops short,”
  “what this actually shows,” “the reality is more complicated,” “both things can be
  true,” “it is important to note,” “in other words,” or corrective “however” pivots.
- Three-part “Not A. Not B. Rather C.” antithesis used as a recurring structure.
- Section-level disclaimers repeated after the evidence has already been qualified.
- One sentence per paragraph, punchline after punchline, or unnaturally symmetrical prose.
- Restating the thesis in every section or explaining why a detail is interesting instead of showing it.
- Academic or bureaucratic abstractions where ordinary language works; unexplained terms such as “functional continuity,” “institutional architecture,” and “redistribution.”
- Treating the absence of a primary document as the absence of evidence.
- Burying the surprising detail beneath source pedigree, caveats or credibility throat-clearing.
- Generic AI transitions and polished essay scaffolding that stop the investigation.
- Conclusions that summarize instead of paying off the mystery.
- Writing the article in claims-ledger or dossier order.
- Re-proving published canon, retrying accepted witness credibility without contrary
  evidence, or spending every corroborating fact on the already accepted premise.
- Ending each section with a sterile restatement of the thesis and its caveat instead of
  opening the next evidentiary door.
