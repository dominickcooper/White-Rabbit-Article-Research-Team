# **How Wireless BCIs Read Thoughts in Real Time**

**[Wireless brain-computer interfaces](https://www.engineering.columbia.edu/about/news/silicon-chips-brain-researchers-announce-new-generation-brain-computer-interface)** (BCIs) enable the detection and interpretation of neural activity without invasive procedures, leveraging non-surgical methods to **capture brain signals in real time**.

These systems process subtle physiological changes associated with cognitive processes, translating them into actionable outputs like device control.

### Detecting Neural Signals Without Surgery

**Imagine your brain** is like a busy city full of tiny electric sparks happening when your **neurons** (brain cells) talk to each other. When a lot of those sparks happen at the same time in the same area (**like when you decide to move your hand** or imagine a picture), it creates an **incredibly small** electrical ripple that travels all the way up to your scalp.

We’re talking ***millionths*** of a volt. *Way* too weak to feel.

A wireless BCI has little sensors that can **pick up those tiny ripples** (this technique is called EEG — **[electroencephalography](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7288588/)**). You’ve probably seen EEG montiors in sci-fi movies and shows:

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/67d73893-7d6d-4b04-91d6-1fd17fc63782_2057x1270.webp)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!VGah!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F67d73893-7d6d-4b04-91d6-1fd17fc63782_2057x1270.webp)

Example of an EEG Monitor

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/0e08fa77-d1aa-4531-9444-eed649147c53_800x830.jpeg)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!ijSp!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F0e08fa77-d1aa-4531-9444-eed649147c53_800x830.jpeg)

The headset (*or implant*):

1. Listens for those faint brain waves,
2. Makes them louder (amplifies them),
3. Cleans up the junk (like removing the hum from nearby lights or Wi-Fi),
4. Sends the cleaned signal to a computer.

The computer (**[with the help of AI](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-14894-4)**) has learned, *“Okay, when the person thinks about moving their right hand, the waves look like THIS pattern.”*

So it watches the cleaned-up brain waves in real time and says, *“Aha! They’re imagining moving right — let’s move the cursor right on the screen.”*

**Now multiple that by thousands of processes by thousands of individuals. Fine-tuned. Improved. Better with each iteration.**

### AI-Driven Interpretation of Cognitive States

Thanks to AI, **modern brain-computer interfaces** got a leap forward.

AI models are **trained** to make sense of dense EEG and fNIRS activity.

**These systems learn** from broad neural datasets and can tease out subtle patterns linked to human thought and perception. Core abilities include:

- **Emotional decoding**:   
  **YES.** fNIRS-based interfaces can sort **positive**, **negative**, and **neutral moods** with striking precision—**often above 93% accuracy**.  
  By tracking small shifts in prefrontal blood-flow timing and curve shapes. EEG systems achieve similar feats, clustering imagined emotional states by mapping changes across evolving neural rhythms.

  [![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/e786ec02-7050-41f3-ac8c-894311412f6d_588x170.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!k2_w!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe786ec02-7050-41f3-ac8c-894311412f6d_588x170.png)

  Excerpt from [2023 Neurophotonics publication](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9896013/)
- **Memory recall**: Explainable AI (e.g., SHAP on wavelet-transformed EEG) [distinguishes encoding vs. retrieval phases with 82% accuracy](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772528625000421), highlighting [theta-band (4-8 Hz) energy and alpha-band (9-14 Hz) energy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4299435/), in anterior regions as a key biomarker for recall detection.
- **Threat perception**: Non-invasive BCIs [detect signatures of perceived threats](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2016.00187/full) through EEG patterns of heightened arousal or fear responses, integrated into emotion decoding frameworks that flag rapid neural shifts.

  [![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/2ab3a378-395e-48e5-ac73-649b321119e7_614x227.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!bGfz!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F2ab3a378-395e-48e5-ac73-649b321119e7_614x227.png)

- **Decision-making patterns**: [AI decodes uninformed choices](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.11.12.688013v1.full#sec-2) from brain-wide EEG signals, alerting users via BCI feedback to improve outcomes, with applications in real-time assistive interfaces. [Within 700 milliseconds](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.11.12.688013v1.full#sec-2) (a little less than a second), the BCI could tell if you were making an informed or uninformed decision.
