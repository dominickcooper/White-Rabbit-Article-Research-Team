### **The Mall Dream**

When I was a kid, I had a recurring dream. It always started in the middle, like dreams do.

No context. Just running.

I was inside what felt like a **mall**, but not one I’d ever seen. It wasn’t bright or busy. It was quiet. I could hear the **echo of my own shoes** on the **marble floor**. There was a circular, dome-shaped **glass ceiling** above me filled with geometric panels and pale light.

And I was being **chased**.

I never saw who it was. I just knew I had to keep moving. Every hallway looked the same.

White walls.   
Corinthian columns.   
Polished floors.

It went on forever.

**Then came the crash.**

The ceiling shattered. Glass fell like ice. A **rescuer** dropped through it and landed in front of me. They never spoke. Just nodded in acknowledgment to my young, childish appreciation. Then they ran off.

I’d be alone again.

That’s when I’d see it. The **red door**.

Not bright red. More like dried blood. Faded. Almost matte. It always stood at the end of the hall, perfectly centered, like it was waiting.

I’d walk toward it, slowly. My feet heavy, like I was walking through water. When I opened it, the mall went silent.

And suddenly I was **outside**.

#### **The Forest Clearing**

The forest was more creepy than the mall. You would expect an empty mall to be quieter than a large forest. A large forest would have junebugs, crickets, etc. All the fanfare of nature.

**But this forest was as quiet as a grave.**

It was night. Thick **fog** covered the **grass**. The air felt charged, like static. I’d turn and, to my left, see a **stone well**. It was evidently old. There was a rope and pulley. A bucket hanging just above the water.

Always the same. Always that clearing.

I’d walk up and look down. And there, under the surface, I’d see a **skull** staring up at me. Perfectly still.

That’s when the dread hit.

It started in my chest and spread through my arms, like my blood had gone cold. I’d turn around and he’d be there.

**Death.**

Or something like him. A **cloaked figure**, tall and silent, walking toward me through the fog. I’d try to scream. No sound. I’d try to move, but it felt like mud. Every time, he got closer.

**And that’s when I’d wake up.**

Heart racing. Sheets damp.

I had that dream at least a dozen times between six and eleven years old. Then it stopped.

**Just stopped.**

#### **Dream Telepathy – The Old Experiments**

Back in the 1960s and 70s, some researchers tested **dream telepathy**. One of the [most famous experiments](https://dn710102.ca.archive.org/0/items/michael-persinger-publications-PDFs/1989%20-%20Persinger%20%26%20Krippner%20-%20Journal%20of%20the%20American%20Society%20for%20Psychical%20Research%20-%20Dream%20ESP%20experiments%20and%20geomagnetic%20activity_text.pdf) came out of **Maimonides Medical Center** in Brooklyn.

Here is how it worked:

- A “sender” focused on an image or scene while awake.
- A “receiver” slept in another room connected to EEG monitors.
- When the receiver entered REM sleep, the researchers woke them and asked about their dreams.

**Across dozens of trials**, they found [statistically significant matches](https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/maimonides-dream-telepathy-research#Robert_Van_de_Castle).

p < .000002 or 1 in 500,000 that the results were random.

Not perfect matches, but close enough to raise eyebrows.

**Then the funding disappeared.** The data was largely forgotten.

Still, the pattern suggested that dreams can sync. Minds can align.
