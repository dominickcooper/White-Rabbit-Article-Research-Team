## Methods Employed in MK Ultra

### **Use of Psychoactive Drugs**

#### LSD and Its Psychological Impact

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/c0094ec5-021d-4428-8582-189fd50bb4ba_290x174.jpeg)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!rZko!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fc0094ec5-021d-4428-8582-189fd50bb4ba_290x174.jpeg)

**Dr. Albert Hofmann, Creator of LSD**

LSD became the central focus of MK Ultra’s experiments, known for its intense mind-altering properties. Created by **Dr. Albert Hofmann**, a Swiss chemist at **Sandoz** **Laboratories**, LSD (lysergic acid diethylamide) was discovered accidentally in 1938 when Hofmann synthesized it while studying ergot fungus derivatives.

Hofmann didn’t experience the drug’s hallucinogenic effects until 1943, when he unintentionally absorbed a small amount through his skin. [On recounting his experience](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/hallucinogenic-effects-of-lsd-discovered), Hofmann noted:

> *At home I lay down and sank into a not unpleasant, intoxicated-like condition characterized by an extremely stimulated imagination. In a dreamlike state, with eyes closed (I found the daylight to be unpleasantly glaring), I perceived an uninterrupted stream of fantastic pictures, extraordinary shapes with intense, kaleidoscopic play of colors. After some two hours this condition faded away.*

LSD was soon distributed by Sandoz Pharmaceuticals under the brand name **Delysid**, advertised to psychiatrists as a tool for studying mental disorders.

When **Sidney Gottlieb** came across Hofmann’s research, he saw LSD as a potential breakthrough for psychological warfare and mind control. Gottlieb and other MK Ultra scientists theorized that LSD could be used to “break down the ego” and create a “blank slate” in which a person could be reprogrammed. Unlike Hofmann, who saw LSD as a tool for insight, **Gottlieb viewed it as a potential weapon.**

##### LSD Dosage in MK Ultra vs. Recreational Use

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/9be75ce2-2c10-4bf0-a6b2-fde2b02cb2eb_354x105.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Xh-L!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F9be75ce2-2c10-4bf0-a6b2-fde2b02cb2eb_354x105.png)

In MK Ultra’s early stages, researchers administered extraordinarily high doses of LSD, far surpassing typical recreational amounts. [Modern recreational doses](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41386-020-00883-6) of LSD generally range from **50 to 200 micrograms**, a dosage level that typically induces intense but manageable hallucinations, euphoria, and heightened sensory perception.

In contrast, MK Ultra subjects were often given doses between **300 and 1,500 micrograms**—several times the upper limit of what is considered a “strong dose” today. [In one reported instance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgewood_Arsenal_human_experiments), MK Ultra participants were given up to **10,000 micrograms** of LSD in a single session, an amount that would be considered exceedingly dangerous in modern settings.

At these high doses, participants often experienced overwhelming sensory distortions, paranoia, loss of self-identity, and extreme emotional states. Rather than producing manageable hallucinations, [these dosages](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41386-020-00883-6) could trigger intense “ego death,” a phenomenon where the individual loses their sense of self entirely, leading to a feeling of disconnection from reality.

As **Dr. Daniel Merkur**, a clinical psychoanalyst, [explained](https://www.google.com/books/edition/Crucified_with_Christ/I1dbADIOv0sC?hl=en):

[The Ego death is] an imageless experience in which there is no sense of personal identity.

It is the experience that remains possible in a state of extremely deep trance when the ego-functions of reality-testing, sense-perception, memory, reason, fantasy and self-representation are repressed…

Muslim Sufis call it fana ('annihilation'), and medieval Jewish kabbalists termed it 'the kiss of death'.

##### Effects of High-Dose LSD on the Mind

The effects of high-dose LSD, such as those administered in MK Ultra, extend far beyond the typical experiences associated with recreational or therapeutic use. [At these levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MKUltra), LSD’s impact on the **mind and psyche** can be highly unpredictable and often dangerous:

1. **Hallucinations and Sensory Overload**High doses can lead to intense visual and auditory hallucinations that are difficult for the brain to process. In MK Ultra, participants were reportedly so overwhelmed by these sensations that they could no longer differentiate between reality and hallucination, resulting in **severe cognitive disorientation**.
2. **Paranoia and Psychosis**LSD interacts with serotonin receptors, specifically **5-HT2A receptors**, which play a key role in mood and perception. [At high doses](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5133947/), LSD can lead to paranoia and delusional thinking. In MK Ultra, these doses often induced **states of psychosis**, where participants experienced profound fear, often believing they were being chased or hunted. Many were left with long-lasting trauma and paranoia that extended beyond the experimental setting.
3. **Dissociation and Ego Death**Ego death is a phenomenon where one’s sense of self completely dissolves, which can be a powerful or disturbing experience depending on the context. For MK Ultra participants who were neither prepared for nor informed about the effects, ego death often led to **dissociative episodes**, where they felt disconnected from their body and identity. This was part of Gottlieb’s objective, as he believed that breaking down the psyche would make a person more susceptible to programming.
4. **Memory Loss and Cognitive Impairment**LSD disrupts neural pathways, particularly those involved in memory and cognition. At extreme doses, MK Ultra subjects frequently experienced **anterograde amnesia** (inability to form new memories) and **retrograde amnesia** (loss of past memories). This cognitive impairment aligned with the CIA’s goal to create a “clean slate” for reprogramming, though in practice, the damage was often irreversible, with subjects struggling with memory and focus for years afterward.
5. **Long-Term Psychological Trauma**Many MK Ultra participants experienced **post-traumatic stress disorder** (PTSD) symptoms after their sessions. This trauma stemmed not only from the effects of the drugs but also from the disorienting conditions of the experiments, often conducted in isolation rooms with minimal stimuli. Former participants felt like they were trapped in a never-ending nightmare, with flashbacks and hallucinations triggered by the trauma.

#### Psychoactive Drugs Beyond LSD in MK Ultra

Beyond its notorious use of **LSD**, MK Ultra experimented with a range of other psychoactive drugs, including **psilocybin**, **barbiturates**, and **amphetamines**. Each of these substances affects the brain in unique ways, creating different psychological states that CIA scientists believed could be harnessed for **mind control** and **behavioral manipulation**.

While LSD alone produced intense hallucinogenic effects, MK Ultra researchers often used these drugs in combination, amplifying their effects to create heightened states of **vulnerability, compliance, and even memory loss**. Such practices pushed the participants far beyond typical recreational experiences, frequently leading to severe, lasting trauma.

##### Psilocybin: Hallucinogenic Effects and MK Ultra Doses

**[Psilocybin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psilocybin)**, a naturally occurring psychedelic compound found in certain mushrooms, produces hallucinations and altered states of consciousness. In therapeutic or recreational settings today, doses of **2-3 grams of dried mushrooms** (containing roughly **10-15 mg of psilocybin**) are common, producing effects that last about 4-6 hours. Psilocybin is known for enhancing sensory perception, creating a feeling of interconnectedness, and often leading to introspective or spiritual experiences.

In MK Ultra, however, doses far exceeded typical recreational amounts. Participants were given **20-40 mg or more** in many cases—double or triple a typical high dose. This level of exposure pushed subjects into intense, disorienting experiences. The psychological effects included **severe hallucinations, intense fear, and ego dissolution**. According to a report from one participant, “It felt like the walls were closing in, my sense of self disappeared… It was as if I was in a living nightmare, without any way out.”

##### Barbiturates: Sedation and Memory Suppression

**[Barbiturates](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK539731/#:~:text=Barbiturates%20are%20a%20category%20of,increased%20intracranial%20pressure%20(ICP).)**, central nervous system depressants, were widely used in the mid-20th century as sedatives, anesthetics, and treatments for anxiety. They work by slowing brain activity, inducing relaxation, drowsiness, and, at higher doses, unconsciousness. Recreational dosages typically range from **50 to 150 mg** depending on the specific barbiturate and the user’s tolerance level.

MK Ultra employed high doses—often **200-300 mg**—to induce sedation so deep it bordered on unconsciousness, creating a suggestible state ripe for **hypnosis and reprogramming efforts**.

High doses of barbiturates can cause amnesia and extreme sedation, impairing a person's ability to form memories. In MK Ultra, barbiturates were sometimes paired with stimulants like amphetamines, a practice known as the **“two-drug disorientation” technique**. Administering a sedative to weaken mental defenses, followed by a stimulant to jolt the subject into confusion, created a mental “fog” that left participants disoriented and susceptible to suggestion.

##### Amphetamines: Inducing Hyperawareness and Paranoia

**[Amphetamines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphetamine)** are stimulants that increase dopamine and norepinephrine levels, heightening alertness, focus, and energy. Recreational doses of amphetamines, like those found in medications such as Adderall, typically range from **5 to 30 mg**. At these levels, users experience a boost in energy and concentration.

Under MK Ultra, participants were given doses of **50 mg or more**, well beyond therapeutic limits, resulting in hyperawareness, paranoia, and, in some cases, psychosis.

The heightened state of arousal made subjects more receptive to **hypnotic suggestion** and questioning, though the extreme dosages often caused panic and confusion.

One internal CIA document noted, “Stimulants at these levels produce hyper-sensitivity and extreme susceptibility. The subject loses all sense of orientation.”

##### Combinations of Drugs and Their Amplified Effects

MK Ultra researchers often combined these drugs to achieve even stronger psychological effects. Some of the most common drug combinations included **LSD and barbiturates**, **barbiturates and amphetamines**, and **LSD with psilocybin**. These combinations amplified each drug's effects in unpredictable ways:

1. **LSD and Barbiturates**LSD was sometimes administered with barbiturates to create an experience where subjects could be simultaneously disoriented by hallucinations and sedated into a more receptive state. Barbiturates helped prevent the participant from spiraling into panic, making them easier to control while under the influence of LSD.
2. **Barbiturates and Amphetamines**The “two-drug disorientation” technique combined barbiturates with amphetamines. First, barbiturates were administered to induce a deep relaxation and partial unconsciousness. Then, participants were given high doses of amphetamines to jolt them awake. This combination caused severe **cognitive dissonance**, leaving participants in a state of mental conflict as they fluctuated between the extremes of sedation and stimulation.
3. **LSD and Psilocybin**Combining LSD and psilocybin intensified the hallucinogenic experience, causing prolonged and powerful hallucinations that went beyond typical experiences. At this level, subjects reported intense feelings of **ego death**, extreme visual and auditory distortions, and feelings of panic.

After both drugs [LSD & Psilocybin], abnormal mental states characterized by feelings of strangeness, difficulty in thinking, anxiety, altered sensory perception (particularly visual), elementary and true visual hallucinations, and alterations of body image were reported by the subjects.

[Comparison of the reactions induced by psilocybin and LSD-25 in man. January 1959](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00408109)

##### Psychological Impact and Aftermath

The combination of these drugs in extreme doses left participants with severe psychological scars. Most reported **paranoia, dissociation, and flashbacks** that persisted for years after the experiments.

Research suggests that this kind of extreme, unstructured exposure can result in **hallucinogen-persisting perception disorder ([HPPD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallucinogen_persisting_perception_disorder#:~:text=Hallucinogen%20persisting%20perception%20disorder%20(HPPD,(THC)%2C%20and%20SSRIs.))**, a condition where individuals experience ongoing visual disturbances and hallucinations.

For **MK Ultra participants**, these drugs were administered in a context designed to break down resistance and exploit vulnerabilities. The prolonged and unrelenting psychological distress from these experiments underscored the risks of using powerful psychoactive substances in an attempt to control or manipulate behavior.

### Electroshock Therapy and Brainwashing in MK Ultra

Electroshock therapy, or **electroconvulsive therapy (ECT)**, was a powerful tool used in MK Ultra as part of the CIA’s efforts to weaken resistance, break down individuals' psychological defenses, and make them compliant to suggestion. ECT involves sending electrical currents through the brain to induce seizures, historically used to treat severe depression and other psychiatric conditions. In MK Ultra, however, **Dr. [Donald Ewen Cameron](https://www.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/mkultra)**—a prominent psychiatrist and key figure in these experiments—took this technique far beyond conventional medical use. Cameron’s methods combined extreme electroshock therapy with **isolation** and **repetitive audio messaging**, a process he coined **“psychic driving.”** His goal was to erase existing memories and personality traits, creating a “blank slate” on which new behaviors and thoughts could supposedly be imprinted.

Cameron's “psychic driving” technique was funded through MK Ultra and represented one of the most extreme attempts to use **brainwashing** to alter the human mind.

Cameron believed that mental disorders, and even undesirable personality traits, could be eliminated by breaking down a person’s sense of identity and reconstructing it from scratch.

#### The Role of Repetitive Audio Messaging

Cameron believed that simply erasing memories and identity was not enough. Once his patients were “depatterned” through electroshock therapy, they were placed in isolation and subjected to **repetitive audio messaging**.

Cameron would play pre-recorded statements or commands on a loop, often for up to **20 hours a day** for several weeks. The content of these messages varied, but they usually involved commands or suggestions intended to “rebuild” the personality according to Cameron’s design.

#### The Psychological and Physical Impact of “Psychic Driving”

The combination of intense ECT and audio repetition left many MK Ultra subjects with profound, irreversible effects.

A study conducted years after the experiments found that **over 80% of Cameron’s former patients** continued to suffer from memory loss, cognitive deficits, and emotional instability.

Among the psychological impacts were:

1. **Severe Memory Loss**: Many patients lost long stretches of their personal history and found it difficult to form new memories. This effect was especially pronounced among those who received repeated high-voltage ECT, combined with the confusion induced by audio suggestion.
2. **Personality Dissociation**: Patients reported feeling disconnected from their previous identity, a phenomenon often referred to as “identity fragmentation.” As Cameron had erased their memories, patients struggled with a sense of self, describing their post-treatment state as one of **detachment and numbness**.
3. **Paranoia and PTSD**: Many former patients developed chronic **paranoia** and **post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD)** symptoms. Flashbacks to their time in sensory isolation and electric shock treatments would return unexpectedly, leading to episodes of panic, confusion, and emotional distress.

### **Sensory Deprivation and Isolation**

Sensory deprivation was one of the most potent tools MK Ultra researchers used to break down individuals' mental defenses, fostering states of disorientation, helplessness, and paranoia that made subjects more open to suggestion and manipulation. The technique involved isolating subjects from any sensory input, including sight, sound, and touch, often for extended periods that pushed the human psyche to its limits. Deprived of external stimuli, participants became highly suggestible, with their mental state resembling that of a blank slate—ideal for attempts at reprogramming or implanting suggestions.

#### How Sensory Deprivation Was Conducted in MK Ultra

MK Ultra [sensory deprivation experiments](https://repository.uel.ac.uk/download/38983638cf076c6186ad63df9a37b7bdef554b7180fc633521e494cc51512e53/218947/Ewen%20Cameron%E2%80%99s%20%E2%80%9Cde-patterning%E2%80%9D%20experiments%20and%20the%20CIA%E2%80%99s%20MK-Ultra%20programme.pdf) typically involved placing subjects in **dark, soundproof rooms**, sometimes called “black rooms,” where all light and sound were blocked. Participants would be put in **small isolation cells** or **floatation tanks**, where they were cut off from all external stimuli. In more intense cases, they would be restrained, with their ears covered, eyes blindfolded, and hands gloved or restrained to prevent tactile feedback. Water tanks were kept at **body temperature**, so participants would lose the sensation of their bodies touching the water—a technique now known as **restricted environmental stimulation therapy (REST)**.

In their 1982 study, "[Compliance and Conversion in a Situation of Sensory Deprivation](https://psycnet.apa.org/record/1983-01024-001)," Serge Moscovici and M. Doms examined how sensory deprivation influences individuals' susceptibility to social influence.

The results indicated that after experiencing sensory deprivation, participants exhibited higher levels of compliance to both majority and minority influence attempts compared to those under normal social influence conditions.

This only corroborates the technique’s effectiveness in inducing a pliable mental state.
