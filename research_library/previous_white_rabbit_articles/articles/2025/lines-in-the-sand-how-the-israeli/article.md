Under the **British Mandate**, Zionist organizations built a parallel state with institutions, land purchases, and even armed militias. Meanwhile, **Palestinians resisted** through general strikes and uprisings, only to be met with **brutal British repression**. The 1939 **White Paper** tried to course-correct—limiting Jewish immigration and proposing joint independence—but by then, the tension had boiled over. Jewish militias had turned against both Arabs and the British. The region was a tinderbox.

Then came **World War II**.

The destruction and horror of the Holocaust didn’t just transform Europe—it supercharged the **Zionist cause**, amplified Jewish immigration to Palestine, and made the question of a Jewish homeland a matter of global urgency. At the same time, the British Empire, stretched thin by war and rebellion, began to **lose its grip on Palestine**.

The conflict was no longer regional. It was global.  
And what came next would change the map—and millions of lives—forever.

## The Holocaust and a Shift in Global Sympathy

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/e135bdaf-6ec0-448c-962f-dbafe4d42be9_460x276.avif)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Yxrp!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe135bdaf-6ec0-448c-962f-dbafe4d42be9_460x276.avif)

[The Holocaust](https://www.britannica.com/event/Holocaust) changed everything.

As Nazi Germany executed its Final Solution, **six million Jews were murdered** in ghettos, concentration camps, and forests across Europe. For surviving Jews, Europe was no longer just inhospitable—it was a graveyard. Displaced and traumatized, many looked to **Palestine** as their only hope.

**But Palestine was still under British rule.**

The 1939 White Paper had **capped Jewish immigration**, fearing it would inflame Arab resistance. As **boats carrying Holocaust survivors** arrived, the British turned many of them away.

For Zionists, **this was unforgivable.**

The movement gained new urgency, momentum, and global sympathy.

### Zionist-British Confrontation Intensifies (1940–1947)

With the Holocaust fresh in the world's mind, the Zionist movement escalated its campaign—not only against Palestinian resistance, but now directly **against the British.**

**Irgun**, **Lehi (Stern Gang)**, and parts of the **Haganah** launched coordinated attacks on British infrastructure. Railways were bombed. Police stations were raided. British officers were assassinated.

Meanwhile, Palestinian Arabs remained politically fragmented and militarily weak. Having been crushed in the 1936–1939 revolt, they had no functioning leadership and no organized armed forces. Caught between Zionist militias and British colonial power, their fate was slipping further out of their hands.

By 1947, Britain had had enough. War-weary and eager to offload the mess, it handed the problem to the newly formed **United Nations**.

### The UN Partition Plan (1947)

The UN [proposed a two-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Partition_Plan_for_Palestine):

- **56% of Palestine** would become a **Jewish state**, despite Jews making up **only about 30% of the population** and owning **roughly 7% of the land**.
- Palestinians, who were still the majority, **would get 44%.**

The **Zionist leadership accepted** the plan publicly, though they privately prepared to take more.

**The Arab world rejected it outright**.

To them is was a **colonial theft** of land and a violation of the right to self-determination.

But the balance of power had already shifted.

### The Civil War in Palestine (1947–1948)

With the British preparing to leave, fighting broke out between Jewish and Arab communities. The **Jewish Agency**, led by **David Ben-Gurion**, began executing a plan: **secure Jewish-majority areas and expand into key Arab regions.**

Zionist militias began attacking Arab towns and neighborhoods. Bombings targeted crowded markets and transit centers. Villages were shelled or stormed. The goal was not just military victory—it was **demographic engineering**.

Nowhere was this clearer than in **Deir Yassin**.

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/478d918f-5cca-4c89-ae28-65125e9ce0fb_573x285.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!3LuT!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F478d918f-5cca-4c89-ae28-65125e9ce0fb_573x285.png)

[Report from the UN Palestine Commission](https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-211346/)

On **April 9, 1948**, Irgun and Lehi fighters massacred **[about 250 villagers](https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-211346/)**, including women and children. British and UN observers reported horrific scenes of executions and mutilation.

“Women and children were stripped, lined up, photographed, and then slaughtered by automatic firing and survivors have told of even more incredible bestialities…Those who were taken prisoners were treated with degrading brutality.”

- [1948 Report](https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-211346/)

The message spread: **leave, or be killed.**

### May 1948 — The Declaration of the State of Israel

On **May 14, 1948**, David Ben-Gurion declared the establishment of the **State of Israel**. The very next day, British forces completed their withdrawal, and armies from neighboring Arab countries entered the former Mandate territory.

But the war was already **half-won.**

The new Israeli army, composed of former Haganah, Irgun, and Lehi fighters, had modern weapons, a unified command, and the support of major powers. The Arab armies were fragmented, poorly coordinated, and suspicious of each other.

Israeli forces not only held their ground—they went on the offensive, **capturing Arab towns and pushing beyond the UN-assigned borders**.

### The Nakba: The Catastrophic Displacement of Palestinians

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/78164fd0-204b-434a-9fb5-208fbebbdf8d_389x314.jpeg)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Och6!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F78164fd0-204b-434a-9fb5-208fbebbdf8d_389x314.jpeg)

Palestine refugees making their way from Galilee in October-November 1948

As towns fell, **hundreds of thousands of Palestinians fled or were forcibly expelled**. In places like **Lydda and Ramleh**, entire populations were marched out at gunpoint. **Over 50,000 people** were expelled from Lydda and Ramleh in what became known as the **[Lydda Death March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_expulsion_from_Lydda_and_Ramle)**.

Homes were looted. Towns were renamed. The geography of Palestine was overwritten.

By the end of 1948, **[over 750,000 Palestinians](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2017/5/23/the-nakba-did-not-start-or-end-in-1948) had become refugees**. Some fled to neighboring countries. Others ended up in camps within what remained of Palestine. Most have never been allowed to return.

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/a2f0a114-4855-4ebb-9164-196be13817af_1024x912.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!X31v!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa2f0a114-4855-4ebb-9164-196be13817af_1024x912.png)

Nahr al-Bared refugee camp in Lebanon, 1952

In Arabic, this moment is called **[the Nakba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakba)** — the **catastrophe**.

### The Aftermath: Partition, Occupation, and a Broken Promise

After the war, **[Israel controlled 77%](https://www.un.org/unispal/history/) of historic Palestine**. The remaining 23% — the **West Bank** and **Gaza Strip** — were annexed by **Jordan** and **Egypt**, respectively.

The United Nations passed **[Resolution 194](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_194)**, calling for the right of Palestinian refugees to return to their homes.

**Israel refused to comply.**

The Palestinians were left stateless, scattered, and politically voiceless.

### Echoes of the Nakba in the Present

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/1b1f7f2e-a367-425b-8f1e-26dd35ad086e_460x276.jpeg)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!4bZx!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F1b1f7f2e-a367-425b-8f1e-26dd35ad086e_460x276.jpeg)

An Israeli soldier watches Palestinians flee to Jordan from the West Bank, 1967.

In the decades since, Israel has continued expanding its territory through settlements, land seizures, and military control. In **1967**, following the Six-Day War, Israel captured the **West Bank** and **Gaza Strip**, bringing around **[1 million Palestinians](https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-209820/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)** under its control:

Today, the populations of Jews and Palestinians under Israeli control are [roughly](https://www.timesofisrael.com/jews-now-a-minority-in-israel-and-the-territories-demographer-says/) **[equal](https://www.timesofisrael.com/jews-now-a-minority-in-israel-and-the-territories-demographer-says/)**. But [the rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Israel) are not:

- **Jewish citizens** vote, move freely, and access courts.
- **Palestinians in occupied territories** live under military law, without [voting rights](https://freedomhouse.org/country/west-bank/freedom-world/2023), passports, or [legal recourse](https://www.state.gov/reports/2022-country-reports-on-human-rights-practices/israel-west-bank-and-gaza/).

International human rights organizations, including **[Amnesty International](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/amnesty-international-releases-report-accusing-israel-of-apartheid)**, **[Human Rights Watch](https://www.hrw.org/report/2021/04/27/threshold-crossed/israeli-authorities-and-crimes-apartheid-and-persecution)**, and **[B'Tselem](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/world/b-tselem-labels-israel-apartheid-regime-first-time-n1253863)**, have all labeled this a system of **apartheid**.

## **A Century of Broken Promises**

From **1517 to 1917**, **Palestine was under Ottoman rule**—a diverse land where Muslims, Christians, and Jews lived side by side. Then **World War I flipped the region upside down**. Britain promised the land to **the Arabs**, to **the French**, and to **the Jews**—all at once.

The result? Chaos.

After the war, Britain took control. As **Zionist immigration grew**, Palestinians watched their homeland slip away. Protests were met with bullets. Then, in **1939**, the British finally offered a deal: **Palestine would become independent in 10 years, shared between Arabs and Jews**.

But **World War II** changed everything. Jewish suffering reached its peak in the Holocaust. Zionist militias turned on the British. And by **1947**, Britain walked away, tossing the problem to the **UN**.

The **UN Partition Plan** gave Jews over **half the land**, even though they were still a minority. Civil war broke out. And in **May 1948**, just before that 10-year promise expired, **Israel was declared a state**.

That was the start of the **Nakba**—the mass displacement of Palestinians, whose homes, lands, and futures were erased overnight.

## **So Why Do the Palestinians Hate the Jews?**

**Let’s cut through the noise.** It’s a heavy question—blunt, often whispered, and too rarely answered with nuance. But if we’re going to talk honestly, we have to face it head-on.

The truth is: **most Palestinians don’t “hate Jews.”** They hate what’s happened to them. They hate being displaced. They hate being silenced. They hate being occupied. And sometimes, they confuse the people with the system. Happens more often than you think—in every country, with every conflict.

But let’s be clear: **this isn’t about religion.**

**Muslims, Jews, and Christians** lived in Palestine for centuries—**neighbors**, **coworkers**, **market traders**. There were tensions, yes, but not this existential hatred. That didn’t start because of who Jews were. It started because of **what was done in their name.**

**Imagine this: you’re a Palestinian farmer.**

Your family has lived on this land for generations. Then one day, armed men come and say the land isn’t yours anymore. Your neighbors are forced out. Your village is bombed. You’re told the people replacing you deserve it—because of something *you* didn’t do, *your ancestors* didn’t do.

And when you protest—nonviolently, politically, or even angrily—you’re called a **terrorist**. You’re told to “accept reality.”

But that **reality is your dispossession**.   
Your exile.   
Your humiliation.

**So the anger builds.** And when anger goes unanswered long enough, it curdles into bitterness. Into rage. Sometimes into hate.

And yes, some Palestinians do grow up with hatred toward Jews—not because of their faith, but because they see the **Israeli military**, the **settlers**, the **checkpoints**, the **home demolitions**, and the **bombings** as being carried out *by Jews*, for Jews. And unless someone breaks that cycle and explains the difference, **they don’t see the nuance—we never taught it to them.**

But here’s the twist: many Jews feel the same way about Palestinians. They hear chants calling for the destruction of Israel. They see stabbings, rockets, riots. They’ve lost family members to suicide bombings. And unless *they* are taught nuance, compassion, and history, they grow up believing Palestinians are **animals, terrorists, or worse.**

**This isn’t genetic hatred. It’s taught. It’s fed. It’s sharpened.**

If you take a child in Gaza and a child in Tel Aviv, and raise them in peace—without fear, without bombs, without propaganda—do they hate each other? Of course not.

So the question isn’t really “Why do Palestinians hate Jews?”  
The real question is: **What have we done that makes hatred feel easier than healing?**  
And what will we do to undo that?

Because until we confront the pain—not just our own, but each other’s—we’re doomed to keep circling this fire, burning everything we love along the way.

## **Where Do We Go From Here?**

History doesn’t move in straight lines—it coils, it breaks, and sometimes, it bleeds.

The **Jewish people**, scattered and persecuted for centuries across Europe and the Middle East, were hunted, burned, exiled, and erased—**not because of anything they did, but simply because they were Jews.**

After the Holocaust, the world finally said, *“never again.”*

And so, a homeland was born. Whether that homeland was *earned*, *given*, or *taken* is still debated—but what isn’t debatable is that **Israel exists**. It's not going anywhere.

And asking a people who have spent centuries without protection to simply “give up land” feels, to many, like asking them to step back into exile.

**Once bitten, twice shy.**

---

But the **Palestinians have a point, too.** A powerful one.

They were there.

While Jews wandered stateless, they built homes, raised families, and tilled the land in Palestine. They were the ones who opened their eyes every morning under that sun, among those olive trees, for generations. They were promised freedom by the British—only to be betrayed. And when they tried peaceful resistance, they were punished. When they tried rebellion, they were crushed. They were evicted not just from land, but from **the story itself**.

*So, where does that leave us?*

---

**There is no way forward until people start talking—and listening.** Not shouting. Not defending their side with memes, missiles, or mandates.

Talking.   
Listening.

Not for **the sake of being right**, but for **the sake of finding a future**.

A *good deal* isn’t when one side gets everything. It’s when **neither side gets exactly what they want**, but both can finally rest.

And while we’re at it, we need to learn how to separate a **nation’s government** from its **people**.

I love America, but I’ll be the first to tell you: **America has done some truly fucked up things.** Very fucked up.

That doesn’t mean I hate Americans.   
*Quite the opposite.*   
It means I love this place enough to want it to be better.

The same goes for **Israel**.

The Israeli government has made devastating, often indefensible decisions. **But** **we can hold a government accountable while loving its people**. We can see their humanity *and* their mistakes. We can mourn the past while still building something honest in the present.

Justice doesn’t always look like vengeance or victory. Sometimes, justice looks like a table, two chairs, and a conversation that finally begins with:

*“Tell me your story. And I’ll tell you mine.”*
