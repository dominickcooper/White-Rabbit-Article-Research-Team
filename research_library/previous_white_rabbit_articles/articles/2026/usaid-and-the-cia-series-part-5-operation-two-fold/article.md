On **May 25, 1973**, CIA Director of Security [Howard J. Osborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_J._Osborn) sent [a short memo](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/project%20two-fold%5B15273998%5D.pdf) upstairs about a project called **TWO-FOLD**.

The memo described two operations running under the same roof. One placed secretly recruited personnel inside the **Bureau of Narcotics and Dangerous Drugs (BNDD)** to investigate corruption. The other used deeply covered personnel against major international narcotics traffickers overseas.

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/f7795b92-ce23-443d-820b-f240d23e7209_804x753.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!xQV6!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ff7795b92-ce23-443d-820b-f240d23e7209_804x753.png)

Then came the instruction (*or really a **“recommendation”***) from the top:

> **Terminate the domestic side. Keep the foreign narcotics side going.**

## **THE PROJECT THEY ONLY HALF-KILLED**

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/20cfef21-1bbc-4444-8af9-b01c3db0cd73_1254x1254.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!mpHn!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F20cfef21-1bbc-4444-8af9-b01c3db0cd73_1254x1254.png)

John “Jack” Ingersoll

CIA records trace TWO-FOLD to **BNDD Director [John Ingersoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Ingersoll)**, who asked Director [Richard Helms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Helms) for help developing an internal-security capability inside his own agency. Corruption was a real problem. Former Federal Bureau of Narcotics (**FBN**) personnel had carried old relationships, rivalries and allegations into the new BNDD, and Ingersoll wanted people who could **investigate the investigators**.

[Helms approved the arrangement](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/project%20two-fold%5B15273998%5D.pdf) on **February 12, 1971**. CIA Security would identify candidates, assess them and quietly move them toward BNDD employment. Once hired, **BNDD’s chief inspector** would assign them to investigate corruption inside the bureau.

The recruiting method was deliberately hidden. CIA used **a proprietary company** and officers posing as private businessmen to approach candidates. **Patrick Fuller**, BNDD’s chief inspector, [later described](https://www.counterpunch.org/2008/01/25/operation-two-fold/) the setup to investigator Douglas Valentine:

> *“We recruited the CIA officers for BNDD through a proprietary company.”*

The [Rockefeller Commission](https://www.fordlibrarymuseum.gov/sites/default/files/pdf_documents/library/document/0005/7288238.pdf) eventually supplied the number: **19 recruits** between late 1970 and July 1973.

**Then there was the other half.**

CIA also arranged **a much smaller group** of commercially covered personnel for foreign narcotics work. The official record counts **two covered agents**. They looked like BNDD employees, BNDD paid the bills, and **CIA handled the concealed administrative side**.

The May 1973 memo says the agents themselves could be kept unaware of CIA’s role.

*Think about that for a second.*

You could be working **an international narcotics assignment for BNDD** while **the CIA** quietly handled the machinery underneath your cover—and you didn’t even know it.

Two years later, Rockefeller concluded that **the project crossed a legal line**, writing that CIA participation violated the 1947 law restricting the Agency from domestic law-enforcement activity.

So Colby’s May 1973 recommendation makes sense **up to a point**: stop the domestic internal-security operation.

But then he adds the part that changes the story.

**Keep the foreign narcotics collection.**

That decision makes a lot more sense **once you look at** what Washington had already built around it.

## **THE AGENT IN THE PSYCH WARD**

In February 1974, DEA agent **Anthony Tripodi** landed in the psychiatric ward of St. Luke’s Hospital in New York. He was in bad shape: **drinking heavily,** possibly suffering from **PTSD**.

*Now remember the official timeline.*

- Tripodi is sitting in that psychiatric ward in **February 1974**…and says the cleanup was still happening.

The inspectors (**Mort Benjamin** and **Chuck Sherman**) who spoke with him initially thought he was delusional. **After all, the program was shut down.**

## **FAQ**

### **What was Operation TWO-FOLD?**

Operation TWO-FOLD was a CIA-assisted BNDD personnel program approved in 1971. CIA secretly recruited people for BNDD internal-security work and also supported a smaller, deeply covered foreign narcotics component. Rockefeller later concluded CIA’s participation in the domestic law-enforcement side violated the 1947 National Security Act.

### **Did TWO-FOLD really end in 1973?**

The official record puts the formal end of CIA recruiting and the two covered-agent arrangements in July 1973, while a May 1973 CIA memo explicitly selected foreign narcotics collection for continuation. Valentine’s interviews with Fuller, Ingersoll, Brosan and others describe a messier cleanup extending into 1974. The safest reading is that the formal project ended while some foreign work, personnel relationships and residual arrangements continued through other channels.

### **Did TWO-FOLD actually uncover corruption inside BNDD?**

Valentine writes that TWO-FOLD never produced a single dismissal. That does not mean the corruption problem was imaginary; BNDD had serious internal-integrity issues. It does raise an obvious question about what the covert personnel program actually accomplished beyond placing CIA-experienced people inside the bureau.

### **Was DEA controlled by CIA?**

The record shows a deep and sometimes problematic relationship: CIA-assisted recruitment, former CIA officers helping build BNDD intelligence, CIA training, overseas liaison, former CIA personnel entering DEA and continuing foreign intelligence cooperation. That is enough to establish significant institutional penetration and dependence without pretending every DEA office or agent answered to Langley.

### **What does this have to do with USAID?**

USAID’s Office of Public Safety and federal narcotics enforcement were separate systems, but both intersected with CIA and foreign police. As Congress dismantled OPS, Section 660 preserved narcotics exceptions; in Laos, an OPS narcotics-advisory function was explicitly assigned to DEA. TWO-FOLD shows what was already being built on the narcotics side as the old USAID police system was being shut down.
