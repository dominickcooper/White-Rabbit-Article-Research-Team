The **Skull and Bones fraternity**, one of the most secretive and controversial organizations in the world, has been a fixture at Yale University since its founding in 1832. Known for its powerful alumni, the society has long been the subject of speculation and conspiracy due to its secretive nature. From U.S. Presidents to influential businessmen, the list of **Skull and Bones members** reads like a who's who of American power. This article will explore the **history of Skull and Bones**, its rituals, notable members, and the controversies that have followed it over the years.

---

## **History of the Skull and Bones Fraternity**

The **Skull and Bones fraternity** was founded at Yale University in 1832 by **William Huntington Russell** and **Alphonso Taft**. The two men, both Yale students, created the organization as a response to the university's existing secret societies. Their goal was to establish a society that would recognize outstanding members of the student body for their leadership and academic achievements.

Russell and Taft designed the Skull and Bones fraternity as an exclusive club, with a focus on cultivating bonds between the nation's future leaders. Over the years, the fraternity has produced some of the most influential figures in American politics, law, and business, creating an aura of mystery and power around the organization.

### **Origins and Purpose**

Modeled after European secret societies, Skull and Bones was designed as a place where a select group of students could discuss ideas and form lasting relationships away from the prying eyes of the public. The fraternity quickly gained a reputation for its exclusivity and the prestigious nature of its membership, a tradition that continues to this day.

Every year, only 15 senior Yale students are "tapped" to join the fraternity. These members, chosen for their leadership qualities and academic prowess, are inducted through secretive initiation rituals and are considered "Bonesmen" for life.

---

Yale University has long been home to a number of **secret societies**, exclusive organizations that have cultivated a sense of mystery and exclusivity on campus for over two centuries. These societies are designed for a select group of students, often seniors, chosen for their leadership abilities, academic excellence, or connections. Though the most famous of these societies is Skull and Bones, several others play significant roles in Yale's culture and have similar clandestine rituals and membership structures.

Here’s a deeper look at Yale’s existing secret societies and how they compare to Skull and Bones:

---

### **Wolf's Head**

Founded in 1883, **Wolf's Head** is one of Yale’s oldest and most prestigious secret societies, alongside Skull and Bones. It was established by students who wanted to create an alternative society to Skull and Bones that offered similar exclusivity without the perceived control by established alumni. Wolf’s Head is known for its discretion and loyalty among its members.

- **Symbolism and Structure**: The society is named after its emblem, a wolf’s head, and it operates in a windowless building called "The Hall" located near the center of the Yale campus.
- **Membership**: Like Skull and Bones, Wolf's Head taps around 15 students during their junior year to join as seniors.
- **Rituals**: Although many of its rituals remain secret, it is believed that they are similar in nature to those of other Yale societies, focusing on bonding and loyalty.

---

### **Scroll and Key**

**Scroll and Key**, founded in 1842, is often considered Yale’s second-most prominent secret society after Skull and Bones. The society was created by John Addison Porter and others who, like the founders of Wolf's Head, were discontent with the exclusivity of Skull and Bones and wanted to provide an alternative for distinguished students.

- **Symbolism and Architecture**: The society is named for its symbols, the scroll and key, which represent knowledge and access to wisdom. The Scroll and Key society building, known as "The House," is one of the most architecturally significant secret society tombs on the Yale campus, built in an ornate, Gothic style.
- **Membership**: Like its counterparts, Scroll and Key taps 15 members each year, choosing students who exhibit leadership, academic excellence, or athletic achievement.
- **Rituals**: While much of the initiation process remains unknown, the society is known for its emphasis on intellectual discussions and the lifelong relationships it fosters between its members.

---

### **Book and Snake**

Founded in 1863, **Book and Snake** is one of the lesser-known but still respected secret societies at Yale. Unlike Skull and Bones or Scroll and Key, Book and Snake has maintained a relatively low profile, though its members have often gone on to hold significant roles in various fields.

- **Symbolism**: The society’s name comes from its symbol, a book and snake entwined, representing wisdom and mystery.
- **Membership**: Like other Yale societies, Book and Snake selects 15 members from each class year, typically based on their achievements and leadership potential.
- **Secrecy**: Book and Snake is known for its discretion, and very little about its internal workings or rituals is publicly known.

---

### **Elihu**

Founded in 1903, **Elihu** is one of the newer secret societies at Yale. It was created as a more inclusive alternative to the older societies, embracing a broader cross-section of students, particularly those from different socioeconomic backgrounds.

- **Inclusivity**: Elihu differs from other secret societies in that it has historically been more open to non-traditional members and does not enforce the same level of secrecy regarding its activities.
- **Focus**: Elihu places a strong emphasis on public service and intellectual discussion, aligning its values with those of the university itself rather than focusing on exclusive connections.

---

### **Berzelius**

**Berzelius**, founded in 1848, is another important but relatively understated secret society. Unlike other secret societies, which tend to focus on leadership in politics and business, Berzelius has been known to tap students from the sciences, reflecting the group’s intellectual focus.

- **Origin**: The society was named after the Swedish chemist Jöns Jacob Berzelius, and it was initially conceived as a place for students interested in scientific endeavors.
- **Membership**: Like Yale’s other secret societies, Berzelius taps around 15 seniors each year, but it has a more academic bent, often selecting students with significant accomplishments in the sciences.
- **Secrecy**: Although Berzelius is more academically oriented than some of its counterparts, it still maintains a high degree of secrecy about its inner workings and rituals.

---

### **Yale Secret Societies: Commonalities and Differences**

Despite their individual histories and unique focuses, Yale’s secret societies share several common characteristics:

- **Tapping and Membership**: All of these societies, including Skull and Bones, use a "tapping" process to select new members. Tapping ceremonies are secretive and involve senior society members approaching juniors who are invited to join.
- **Exclusive Membership**: Only about 15 students are tapped each year for each society, ensuring that membership remains exclusive and prestigious.
- **Lifelong Bonds**: Many of Yale’s secret societies emphasize the importance of lifelong relationships between members, leading to powerful networks that extend well beyond the university.
- **Rituals**: Rituals, initiations, and meetings are conducted in private, and members are often sworn to secrecy about what occurs behind closed doors.

## **Notable Members of Skull and Bones**

The fraternity's long list of alumni includes prominent politicians, businessmen, and cultural leaders, many of whom have gone on to wield significant influence in various sectors of society. Some of the most notable **Skull and Bones members** include:

- **George H.W. Bush**: The 41st President of the United States, Bush was tapped for Skull and Bones in 1948.
- **George W. Bush**: Like his father, the 43rd President of the United States also became a Bonesman while attending Yale.
- **John Kerry**: The former U.S. Secretary of State and 2004 Democratic presidential candidate was also a member of Skull and Bones, tapped in 1966.
- **William Howard Taft**: The 27th President of the United States and later Chief Justice of the U.S. Supreme Court, Taft’s father, Alphonso Taft, was one of the fraternity’s co-founders.

The presence of such high-profile individuals in the ranks of Skull and Bones has led to much speculation about the fraternity’s influence on American and global affairs.

---

## **Secret Rituals and Symbols**

Much of the mystery surrounding the **Skull and Bones fraternity** stems from its secretive rituals, many of which are said to be rooted in symbolism related to death. The most iconic symbol of Skull and Bones is the skull and crossbones emblem, often accompanied by the number "322," a reference to the society's founding year.

### **Initiation Rites**

Though details of the initiation ceremony remain largely unknown, rumors suggest that new members are required to confess their deepest secrets and engage in symbolic acts related to death and rebirth. One of the more infamous aspects of the initiation is the alleged use of a skull, which is said to be part of the fraternity's rituals.

### **The Tomb**

The **Skull and Bones headquarters**, known as "The Tomb," is located on Yale’s campus and has become a focal point for much of the speculation surrounding the society. The Tomb is a windowless, fortress-like building where members are said to gather for meetings and ceremonies. The secrecy surrounding what takes place within The Tomb has only heightened the mystique of the fraternity.

### **Rituals of Power and Influence**

Allegedly, the fraternity’s rituals aim to create a sense of brotherhood and loyalty among its members, which many believe is the reason for the enduring influence of Skull and Bones alumni. The secrecy and intensity of the rituals are designed to foster an unbreakable bond, ensuring that Bonesmen support one another throughout their careers.

---

## **Influence of Skull and Bones on Global Affairs**

Due to the prominence of its members, the **Skull and Bones fraternity** has long been suspected of wielding considerable influence over global politics, economics, and culture. Members of Skull and Bones are often accused of forming a shadowy elite, with some conspiracy theorists believing the fraternity to be part of a larger globalist agenda.

### **Political Power**

Several U.S. Presidents, Senators, and high-ranking government officials have come from Skull and Bones, leading to theories that the society exerts undue influence over American politics. During the 2004 U.S. Presidential Election, both the Republican and Democratic candidates—George W. Bush and John Kerry—were Bonesmen, leading to increased scrutiny of the fraternity's role in the political landscape.

### **Economic and Corporate Influence**

Beyond politics, Skull and Bones members have also left their mark on the business world. Many Bonesmen have gone on to lead major corporations, including the oil, banking, and media industries. Their shared backgrounds and the secrecy of their society have led to accusations of a "Bonesman network" that operates behind the scenes to promote the interests of its members.

---

## **Controversies and Conspiracy Theories**

The **Skull and Bones fraternity** has been the subject of numerous conspiracy theories over the years, with many believing that the society operates as a secret government within a government. These accusations are often fueled by the fraternity’s tradition of secrecy, the high-profile positions of its members, and the perception that Bonesmen are connected to global financial and political elites.

### **Accusations of a New World Order**

One of the most persistent conspiracy theories is that Skull and Bones is part of a **New World Order**, a secretive group of elites working toward global domination. Proponents of this theory believe that the society’s members use their powerful positions to steer world events, from wars to financial crises, in a way that benefits the wealthy and powerful at the expense of ordinary people.

### **Grave Robbing Allegations**

In one of the most infamous stories surrounding the society, Skull and Bones has been accused of stealing the skull of Native American leader **Geronimo**. Allegedly, members of the fraternity, including Prescott Bush (the grandfather of George W. Bush), exhumed Geronimo’s skull during World War I and brought it back to The Tomb as a part of their rituals. While this story remains unproven, it has added to the sinister aura surrounding Skull and Bones.

---

## **Conclusion**

The **Skull and Bones fraternity** remains one of the most secretive and influential societies in American history. From its mysterious rituals to its powerful members, the fraternity has long been a source of fascination and controversy. Whether viewed as an elite networking group or a shadowy organization shaping world events, Skull and Bones continues to capture the public’s imagination. Its influence on politics, business, and society shows no sign of waning, as new generations of Yale graduates continue to be inducted into its ranks.
