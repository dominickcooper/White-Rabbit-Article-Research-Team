**But it wouldn’t stay civilian for long.**

But it was too late. The machine was already **far beyond his control**.

Truman’s containment doctrine, reinforced by **George F. Kennan’s** *[Long Telegram](https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/coldwar/documents/episode-1/kennan.htm)* and the famous **[“X” Article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_Article)**, provided the ideological fuel.

> **Summary of George F. Kennan’s Long Telegram and "X" Article**
>
> *In 1946, George F. Kennan, a U.S. diplomat stationed in Moscow, sent what would become known as the **Long Telegram** to the U.S. State Department. This 5,000-word cable offered a detailed analysis of Soviet foreign policy and became a cornerstone of American Cold War strategy. Kennan’s key argument was that the Soviet Union was inherently expansionist and that the U.S. must adopt a policy of "containment" to prevent the spread of communism. He believed the Soviets would push for influence wherever they could, but that they were also deeply paranoid and would retreat if met with determined opposition.*
>
> *Kennan’s **"X" Article**, published in 1947 in Foreign Affairs, expanded on the ideas in the Long Telegram. This article, titled "The Sources of Soviet Conduct," called for a sustained, long-term strategy to counter Soviet power. Kennan argued that the U.S. should **not attempt to dismantle** Soviet communism but should **work to prevent its spread** through political, economic, and military means. He introduced the concept of **"containment"** as the central principle of U.S. foreign policy during the Cold War, marking a shift in America’s approach to dealing with the Soviet Union.*

Kennan’s model wasn’t just about defending borders—it was about **projecting influence**, especially in gray zones where conventional militaries couldn’t tread.

“[The Soviet Union] does not take unnecessary risks. [It is] impervious to logic of reason, and it is highly sensitive to **logic of force**.”  
—George F. Kennan, [Long Telegram](https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/coldwar/documents/episode-1/kennan.htm)

The CIA became the **muscle** of that projection, and **NATO**—formed under Truman and spearheaded diplomatically by Secretary of State **Dean Acheson**—was its shield.

**Some interesting things** to note about these individuals:

### **Harry S. Truman**

- **Signed the National Security Act (1947), creating the CIA** as an intelligence-gathering agency—not a covert operations outfit, as he clarified in a 1963 *Washington Post* op-ed.

- Though not directly involved in **CPC/ACC**, his **appointment of [Dwight D. Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_D._Eisenhower) as NATO’s first SACEUR (1950)** linked **military command with intelligence coordination**, paving the way for stay-behind operations.

### **[George F. Kennan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_F._Kennan)**

- **Author of the "Long Telegram" and the containment doctrine**, which became the **philosophical foundation** of both the CIA and NATO.
- **Worked closely with [Frank Wisner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Wisner)** (OPC chief) to support early CIA covert ops like **[Radio Free Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Free_Europe/Radio_Liberty)**.

- Despite later criticism, his early framing of the Soviet threat **justified NATO’s existence and CIA's global mission.**

### **[Dean Acheson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_Acheson)**

- As **Secretary of State (1949–1953)**, Acheson was **NATO’s primary architect**—negotiated the treaty, signed it, and led its ratification push.
- Championed NATO as an extension of the **[Truman Doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truman_Doctrine) and [Marshall Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Plan)**, holding early NATO ministerial meetings and solidifying its institutional structure.
- Backed the **CIA’s creation** as Under Secretary (1945–1947), and later **relied on CIA intelligence during key Cold War flashpoints**, including the [Korean War](https://koreanwarlegacy.org/chapters/prewar-context-western/).
- Maintained a close alliance with **CIA Director [Walter Bedell Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Bedell_Smith)**, reinforcing coordination between **NATO planning and CIA black ops**.
- Declassified NATO and CIA documents confirm that Acheson **approved intelligence-sharing protocols** and indirectly supported **CPC/ACC sabotage operations**, even as formal links remained classified.

**Truman, Acheson, and Kennan formed the ideological and structural triad behind the postwar security apparatus.**

Truman provided the executive force—signing into law both the **CIA and NATO**, while Acheson executed the diplomacy to bind them together as a unified front against Soviet expansion.

**Kennan supplied the strategic doctrine**—his containment theory became the **justification for covert operations**, enabling the CIA to evolve from intelligence-gathering into **actionable black operations** aligned with NATO planning, laying the groundwork for what would later become the **International Syndicate.**
