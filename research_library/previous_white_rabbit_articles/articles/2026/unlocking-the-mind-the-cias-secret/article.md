## What is the Gateway Process?

At its core, the Gateway Process is a method designed to alter brainwave patterns and expand consciousness. It combines advanced sound technology, meditation techniques, and biofeedback to help participants achieve a state where the brain hemispheres are synchronized. This synchronization, known as 'Hemi-Sync,' enables users to explore altered states of consciousness, from heightened creativity to out-of-body experiences.

The Gateway Process has its roots in the Monroe Institute, founded by Robert Monroe, a pioneer in consciousness studies. Monroe discovered that specific sound frequencies could synchronize brain hemispheres, a phenomenon he dubbed Hemi-Sync. With the advent of Cold War tensions, the CIA took interest in Monroe’s research as part of their broader exploration of human potential and psychic phenomena, leading to a collaboration that would result in the Gateway Process.

## Who is Major General Stubblebine

There is much more information on Major General Albert N. Stubblebine III. MG Stubblebine was a highly influential figure in U.S. military intelligence. He was commissioned a 2nd Lieutenant of Armor in 1952 following his graduation the US Military Academy. The first third of his career, he was an instructor at his alma mater where he taught chemistry.

He served in Vietnam, attended the National War College in 1971, and a slew of [other appointments](https://www.ikn.army.mil/apps/MIHOF/biographies/Stubblebine,%20Albert.pdf) after that:

- **1968**: Intelligence Officer (G-2) for the **25th Infantry Division** during the Vietnam War.
- **1971**: Attended the **National War College**.
- **Early 1970s**: Took command of the **US Army Imagery Interpretation Center**, Fort Holabird.
- **1970s**: Served as **Executive to the Deputy Director** of the **Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)** and the **DA Chief of Research, Development, and Acquisition**.
- **1970s**: Commanded the **902nd Military Intelligence (MI) Group**.
- **1970s**: **Director of Intelligence Systems** at the **Army Materiel Command**.
- **1970s**: **Director, Tactical/Strategic Intelligence**, Office of the Assistant Chief of Staff for Intelligence, Department of the Army (OACSI-DA).
- **1977-1979**: Commandant of the **U.S. Army Intelligence Center and School**, where he was instrumental in developing the first Army Intelligence architecture.
- **1979**: Commanded the **U.S. Army Electronics Research and Development Command**, overseeing the research, development, and acquisition of intelligence systems.
- **1981-1984**: Commanding General of the **U.S. Army Intelligence and Security Command (INSCOM)**, during which he advocated for remote viewing and psychic research as part of the **Stargate Project**.
- **1984**: Retired from active duty following controversy over his involvement in paranormal experiments.
- **1990**: Inducted into the **Military Intelligence Hall of Fame** for his contributions to military intelligence.

You probably noticed the **Stargate Project**. This particular project explored **remote viewing**—the ability to perceive distant or unseen targets psychically. This project, along with others like the **Gateway Process**, was aimed at investigating human consciousness and its potential for military applications.

*Yes, this sounds like an episode of Fringe, The Twilight Zone, and a Phillip K Dick novel all wrapped in one.*

British journalist and author Jon Ronson published a book titled “The Men who Stare at Goats" that discusses the Stargate Project, Gateway Process, Stubblebine and others. among other things. In it, Ronson discusses how Stubblebine was deeply invested in exploring **paranormal abilities**.

Stubblebine insisted that all of his **battalion commanders** master the technique of spoon-bending, modeled after the famous psychic **Uri Geller**. He also personally attempted numerous **psychic** **demonstrations**, including trying to **walk through solid walls**, **levitate**, and even manipulate clouds **using his mind**. The belief within his unit was that soldiers could acquire extraordinary abilities, such as **becoming** **invisible**, passing effortlessly through physical barriers, and, most disturbingly, being able to **kill goats simply by staring at them​**.

Stubblebine's push for **psychic** **research**, including **remote** **viewing**, faced opposition from more conservative military figures, eventually leading to his **early** **retirement** in 1984. After his retirement, the Stargate Project and similar initiatives continued under different leadership but ultimately faced defunding and termination in 1995​.

### Hemi-Sync Technology: A Core Component

The linchpin of the Gateway Process is Hemi-Sync technology. This system uses **binaural beats**—two slightly different sound frequencies played in each ear—to produce a single, unified brainwave pattern.

By playing two slightly different frequencies, the brain perceives a third "**phantom**" frequency. This phenomenon has been well documented:

- **Oster, G. (1973). "[Auditory Beats in the Brain](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/auditory-beats-in-the-brain/)"** – This foundational study explains how binaural beats are perceived when two different frequencies are played in each ear, creating a phantom beat frequency in the brain.
- **Draganova et al. (2007). "[Cortical steady-state responses to central and peripheral auditory beats](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17827173/)"** – Shows how the brain processes binaural beats and the resulting phantom perception.
- **Schwarz & Taylor (2005). "[Binaural Interaction in the Human Auditory System](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Human-auditory-steady-state-responses-to-binaural-Schwarz-Taylor/c0b5f23d9fe2c64e7b06cc0034492f3af3d7b6eb)"** – Discusses the neurological basis for the brain's perception of phantom beats. This trick of auditory processing encourages brainwave synchronization, guiding participants into states of deep relaxation or heightened awareness, depending on the desired outcome.

## So, is the Gateway Process real?

The potential applications of the Gateway Process are vast, if real:

- Remote Viewing and Psychic Phenomena

The **[Monroe Institute](https://www.monroeinstitute.org/?srsltid=AfmBOorBz0wZm4Tw0uMIypz_u-d1yla68vxZVIes5pYBnZHMWD65a8gs)**, founded by **Robert Monroe** in the 1970s, was behind the development of the **Gateway** **Process**. The institute offers courses, workshops, and training programs designed to help individuals experience out-of-body travel, remote viewing, and other forms of “expanded awareness.”

**Robert Monroe**, the founder, was originally a [radio broadcasting executive](https://hemi-sync.com/learn/about-us/). He served as Vice President and member of the Board of Directors of the Mutual Broadcasting System network.

Mutual was most famous for being the original network for shows like **The Lone Ranger**, **The Adventures of Superman**, and **The Shadow**. It also served as a long-time broadcaster for **Major League Baseball**, including the **All-Star Game** and **World Series**, as well as for the **National Football League** and **Notre Dame Fighting Irish football**. In addition to its sports coverage, Mutual operated a respected news service and featured popular commentary programs from the mid-1930s until it shut down in 1999. In the 1970s, Mutual made waves by pioneering the first nationwide late-night call-in talk shows, introducing hosts like **Larry King** and later, **Jim Bohannon**, to the country.

## Why was the CIA interested in the Gateway Process?

Why did the CIA take such a keen interest in the Gateway Process? For the CIA, this program wasn’t about personal growth—it was about exploring the potential for psychic abilities and intelligence-gathering.

In the early 2000s, several documents related to the **Gateway** **Process** were declassified, sparking widespread interest. These files, including the infamous "**Gateway** **Report**," detailed how the CIA **conducted** **experiments** with participants using the **Gateway** **techniques**, some even exploring out-of-body experiences.

The Gateway Process was part of a broader set of CIA programs focused on mind control and psychic phenomena, with programs like **MKUltra**.

**Is the Gateway Process still being used?**

While the **1983 "Analysis and Assessment of Gateway Process"** report details the CIA's interest in using altered states of consciousness for military and intelligence purposes, there are no follow-up documents showing continued involvement after the declassification.

While there is no explicit statement from the CIA saying they completely stopped using the **Gateway** **Process**, the termination of related projects like **Stargate**, along with the declassification of documents and a shift to more traditional intelligence methods, strongly suggests that the CIA discontinued their involvement with the Gateway Process by the mid-1990s.

However, related to the termination of Stargate, there is an **interesting note**.

After the **American Institutes for Research** (**AIR**) conducted [a review in 1995](https://irp.fas.org/program/collect/air1995.pdf) (a 183 page document), the **CIA** concluded that the findings from psychic research, including remote viewing, were inconsistent and unreliable for intelligence purposes.

> *“The conditions under which the remote viewing phenomenon is observed in laboratory settings do not apply in intelligence gathering situations. For example, viewers cannot be provided with feedback and targets may not display the characteristics needed to produce hits.*
>
> *The end users indicating that, although some accuracy was observed with regard to broad background characteristics, the remote viewing reports failed to produce the concrete, specific information valued in intelligence gathering.”*

However, they did **not** assert that the methods themselves were ineffective or fake.
