On a cold afternoon steps from the White House, two unarmed National Guardsmen patrolling near the Treasury Building were shot in the head at point-blank range. Their alleged attacker: **Rahmanullah Lakanwal**, a 29-year-old Afghan refugee.

***And quite possible a CIA-trained commando.***

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!pPCs!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fca859e9b-1219-49c0-827c-c0b0d5263a65_518x679.jpeg)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!pPCs!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fca859e9b-1219-49c0-827c-c0b0d5263a65_518x679.jpeg)

The contrast is jarring. A man once trained, armed, and housed inside CIA compounds—paid to hunt Taliban and al-Qaeda leaders in some of the darkest corners of Afghanistan—is now accused of trying to kill U.S. soldiers on Pennsylvania Avenue while reportedly shouting *“Allahu Akbar.”*

## **The Making of a CIA Commando**

From roughly 2010 until the fall of Kabul in 2021, there was an elite class of Afghan partner forces that answered not to the Afghan Ministry of Defense, but directly to the CIA.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!mLz0!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5de82954-d63c-4d65-bbf4-46aef548631d_703x171.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!mLz0!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5de82954-d63c-4d65-bbf4-46aef548631d_703x171.png)

Excerpt from [2019 Afghanistan Analysts Network Article](https://www.afghanistan-analysts.org/en/resources/human-rights/cia-backed-strike-force-units-in-afghanistan/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

If *this* Rahmanullah was the same as [the refugee who lived in Bellingham, WA](https://www.gofundme.com/f/afghan-refugee-family-housing-needs), then the evidence suggests he was almost certainly a member of **[Ktah Khas](https://www.hrw.org/report/2019/10/31/theyve-shot-many/abusive-night-raids-cia-backed-afghan-strike-forces?utm_source=chatgpt.com) (KKA)**—known internally as **[Commando Force 333 or 444](https://afghanwarnews.info/police/CF-333.html)**—or part of parallel **National Directorate of Security (NDS)** [strike teams](https://www.hrw.org/report/2019/10/31/theyve-shot-many/abusive-night-raids-cia-backed-afghan-strike-forces?utm_source=chatgpt.com) such as **Unit 01, Unit 02, or CRU-222**.

These units were, in effect, **[the CIA’s private army](https://www.afghanistan-analysts.org/en/resources/human-rights/cia-backed-strike-force-units-in-afghanistan/?utm_source=chatgpt.com).**

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!IXEA!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F28ee283f-22cf-4951-9f92-ebfa0d4d2a93_1202x800.webp)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!IXEA!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F28ee283f-22cf-4951-9f92-ebfa0d4d2a93_1202x800.webp)

They were hand-picked, paid in cash, and in many cases quartered at CIA forward operating sites like **[Camp Gecko](https://www.foxnews.com/story/u-s-special-forces-using-former-taliban-base)** in Kabul or **[Camp Chapman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Chapman_attack)** in Khost.

**Ktah Khas and NDS special operators** carried out [hundreds of night raids](https://www.hrw.org/report/2019/10/31/theyve-shot-many/abusive-night-raids-cia-backed-afghan-strike-forces), the signature (and most controversial) tactic of America’s covert war in Afghanistan. They kicked in doors, seized suspects, and, according to repeated reports from the United Nations and Amnesty International, sometimes killed civilians by mistake.

And **[sometimes an Army Ranger](https://www.military.com/daily-news/2019/08/27/army-ranger-killed-during-2018-raid-was-accidentally-shot-afghan-commando.html)**. *By mistake.*

## **From Hero’s Welcome to the Pacific Northwest**

Rahmanullah Lakanwal arrived in the United States in September 2021 with his wife, **Khamila**, and their five young sons.

For a brief moment, his story seemed like a rare bright ending in the long, bitter war.

A couple in Washington State (Stan and Val) hosted the family for two months. They launched a fundraiser calling Lakanwal a brave special-forces veteran who had fought “alongside U.S. service members” for more than a decade.

The family later settled in **Bellingham, Washington**, where they lived a quiet life.

Lakanwal’s humanitarian parole expired in 2023; his Temporary Protected Status lapsed earlier this year. By fall 2025, **he was undocumented.**

What happened between 2021 and 2025 remains deeply unclear. He kept no *verified* public online presence. No criminal history emerged.

Whatever grievances, trauma, or ideology may have built inside him did so in silence.

## **The Attack**

On November 26, 2025, Lakanwal allegedly approached three National Guardsmen performing a routine security patrol near the White House perimeter. According to witnesses and law-enforcement sources:

- He produced a handgun without warning.
- He shot two Guardsmen in the head at close range.
- The third soldier (unarmed) tackled him and managed to stab him with a pocket knife, stopping further gunfire and likely preventing mass casualties.

Police officers then opened fire, wounding Lakanwal. He remains hospitalized in stable condition and faces federal attempted-murder charges.

The FBI is treating the case as a targeted act of terrorism. Officials have not disclosed whether Lakanwal left behind a manifesto, recordings, or any communication of motive.

## **A Bitter Reckoning**

Even as details emerge, the Lakanwal case forces a painful conversation about the long tail of America’s war in Afghanistan.

### **1. The CIA built a ferociously effective proxy force with little long-term screening.**

Hundreds of Ktah Khas and NDS operators were brought to the U.S. in 2021 with minimal mental-health evaluation, no deradicalization assessments, and no structured system to monitor former covert fighters adjusting to civilian life.

### **2. Afghanistan’s collapse didn’t end the war.**

The trauma, displacement, and collapsing alliances of August 2021 continue to reverberate. Some of those shockwaves are now appearing, unpredictably, inside the United States.

### **3. The line between ally and threat is thinner than anyone wants to admit.**

The skills that once made Lakanwal valuable—stealth, close-quarters lethality, and an ability to act without hesitation—were allegedly turned against the very country that granted him refuge.

## **Unanswered Questions**

As the two critically wounded Guardsmen fight for their lives, investigators are trying to understand the transformation of a decade-long CIA partner into a suspected domestic attacker.

**Did he feel abandoned after losing legal status?**  
**Was he suffering undiagnosed PTSD from years of night raids?**  
**Did he harbor extremist beliefs that went undetected?**  
**Did something trigger him recently—or had something been brewing for years?**

***Or was it a hit?***

It’s possible that no answer will fully make sense.

## **The War Finds Its Way Home**

The shooting near the White House is more than a criminal investigation. It’s a grim reminder that America’s longest war did not conclude with a withdrawal ceremony in 2021.

It ended with an airlift, an exodus, a collapse, and a long shadow. A shadow that still reaches across oceans and, on some days, onto the streets of Washington, D.C.

As the sun sets tonight over Pennsylvania Avenue, one question lingers: a question that may never have a satisfying answer:

**How does a man** ***allegedly*** **shaped by the CIA’s shadow war become the one who allegedly tries to execute U.S. soldiers a block from the White House?**

The war, it seems, is still coming home.
