# The Moon Landing (Hoax?): 7 Questions That Still Haunt The Apollo Program

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/c530c796-0faa-492f-a9ed-7287cfbb565f_1916x821.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!c-mi!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fc530c796-0faa-492f-a9ed-7287cfbb565f_1916x821.png)

**I grew up believing it all** - it was exciting!

**[John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy)** stood before the country and gave us a line that still makes the hair stand up on the back of your neck: “We choose to go to the Moon…not because it is easy, but because it is hard.”

I mean **WOW!**   
Talk about **motivation to do great things.**

It has the same raw pull as **Rocky dragging himself off the mat**, or an **American sprinter** breaking the tape with the flag on his shoulders, or a **gold-medal hockey team** piling on each other while a whole nation loses its mind. It was more than a space mission.

**[Neil Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Armstrong)** stepping down from the lunar module. The strange, slow movements. The black sky. The bright dust. The words everyone knows by heart: “That’s one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind.”

**My father watched it live.** He told me about that night many times in perfect clarity. Armstrong stepped onto the Moon at 10:56 p.m. Eastern time on July 20, 1969, and for a moment, the whole world seemed to hold its breath.

*How could you not believe it?*

**Then the questions start.**

## SOME THINGS JUST DON’T MAKE SENSE

- *Why did we supposedly master lunar travel in 1969 and then stop?*
- *Why have we not landed humans back on the Moon since 1972?*
- *Why does some of the footage look so strange?*
- *How did astronauts safely pass through the Van Allen radiation belts?*
- *Who filmed the lunar module leaving the Moon?*
- *Why do the astronauts look so uneasy in certain post-mission interviews?*

One question alone may not break the story.

**But questions have a way of stacking.**

And once they stack high enough, you stop seeing Apollo 11 as a neat chapter in a schoolbook. You start seeing it as a conspiracy. A cover-up.

**That is where my own view began to shift.**

Not because I wanted the Moon landing to be fake. I didn’t!

In some ways, I still wish the old story felt as simple as it used to. But the more I looked, the more I felt the shine coming off the myth.

And underneath that shine was a question far more troubling.

*If the moon landing was faked, **why cover it up all these years later?***

## 1. THE TECHNOLOGY

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/fda8666f-7f26-4ba4-9f00-a54999b8703e_2560x1384.jpeg)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!lDXH!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ffda8666f-7f26-4ba4-9f00-a54999b8703e_2560x1384.jpeg)

The official **Apollo 11** story asks us to believe something very strange:

That **NASA** had the working technology to send men to the Moon in **1969**, land them there, let them walk around, film it, launch them back up, dock them with the command module, and bring them safely home…

…and then, somehow, that same technology was lost.

That is not a small problem.

This was not a homemade engine built in a shed, where the plans got tossed out after much frustration. This was the **Apollo Program** — the crown jewel of American engineering, the high altar of the **Space Race**, the single greatest technological achievement in human history (*if the official story is true*).

And yet, when people ask, “Why can’t we just go back?” the answer is often not confidence.

Instead, the answer has often sounded a lot closer to: **we do not have that technology anymore**.

Wait.

*What?*

### **NASA “Lost” the Technology to Go Back?**

One of the strangest comments tied to this subject came from **NASA astronaut Don Pettit**, who said in a widely circulated interview clip that he would go to the Moon “in a nanosecond,” but that “we don’t have the technology to do that anymore.”

He added that we used to have it, but **“destroyed that technology,”** and that rebuilding it would be painful.

Now, NASA defenders usually jump in here and say, “He did not mean the knowledge vanished. He meant the old Apollo hardware, factories, supply chains, tooling, and production systems were gone.”

*OK…*

*But that does not make the answer less strange.*

**It makes it stranger.**

Because now the claim is that **the whole living machine** that made Apollo possible was allowed to die. The **Saturn V** production base. The contractor web. The old launch systems. The men who knew the quirks. The tools. The parts. The test rigs. The institutional know-how.

In other words, the bridge to the Moon was built once, crossed a few times, then taken apart **plank by plank**.

*And we are supposed to shrug that off?*

NASA’s own page on **[The Apollo Program](https://www.nasa.gov/the-apollo-program/)** describes Apollo as a vast system, not a single rocket. It involved the **[Saturn V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturn_V)**, the **[command module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_command_and_service_module)**, the **service module**, and the **[lunar module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_Lunar_Module)**, all working together through **lunar orbit rendezvous**. That actually makes the problem worse, not better.

Because if Apollo was a huge system, then losing the system seems unlikely.

**Or intentional.**

### **The Original Moon Landing Tapes Problem**

“An exhaustive, three-year search for some tapes that contained the original footage of the Apollo 11 moonwalk has concluded that they were **probably destroyed** during a period when NASA was **erasing old magnetic tapes** and **reusing them** to record satellite data.”

— “Houston, We Erased The Apollo 11 Tapes”, [2009 NPR Article](https://www.npr.org/2009/07/16/106637066/houston-we-erased-the-apollo-11-tapes)

[NASA’s explanation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_11_missing_tapes) is that the public broadcast of the **Apollo 11 Moonwalk** still exists. But the cleaner, rawer **slow-scan television tapes** — the original source recordings from the first Moonwalk transmission — were apparently lost, erased, or reused.

Again.

*What??*

We are talking about **mankind’s first alleged steps** on another world.

According to reporting from **[WIRED](https://www.wired.com/2007/01/nasa/)**, the footage seen by the public in 1969 was not the original raw Moon signal. It had to be converted for broadcast television, which degraded the image. The raw **slow-scan footage** was recorded on large magnetic reels and sent to NASA for safekeeping.

Then, somehow, **those tapes went missing.**

**…**

A NASA Technical Reports Server paper on **[Apollo Lunar Surface Experiments Package data recovery](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20190032593/downloads/20190032593.pdf)** says large portions of raw Apollo-era lunar data were not properly archived, thousands of magnetic tapes were scattered, and many were lost over the decades.

“At the conclusion of the ALSEP program in 1977, **thousands of magnetic tapes** containing raw instrument data existed at **various locations**. Since **the decision was made** not to archive the raw data, many of these tapes have been lost in the decades since.

**The people involved** in the ALSEP operation have long been **retired or passed away**, and their knowledge on whereabouts of the archival tapes was not passed on to the current generation.”

— 2019 [Research Paper on Apollo Lunar Surface Experiments Package (ALSEP)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20190032593/downloads/20190032593.pdf)

Let that sit for a second.

The original raw data trail from the most famous achievement in human history was treated with less care than a wedding photographer’s backup drive.

**That should bother people.**

Even if someone believes the Moon landing happened exactly as NASA says, it should still bother them.

Because the official story asks us to accept a strange mix of claims:

- **NASA preserved the legend.**
- **NASA preserved the Moon rocks.**
- **NASA preserved the speeches.**
- **NASA preserved the official story.**
- But somehow, **NASA did not preserve the original raw tapes** the way any sane person would expect.

And it wasn’t a few magnetic tapes either.   
It was **[thousands of photos](https://www.flickr.com/photos/projectapolloarchive/).**

### **Sixty Years Later, and It’s Still Too Hard?**

This is the part that sticks in the throat.

We are nearly **sixty years** past Apollo 11.

**Since then, technology has exploded.** We have smartphones stronger than old room-sized computers. We have reusable rockets. We have private aerospace firms. We have drones, artificial intelligence, GPS, advanced materials, satellite internet, 3D printing, and cameras that can read a license plate from the sky.

But no human being has landed on the Moon since **Apollo 17** in **1972**.

The first airplane barely got off the ground. A few decades later, jets crossed oceans. The first computers filled rooms. Now children carry touchscreens in their pockets. The first cars rattled down dirt roads. Now cars can park themselves.

*But the Moon?*

Somehow, that progress went backward.

Is it really harder to build great things now?

And when it comes to the moon, why should replicating a feat the U.S. accomplished more than half a century ago take so long?

— **[Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/why-is-it-so-much-harder-for-nasa-to-send-people-to-the-moon-now-than-it-was-during-the-apollo-era/)**

**[The official answer is](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/why-is-it-so-much-harder-for-nasa-to-send-people-to-the-moon-now-than-it-was-during-the-apollo-era/)**that budgets changed, public interest faded, and America had already beaten the Soviet Union.

NASA shifted priorities.   
The Space Race was over.

*Does that feel right?*

Does it really feel right that the same country that supposedly mastered lunar landings with **1960s technology** simply walked away from the Moon like it was an old fairground ride?

How did NASA allegedly achieve something in **1969** that modern America still has not repeated?

And if the answer is, “We destroyed the technology,” then the next question should be obvious:

**Why?**

- Why destroy it?
- Why fail to preserve the original tapes?
- Why allow thousands of reels and raw records to disappear?
- Why let the industrial base vanish?

The official story says Apollo was a triumph of planning, science, and national will.

But the aftermath looks less like a triumphal highway and more like a locked room.

## **2. The Appearance of the Astronauts**

One of the strangest parts of the **Apollo 11** story is not technical. It is human.

Before the mission, **Neil Armstrong**, **Buzz Aldrin**, and **Michael Collins** looked exactly how you would expect: confident, proud, and ready. In NASA’s own **[Apollo 11 image gallery](https://www.nasa.gov/gallery/apollo-11/)**, the crew appears composed and positive. They look like men about to do something historic.

Then they came back.

And in the famous **[Apollo 11 post-flight press conference](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20010116507)**, the mood feels completely different.

No visible elation. No glow of triumph. No sense of, “We just walked on the Moon.” Instead, they look blank. Heavy. Almost haunted.

Yes, there are normal explanations. They had just completed a dangerous mission. They had gone through splashdown, medical checks, public pressure, and **[quarantine](https://www.nasa.gov/history/50-years-ago-apollo-11-astronauts-leave-quarantine/)**. Maybe they were exhausted. Maybe they were overwhelmed. Maybe they were simply serious men trained not to show much emotion.

But still.

The contrast is eerie.

Before the mission, they looked **positive, confident, and proud**.

Afterward, they looked **sad, distressed, and uneasy**.

Some skeptics would even say they looked **guilty**.

Of course, a facial expression does not prove a **moon landing hoax**. A tired man can look guilty when he is not. A stressed man can seem blank when he is merely drained.

But the emotional mismatch matters.

This was supposed to be mankind’s greatest achievement. The victory lap of the century. Yet the three men at the center of it looked less like heroes basking in glory and more like men carrying a burden.

And that is why this moment still bothers people.

Sometimes the question is not hidden in missing tapes or rocket diagrams.

Sometimes it is written on a man’s face.

## **3. THE VAN ALLEN RADIATION BELTS**

[![What are the Van Allen radiation belts? - Space Center Houston](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/bc4ab978-6da8-4b13-81c5-24aaf99afd50_985x554.jpeg "What are the Van Allen radiation belts? - Space Center Houston")](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!CRyP!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fbc4ab978-6da8-4b13-81c5-24aaf99afd50_985x554.jpeg)

The **[Van Allen radiation belts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Allen_radiation_belt)** sound like the perfect problem for the **Apollo 11** story.

They are real. They are dangerous. They surround Earth. And every mission to the Moon has to deal with them.

NASA describes the belts as regions of **trapped charged particles**, mainly high-energy protons and electrons, held in place by Earth’s magnetic field. In other words, these are not imaginary “conspiracy objects.” They are a known radiation hazard. You can read NASA’s own explanation here: **[What Are the Van Allen Belts?](https://science.nasa.gov/biological-physical/stories/van-allen-belts/)**

So naturally, the question comes up:

*How did the Apollo astronauts pass through them and survive?*

At first, this seems like one of the strongest **moon landing hoax** arguments. But the more I looked into it, the more I started to think the Van Allen belt claim may be something else.

Not exactly a **red herring**.

More like a **poison-pill argument**.

It is a claim that sounds devastating, spreads easily, and then gets debunked quickly — making the rest of the skeptical case look foolish by association.

### **The 35,000 Degree Claim**

One common version of the argument says the **Van Allen belts** reach temperatures of **3,000°F**, **10,000°F**, or [even](https://www.aircooledheatexchangers.net/if-the-van-allen-radiation-belt-reaches-temperatures-of-35000c-how-could-it-be-harnessed/) **[35,000°F](https://www.aircooledheatexchangers.net/if-the-van-allen-radiation-belt-reaches-temperatures-of-35000c-how-could-it-be-harnessed/)**, so the astronauts should have been cooked alive.

That sounds powerful, but it is also misleading.

In space, **temperature** does not work the way it does inside an oven. The Van Allen belts are made of sparse charged particles in near-vacuum. Those particles can be extremely energetic, but there is not enough matter there to transfer heat to a spacecraft the way hot air, boiling water, or fire would.

So the real question is not:

**“Why didn’t the astronauts burn up?”**

The real question is:

**“How much radiation dose did they receive?”**

That is where the debate gets more serious.

### **The Dose Problem**

NASA’s old Apollo radiation reporting says the Apollo crews received relatively small doses. In the **[Apollo Experience Report: Protection Against Radiation](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19730010172/downloads/19730010172.pdf)**, Apollo 11’s average recorded skin dose is listed as **0.18 rad**, or roughly **1.8 mGy**.

That is not a fatal dose.

It is not even close.

For comparison, [the FDA says](https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/medical-x-ray-imaging/what-are-radiation-risks-ct) typical **CT scans** often fall in the range of **1 to 10 mSv**, depending on the scan type (about the same recorded skin dose the astronauts got).

So if NASA’s Apollo dose numbers are accurate, Apollo 11’s reported radiation exposure was closer to a medical imaging comparison than a **Chernobyl** comparison.

This means the dramatic version of the Van Allen argument — “they would have instantly died” — is probably not the strongest argument against Apollo.

It may actually be one of the weakest.

### **The 2024 Artemis I Comparison**

[![Two Years Ago: Artemis I Liftoff - NASA](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/4874d148-44c3-45e8-8b29-2a71ec1f0ac8_4957x2490.jpeg "Two Years Ago: Artemis I Liftoff - NASA")](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!kpVh!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4874d148-44c3-45e8-8b29-2a71ec1f0ac8_4957x2490.jpeg)

The Van Allen radiation question also got harder for skeptics after **Artemis I**.

[A 2024 paper in](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07927-7) **[Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07927-7)** reported radiation measurements from the uncrewed Orion spacecraft. According to the paper, the total mission dose equivalent was **26.7 to 35.4 mSv**, with only **1.80 to 3.94 mSv** attributed to the Van Allen belt passes.

That’s [less than half](https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/medical-x-ray-imaging/what-are-radiation-risks-ct?utm_source=chatgpt.com) of one full-body CT.

And those measurements support the idea that a spacecraft can pass through the Van Allen belts without the crew receiving a fatal dose, especially if the route, shielding, and timing are planned properly.

So the Van Allen belts are not a magic death wall.

They are a hazard.

There is a difference.

### **A Weak Argument Can Hide Stronger Ones**

This is why the Van Allen belt issue may function as a **poison pill** inside the broader Moon landing debate.

If skeptics repeat the **35,000°F** claim, defenders can quickly debunk it. Then they can turn around and say, “See? All Moon landing questions are this dumb.”

But that does not follow.

A weak argument does not erase stronger questions about **missing tapes**, **lost technology**, **astronaut behavior**, **unusual footage**, or the fact that no human being has landed on the Moon since **1972**.

## **4. THE APOLLO 17 DEPARTURE VIDEO**

The **Apollo 17 departure video** is one of those clips that makes people pause.

The lunar module lifts off from the Moon, and somehow the camera follows it. It does not just sit there frozen. It **tilts upward**, tracks the craft, and frames the shot like it was in a studio.

*Did they leave the cameraman behind??*

*(Heehee).*

NASA says the shot was captured by a **television camera mounted on the lunar rover**, which the astronauts parked about **145 meters east** of the lunar module before leaving. The camera was remotely controlled from Houston by **Edward “Ed” Fendell**, known as “Captain Video.”

But the footage still feels strange.

The timing is almost **too good**. (*Remember, this is 1972*)

The camera moves with the ascent. It pans up as the module rises. And then there is that weird burst at liftoff — what looks like a **confetti explosion** or bits of foil and dust flying in every direction.

It looks odd.

There is no grand rocket flame like people expect. No towering fireball. Just a sudden pop, a scatter of bright material, and the craft rises away while the camera follows it like a well-rehearsed movie shot.

The last human beings to supposedly leave the Moon.  
Rising into the black sky.  
The closing shot of a film.

The **Apollo 17 departure video** is one of those moments where the official story sounds possible (*aka, we planned it this way*), yet the footage feels theatrical. And once you are already asking questions about **missing tapes**, **lost technology**, and the long failure to return, this clip becomes hard to watch without feeling that something is off.

## 5. THE CHALLENGER DEBACLE

[![Chronicle Covers: When the Challenger explosion shook us all](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/27824c08-ab7a-4356-b172-b70258857f7d_1920x1440.jpeg "Chronicle Covers: When the Challenger explosion shook us all")](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!wX90!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F27824c08-ab7a-4356-b172-b70258857f7d_1920x1440.jpeg)

On **January 28, 1986**, the **[Space Shuttle Challenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Challenger_disaster)** broke apart 73 seconds after launch, killing all seven crew members, according to NASA’s official account. NASA’s own **[Rogers Commission Report](https://www.nasa.gov/history/rogersrep/genindex.htm)** later blamed the disaster on the failure of an **O-ring seal** in the right solid rocket booster, made worse by cold weather and flawed launch decision-making.

*That is the official story.*

And even that official story is bad enough.

The Rogers Commission did not simply say, “A part failed.” It found that the **decision to launch was flawed**, that concerns about the O-rings were mishandled, and that contractor warnings were not properly carried through the chain of command. NASA’s own report says the decision-makers were unaware of key written recommendations against launching below certain temperatures.

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/81263c43-57d2-4dba-a95c-1f516869e1b7_940x306.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!d69j!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F81263c43-57d2-4dba-a95c-1f516869e1b7_940x306.png)

[Report of the PRESIDENTIAL COMMISSION on the Space Shuttle Challenger Accident](https://www.nasa.gov/history/rogersrep/v1ch5.htm)

So even without any theory, **Challenger** shows something important:

**NASA can be wrong. NASA can ignore warnings. NASA can protect the mission narrative at all costs.**

Turns out, once an institution loses the benefit of the doubt, people start re-examining its older stories too.

### **The “Astronauts” Are Alive**

This is more than a bit bizarre.

**I can understand doppelgangers.** Sure.

But the uncanniness. Along with **the name similarity**.

*Of ALL of them??*

What are the chances of that…

**My guess is that the official story is not the whole story.**

## **7. THE BIGGEST QUESTION OF THEM ALL**

After all the footage, all the missing tapes, all the odd behavior, all the strange explanations, one question still towers over the rest:

**Why didn’t we go back to the Moon?**

That is the question that will not stay buried.

NASA’s official answer is familiar by now: **budget cuts**, fading public interest, the end of the **Space Race**, shifting priorities, and political exhaustion. [NASA itself says](https://www.nasa.gov/history/50-years-ago-nasa-cancels-apollo-20-mission/) tightening federal budgets led to the cancellation of later Apollo missions, including **Apollo 20**, and that reductions later killed off **Apollo 18 and 19** as well.

Fine.

Budgets matter.

Politics matter.

But come on.

We are talking about the **Moon**.

If America truly reached another world in **1969**, why did the whole thing just fade out after **Apollo 17** in **1972**? Why did the greatest achievement in human history become a dusty photo album? Why did the path to the Moon become ***harder***, not easier?

And no, **“we lost the technology”** is not good enough.

**It does not explain the deeper problem.** Technology is supposed to build on itself. Airplanes did not vanish after Kitty Hawk. Computers did not vanish after ENIAC. Nuclear submarines did not become impossible after the first one worked.

*So why did the Moon become unreachable?*

### **Was Apollo a Psychological Operation?**

Was the Apollo Program a **Cold War psy op**?

*It’s a valid question.*

It is possible that the entire program was a geopolitical weapon — a way to humiliate the Soviet Union, claim technological supremacy, dominate global imagination, and force the enemy into an expensive contest it could not win.

The **Space Race** was never just about rockets. It was about power. Prestige. Fear. Military advantage. Propaganda. The question was not merely, “Who can reach the Moon?”

The real question was:

**Whose system owns the future?**

The United States spent enormous money on Apollo. The Planetary Society notes that [NASA’s budget](https://www.planetary.org/space-policy/nasa-budget) peaked during the Apollo era and then fell sharply after America won the race to the Moon.

That is the part that makes the theory interesting.

If Apollo’s real value was not lunar science, but **global psychological dominance**, then the sudden stop makes more sense. Once the propaganda victory was achieved, the mission had served its purpose.

**The** **optics** were more important than the reality.

### **But If It Was a Psy-Op, Why Hide It Now?**

If Apollo was staged, exaggerated, or partly simulated as a **national security operation**, why keep it hidden today?

The Soviet Union is gone.   
The Cold War is over.   
The men at the top are mostly dead.   
The world has moved on.

*So what are they still protecting?*

Maybe the answer is simple: **the lie is too big to confess**.

If the Moon landing story unraveled, it would not just embarrass NASA. It would shake trust in the entire postwar American myth. It would implicate the White House, NASA leadership, defense contractors, intelligence agencies, media institutions, universities, scientists, and generations of officials who repeated the story as fact.

It would not be one scandal.

It would be a crater.

Because then the question would not be, “Did they fake the Moon landing?”

The question would be:

**What else did they fake?**

**And that may be the real reason the door stays locked.**
