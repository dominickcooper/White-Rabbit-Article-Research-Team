# **GET CAUGHT UP:**

Part 1 had five words in a presidential order that we focused on:

***“except for their covert aspects.”***

That phrase appears in [National Security Action Memorandum 177](https://www.jfklibrary.org/asset-viewer/archives/JFKNSF/338/JFKNSF-338-004), the order that gave the Agency for International Development (AID) operating and funding responsibility for foreign-police programs.

Kennedy drew a boundary. He did not tell us who was responsible for the “covert aspects”.

**So what happened at the handoff?**

What I found was not a theory about agencies vaguely “working together.”

It was a **four-week school** foreign officers did not know belonged to CIA. It was instructors recording which students looked pro-American. It was names and biographies moving from an AID program to CIA components for **“operational use.”**

And it was the **CIA Police Group** saying, [in its own memorandum](https://www.archives.gov/files/research/jfk/releases/2023/docid-32989624.pdf), that liaison with AID’s police program happened **every day**.

## THE PUBLIC SCHOOL

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/31eaadda-31cf-4f06-bdb5-71949a42f903_1508x1043.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Dj7L!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F31eaadda-31cf-4f06-bdb5-71949a42f903_1508x1043.png)

Police Operations Control Center, International Police Academy.

The visible institution was the **International Police Academy**, opened by AID’s Office of Public Safety in Washington in 1963. Foreign officers came to the United States to study management, criminal investigation, records, patrol work and internal security.

And the pluses were great:

- Police could investigate bombings and organized violence.
- A professional civilian force might keep soldiers out of ordinary law enforcement.
- Training allied officers cost less (and attracted less political attention) than deploying American troops.

But the broader strategy was never just about **better case files**.

An [internal 1962 planning draft](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP80B01083A000100120014-7.pdf) argued that America’s role in counterinsurgency should remain **ancillary and inconspicuous**. Washington would advise, assist and train indigenous forces. Local institutions would do the visible work.

*Keeps our hands clean.*

But the draft is much broader than a simple plan for stopping Communist insurgencies. It explicitly considers situations where the United States might remain aloof, **covertly use American resources against an insurgency**, or—if Washington opposed the government—**“covertly assist the insurgents.”** It even lists **“foreign and/or US-supported insurgency in pursuit of US interests”** as a separate category.

[![Highlighted 1962 counterinsurgency draft distinguishing four types of insurgency, including covert U.S. support for insurgents and U.S.-supported insurgency pursued in American interests](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/d0c856af-77c4-4ba0-9eda-0c59141267c1_711x758.png "Highlighted 1962 counterinsurgency draft distinguishing four types of insurgency, including covert U.S. support for insurgents and U.S.-supported insurgency pursued in American interests")](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!5YW8!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fd0c856af-77c4-4ba0-9eda-0c59141267c1_711x758.png)

Excerpt from [internal 1962 planning draft](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP80B01083A000100120014-7.pdf)

That changes the meaning of “counterinsurgency” a little.

**Insurgency itself was not automatically the enemy.** Depending on who was fighting whom (and whether the outcome served U.S. interests) Washington might suppress it, stay out of it, or **quietly help it.**

Then there is this phrase:

“In internal war it is always better for one national to kill another than for a foreigner — especially one with a different skin coloration — to do so.”

*Yikes. **How many times have we seen that play out?***

## THE SCHOOL BEHIND THE SCHOOL

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/263f8942-253b-4d23-a6f8-c6a5f7ca1ac1_1448x1086.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!tIt9!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F263f8942-253b-4d23-a6f8-c6a5f7ca1ac1_1448x1086.png)

The second institution was called **International Police Services**, or **IPS**.

The clearest account comes from the suppressed Pike Committee investigation, preserved in a [CIA-held Pike report reproduction](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP03-01541R000200420004-8.pdf).

Here is what it says:

- **CIA operated IPS as a proprietary** in the Washington area from the early 1950s until late 1973.
- An officer could finish a **14-week OPS course**, then receive **four additional weeks at IPS** under a contract with AID.
- Students were not told they had entered a CIA facility.
- Instructors recorded officers who showed a pro-American attitude.
- OPS and IPS student lists and biographies went to CIA components for **operational use**.

*In layman’s terms, **we** **call that a pipeline.***

The [Rockefeller Commission’s 1975 report](https://www.fordlibrarymuseum.gov/LIBRARY/document/0005/1561495.pdf) independently confirms the basic architecture without naming IPS in the relevant passage.

It traces the CIA school to **[a Virginia farmhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Peary)** in 1952 (*where it got the name ‘The Farm’*), then to [a joint school](https://digitalcommons.chapman.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1043&context=war_and_society_theses) in the **Panama Canal Zone**, where CIA supplied faculty and State and OPS shared costs. When the Panama school closed in 1963, the program moved to the United States under a commercial contract with a domestic corporation that was a CIA front (INPOLSE).

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/a51d5a14-385a-4851-b387-09ec4d8d425f_466x373.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!lBL2!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa51d5a14-385a-4851-b387-09ec4d8d425f_466x373.png)

Excerpt from Intelligence - [Rockefeller Commission Report - Final (4)](https://www.fordlibrarymuseum.gov/sites/default/files/pdf_documents/library/document/0005/7288238.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The Pike and Rockefeller investigations tell the same story:

**IPS was a CIA-controlled company training foreign police under an AID contract.**

## THE STUDENTS DID NOT KNOW

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/c7b4e2ef-bf01-4675-a8f1-782bb7efec5e_1463x1075.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!2Pds!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fc7b4e2ef-bf01-4675-a8f1-782bb7efec5e_1463x1075.png)

Marksmanship training at the International Police Academy

Pike investigators estimated that as many as **5,000 foreign police officers from more than 100 countries** received CIA training without knowing it.

The 5,000 number, by the way, does **not** include everyone OPS trained worldwide.

Thousands of officers entered what looked like an American police-assistance program. Some moved into training **controlled by CIA**. Many later became **senior officials** in their own countries.

The same report contains another strange detail: the proprietary held a federal firearms-dealer license and sold weapons and police equipment, mostly to students after their courses. Annual gross sales ranged from roughly **$6,000 to $48,000**.

That would be equivalent in purchasing power to about **$68,000 - $540,000** in 2026.

The commission considered the education itself within CIA’s statutory authority. It considered the gun business questionable and recommended that it not be repeated.

***Weapons trafficking**, anyone?*

## THE OFFICE THAT WATCHED THE PIPELINE

IPS was one channel. The **CIA Police Group** was another.

Its own secret memorandum defines the group as the operations directorate’s coordinator for assistance to foreign police and security forces. The stated purpose was to exploit those programs **for intelligence purposes**.

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/8ef08742-13b6-427d-b4e7-45e8687d1c77_630x319.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!L2ph!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F8ef08742-13b6-427d-b4e7-45e8687d1c77_630x319.png)

[Excerpt from CIA Memo](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/DOC_0001451843.pdf)

**Like we stated before**, the group maintained a daily liaison with OPS and the International Police Academy.

The memorandum describes what that meant:

- **exchanging information** on academy participants;
- placing **CIA-sponsored officers** **inside** IPA and OPS training programs;
- arranging briefings and tours for foreign police and security officials **sponsored by CIA area divisions**;
- **connecting selected participants** to further courses whose details remain redacted.

*So let’s look at this pipeline again:*

- **OPS and IPA** provided **the overt course and access** to foreign officers.
- **IPS** provided undisclosed **follow-on training and assessment**.
- **The Police Group** **moved participant information**, inserted Agency-sponsored officers and connected selected visitors to additional activity.
- **CIA components** received **names** and **biographies** for possible **operational use**.

The records stop before recruitment.

## DID THEY RECRUIT THEM?

The files show **spotting and assessment**. IPS instructors noted students who appeared pro-American, and OPS and IPS sent their names and biographies to CIA components.

They were looking for officers who **might be recruitable later.**

According to the Pike account, CIA apparently did not recruit students *while they were in the United States*.

What happened ***after*** they returned home is still unclear.  
**But you don’t pour resources into something like this and not get a return on your investment.**

And especially not **the CIA.**

### ONE OFFICER THROUGH THE PIPELINE

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/2ed7c581-15e2-465f-b77f-076897b9d1f2_1154x1363.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!3n6N!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F2ed7c581-15e2-465f-b77f-076897b9d1f2_1154x1363.png)

Philip Agee

There is **at** **least one account** that puts a name inside this system.

Former CIA officer **[Philip Agee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Agee)** [wrote](https://archive.org/download/pdfy-DAzR701tP2dL_DNu/inside-the-company-cia-diary-philip-agee.pdf) that in 1965 he arranged through the Public Safety mission for **[Alejandro Otero](https://www.montevideo.com.uy/Noticias/Murio-el-ex-comisario-Alejandro-Otero-uc209573)**, head of police intelligence in Montevideo, to receive training in Washington.

Agee originally expected Otero to attend the **International Police Academy** and then receive **CIA instruction** in **surveillance**, **targeting**, **spotting**, **recruitment** and **agent handling**.

*Headquarters changed the plan.*

Otero was sent instead to the **International Police Services School**.

Agee described IPS as **a CIA headquarters training facility** operating under commercial cover. Then he added a detail that fits almost perfectly with everything we’ve uncovered so far:

> **“AID cover for the training, however, is retained.”**

By March 1966, Agee wrote that **Otero had finished the IPS course** and was receiving additional intelligence training **from CIA instructors**.

Agee was on **the inside.** And the route he describes looks an awful lot like the pipeline in the official records.

### THE PIPELINE DIDN’T END AT THE SCHOOL

This is where Agee’s account gets even more interesting.

> The **Public Safety Missions** are valuable to the CIA because **they provide cover for CIA officers** who are sent to work full time with the intelligence services of the police and other civilian services.
>
> Station officers under other cover may work with military intelligence and, at times, officers undercover as businessmen, tourists or retired people **may be assigned to work with local services**.
>
> CIA assistance to local services **through Public Safety Missions** or **other forms of cover** are not only designed to help improve the professional capability of the local service.
>
> **Operational targeting of the local service is guided by CIA liaison officers** so that the local service performs tasks that are lacking in the overall station operational programme.
>
> **In other words local services are to be used for the benefit of the CIA, and this includes keeping the local service away from station unilateral operations.**
>
> — *[Inside the Company: CIA Diary](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0553202561?lv=shuf&channelId=500&plpRedirect=mhFallback)*

So according to Agee, those relationships had **another purpose.**

CIA liaison officers were expected to **spot and assess officers inside the local services for possible recruitment as penetration agents**.

Training, equipment and money helped build the relationship. CIA gained access to local intelligence, surveillance capabilities and security institutions. Selected officers could then become much more useful to the Agency.

Agee described the goal bluntly:

Using the local service **“for the benefit of the CIA.”**

And he gives us the reverse version too.

In Mexico, where an overt AID Public Safety mission was politically sensitive, Agee wrote that CIA officers quietly performed the same functions themselves. He called them **“the equivalent of an AID Public Safety mission.”**

Think about that for a second.

Where AID could operate openly, its **Public Safety program gave CIA access.**

Where AID couldn’t operate openly, Agee says CIA **recreated the function covertly.**

**Front door. Back door.**Either way, they were getting in **if they wanted to get in.**

That makes Pike’s language about **assessing police students** for future recruitability much harder to dismiss as “bureaucratic curiosity.”

*“Oh, this kid could be useful.”*

No. The school could identify the prospect.

**The field could do the rest.**

## FAQ

### Was International Police Services a CIA proprietary?

Yes. The CIA-held Pike report identifies IPS by name as a CIA proprietary operating in the Washington area until late 1973. The Rockefeller Commission independently describes the contracted private corporation as a CIA front.

### Did every International Police Academy student receive CIA training?

No. The investigations describe an OPS course augmented by IPS training and estimate about 5,000 CIA-trained officers over more than 20 years. No complete roster survives.

### Were foreign police trainees recruited by CIA at IPS?

They were assessed for pro-American orientation and their information was sent to CIA components. According to Pike investigators, recruitment apparently did not occur while students were in the United States; later recruitment outcomes remain unresolved.

### Did AID officials know IPS belonged to CIA?

Knowledge was compartmented. The OPS director and a small number of AID officials reportedly knew, while the AID administrator reportedly did not.

### Did OPS routinely teach foreign officers to build bombs?

No. A 1973 joint specialist course for 26 participants included hands-on explosives, incendiaries and booby-trap work, framed as technical investigation and counterterrorism training.
