## What is the Deep State?

The deep state refers to a clandestine network of influential members within government agencies, financial institutions, and other powerful organizations that work behind the scenes to control or manipulate political decisions. Often, this network operates independently of elected officials and aims to protect its interests over public interest. This concept is closely associated with conspiracy theories but has been rooted in historical events where secretive actions by governments or entities are evident.

The term “deep state” is believed to have originated in Turkey (**derin devlet** in Turkish), where it was used to describe military factions operating in the shadows, controlling policies without the knowledge or consent of elected officials.

*cough cough…**[Operation Gladio](https://thewhiterabbitreport.substack.com/s/operation-gladio)*****.**

The concept of a deep state arose in the 20th century, particularly in the aftermath of political instability in Turkey, including military coups (e.g., in 1960, 1971, 1980, and 1997). The military and intelligence agencies played a significant role in maintaining secularism and stability, often acting independently of elected governments.

Events like **the [Susurluk scandal](https://www.newturkey.today/Gladio-B/Susurluk-scandal/index.html)**in 1996 exposed connections between the Turkish government, military, police, and organized crime, bringing the concept of a deep state to public attention.

While the idea of the deep state is often controversial, it is undeniable that power structures within governments don’t always work transparently. Think about intelligence agencies, lobbyists, or defense contractors – these groups wield enormous influence but are rarely held accountable by voters. Their agendas can steer political decisions without public oversight, reinforcing the notion of a shadow government.

So why know these words?

**Because words have power.** By being able to *name* something, you are able to identify something. Knowing these terms will help you not only understand what other Deep State investigators are talking about, but also open your eyes to the events happening around.

## **Common Terms Related to the Deep State**

### False Flag

A **false flag** is a covert operation designed to deceive. The action appears to be carried out by one group, but it is actually orchestrated by another party. These operations are used to create a pretext for war, suppress opposition, or implement policies that might not be accepted otherwise.

**Example**: The **[Reichstag fire](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/true-story-reichstag-fire-and-nazis-rise-power-180962240/)** in 1933 was blamed on a Dutch communist, but investigations and research has shown that it was a false flag operation by the Nazis to justify cracking down on political opposition and increasing state power. It was this **false flag** event that induced **President Paul von Hindenburg** to issue the Reichstag Fire Decree suspending civil liberties, and pursue a "ruthless confrontation" with the Communists.[

### Psy Ops (Psychological Operations)

**Psy Ops** refers to psychological operations aimed at **influencing** the emotions, motives, and objective reasoning of individuals or groups, typically during conflicts. These operations often use **propaganda**, **disinformation**, or **emotional manipulation** to shape public opinion and behavior.

**Example**: During the **Gulf War**, leaflets were dropped by U.S. forces over Iraqi troops, warning them of **imminent** **attacks** and encouraging them to surrender, which significantly impacted Iraqi morale.

### Controlled Opposition

**Controlled opposition** refers to political figures or movements that *appear* to oppose the established power but are **secretly working for it**. These groups absorb dissent, keeping it under control while maintaining the illusion of opposition.

**Example**: The **FBI** used controlled opposition with the **Black Panther Party** of the 1960s. The FBI [infiltrated the Black Panthers](https://onlineexhibits.library.yale.edu/s/black-panther-may-day/page/the-black-panthers-and-the-fb) with informants and agents who posed as radical activists. These informants would **encourage extreme and violent actions**, which **discredited the Panthers** and made them **easier to target** by law enforcement.

### Black Swan Event

A **black swan event** is an unpredictable and rare event with extreme consequences. The term is often used to describe major financial or geopolitical shocks that seem obvious in inappropriately rationalized in hindsight but were unforeseeable beforehand.

**Example**: The **2008 financial crisis** is considered a black swan event because the collapse of global markets was largely unexpected, with widespread consequences for the world economy. After the fact, there was a [bit of a](https://www.nytimes.com/2011/01/26/business/economy/26inquiry.html) *[mea culpa](https://www.nytimes.com/2011/01/26/business/economy/26inquiry.html)*from the financial firms saying they could have predicted this.

### Disinformation

**Disinformation** is deliberately false or misleading information spread with the intent to deceive. It is often used by governments or organizations to manipulate public opinion or obscure the truth.

**Example**: Before the **Gulf War**, [a story](https://www.theguardian.com/education/2001/oct/04/socialsciences.highereducation) claimed that **Iraqi soldiers** had **removed babies from incubators** in Kuwaiti hospitals. This information was presented by a witness (later revealed to be the **daughter of the Kuwaiti ambassador**) and was used to galvanize support for **U.S. intervention**.

### Misinformation

**Misinformation** refers to false information that is shared without malicious intent. Unlike disinformation, the spreaders of misinformation usually believe it to be true and are not aware of its inaccuracy.

**Example**: In April 2020, an **[in vitro study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0166354220302011)** published in *Antiviral Research* showed that ivermectin could inhibit the replication of SARS-CoV-2 in cell cultures, reducing viral RNA by 99.8% within 48 hours. However, Ivermectin was not approved by the NIH or FDA for the treatment of SARS-CoV-2 (Covid-19). In response, [many doctors were punished](https://www.cnn.com/2024/03/27/health/fda-ivermectin-lawsuit/index.html) for prescribing it to their patients.

Since that time, [105 controlled studies](https://c19ivm.org/) from 1,206 scientists with 220,423 patients in 30 countries have shown dramatic improvements when treated with ivermectin.

### Government Overreach

**Government overreach** occurs when the government extends its power beyond its legal or constitutional limits, often infringing on citizens' rights or liberties.

**Example**: The use of **[Patriot Act](https://www.justice.gov/archive/ll/what_is_the_patriot_act.pdf)** provisions after 9/11 allowed for increased surveillance and detention powers that constituted government overreach in the name of national security.

- **Warrantless Wiretapping (2001-2007)**: The NSA conducted [warrantless wiretaps](https://www.nyclu.org/report/nsa-warrantless-wiretapping-timeline) on American citizens' phone and internet communications, bypassing the courts. This was later [ruled illegal](https://web.archive.org/web/20130112211357/http://fl1.findlaw.com/news.findlaw.com/nytimes/docs/nsa/aclunsa81706opn.pdf) by a federal court, but the NSA had been doing this [since 1952](https://www.nytimes.com/2005/12/16/politics/a-halfcentury-of-surveillance.html)…Judicial Watch, [through their investigations](https://web.archive.org/web/20080327032011/http://www.judicialwatch.org/printer_5862.shtml), found interesting evidence that suggests the suit may have been a co-op (you’ll learn what that means later).
- **Section 215 (Metadata Collection)**: The government collected bulk phone metadata on millions of Americans without individualized suspicion, which was [ruled illegal](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca2/14-42/14-42-2015-05-07.html) by the Second Circuit Court of Appeals in 2015.

*In both these cases though, I’m sure the errant parties destroyed all previous data collections…*

### Surveillance State

A **surveillance state** refers to a government that monitors and tracks its citizens through widespread surveillance programs, often using technology to collect data on individuals without their consent.

**Example**: **[China's social credit system](https://www.technologyreview.com/2022/11/22/1063605/china-announced-a-new-social-credit-law-what-does-it-mean/)** is a prime example of a surveillance state, where citizens' behaviors are constantly monitored, and rewards or punishments are meted out based on their actions.
