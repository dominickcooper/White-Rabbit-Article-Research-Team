## **How Big Pharma Hijacked Nitric Oxide**

### **What Is Nitric Oxide (NO) and Why Does It Matter?**

Nitric oxide (**NO**) is a **critical molecule** in your body. It regulates **blood flow, oxygen delivery, and vascular function**. Without it, you’d be a mess—your **brain wouldn’t function properly**, your **heart would struggle**, and yes, your **manhood wouldn’t work** either. NO is literally what makes **blood vessels relax and expand**, allowing for healthy circulation and strong cardiovascular health.

## **How to Increase Nitric Oxide Naturally (No Pills Required!)**

Big Pharma doesn’t want you to know that you can **boost NO levels naturally**, without ever touching their **overpriced** **supplements**. Here’s how:

### **1. Eat Nitrate-Rich Foods**

- **Beets**: A 2012 study published in the British Journal of Clinical Pharmacology found that consuming 500 mL of beetroot juice (containing approximately 6.2 mmol of nitrates) increased plasma nitrite levels by 2.5-fold and reduced blood pressure by 5.4 mmHg in healthy adults ([Beetroot juice and blood pressure: a randomized controlled trial](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3575935/)). This effect is attributed to NO’s vasodilatory properties.
- Spinach: According to a 2016 study in Food Chemistry, spinach contains about 250-300 mg of nitrates per 100 g, making it a potent NO booster ([Nitrate content in leafy vegetables](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0308814615013552)). Regular consumption can enhance NO levels and improve endothelial function.
- Arugula: Arugula tops the list with around 480 mg of nitrates per 100 g, as noted in [10 Healthy Foods to Boost Nitric Oxide - WebMD](https://www.webmd.com/diet/healthy-foods-nitric-oxide). A 2020 study in Nutrients showed that arugula consumption increased NO bioavailability, improving vascular health in older adults ([Dietary nitrates and vascular function](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/12/8/2334)).
- Celery: Celery provides about 110 mg of nitrates per 100 g, per the same WebMD source. A 2019 study in Frontiers in Nutrition found that celery juice consumption improved blood flow markers in hypertensive patients, likely due to NO production ([Celery juice and cardiovascular health](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnut.2019.00068/full)).

These foods **naturally increase NO** without any synthetic additives.

### **2. Exercise (Especially HIIT & Strength Training)**

Physical activity triggers **natural NO production** by improving endothelial function and circulation.

HIIT: A 2017 study in The Journal of Physiology found that 4 weeks of HIIT (3 sessions/week, 20 minutes each) increased eNOS activity by 30% and improved flow-mediated dilation (a marker of NO production) by 9% in sedentary men ([HIIT and endothelial function](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5539205/)). This suggests HIIT directly boosts NO levels through shear stress on blood vessels.

Strength Training: A 2015 study in Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise showed that 8 weeks of resistance training (3 sets of 10 reps at 70% 1RM, 3 times/week) increased NO production by 25% in healthy adults, as measured by plasma nitrite levels ([Resistance training and NO production](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25700044/)). The mechanical stress from lifting weights activates eNOS, enhancing NO synthesis.

### **3. Get Sun Exposure**

- A 2014 study in The Journal of Investigative Dermatology found that 20 minutes of UVA exposure (at 20 J/cm²) increased skin NO levels by 40%, leading to a 5 mmHg drop in blood pressure in healthy volunteers ([UVA exposure and NO release](https://www.jidonline.org/article/S0022-202X(15)36709-8/fulltext)). This effect is due to the photolysis of nitrite in the skin, releasing NO into the bloodstream.
- Another 2018 study in Hypertension confirmed that regular sun exposure (30 minutes, 3 times/week) over 12 weeks improved NO-dependent vasodilation by 15% in pre-hypertensive adults, reducing cardiovascular risk ([Sun exposure and cardiovascular health](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.118.11263)).

### **4. Avoid Fluoride & Processed Foods**

- Diets high in processed foods, often loaded with saturated fats and sugar, impair NO production. A 2010 study in The American Journal of Clinical Nutrition showed that a diet high in saturated fats (40% of calories) reduced NO bioavailability by 20% compared to a diet rich in unsaturated fats, due to decreased eNOS activity ([Saturated fats and endothelial function](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2943064/)). Processed foods also lack nitrates, further limiting NO synthesis, as noted in [Regulation of NO Production in Health and Disease - PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2953417/)).

### **5. Use Natural NO Precursors**

- **L-arginine & L-citrulline** (found in watermelon, nuts, and fish)
- **Antioxidants** (like Vitamin C & polyphenols) protect NO from breaking down.

Big Pharma may **not want you to know** about these natural methods—eating nitrate-rich foods like beets and spinach, exercising with HIIT or strength training, getting sun exposure, avoiding fluoride and processed foods, and using natural precursors like L-arginine and L-citrulline—**because they’re effective and cheap**.
