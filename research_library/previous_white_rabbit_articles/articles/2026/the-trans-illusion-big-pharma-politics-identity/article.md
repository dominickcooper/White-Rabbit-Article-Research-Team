## WHAT IS TRANSGENDERISM?

**Transgenderism** refers to identifying with a gender different from your biological sex.

*I think everyone’s on board with that.*

It's often tied to **gender dysphoria**, that intense distress when your body doesn't match your mind. But is this a natural variation or a sign of deeper psychological turmoil?

**Many experts say the latter**, and we'll explore why.

### The Official Narrative vs. The Reality

The mainstream tells us **transgenderism** isn't a **mental disorder**—it's just **diversity**. But dig a little, and you find cracks. Organizations like **the APA** and **WHO** have shifted their stances, but is that based on science or **pressure**?

## HISTORICAL CLASSIFICATION

History doesn't lie, folks.

The transition from **Gender Identity Disorder (GID)** in DSM-IV to **Gender Dysphoria (GD)** in DSM-5 wasn’t just a name change—it’s a shift in how we view transgender experiences. Picture it like swapping a heavy label that screams “you’re broken” for one that says, “your distress matters, let’s talk.”

*But is that all that’s happening?*

Let's rewind the clock on how **transgender psychology** has been viewed.

### DSM-III: The Birth of Transsexualism

#### Defining Transsexualism in 1980

Back in 1980, [DSM-III](https://archive.org/details/diagnosticstatis00amer_1) introduced **Transsexualism** for folks showing at least two years of persistent desire to change their bodies and social roles to align with their gender identity. It was specific: This applied to post-pubertal individuals with a deep need to shed their biological sex characteristics. The manual noted “moderate to severe coexisting personality disturbance,” often tied to anxiety and depression blamed on the inability to live as their desired gender.

Excerpt from the [DSM-III about Transsexualism](https://archive.org/details/diagnosticstatis00amer_1/page/74/mode/2up)

#### Early Observations and Predispositions

DSM-III got candid about predisposing factors. Extensive “childhood femininity in a boy or masculinity in a girl” upped the odds of **Transsexualism**. It often stemmed from strained relationships with parents, painting a picture of psychological roots. This framing set the stage for later debates: Is this a disorder or a response to environment?

### DSM-IV: Gender Identity Disorder Takes Center Stage

#### Labeling Identity as a Disorder

By 1994, [DSM-IV](https://archive.org/details/diagnosticstati00amer) rebranded it **Gender Identity Disorder (GID)**, cementing it as a **mental disorder**. The criteria? Persistent cross-gender identification plus significant distress or impairment, affecting roughly 1 in 30,000 adult males and 1 in 100,000 females seeking sex-reassignment surgery, based on European data. It was a stark label, implying the identity itself was the issue, not just the distress.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!5hEm!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4929f0f2-0f06-4a0b-bdf1-14ad8345b06a_572x118.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!5hEm!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4929f0f2-0f06-4a0b-bdf1-14ad8345b06a_572x118.png)

[Excerpt from DSM-IV](https://archive.org/details/diagnosticstati00amer/page/534/mode/2up) regarding the prevalance of transgenderism

#### Children and GID: A Nuanced Picture

For kids, DSM-IV noted **GID** symptoms typically surfaced between ages 2 and 4, like preferring cross-gender activities or roles.

But here’s the kicker:

Only a “very small number” of these kids   
carried **GID** into adolescence or adulthood.

About 75% of boys with childhood **GID** later identified as homosexual or bisexual without **GID**, while most others became heterosexual, also without **GID**. In adults, **GID** was often chronic, though spontaneous remission happened.

[Excerpt from DSM-IV](https://archive.org/details/diagnosticstati00amer/page/536/mode/2up) in regards to transgenderism in children

#### Clarifying Misconceptions

DSM-IV was careful: Insisting you’re the other gender wasn’t a delusion—folks meant they *felt* like the other gender, not that they *were* it biologically.

If someone truly believed they were the opposite sex, DSM-IV suggested schizophrenia alongside severe **GID**. It also clarified that “tomboyishness” or “sissyish” behavior alone didn’t warrant a diagnosis unless the full syndrome was present.

#### Associated Struggles

**GID** came with heavy baggage: Suicide attempts and substance-related disorders were common. This painted a grim reality—distress wasn’t just about identity but tangled with broader mental health challenges.

### **DSM-5: Gender Dysphoria Redefines the Narrative**

By 2013, [DSM-5](https://dn721204.ca.archive.org/0/items/dsm-5-tr_202409/DSM%205%20TR.pdf) retired *Gender Identity Disorder* and replaced it with *Gender Dysphoria (GD)*. The focus shifted from labeling identity as pathological to highlighting the distress that comes from gender incongruence. The American Psychiatric Association explained the change as an effort to reduce stigma: The problem wasn’t “who you are,” but the suffering tied to that mismatch. On paper, it sounded like progress—but it also marked a philosophical pivot in how psychiatry approached sex and gender.

#### **Expanding Definitions of Sex and Gender**

DSM-5 introduced new language, drawing sharper lines between *sex* (biological markers), *gender* (social roles), and *gender identity*. Alongside this came a wave of fresh terminology: “natal sex,” “birth-assigned gender,” “gender diverse,” and more. Supporters saw this as nuance; critics saw it as deliberate blurring of biological categories. What was once a straightforward concept rooted in physiology became a fluid spectrum, raising questions about whether psychiatry was reflecting science or ideology.

#### **Childhood Indicators and Developmental Patterns**

Much like earlier editions, DSM-5 described **childhood signs of GD** in familiar ways—preferences for cross-gender roles, behaviors, or clothing. For children assigned female at birth, this could mean rejecting dresses, gravitating toward “boyish” play, or insisting they were boys.

For those assigned male, it often showed as adopting female roles in games, favoring dolls, or resisting stereotypically masculine toys.

Puberty frequently intensified distress, as the body’s changes—menstruation, breast development, or facial hair—clashed with the individual’s internal sense of self.

#### **Prevalence and Outcomes**

**DSM-5** updated the numbers, estimating that **0.5–0.6%** of people identified as transgender, **0.6–1.1%** experienced gender incongruence, **2.1–2.6%** reported feeling like another sex, and **0.2–0.6%** sought medical intervention.

**Remarkably**, it claimed that gender-nonconforming behavior could typically be observed **as early as ages 2 to 4**—an age when most children are **barely toilet-trained** and still believe in **Santa Claus**.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!QH-j!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F3ab4f166-4bf6-4c66-93bc-ba656baca18d_642x61.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!QH-j!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F3ab4f166-4bf6-4c66-93bc-ba656baca18d_642x61.png)

Persistence into later life, the manual admitted, was **anything but consistent**, ranging from 2–39% for males and 12–50% for females.

**These shaky numbers raise a larger question**: if psychiatry is willing to root identity in toddler playtime, what does that mean for the mental health landscape surrounding gender distress?

### Mental Health and Broader Connections

#### Comorbidities and Suicidality

**Gender Dysphoria rarely stands alone**. [Research shows](https://williamsinstitute.law.ucla.edu/press/transpop-suicide-press-release/) transgender individuals carry an outsized burden of mental health struggles, with suicidality rates reported anywhere from 30–80%. Trans youth referred to gender clinics face even higher risks, often flagged as vulnerable populations.

#### Disorders of Sex Development (DSD)

The DSM also acknowledges individuals with *Disorders of Sex Development* (DSD), whose gender **distress** often emerges **after invasive medical decisions** made in childhood—procedures like **gonadectomies** or early “**gender assignment**” surgeries. Infertility, either from the condition itself or from interventions, is common.

These cases raise thorny ethical questions:

To what extent are we *affirming* an identity versus *creating* one through irreversible medical choices made before the patient could consent?

## WHY THE SHIFT?

### **Evolution of the DSM: From DSM-III to DSM-V**

- **DSM-III (1980)** introduced *Transsexualism*, narrowly defining it as a persistent desire to change one’s body and social role. It assumed a more medical/psychiatric framing with overt pathologizing language.
- **DSM-IV (1994)** replaced that with *Gender Identity Disorder (GID)*, codifying identity itself as disordered when suffering or impairment coexisted.
- **DSM-5 (2013)** swapped GID for *Gender Dysphoria (GD)*—a semantic shift, but with important structural consequences. The new focus was distress (“dysphoria”) rather than identity, and cross-gender identification alone was no longer enough.

  - [The APA explicitly](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26054486/) acknowledged that the shift aimed, in part, to reduce stigma and to frame the condition in ways **more acceptable to clinicians and insurance systems**.
  - [Some internal discussions](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26054486/) acknowledged that retaining a diagnosable category was necessary so insurance companies would continue covering “transition-related treatments.”

This progression looks less like purely scientific refinement than like a negotiated path—balancing pressures from **clinical, political, and social constituencies**.

---

### **Institutional Pressures: Insurance, Reimbursement, Advocacy**

One of the engines behind classifying transgenderism as a diagnosable “disorder/dysphoria” is the infrastructure of modern medicine and insurance.

- Without a recognized diagnosis, many medical treatments (hormones, surgeries) could be classified as “**cosmetic**” and hence **excluded from coverage**. [Advocates worried](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233106777_Opinions_About_the_DSM_Gender_Identity_Disorder_Diagnosis_Results_from_an_International_Survey_Administered_to_Organizations_Concerned_with_the_Welfare_of_Transgender_People?utm_source=chatgpt.com) that eliminating GID entirely from the DSM would remove the footing for reimbursement.
- [A 2010 survey of 201 organizations](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233106777_Opinions_About_the_DSM_Gender_Identity_Disorder_Diagnosis_Results_from_an_International_Survey_Administered_to_Organizations_Concerned_with_the_Welfare_of_Transgender_People) concerned with transgender welfare found that **55.8%** believed GID should be excluded, but many simultaneously conceded that keeping it in was vital **for securing access** to care.
- The APA’s work group reportedly [faced pressure from both advocacy groups and insurance interests](https://journalofethics.ama-assn.org/article/proposed-dsm-5-revisions-sexual-and-gender-identity-disorder-criteria/2010-08) to preserve a diagnosis that legitimized transition-related care under health plans.

Thus, **the DSM’s evolution** cannot be disentangled from **the economics of medicine**: who pays, under what codes, and who stands to gain from broadening the patient base.

---

### **Lobbying, Identity Politics, and Narrative Control**

Classifying transgenderism (or gender incongruence) as a medical condition creates a **powerful narrative tool**: it frames trans people as a distinct *group* with clinical needs, rather than a diffuse social variation. That grouping serves multiple strategic purposes.

1. **Us vs. Them Framing**  
   When transgender people are positioned as a diagnosable minority, it enables a binary: **“us” vs. “them** (opponents, skeptics, legislators).” **That binary is emotionally potent**, simplifying complex nuance into moral categories (“health care rights,” “civil rights”) that are **easier to mobilize** politically.
2. **Legislative & Policy Leverage**  
   Once transgender identity is medicalized, it becomes more amenable to laws mandating coverage, anti-discrimination protections, or restrictions on “conversion” therapies. The language of health care rights becomes a powerful lever for asserting policy demands.

   - Advocate pressure on the APA to change DSM terms was active. For example, [transgender advocacy groups lobbied the APA](https://www.medicaldaily.com/transgender-advocates-lobby-apa-change-or-remove-classification-mental-illness-241504) to remove or rewrite pathologizing language around GID.
   - Some jurisdictions now anchor **legal protections or mandates** around “gender dysphoria treatment” in statutes that presume legitimacy of such care.
3. **Pharmaceutical & Clinical Pathways**  
   The classification opens the door to pharmaceutical and procedural interventions (hormones, pubertal blockers, surgeries) as **normative responses** to a “condition.” It converts identity into a patient journey, which in turn drives demand for **medical products**, **consultations**, and **procedural infrastructure**.

   - Opponents argue this structure aligns with what is sometimes called the “gender affirmation industry”—where **financial and institutional incentives** align around **expanding treatment**, **clinics**, **guidelines**, and **research funding**.
   - **[“Big Pharma” also benefits](https://nypost.com/2021/08/20/gender-ideology-a-boon-to-big-pharma-and-threat-to-parental-rights/)**from the growth of **hormone prescriptions** and **surgeries** tied to transgender care. While evidence is less direct than in, say, chronic disease drug markets, the incentive logic is suggestive.

4. **Group Think, Academic Capture, & Narrative Gatekeeping**  
   Once a medical consensus is established and institutionalized in manuals like DSM, **dissent becomes harder**. Critics may be marginalized as **outside the medical mainstream** or accused of denying care. That consolidates narrative control: whoever **writes the textbooks**, **funds the studies**, **chairs the committees**, **sets the standards** becomes the **arbiter of legitimacy**.

   - The 2015 article *[The DSM-5 and the Politics of Diagnosing Transpeople](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26054486/)* argues that the semantic shift is ideological as much as scientific—that the inversion (pathologizing dysphoria rather than identity) **still** casts nonconformity as deviant.
   - The architecture of diagnosis, peer review, and clinical protocols helps gatekeep **which studies get published**, **which care pathways are approved**, and which dissenting voices are sidelined.

### **The Real Motive?**

When you align these elements—DSM revisions, reimbursement needs, identity-based narrative control, pharmaceutical pathways—you get a potent engine:

- A medical diagnosis **ensures legitimacy and revenue**
- That diagnosis builds a **collective identity** (patients)
- The identity becomes the **basis for political claims**
- Political claims drive **mandates**, **protections**, and **normalization**
- Normalization expands **markets**, **institutions**, and **influence**
- Influence consolidates power over **discourse**, **research**, and **law**

It’s not just about recognizing suffering—it’s about **channeling suffering** into a system that rewards compliance, growth, and institution-building.

**The shift from pathologizing identity** in DSM-III and DSM-IV to reframing it as *dysphoria* in DSM-5 was not just a softer tone—it was a restructuring that enabled **legislation**, **insurance mechanisms**, and **institutional expansion**.

The real result: the medical class, advocacy class, and political class now share an interest in the continuing expansion of “gender care” as **policy**, **medical practice**, and **identity framework**.

After all, you’ve got to keep the **wheels of medical economics** greased so everything runs smoothly.

### **Transition Without Parental Consent**

In states like [Minnesota](https://www.americanexperiment.org/yes-walz-technically-signed-a-bill-allowing-the-state-to-take-custody-and-order-gender-affirming-surgeries-on-children/), minors can access **gender transition services** without parental knowledge or approval. It’s not a joke. [Here’s the bill](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/laws/2023/0/Session+Law/Chapter/29/).

Minnesota courts can assert **initial child custody jurisdiction** if a child is physically present in the state solely to obtain GAHC (*§ 518D.201(d)*). This means a minor traveling to Minnesota (e.g., from a state banning GAHC) can trigger **local oversight**, potentially shielding them from parental recall or home-state custody orders requiring parental consent.

Minnesota can exercise temporary emergency jurisdiction over a child if they “**have been unable to obtain gender-affirming health care**” (*§ 518D.204(a)(3)*). This could apply to a minor fleeing parental opposition or a restrictive home state, enabling immediate GAHC without waiting for parental approval.

[History reminds us](https://thedailyeconomy.org/article/how-government-prolonged-the-lobotomy/) how “progressive” medicine can become tomorrow’s horror story. Between **1936 and 1972**, nearly **60,000 people** underwent lobotomies in the United States alone. Once hailed as groundbreaking treatment, lobotomies were praised by psychiatrists, backed by institutions, and [even rewarded with a](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/lobotomy-inventor-wins-nobel-prize/) **[Nobel Prize](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/lobotomy-inventor-wins-nobel-prize/)**.

Most were performed without the patient’s consent. By 1941, the **American Medical Association** had already denounced the procedure as ineffective.

**But instead of halting**, lobotomies exploded into a “boom” through the mid-1940s and early 1950s. It took decades before the medical establishment finally admitted what was obvious in hindsight:

**Driving an ice pick into a person’s brain was not medicine.**

**Fast-forward to today:** we’ve swapped the **ice pick** for **scalpels and hormones**.

We call it “**affirmation**,” but it’s no less invasive. The belief that mutilating healthy organs is somehow a cure for mental distress will one day be seen with the same horror as lobotomies.

The tragedy is that, just as with lobotomies, the damage is permanent—and the patients are the ones left broken while the institutions, pharmaceutical companies, and political movements profit.
