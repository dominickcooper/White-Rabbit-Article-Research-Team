### Discover how Operation Gladio influenced Italy's political violence during the Cold War, fueling unrest and shaping the nation’s turbulent Years of Lead.

Operation Gladio, NATO's covert Cold War program, was originally designed as a secret defense against a potential Soviet invasion. However, in Italy, it has become infamous for its alleged role in domestic terrorism and political manipulation during the Years of Lead (1960s–1980s). This period of intense political violence, marked by bombings and assassinations, left a lasting scar on Italy’s political landscape. As more details have emerged over the years, Operation Gladio’s influence on domestic terrorism has sparked controversy and debate.

## **Operation Gladio: NATO's Secret War in Italy**

Born from Cold War paranoia, Operation Gladio was part of a broader NATO strategy to create secret paramilitary networks across Europe. These "stay-behind" armies were tasked with launching guerrilla operations if the Soviet Union invaded Western Europe. Italy, a NATO member, played a crucial role due to its geographic position and the strength of its Communist Party.

However, as the Soviet threat receded, allegations surfaced that Gladio shifted from a defensive program to one involved in manipulating domestic politics. Many claim that Gladio operatives conducted false flag attacks and backed far-right extremists to discredit left-wing groups, all while maintaining political control.

Daniele Ganser, a Swiss historian and author of *NATO’s Secret Armies*, remarked, “The Gladio network was intended for Cold War defense, but it also became a tool for political manipulation. Evidence suggests these stay-behind armies were involved in far more than defense; they participated in a deliberate strategy of tension.”

## **The Years of Lead: A Period of Political Chaos**

Italy’s Years of Lead, spanning the 1960s to the 1980s, were marked by widespread violence, as far-left and far-right groups engaged in bombings, assassinations, and clashes. Key events such as the Bologna Massacre and the kidnapping and murder of Prime Minister Aldo Moro were major flashpoints in this era of instability.

During this period, terrorist organizations like the Red Brigades (far-left) and Ordine Nuovo (far-right) carried out high-profile attacks. However, evidence suggests that much of this violence was not as spontaneous as it appeared. Many believe that far-right extremists, some with ties to Gladio, orchestrated violent acts to blame on left-wing groups, discrediting the Communist Party and driving public opinion toward the right.

Giovanni Pellegrino, former head of the Italian Parliamentary Commission on Terrorism, asserted, “The evidence we gathered suggests that Operation Gladio fueled the violence of the Years of Lead. There were elements within the state manipulating public perception, framing left-wing groups for acts of terror.”

## **The Bologna Massacre: A Turning Point in Italy's Unrest**

On August 2, 1980, a bomb exploded at the Bologna railway station, killing 85 people and injuring over 200. Initially, the attack was attributed to far-left extremists, but investigations later pointed to the far-right group Nuclei Armati Rivoluzionari (NAR), which allegedly had connections to Gladio and state security elements.

The Bologna Massacre remains one of Italy’s darkest moments, highlighting the manipulation of terror for political purposes. Though some individuals were convicted for their roles in the bombing, many believe key Gladio operatives were never held accountable.

Judge Felice Casson, who uncovered evidence of Gladio’s existence, commented, “The Bologna Massacre is a tragedy steeped in unanswered questions. The involvement of state elements suggests there were forces willing to sacrifice innocent lives to maintain political control.”

## **False Flag Attacks and the Strategy of Tension**

Central to Operation Gladio’s alleged activities is the *Strategy of Tension*, a tactic that involved staging terrorist attacks and blaming them on left-wing groups. The goal was to create fear and chaos, pushing the Italian public to embrace authoritarianism and reducing the influence of the Communist Party.

False flag operations during the Years of Lead were not isolated incidents but part of a broader pattern. The Piazza Fontana bombing in 1969, which killed 17 people, was initially blamed on anarchists but later linked to neo-fascist groups and Gladio operatives. Stefano Delle Chiaie, a notorious neo-fascist leader, was implicated in several false flag attacks, including Piazza Fontana. His activities exemplify the blurred lines between far-right extremism, intelligence operations, and NATO’s clandestine efforts to curb communism.

“The Strategy of Tension was designed to manipulate the public through fear,” said Ganser. “By blaming left-wing groups for acts of terror, Gladio operatives aimed to shift the political climate to the right and prevent the rise of communism in Italy.”

## **The Moro Affair: Was Gladio Involved?**

The 1978 kidnapping and murder of Aldo Moro, former Prime Minister and leader of Italy’s Christian Democrats, by the Red Brigades remains one of the most infamous episodes of the Years of Lead. Moro, a key figure in Italy’s political landscape, was negotiating a historic compromise to bring the Communist Party into government. His death effectively ended this effort and has led to speculation that elements within the government, including Gladio operatives, may have played a role in facilitating his capture.

While there is no direct evidence linking Gladio to Moro’s murder, the political ramifications of his death point to the possibility of state complicity. Many believe that his assassination served the interests of those opposed to the Communist Party’s growing influence in Italian politics.

### **The Legacy of Judge Felice Casson’s Investigation**

Judge Felice Casson’s investigation into Operation Gladio marked a turning point in Italy’s efforts to uncover the truth about the dark, clandestine operations that had been manipulating its political landscape for decades. His work not only brought to light the existence of NATO's stay-behind networks, but it also exposed the extent of Gladio’s involvement in domestic terrorism and false flag operations during the Years of Lead. Through meticulous investigation, interviews, and relentless pursuit of the truth, Casson set the stage for one of the most significant revelations in Cold War history, earning him both acclaim and enemies along the way.

#### **The Investigation Begins: Uncovering Gladio**

Judge Casson’s investigation began in the 1980s when he started looking into unexplained terrorist attacks in Italy, including the notorious 1972 Peteano bombing. The attack had originally been blamed on left-wing extremists, but Casson’s research uncovered new evidence pointing to neo-fascist groups. In particular, he discovered links between these far-right extremists and the secret Gladio network.

In 1984, while reviewing declassified documents, Casson found evidence that implicated Gladio in orchestrating false flag attacks designed to discredit left-wing organizations and push Italy toward a more authoritarian right-wing government. His persistence led him to dig deeper into the operations of Italy's intelligence services, which were closely tied to NATO's secretive stay-behind armies.

#### **Interviewing Key Figures**

As Casson delved deeper into his investigation, he interviewed numerous military officials, politicians, and intelligence officers who had been involved in Gladio or were aware of its operations. These interviews painted a disturbing picture of how Gladio had not only prepared for a Soviet invasion but had also manipulated Italy’s internal political affairs.

Among the figures Casson interviewed was General Gerardo Serravalle, a former commander of the Gladio network in Italy. Serravalle confirmed that Gladio operatives had been trained for guerrilla warfare and sabotage in the event of a Soviet occupation, but he denied any involvement in domestic terrorism. However, the evidence uncovered by Casson suggested that elements of the Gladio network had indeed crossed the line into political manipulation and violence.

Casson’s work also led him to question former Prime Minister Giulio Andreotti, who initially tried to downplay the significance of Gladio. However, under pressure, Andreotti admitted to the Italian Parliament in 1990 that Gladio existed, a revelation that shocked the public and triggered widespread calls for further investigation. Andreotti’s reluctant admission opened the floodgates, and the secret nature of Gladio was no longer hidden from the public.

#### **Quotes from Casson**

Judge Felice Casson has spoken out numerous times about his findings and the importance of transparency in government operations. In one interview, he famously remarked, “The truth is that we were deceived for years. We were told that these networks were created to defend our freedom, but they were used to manipulate our democracy.”

Casson also highlighted the troubling nature of how deeply the Gladio network was embedded within Italy’s military and intelligence services. “It wasn’t just a question of a few rogue agents or a secret group operating in isolation. This was a coordinated, deliberate strategy involving the highest levels of government and intelligence.”

#### **Threats and Pushback Against Casson**

Judge Casson’s investigation was not without risks. As he began to unravel the web of deceit surrounding Gladio, he faced significant pushback from powerful figures in Italy's government and intelligence communities. While specific individuals behind the threats remain unnamed, there were numerous reports that Casson received warnings and intimidation meant to halt his inquiry into the state's covert operations.

The threats, which included both anonymous messages and direct warnings from influential figures, were meant to dissuade him from continuing his investigation. However, Casson remained undeterred. In a later interview, he admitted, “Of course, there were people who wanted me to stop, who didn’t want the truth to come out. But I was doing my job—searching for justice and for answers for the victims of terrorism in Italy. There was no turning back.”

Despite the resistance, Casson’s efforts paid off when he uncovered classified documents stored in the archives of Italy’s military intelligence agency, which further implicated Gladio in domestic terrorism. His findings led to the re-opening of several cold cases involving bombings and other terrorist attacks.

#### **The Impact of Casson’s Work**

Casson’s revelations had a profound impact on both Italy and Europe as a whole. His findings prompted further inquiries into the operations of Gladio networks in other countries, including Belgium, France, and Germany. In Italy, his work led to several high-profile parliamentary investigations and fueled public demands for accountability and transparency.

Although no high-ranking officials were ever prosecuted for their role in Gladio’s domestic terrorism, Casson’s investigation remains a pivotal moment in Italy’s history. It exposed the depth of state-sponsored manipulation and the dangerous lengths to which intelligence agencies were willing to go to maintain control over the political landscape.

In reflecting on his investigation, Casson once said, “History will judge us by how we confront the truth. Gladio was a dark chapter in our nation’s past, and it is our duty to ensure that its lessons are not forgotten.”

“The revelations from Judge Casson’s investigations rocked Italy,” said historian Ganser. “However, the failure to hold key players accountable has left a legacy of distrust in the government.”
