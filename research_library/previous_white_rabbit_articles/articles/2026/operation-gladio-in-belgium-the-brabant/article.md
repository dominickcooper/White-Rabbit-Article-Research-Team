Operation Gladio was a covert NATO operation designed to resist a Soviet invasion in post-World War II Europe by creating "stay-behind" armies. In Belgium, this secret network allegedly transformed into something far more sinister: the Brabant Massacres. This series of brutal killings between 1982 and 1985 has long been linked to Gladio’s operations, raising questions about the true motives behind the violence and its implications for Belgium’s political landscape.

### **What Was Operation Gladio?**

Operation Gladio was part of a broader NATO initiative to create stay-behind armies throughout Western Europe during the Cold War. These networks were intended to operate behind enemy lines in case of a Soviet invasion, engaging in guerrilla warfare, sabotage, and other covert activities. While the initial intent of the operation was defensive, many allegations suggest that Gladio operatives were involved in terrorism and political manipulation.

**Daniele Ganser**, a Swiss historian and expert on NATO’s secret armies, stated, "The concept of Gladio was based on creating chaos and fear to push European countries toward stronger, more authoritarian governments under the guise of fighting communism. This approach left a trail of destruction and unanswered questions across Europe."

### **The Brabant Massacres: A Reign of Terror**

From 1982 to 1985, Belgium was terrorized by a series of violent attacks known as the Brabant Massacres. Masked gunmen, later dubbed the Brabant Killers, targeted supermarkets, restaurants, and even a weapons depot. Over 28 people were killed, and dozens were injured, many of whom were shot execution-style. The level of violence shocked the nation, as the attackers made off with relatively small sums of money or goods, leading many to believe that the motive was not financial.

The random nature and brutality of the attacks left the Belgian public gripped by fear. Despite a large-scale investigation, the perpetrators were never identified or apprehended. The unanswered questions surrounding the motive for these attacks and the precision with which they were carried out have fueled speculation that they were part of a broader strategy to destabilize the country.

### **Links to Operation Gladio**

The Brabant Massacres have long been suspected of being connected to Belgium’s stay-behind network, part of NATO’s Operation Gladio. Many believe that these attacks were not just the work of violent criminals but were orchestrated as part of a "Strategy of Tension" designed to push Belgium towards authoritarianism by creating fear and political instability.

Supporters of this theory point to the military precision of the Brabant Killers, who used sophisticated tactics in their raids, resembling trained operatives rather than common criminals. Additionally, similar attacks across Europe during the Cold War, such as the **Bologna massacre** in Italy, have been linked to Gladio operatives, further supporting the theory that Gladio’s influence extended into Belgium.

Judge **Jean-Claude Lacroix**, who investigated the Brabant Massacres, commented, "There are indications that the attacks were part of a broader strategy of tension, designed to create fear and division in Belgian society. We must consider the possibility that external forces were involved."

### **The Strategy of Tension**

At the heart of the Gladio conspiracy lies the Strategy of Tension, a psychological operation that involved carrying out violent acts to create fear and instability. By blaming left-wing or communist groups for these attacks, right-wing extremists or intelligence agencies could shift public opinion toward more authoritarian governance.

In Belgium, the Brabant Massacres may have been intended to create a sense of lawlessness and insecurity, prompting the government to crack down on civil liberties and implement harsher security measures. **Daniele Ganser** explained, "The Strategy of Tension was a classic psychological operation, designed to manipulate the public through fear. By creating a climate of terror, governments could justify repressive measures and strengthen their hold on power."

### **Unsolved Mysteries: Why Was There No Justice?**

Despite extensive investigations, including multiple parliamentary inquiries, the Brabant Killers were never caught. This failure has only deepened suspicions that elements within Belgium’s government or intelligence services may have been complicit in covering up the involvement of Gladio operatives.

Key pieces of evidence, including military-grade weapons and sophisticated communications equipment, were linked to Belgium’s stay-behind network. Additionally, documents related to Operation Gladio were destroyed or classified, making it difficult for investigators to uncover the full extent of the operation’s activities in Belgium.

This lack of accountability has left many Belgians frustrated and distrustful of their government. **Marc Toussaint**, a relative of one of the massacre victims, stated, "We deserve answers. How can it be that after all these years, we still don’t know who killed our loved ones? The truth must come out."

### **Political Manipulation: A European Trend**

Belgium was not the only country to experience such violence. Italy’s Years of Lead, a period marked by bombings and political assassinations, has been similarly linked to Operation Gladio. In the case of the **Bologna massacre**, it was revealed that neo-fascist groups with ties to Gladio were responsible for the bombing, which killed 85 people.

These events across Europe suggest a broader NATO strategy aimed at suppressing left-wing political movements by fostering fear and instability. The use of violence and terror to manipulate political outcomes was not limited to one country, but a tactic seen across the continent during the Cold War.

### **Individuals Allegedly Involved in Operation Gladio in Belgium: Where Are They Today?**

Operation Gladio, a NATO-backed covert operation intended to create stay-behind armies to resist Soviet invasion, has left a long shadow across Europe. Belgium, in particular, remains haunted by the Brabant Massacres, a series of violent attacks linked to Gladio. Over the years, several individuals have been either confirmed or allegedly connected to Gladio operations in Belgium. While some have faced scrutiny, others have lived out their lives with relative anonymity. Here’s a look at key figures who were involved or implicated in Operation Gladio in Belgium and what has become of them today.

### **Martial Lekeu: The "Super Gendarme"**

Martial Lekeu, a former officer in the Belgian Gendarmerie (military police), has long been suspected of involvement in Gladio operations. Lekeu was part of the Diane anti-terrorism squad, which was trained to respond to extreme threats within Belgium. It is alleged that Lekeu had knowledge of, or perhaps direct involvement in, the Brabant Massacres due to his connections to Belgian military intelligence and the stay-behind networks. While no concrete evidence has directly linked him to the attacks, Lekeu’s association with right-wing extremist circles has fueled suspicion.

Today, Lekeu continues to live in Belgium, largely avoiding the spotlight. Despite being questioned several times during investigations into the Brabant Massacres, he was never formally charged. Many see him as a symbol of the unresolved mystery surrounding Operation Gladio, representing those in powerful positions during the Cold War whose secrets remain buried.

### **Paul Vanden Boeynants: Former Prime Minister and Political Powerhouse**

Paul Vanden Boeynants, who served as Belgium’s Prime Minister twice (in the late 1960s and mid-1970s), was also a controversial figure linked to the secret operations of Gladio. Vanden Boeynants was known for his deep ties to Belgian military and intelligence communities. He was an outspoken opponent of left-wing political movements and was accused of promoting right-wing extremism to maintain power. During the Brabant Massacres, Vanden Boeynants was allegedly involved in creating the political climate that allowed for covert operations like Gladio to thrive.

After his political career, Vanden Boeynants became embroiled in a major scandal when he was kidnapped by a criminal gang in 1989. Though he was released unharmed, the incident underscored the chaotic and corrupt political atmosphere in Belgium during that time. He passed away in 2001, but his legacy as a politician with potential ties to Gladio remains a contentious topic in Belgian political history.

### **Michel Libert: A Neo-Fascist With Dark Connections**

Michel Libert, a leader of the Westland New Post (WNP), a far-right extremist group, has been directly linked to the Brabant Massacres and Operation Gladio. The WNP was deeply involved in neo-fascist activities in Belgium, and many believe that the group collaborated with stay-behind networks to carry out acts of terror as part of the Strategy of Tension. Libert, in particular, has been accused of leading operations that aimed to destabilize Belgium during the Cold War.

Libert has vehemently denied any involvement in the Brabant Massacres, claiming that he was a victim of political persecution. Despite his claims of innocence, Libert remains a polarizing figure. He continues to live in Belgium and maintains a low public profile, occasionally speaking out in interviews to deny his alleged connections to Gladio. Investigations have so far failed to definitively prove his involvement, but suspicions linger.

### **Jean-François Calmette: The Mysterious Spy**

Jean-François Calmette was a French intelligence officer rumored to have worked closely with Belgian stay-behind networks. Although his role in Operation Gladio remains largely speculative, Calmette was known to operate within NATO intelligence circles during the 1980s, when the Brabant Massacres took place. He is believed to have had connections to key Belgian officials involved in Gladio operations.

After the end of the Cold War, Calmette disappeared from public view. His current whereabouts are unknown, and many believe that his involvement in covert operations may have allowed him to slip into obscurity. His case serves as a reminder of the opacity of intelligence work, where key players often vanish without leaving a trace.

### **Pino Rauti: Italian Fascist Ties and Influence on Gladio**

While not directly Belgian, Pino Rauti, an Italian neo-fascist and leader of the far-right political group Ordine Nuovo, played an influential role in shaping the ideology behind Gladio operations throughout Europe, including Belgium. Rauti’s connections to Belgian neo-fascist groups like the WNP further highlight the transnational nature of Gladio’s influence. His teachings and political philosophy inspired far-right networks across Europe to adopt the Strategy of Tension as a tool to maintain right-wing control and undermine left-wing movements.

Rauti remained a controversial figure in Italy until his death in 2012. His involvement in far-right terrorism and Gladio networks has left a dark legacy in both Italy and Belgium, where his ideas helped fuel violent extremism.

### **Johan Demol: The Gendarmerie Connection**

Johan Demol, another former officer of the Belgian Gendarmerie, has also been implicated in connections to Gladio and the Brabant Massacres. Demol was part of the security apparatus at a time when Belgium was plagued by terror attacks. Although there is no direct evidence linking Demol to the killings, his association with Gladio and his involvement in security operations during the period have cast a shadow over his career.

Today, Demol remains active in Belgian politics, serving as a member of the right-wing Vlaams Belang party. His political career has been met with both praise and criticism, largely due to the unresolved questions about his past involvement with Belgium’s intelligence and military apparatus. Demol has consistently denied any wrongdoing, but his name continues to surface in discussions about Gladio’s secret networks.

### **The Legacy of Operation Gladio in Belgium**

The existence of secret NATO-backed armies within Belgium has raised serious concerns about sovereignty and democratic accountability. The possibility that these networks may have orchestrated violence against their own citizens represents a profound breach of public trust.

In recent years, there have been renewed calls to investigate Gladio’s activities, not just in Belgium but across Europe. Several former Gladio operatives have come forward, providing testimony about their involvement in covert operations. However, many documents remain classified, and there is still resistance from governments to fully disclose the extent of these networks.

**Luc Reychler**, a Belgian political scientist, remarked, "The legacy of Gladio is one of secrecy and suspicion. Until the full truth is revealed, Belgium will continue to grapple with the consequences of these dark years."
