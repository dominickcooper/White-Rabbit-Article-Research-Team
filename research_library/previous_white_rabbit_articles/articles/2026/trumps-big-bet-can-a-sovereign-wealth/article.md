## **Understanding Sovereign Wealth Funds (SWFs)**

### **What is a Sovereign Wealth Fund?**

A **Sovereign Wealth Fund (SWF)** is a state-owned investment fund used by governments to manage national assets and generate wealth. These funds are commonly built from excess reserves, such as trade surpluses, resource revenues, or foreign exchange reserves.

Many countries have established SWFs to strengthen their economies:

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!n_-V!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F183788f6-cc72-44e1-ad42-b0b2b42ba51a_1200x628.jpeg)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!n_-V!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F183788f6-cc72-44e1-ad42-b0b2b42ba51a_1200x628.jpeg)

- **Norway’s Government Pension Fund Global** – The world’s largest SWF, valued at over **$1.7 trillion**, primarily funded by oil revenues. The GPFG has significantly contributed to Norway's **economic** **stability** and **prosperity**. By investing in a diversified global portfolio, the fund has **generated substantial returns**, enabling the government to finance **public** **services** and **infrastructure** **projects** without over-reliance on taxation. For instance, in 2023, the GPFG achieved a [return of 16.1%](https://www.nbim.no/contentassets/75e18afc40974cb189e3747164def669/gpfg-annual-report_2023.pdf), equivalent to 2,222 billion kroner (**about** **$200M**), bolstering the nation's fiscal health.
- **China Investment Corporation (CIC)** – Uses foreign exchange reserves to invest in global markets and domestic industries. A notable example is CIC's investment in **infrastructure** **projects** across various countries, which not only provides financial returns but also strengthens China's economic ties globally. Domestically, CIC has funded **technological advancements** and **industrial upgrades**. CIC has been described as a [unicorn-maker](https://www.jstor.org/stable/jj.2915805) for its role in fueling the growth of Chinese tech sector through its early support of Alibaba and DiDi.
- **Singapore’s Temasek Holdings** – Focuses on long-term economic resilience by investing in diverse sectors like technology, real estate, and healthcare. A prime example is Temasek's [early investment in Alibaba](https://www.insead.edu/sites/default/files/assets/dept/centres/gpei/docs/singapore-expanding-its-stake-in-overseas.pdf), capitalizing on China's burgeoning e-commerce market. This strategic move yielded substantial returns, bolstering **Singapore's national wealth**. Additionally, Temasek's investments in biotechnology and healthcare have supported Singapore's development as a **hub for medical innovation**, enhancing public health outcomes and economic diversification.

These funds provide financial stability, reduce national debt, and support economic expansion—all goals the Trump administration hopes to achieve with its proposed SWF.

## **The U.S. Sovereign Wealth Fund: A New Endeavor**

On February 3, 2025, President Trump signed an **executive order establishing a U.S. Sovereign Wealth Fund**, marking a historic shift in America’s financial strategy.

*The goal?*

To establish a **self-sustaining sovereign wealth fund** that strategically invests in **infrastructure, technological innovation, and national security assets**.

These are critical sectors that have historically relied on taxpayer funding through federal budgets, deficit spending, and government-issued bonds. Traditionally, **infrastructure projects** like highways, bridges, and energy grids have been financed through **fuel taxes, municipal bonds, and congressional appropriations**, often leading to increased national debt.

**Innovation and research** have largely been supported by **federal grants, defense contracts, and public-private partnerships**, while **national security investments** have depended on discretionary spending allocated by Congress.

By redirecting **tariff revenues and monetized federal assets** into this sovereign wealth fund, the U.S. could create a **perpetual investment vehicle** that **supplements or eventually replaces** the need for taxpayer-funded programs. Over time, as the fund grows through strategic investments, its returns could finance major infrastructure overhauls, boost cutting-edge technology development, and secure vital defense capabilities—all without further increasing the national debt or requiring additional taxation.

If successful, this model could shift the U.S. toward **a more sustainable, investment-driven approach to funding national priorities** rather than relying on deficit spending.

## **Funding the U.S. Sovereign Wealth Fund**

The biggest challenge for the SWF is ensuring adequate capital to drive growth. The Trump administration proposes multiple funding mechanisms:

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!_0qw!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F57f7584c-8725-4aa9-94a7-b8114a36be70_700x500.jpeg)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!_0qw!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F57f7584c-8725-4aa9-94a7-b8114a36be70_700x500.jpeg)

1. **Tariff Revenues**  
   One proposed method involves redirecting proceeds from tariffs on imported goods into the SWF. **By** **imposing** **tariffs**, the government can generate significant revenue, which can then be allocated to the fund. This approach not only aims to **protect domestic industries** but also seeks to create a steady income stream for national investments.   
     
   However, **it's important to note** that [the executive order](https://www.reuters.com/markets/wealth/trump-signs-executive-order-create-sovereign-wealth-fund-2025-02-03/) establishing the SWF directs the Treasury and Commerce Departments to submit a **plan within 90 days**, including recommendations on "funding mechanisms, investment strategies, fund structure, and a governance model," indicating that the specifics of funding sources are still under consideration.
2. **Federal Asset Monetization**   
   Another strategy is the **monetization of federal assets**, which involves selling or leasing government-owned properties, infrastructure, or companies to generate capital for the SWF. The U.S. government holds a vast **portfolio of assets**, including **real** **estate**, **mineral** **rights**, and **various** **enterprises**.   
     
   By strategically divesting or leasing these assets, the government can unlock significant value. For instance, the administration has considered the [acquisition of companies like TikTok](https://nypost.com/2025/02/03/us-news/trump-signs-order-creating-first-ever-us-sovereign-wealth-fund/) as part of the SWF's portfolio, suggesting a proactive approach to asset management.
3. **Strategic Investments** – Investing in industries with high returns, such as **technology**, **energy**, and **national** **defense**. By targeting sectors with strong growth potential, the fund aims to generate **substantial** **returns** that can be reinvested into the economy.   
     
   **This approach mirrors strategies** employed by other nations with successful SWFs. For example, **Australia's** **Future** **Fund**, established in 2006, has effectively utilized strategic investments to grow its assets, achieving a **12.2% return** in 2024, primarily from U.S. shares.

By creating **a perpetual investment cycle**, the fund could grow over time and eventually **reduce reliance on foreign debt** while reinvesting profits into domestic programs.

## **Challenges: What Could Go Wrong?**

Despite the bold vision, **several factors could derail the plan**, including:

1. **Trade Wars & Retaliation**

   If foreign nations **retaliate against U.S. tariffs**, it could lead to **higher consumer prices and strained diplomatic relations**.
2. **Mismanagement of the SWF**

   Poor investment decisions or political interference could **turn the fund into a financial liability** rather than an asset.
3. **Economic Downturn**

   A recession could **limit government revenues** and slow SWF growth.
4. **Legal & Political Hurdles**

   Congressional opposition and legal challenges could **delay or block SWF implementation**.

But all of these, **if anticipated**, can be addressed:

1. **Solution 1 - Trade Wars:** Negotiate targeted trade agreements while keeping tariffs flexible. We saw this with Mexico just last week.
2. **Solution 2 - Mismanagement:** Establish **an independent oversight board** and implement strict transparency measures. This oversight board must be able to show that their own holdings (personal and overseen), would not create a conflict of interest in investment recommendations.

   We saw this happen with [Rice Energy and Blackrock](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2015-71).
3. **Solution 3 - Economic Downturn:** Diversify investments to protect against downturns and prioritize **long-term stability** over short-term profits. While putting all of the US on crypto may sound exciting (or terrifying), it would not be a sound financial move.
4. **Solution 4 - Legal & Political Hurdles:** Work with bipartisan leaders to build broad-based support and ensure legal frameworks are robust. To gain **broad-based support**, the administration should employ **a mix of financial incentives, state-level investments, and strategic policy compromises** to appeal to **both Republican and Democratic lawmakers**. Here’s how this could work:

   1. **Direct State-Level Investment Guarantees**

      1. Offer **earmarked funding** from the SWF for **state infrastructure projects** in both **red and blue states**, ensuring every lawmaker sees **tangible benefits** for their constituents. What projects are funded can be voted on by the tax payer.
   2. **Revenue-Sharing Model for States**

      1. Establish a **profit-sharing mechanism** where a percentage of **SWF returns** is distributed to **state governments** to fund **education, healthcare, and infrastructure**.
      2. This could mirror how **Alaska’s Permanent Fund** (also a wealth fund) provides **annual dividends** to its residents (about [$1k-$3k per person](https://alaskapublic.org/news/2024-09-19/alaska-permanent-fund-dividend-for-2024-is-1702)), making it a politically popular model.

By **proactively addressing these risks**, Trump’s economic strategy could **achieve lasting success.**

## **THE MATH BEHIND PROJECTIONS**

We based these estimates on **historical data, economic models, and trends from similar investments in other nations**. Below, we outline the logic and math behind each projection.

#### **1. Wage Growth: $75,000 (2025) → $90,000–$95,000 (2030)**

##### **Logic:**

- Historically, **U.S. wages grow at ~3% annually** in a stable economy ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov)).
- Investment in **manufacturing, technology, and infrastructure** typically **accelerates wage growth** by at least **0.5–1.0 percentage points per year** ([Brookings Institution](https://www.brookings.edu)).
- If a **2.5%–3.5%** wage growth rate is sustained, we estimate:

##### **Math:**

- **Lower Estimate (2.5% growth per year):**

  - $75,000 × **(1.025^5)** = **$84,880**
- **Higher Estimate (3.5% growth per year):**

  - $75,000 × **(1.035^5)** = **$89,000**
- Rounding conservatively, we estimate **$90,000–$95,000** for 2030.

#### **2. Healthcare Cost Reduction: $6,500 (2025) → $4,500 (2030)**

##### **Logic:**

- **Medical inflation historically averages ~5% per year**, meaning costs should rise without intervention ([Health Affairs](https://www.healthaffairs.org)).
- Investment in **biotechnology and AI-driven medical advances** can **reduce treatment costs** ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com)).
- Nations with **sovereign wealth investment in healthcare (e.g., Singapore, Norway)** have seen healthcare cost growth **slow or even decline**.
- Estimated **2% cost reduction per year** due to biotech breakthroughs and insurance competition.

##### **Math:**

- **Projected Cost Without Innovation:** $6,500 × **(1.05^5)** = **$8,300**
- **Projected Cost With Biotech Innovation:** $6,500 × **(0.98^5)** = **$5,900**
- Factoring in competition-driven pricing and AI-driven efficiencies: **$4,500–$5,000** estimate.

#### **3. Reduced Commute Time: 55 min/day (2025) → 45–50 min/day (2030)**

##### **Logic:**

- The **American Society of Civil Engineers (ASCE)** found that **modernized highways reduce congestion by 10-15%**.
- Cities that improved public transit and infrastructure saw **10–20% reductions in commute times** ([Department of Transportation](https://www.transportation.gov)).
- A conservative **10–15% time reduction** is applied to the 55-minute average commute.

##### **Math:**

- **Lower Estimate (10% reduction):** 55 min × 0.90 = **49.5 min**
- **Higher Estimate (15% reduction):** 55 min × 0.85 = **46.75 min**
- Rounding: **45–50 minutes per day**

- **Crude oil costs account for ~55% of gasoline prices.**
- If **U.S. crude prices fall from $80 to $65 per barrel**, gasoline price could fall by **(80-65) × 0.55 = 8.25%**.
- That would drop **$3.50/gal by ~30 cents to $3.20/gal**.
- Additional savings from infrastructure and refinery investments could **bring the range to $2.75–$3.00/gal**.
