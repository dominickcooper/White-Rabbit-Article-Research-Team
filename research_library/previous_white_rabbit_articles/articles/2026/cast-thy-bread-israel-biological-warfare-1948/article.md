For decades, the story sounded like propaganda: two agents caught near Gaza with bacteria, a typhoid outbreak before Acre fell, and rumors of poisoned wells.

Then Israeli military cables surfaced with a biblical code name—**Cast Thy Bread**—and the allegation changed from rumors to something else.

The question was no longer whether an Israeli biological-warfare campaign existed in 1948. It was **where it was attempted, what it accomplished, and why the evidence remained scattered for so long**.

## THE CABLE THAT CHANGED THE STORY

On April 10, 1948, Yigael Yadin, the Haganah’s acting military chief, sent an instruction to Jerusalem commander David Shaltiel:

Fulfill Moshe Dayan’s request concerning **“Cast Thy Bread.”**

Other messages discussed “material,” thermos flasks, couriers, wells, and places where the operation had been or would be activated. On April 23, Dayan reported that it had been implemented in **Biddu and Bayt Surik**, two villages northwest of Jerusalem.

Those details come from the reconstruction by historians [Benny Morris and Benjamin Z. Kedar](https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/cast-thy-bread-israeli-biological-warfare-during-the-1948-war/), who located dispersed records in Israel Defense Forces and Defense Ministry Archive files.

## WHAT THE RECORD ACTUALLY PROVES

Now, the evidence is uneven, so the findings should not be treated as one undifferentiated confession. Here’s what I’ve found:

- **Documented fact:** senior Haganah and later IDF channels planned and moved biological “material” under the Cast Thy Bread code name.
- **Documented fact:** operations were ordered or reported at villages near Jerusalem, in the Negev and Beersheba area, and at other water sources; a later memoir records an aborted attempt at **ʿAylabun**.
- **Documented fact:** two Israeli Arab Platoon operatives were captured near Gaza during a mission that later Israeli testimony and memoir evidence describe as a well-poisoning assignment.
- **Strong inference:** the bacteria were produced or prepared by **HEMED B**, the biological section of the military Science Corps, and included typhoid and dysentery organisms.
- **Strong inference:** David Ben-Gurion authorized the campaign at the highest political level, while Yadin transmitted and supervised operational orders.
- **Unresolved:** the surviving public record does not show the complete scientific production chain, every location actually treated, or the exact number of illnesses caused.

That last category matters. A plan, a shipment, an attempt, and a successful epidemic are four different things.

But no matter what way you cut it…biological warfare is a big no-no.   
**At least according to the [Geneva convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_warfare).**

From what we can verify, **Ben-Gurion most likely knew about and authorized Cast Thy Bread**.

**Authorization reached the highest level.**

## ACRE: THE STRONGEST CLAIM AND THE HARDEST VERDICT

Before **the Haganah** captured the city on May 17–18, an enteric-disease outbreak had frightened residents, refugees, and British troops.

Later testimony attributed an attack on the **Kabri aqueduct** to Yaacov Pundaq, a Carmeli Brigade company commander, who said **Moshe Dayan** gave him “**flasks and instructions**.” Morris and Kedar identify the contents as **typhoid**, or **typhoid and dysentery bacteria**.

*Nasty stuff.*

**Other evidence** also confirms a waterborne crisis.

At a May 6 conference, **British health-services director [Brigadier Arthur Beveridge](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/119263445/arthur-joseph-beveridge)** said medical representatives regarded the infection as “**waterborne**.” On May 8, ICRC delegate Maximilien de Meuron reported roughly **ninety** local and **sixty-five** British cases.

But a British medical study creates a serious problem for a simple causal claim. In [“Double Enteric Infection,”](https://doi.org/10.1017/S002217240004417X) A. Batty Shaw and H. A. F. Mackay reported that Acre’s chlorinator had run out of chlorine on **March 17**, its engineer left the next day, and the aqueduct had been damaged and poorly maintained.

A water sample on April 13 contained **heavy fecal contamination**.

Based on illness onset and incubation, the British troops’ likely infection window was roughly **April 9–13**, with wider possible limits from March 18 to April 19. No salmonella organisms were isolated from water samples, though the investigators considered the epidemic waterborne.

The evidence points toward a narrow and strange conclusion:

**An** **Israeli biological operation targeted Acre’s water   
after a naturally explicable waterborne epidemic

CTB was likely an **aggravating act inside an existing crisis**, not the sole origin of the epidemic. It happened — **“flasks and instructions”** make that clear. But it was likely to make a bad problem worse. Its clearest demonstrated military effect was psychological: it entered a city already strained by siege, disease, rumor, and collapsing services and gave **panic** another reason to spread.

## LONDON KNEW ENOUGH TO BE DISGUSTED—AND STAYED OUT

Egypt did not confine its complaint to a Security Council speech.

It sent the matter to London, where the Foreign Office opened a file under the unusually blunt title **“[Contamination of drinking water by Zionist agents](https://kutuphane.ttk.gov.tr/details?id=587250&materialType=MF&query=DO%C4%9EU+SORUNU++--++FRANSA&utm_source=chatgpt.com)”**.

The surviving catalogue describes Egyptian allegations of Jewish bacteriological warfare; the underlying minute, cited in Benny Morris’s [history of the 1948 war](https://yalebooks.yale.edu/book/9780300145243/1948/), records the department’s choice to “keep out.”

One official called the episode so “**obnoxious**” that Britain might express its disgust to the Israelis if an opportunity arose.

So basically: *Shame on you.*

Had officials believed the story was fabricated, “disgust” would make little sense.

**Had they wanted accountability,** keeping out would make even less sense.

*So why would the British avoid it?*

Well, by late May **[the Mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_for_Palestine)** had ended, Britain was trying to manage a widening **Arab-Israeli war** through the UN, and any direct confrontation with the new Israeli government carried costs.

*Plus the Rothschilds. **More on that in the article below:***

The most likely explanation is not a proven cover-up; there is no evidence here of Britain destroying or falsifying the record. It is **deliberate non-escalation**: officials considered the charge plausible and repellent enough to discuss privately, but **not important enough to disrupt larger policy.**

*The American trail is thinner.*

The U.S. Embassy in Cairo monitored the Egyptian coverage and circulated it internally to embassy personnel. Washington’s public [FRUS collection for the 1948 Palestine crisis](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1948v05p2) contains **extensive high-level traffic** about **invasion**, **recognition**, **truce**, and **diplomacy**, but this investigation found no published State Department instruction to test the allegation, demand Egyptian laboratory evidence, protest to Israel, or defend Israel on the facts.

At the UN, the rebuttal came from **Abba Eban**, not an American official.

*So, what did American officials believe?*

From what we can tell, the embassy treated the story as **significant enough to collect**, but its press review contains no independent assessment. The best-supported judgment is narrower than Britain’s: **the United States knew the allegation was circulating and preserved it, but no accessible record shows that it investigated or elevated the matter.**

So as far as Britain and America were concerned:

*Not our problem.*

## THE OPERATIONS THAT DID—AND DID NOT—HAPPEN

**Cast Thy Bread** was a campaign, not a single incident. The surviving record supports a ladder of outcomes:

- **Biddu and Bayt Surik:** Dayan reported CTB implemented on April 23.
- **Jerusalem/Jericho corridor:** repeated plans, transport problems, and delayed “material” are documented.
- **Acre/Kabri aqueduct:** deliberate contamination is strongly supported.
- **Gaza:** attempted contamination is strongly corroborated.
- **Halutza and Beersheba/Negev:** brigade reports said CTB operations were carried out in May and June. A memoir describes bottles intended for Egyptian water sources, some destroyed after internal opposition, and other attempts that harmed no Egyptian soldiers but allegedly sickened several Bedouin and Israeli soldiers.
- **ʿAylabun:** a HEMED-linked operative arrived with written General Headquarters instructions to poison a village well.
- **Cairo:** in September, Yadin initiated work to activate CTB abroad. Reuven Shiloah’s intervention appears to have stopped the plan, and a poison capsule was destroyed.
- **Lebanon and Syria:** operatives surveyed water systems and other infrastructure. The evidence supports target reconnaissance, not a completed biological attack.

## WHY CAST THY BREAD STAYED HIDDEN

The obscurity was not caused by a total absence of records. It resulted from **compartmentation, euphemism, censorship, and dispersal**.

- **The scientists’ files remained closed.**
- **Crucial information** vanished from the published Ben-Gurion diary.
- CTB records survived across ordinary unit files whose reviewers apparently **did not recognize the biblical code name.**
- **Morris and Kedar** found one key Ben-Gurion letter in private hands and used unpublished interviews and obscure memoirs to bridge gaps. Their reconstruction remains “skeletal” because the production and weaponization records **are still unavailable.** (*surprise, surprise*)

Could “biological materials $2,000” have disappeared through ordinary abridgment?

*In isolation, yes.*   
**Editors shorten diaries.** It’s not uncommon.

But this was not a stray grocery expense: it identified **sensitive biological procurement** during the exact week **CTB** expanded, and it was omitted while adjacent purchases remained.

Combined with continued classification of **HEMED B records**, the most plausible reading is **purposeful removal of militarily sensitive (or politically embarrasing) information**.

The wider archival environment matters too. **Akevot** has documented known barriers to **Israeli government records** and the **reclassification of previously open material**.

*Here today, gone tomorrow.*

## THE STRONGEST CONVENTIONAL EXPLANATION

The conventional explanation is not that nothing happened.

The cables prove it did.

The “clean explanation” is that **CTB was small, improvised, operationally clumsy, and mostly ineffective**, while **wartime sanitation failures** account for much of the disease attributed to it.

*In layman’s terms: **We tried to do this, but it didn’t really work. People got sick from bad water.***

That tidy explanation accounts for a great deal:

- Broken aqueducts
- Absent chlorine
- Fecal contamination
- Refugees living in crowded conditions
- Contradictory disease labels
- Failures to transport viable cultures
- Bottles destroyed by dissenting soldiers
- No documented epidemic outside Acre.

Plus biological agents are hard to keep viable and harder to dose predictably in open water.

Even sympathetic participants described **poor results.**

It also fits the broader 1948 health environment. The UN mediator’s [progress report](https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-198227/) found **typhoid**, **enteritis**, and **dysentery** among refugees and warned that scarce water and inadequate medical systems favored waterborne epidemics.

*Case closed!*  
**Well, not quite.**

Natural disease and operational failure do not explain **the orders.**

- HEMED B’s containers
- The Gaza operatives
- Guttman’s objection
- Negev brigade summaries

Nor do they explain why Ben-Gurion’s diary editors removed the phrase “biological materials $2,000.”

The honest synthesis is uncomfortable for both maximalists and skeptics.

**Israel operated a documented biological-warfare campaign in 1948.**

- It targeted water used by Palestinian communities and Arab military forces.
- Several deployments were attempted; some were reported completed. Yet the public evidence (**emphasis on public**) shows limited military effect, uncertain casualty totals, internal resistance, and a particularly complicated causal record at Acre.

## THE UNRESOLVED QUESTION

**Cast Thy Bread** no longer belongs in the category of rumor. The code name, chain of command, couriers, targets, and internal objections are too well documented. What remains uncertain is the part a biological weapon is supposed to make measurable: **who became sick because of it**.

The most important missing records are **HEMED B’s laboratory and production files**, complete operational logs, bacteriological test results from Gaza, and any surviving target or after-action reports.

Those records could distinguish viable cultures from failed shipments, planned targets from treated wells, and a frightening program from a militarily consequential one.

***But I’m not holding my breath.***

Perhaps that is why this story matters beyond 1948. **Secret programs** are often remembered by their intentions and denied through their failures. **Cast Thy Bread** sits in the gap between the two—documented enough to establish the operation, fragmented enough to keep its human cost uncertain.

## FAQ

### Was Operation Cast Thy Bread real?

Yes. Israeli archival cables and later Israeli testimony establish that Cast Thy Bread was a Haganah/IDF biological-warfare campaign involving water contamination. The existence of the operation is documented even though its complete effects are not.

### Did Israel cause the 1948 typhoid outbreak in Acre?

Israeli contamination of the Kabri water system is strongly supported, but the surviving epidemiology does not prove it caused the entire outbreak. Acre had lost chlorination, its aqueduct was damaged, and British investigators found severe fecal contamination with a likely infection window that may have begun before the alleged attack.

### Did the CIA confirm the Gaza well-poisoning mission?

No. A CIA archive preserves a U.S. Embassy Cairo report summarizing Egyptian press coverage. That is contemporaneous diplomatic evidence, not an independent CIA laboratory confirmation. Later Israeli records and testimony provide the stronger corroboration.

### Which pathogens were involved?

The most consistent records identify **typhoid and dysentery bacteria**. Some contemporaneous documents used “typhus” imprecisely, and Ben-Gurion’s diary mentioned “malaria bacteria,” apparently reflecting a mistaken or garbled report.

### Does Cast Thy Bread prove Israel has a current offensive biological-weapons program?

No. It proves a 1948 campaign and an institutional lineage from military science personnel to IIBR. Later assessments recognized Israeli scientific capability but did not establish continuous offensive activity through the following decades.
