# OPERATION SUSANNAH: THE ISRAELI FALSE FLAG THAT REALLY HAPPENED

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/6699b725-3db5-4275-88f7-59364ac04ed1_1374x1145.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!tbQq!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F6699b725-3db5-4275-88f7-59364ac04ed1_1374x1145.png)

In 1954, an Israeli military-intelligence network planted incendiary bombs in American and British-linked sites across Egypt—and intended the blame to fall on Egyptians.

The operation is documented.   
**The order behind it remains a mystery.**

The bomb began burning inside **Philip Natanson’s pocket** as he approached Alexandria’s Rio cinema on July 23, 1954. It was hidden in an eyeglass case, designed to ignite later, after he had walked away.

*Whoops.*

That accident exposed **Operation Susannah**, the covert campaign that became Israel’s **Lavon Affair**. The plot was not an allegation assembled decades later from suspicious coincidences.

The [Israeli Ministry of Defense archive calls it a false-flag operation](https://archives.mod.gov.il/sites/English/Exhibitions/Pages/The-Lavon-affair.aspx): Israeli military intelligence **recruited Egyptian Jews** to **bomb civilian sites** while directing suspicion toward the **Muslim Brotherhood**, **Egyptian communists**, **local nationalists**, or other “**malcontents**.”

The bombs were real.   
The Israeli network was real.   
The intended deception (*aka **False Flag***) was real.

But the simplest question—*who gave the order?*—would tear through Israeli politics for years without producing an answer everyone accepted.

## THE OPERATION IS NOT A THEORY

It helps to separate two mysteries that are often blended together. There is no uncertainty about whether Israel ran the network. The uncertainty concerns ***who*** at the top authorized its activation.

Israel’s own defense archive says the group was recruited by **Israeli military intelligence**. The Israeli Intelligence Heritage and Commemoration Center likewise describes [a covert military-intelligence operation against Western targets](https://modiin.ussl.co.il/%D7%A2%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%93-%D7%A4%D7%A0%D7%99%D7%9E%D7%99-2/) whose purpose was to **damage Egypt’s relations with the United States** and Britain and **prevent British withdrawal** from the Suez Canal.

That distinction matters.

“We cannot prove *which official* spoke the final order” is not the same statement as “we cannot prove ***which state*** built and operated the network.”

The first is uncertain.   
**The second is historical record.**

## THREE ROUNDS OF FIRE

The operation unfolded in three waves. On **July 2**, members placed small incendiaries in Alexandria post boxes. On **July 14**, devices ignited at American information or cultural facilities in Cairo and Alexandria. On **July 23**, operatives moved against cinemas in both cities and a rail facility in Alexandria.

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/d792fc63-6964-4f2f-9bb1-71e56274b90c_1672x941.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!n_-G!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fd792fc63-6964-4f2f-9bb1-71e56274b90c_1672x941.png)

**These were not battlefield targets.**

They were **civilian places** tied to Western presence and prestige: libraries, educational centers, cinemas, and transportation infrastructure. The devices were small and timed to ignite after closing, a design that reduced—but could never eliminate—the chance of casualties.

The absence of deaths in the attacks does not make them harmless either. Fire in a cinema or library can spread unpredictably. Nor did the plot spare its own recruits: arrests, torture allegations, imprisonment, suicide, and execution followed the operational collapse.

The target pattern does reveal something important. This was not an attempt to defeat Egypt’s army by destroying matériel.

It was an attempt to **alter what Washington and London** *believed* about Egypt.

## THE YOUNG PEOPLE INSIDE THE MACHINE

The network was built largely from young Egyptian Jews in Cairo and Alexandria. **[Moshe Marzouk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshe_Marzouk)**, a physician; **[Shmuel Azar](https://nidoneykahir.org.il/en/the-cairo-martyrs/samuel-azar/)**, a teacher; **[Marcelle Ninio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcelle_Ninio)**, who served as a courier and liaison; **[Philip Natanson](https://nidoneykahir.org.il/en/the-cairo-martyrs/philippe-nathanson/)**, **[Victor Levy](https://nidoneykahir.org.il/en/the-cairo-martyrs/victor-levi/)**, **[Robert Dassa](https://nidoneykahir.org.il/en/robert-dassa/)**, **[Meir Meyuhas](https://www.izkor.gov.il/en/fallen/Meir%20Meyuhas/en_0da3d99707129761ccb6607862cbdd44)**, and **[Meir Za’afran](https://nidoneykahir.org.il/en/the-cairo-martyrs/mayer-zafran/)** became entangled in a clandestine structure created far above them.

The participant-centered book *[Operation Susannah](https://archive.org/details/operationsusanna0000gola/page/n5/mode/2up)* describes recruits traveling secretly for **training in Israel and Europe**. They learned radio communication, surveillance, clandestine procedure, and sabotage.

*To readers of The White Rabbit, **this sounds like Operation Gladio.***

Back in Egypt, they maintained transmitters, acquired chemicals, reconnoitered locations, and waited.

They were **motivated Zionists** who believed they were serving Israel.

But belief was not preparation. According to historians **Ian Black** and **Benny Morris**, the network lacked proper compartmentation and its members were not adequately taught how to behave if arrested. A dormant network created for wartime use was being turned into a peacetime political instrument without the tradecraft needed to survive exposure.

*Growing pains, **if you ask me.***

## UNIT 131: SABOTAGE MEETS BLACK PROPAGANDA

The most revealing rabbit hole begins before 1954. Black and Morris trace **[Unit 131](https://nidoneykahir.org.il/en/the-affair/)** to **Heker 2**, a secret section created in 1948 inside the Foreign Ministry’s Political Division. Its assigned mission combined two things: sabotage behind enemy lines and **black propaganda**.

**BLACK PROPAGANDA:**

Black propaganda is covert disinformation deliberately attributed to a false source—often the very group it aims to discredit—in order to undermine that group’s credibility.

After the 1948 war, the unit moved into the **Israel Defense Forces**.

In 1951, its commander **Mordechai “Motke” Ben-Tzur** sent **Avraham Dar** into Egypt under the British identity “John Darling.”

[![Avraham Dar aka “John Darling”](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/b5c55a4b-995c-4324-858b-9466388561ce_250x338.jpeg "Avraham Dar aka “John Darling”")](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!HT1q!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb5c55a4b-995c-4324-858b-9466388561ce_250x338.jpeg)

Avraham Dar aka “John Darling”

Dar recruited the cells. The young operatives were trained and then left dormant until a crisis gave headquarters a reason to activate them.

So, **deceptive** **attribution was embedded in the institutional ancestry of the unit that ran Susannah**. The false flag was not an improvised excuse after the network was caught.

Concealing the Israeli hand and generating a misleading political interpretation were central to the method.

## THE BRITISH WERE LEAVING

To understand why anyone thought this scheme made sense, look west from Cairo toward the Canal Zone.

Britain had occupied Egypt in one form or another for decades and **maintained a huge military base around Suez**. Egypt’s post-1952 revolutionary government wanted the troops gone. Britain wanted withdrawal terms that preserved access to the base in a “future emergency.”

*We’ve heard that before.*

Israel saw the British presence as a buffer. If British forces withdrew, Egyptian power and prestige would grow, and the physical obstacle between Egypt’s army and Israel would disappear. Washington was also exploring military and economic assistance to Cairo as part of a broader attempt to draw Egypt toward the West during the Cold War.

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/2167584a-2bdb-4781-93ea-ea05728a05c6_1672x941.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!YJGq!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F2167584a-2bdb-4781-93ea-ea05728a05c6_1672x941.png)

The diplomatic clock was ticking while the devices were being planted. A U.S. State Department [record of the Anglo-Egyptian negotiations](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1952-54v09p2/d1348) states that negotiators initialed the Heads of Agreement on **July 27, 1954**, four days after Natanson’s arrest.

**Had Natanson successfully planted his incendiary inside the British-owned Rio Theatre, the political damage could have mattered far more than the physical damage.**

An attack on **a British target** by “Egyptian extremists” (Israel, in reality) could have strengthened the argument that Egypt was unstable, that British interests remained under threat, and that withdrawal was dangerously premature.

At the very least, another unexplained attack might have injected precisely the kind of fear and mistrust capable of **delaying**, **hardening**, or even **disrupting negotiations** that were only days from agreement.

**But that didn’t happen.**   
Thanks to the bomb igniting in his pocket.

The final agreement was signed on **October 19**, providing for British troop withdrawal and redefining the base’s future use.

## WHY BOMB AMERICAN LIBRARIES?

The operational logic was cold and indirect. If fires erupted at American and British-linked civilian sites, and if blame settled on the Muslim Brotherhood, communists, or nationalist extremists, Western governments might conclude that **Gamal Abdel Nasser’s Egypt was unstable, hostile, or incapable of protecting Western interests**.

That could sour Washington on aid and **make London reconsider leaving Suez**.

Scholar **Leonard Weiss** quotes the planning objective as breaking Western confidence in Egypt’s regime, causing arrests and unrest, hiding the Israeli origin, and shifting attention to another culprit. His [historical analysis of the Lavon Affair](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0096340213493259) is especially valuable because it separates the operation’s documented mechanism from the later fight over authorization.

The plot was strategically grandiose and operationally tiny. A few young people with homemade delayed incendiaries were expected to **bend negotiations** among Egypt, Britain, and the United States. That mismatch (between the scale of the means and the scale of the hoped-for result) is one of the clearest signs of how distorted **covert planning** can become inside a closed chain of command.

## DOES IT REALLY QUALIFY AS A FALSE FLAG?

Yes. Not every secret attack is a false flag. A clandestine state operation can hide its sponsor without actively pointing blame elsewhere. A false flag adds **deliberate misattribution**: the action is constructed so observers believe a different actor was responsible.

This has been done time and time again in history:

- [The Mukden Incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukden_incident) (1931)
- [The Gleiwitz Incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gleiwitz_incident) (1939)
- [The Gulf of Tonkin Incident](https://thewhiterabbitreport.substack.com/p/gulf-of-tonkin-incident-lie-vietnam-war?utm_source=publication-search) (1964)
- [Operation Northwoods](https://thewhiterabbitreport.substack.com/p/operation-northwoods-the-secret-us?utm_source=publication-search) (1962 - Proposed)

*Just to name a few big ones.*

Susannah meets that test in four ways:

- **Israeli sponsorship was concealed.** The operatives used local identities and covert communications.
- **The selected targets carried Western political meaning.** American and British-linked civilian facilities were chosen to shape foreign perceptions.
- **Other groups were intended to receive the blame.** Israel’s own archive names the Muslim Brotherhood, communists, nationalists, and unspecified malcontents.
- **The desired result depended on the deception.** Fires openly claimed by Israel would not demonstrate Egyptian instability; they would demonstrate Israeli sabotage.

The White Rabbit archive’s [guide to false flags and psychological operations](https://thewhiterabbitreport.substack.com/p/deep-state-vocabulary-false-flags) provides the broader vocabulary.

But Operation Susannah is ***unusually clear*** because the official Israeli record itself applies the label.

## THE DEVICE THAT BROKE THE STORY

On July 23, Natanson approached the Rio cinema carrying the incendiary device. It ignited early. Egyptian police arrested him, followed the evidence, and rolled up the network.

*Not everything is a Michael Bay production.*

**This mundane failure** is a useful antidote to the mythology of all-powerful intelligence services. The operation did not collapse because a heroic counterspy decoded an impossibly sophisticated plot. It collapsed because a crude device burned the man carrying it, while poor compartmentation allowed one arrest to endanger nearly everyone.

However, **there is a darker unresolved possibility…**

The network’s field commander, **Avri Elad**, escaped Egypt and later became the subject of betrayal allegations. He had entered under a German identity, “Paul Frank,” after being recruited into **Unit 131** despite a troubled military record. Later, Israeli authorities discovered that he had contacts with an Egyptian intelligence officer; he was tried secretly and imprisoned.

## TRIAL, SUICIDE, EXECUTION, PRISON

The Cairo trial began in December 1954. Binnet, an Israeli intelligence operative whose contact with the network violated compartmentation, died by suicide in prison on December 21.

On January 27, 1955, the court delivered its judgments.

A contemporary State Department [telegram recording the Cairo verdicts](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1955-57v14/d11) reported that two defendants were sentenced to death, two were acquitted, and six received hard-labor sentences ranging from two years to life. **Moshe Marzouk and Shmuel Azar were hanged on January 31.**

A [1971 Jewish Telegraphic Agency report](https://www.jta.org/archive/six-israelis-sentenced-as-spies-in-egypt-15-years-ago-living-in-israel) disclosed that six convicted members were living in Israel, though officials still withheld details of their return.

## WHO GAVE THE ORDER?

[![Pinhas Lavon (colorized) | Lavon Affair](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/23483752-0f53-40ae-8153-4bbf5496a292_1448x1086.png "Pinhas Lavon (colorized) | Lavon Affair")](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!b5Lq!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F23483752-0f53-40ae-8153-4bbf5496a292_1448x1086.png)

Defense Minister Pinchas Lavon, left, and IDF Chief of the General Staff Moshe Dayan, with Shimon Peres in the background, on February 8, 1953 (colorized)

When the operation exploded into scandal, it didn’t take long for finger pointing to start.

Defense Minister **[Pinhas Lavon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinhas_Lavon)** denied authorizing it. The director of military intelligence, **Binyamin Gibli**, said Lavon gave him a verbal instruction. Chief of Staff **Moshe Dayan** was abroad during the crucial activation period. Prime Minister **Moshe Sharett** had been kept outside the operation.

Sharett appointed Supreme Court president **Yitzhak Olshan** and former IDF chief of staff **Ya’akov Dori** to investigate. Their commission confronted contradictory testimony and **could not determine** whether Lavon or Gibli had given the order. Lavon resigned on February 17, 1955, and **Ben-Gurion** returned from retirement to become defense minister.

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/d8386c33-aeab-4464-8a50-310aa694ed3b_1536x1024.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!lPNW!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fd8386c33-aeab-4464-8a50-310aa694ed3b_1536x1024.png)

David Ben-Gurion with some of the released prisoners on January 1, 1967

The phrase “**Lavon Affair**” therefore contains a quiet injustice. It permanently attaches the operation to the official whose personal authorization was never proved—and whose accusers relied, in part, **on corrupted evidence.**

## THE THIRD MAN SAYS HE WAS THE SCAPEGOAT

Remember **Avri Elad**?

The field commander who escaped Egypt while almost everyone beneath him was arrested—the man later suspected of betraying the network to Egyptian intelligence?

Elad eventually offered a ***very*** **different** **explanation** for his place in the scandal.

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/63461db1-9681-41da-8dfe-9e1c142296cf_1448x1086.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!QYBY!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F63461db1-9681-41da-8dfe-9e1c142296cf_1448x1086.png)

Avri Elad

By 1960, Israel had put him on trial behind closed doors for security offenses involving contact with a foreign agent. During that proceeding, Elad claimed something explosive:

**He said his superiors had pressured him to lie during the original Olshan-Dori investigation in order to make Pinhas Lavon look responsible for Operation Susannah.**

According to the **Israel Democracy Institute’s history of the affair**, Elad said he had been induced to give false testimony before the commission so evidence would point toward Lavon as the man who gave the order. He went even further, arguing that the later case against him **had been manufactured** to keep him from exposing the conspiracy in which he had participated.

That second accusation did **not** hold up.

The court rejected Elad’s claim that his arrest itself had been fabricated ***simply*** to silence him.

But then came the part that makes this rabbit hole considerably harder to dismiss.

**The court accepted Elad’s account that he had given false testimony during the original investigation.**

In other words, the alleged traitor **was telling the truth** about at least one part of the cover-up.

Elad’s revised story also created a devastating problem for **Binyamin Gibli’s timeline**.

The case against Lavon depended heavily on the idea that Lavon had personally authorized action in mid-July. But histories of the operation place Elad’s activation instructions much earlier:

**Motke Ben-Tzur met Elad in France on May 26, 1954, before Elad returned to Egypt to take command of the network.**

Academic historian Tamar Amar-Dahl likewise describes **Gibli** sending the Unit 131 commander to Paris in May, where Elad received **instructions to activate the sabotage unit**.

That matters.

- The first incendiaries went into Alexandria post boxes on **July 2**.
- The next wave struck American-linked facilities on **July 14**.

So if Elad had already been ordered to activate the network before those attacks, a supposed **July 16 authorization from Lavon** could hardly explain how the operation had already been underway for two weeks.

*Time travel would certainly clear a few things up.*

Elad said the explanation was much simpler:

**Lavon was being framed after the operation blew up.**

And Elad had helped do it.

This gives us a strange problem when deciding **what to make of the man**. There were legitimate reasons to distrust him. Israeli investigators later uncovered his unauthorized contacts with an Egyptian intelligence officer, and he was convicted of serious security offenses. The suspicion that he had betrayed the Egyptian network did not materialize out of thin air.

But Israel apparently never proved that **Elad was the man who gave Natanson and the others to the Egyptians in 1954**. Suspicion of betrayal remained separate from the offenses for which Israel actually imprisoned him.

And being guilty of one thing does not make a man guilty of everything.

Elad spent years insisting that he had become **a convenient scapegoat**—a compromised intelligence officer **who knew too much** about what his superiors had done after Operation Susannah failed. When his memoir *Decline of Honor* appeared in 1976, the controversy became serious enough that Prime Minister **Yitzhak Rabin’s government still refused to release the secret verdict from Elad’s trial.**

*So was Elad merely trying to save himself?*

**Maybe**.

But here is the uncomfortable part.

**The machinery he described actually existed.**

- Elad said testimony had been coordinated to implicate Lavon.
- The court accepted that false testimony had occurred.
- Later investigations uncovered evidence that documents connected to the original inquiry had also been falsified.
- Contemporary reporting described both **false testimony against Lavon and falsified records** emerging from the reopened investigation.

That does not prove every accusation Elad ever made.

It does something more interesting.

It means the man accused of betraying Operation Susannah may also have been **a participant in—and later a witness against—the effort to rewrite who authorized it.**

Traitor?

Scapegoat?

**Possibly both.**

And once investigators stopped listening only to the witnesses and started looking at the actual paperwork, **things got worse.**

## THE FORGED PAPER TRAIL

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/038d0fb4-486c-4075-9e85-f27aa882faf0_1544x1019.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!DLGJ!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F038d0fb4-486c-4075-9e85-f27aa882faf0_1544x1019.png)

Levi Eshkol in a work meeting with his staff in the Ministry of Finance during the late 1950s, with **Dalia Carmel** standing near the center. Staff members including Shlomo Amir, David Kochav, Aharon Keren, and Simcha Sidoror.

Years later, the inquiry itself began to unravel.

Gibli’s former secretary, **Dalia Carmel**, said she had **retyped** the second page of a July 19 letter so it would say the Egyptian actions were carried out **“on Lavon’s instructions.”**

*I’m sorry, what? Retyped??*

She described selecting older-looking paper, recreating the quirks of her original typing, and altering a document log.

**[“Experts” initially accepted the document as authentic.](https://icgs.org.il/en/publications/the-evolution-of-inquiry-committee/)**Later investigations found perjury and forgery in the case assembled against Lavon. That did not magically reveal the real command decision, but it demolished the clean story in which paperwork proved Lavon guilty.

Here the affair becomes larger than a failed bombing campaign. **A secret institution** attempted to manipulate foreign perception through deniable attacks. After exposure, people connected to **that institution** manipulated the domestic record through false testimony and altered documents.

**The operation failed; the method survived and turned inward.**

## THE SCANDAL THAT BROUGHT BEN-GURION BACK

Lavon’s resignation opened the Defense Ministry to **[David Ben-Gurion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ben-Gurion)**, who soon returned as prime minister. The dispute resurfaced in 1960 when new evidence about the forged record strengthened Lavon’s **demand for exoneration.**

Ben-Gurion insisted on a judicial inquiry; party leaders sought a political settlement. The conflict helped fracture **Mapai** (*think democratic socialist*) and contributed to Ben-Gurion’s later resignation and breakaway **Rafi party** (*think Cold War Democrats*).

It would be too neat to say one failed operation single-handedly remade Israeli politics. Personal rivalries, security doctrine, party machinery, and arguments over civilian control were already present. But **Operation Susannah** acted as an **accelerant** because it forced every faction to answer the same corrosive question:

***Who controls secret force** when no one will own the order?*

## THE UNEXPECTED ROAD TO FRANCE

**Weiss** follows the consequences **one step further.**

He argues that the affair weakened Sharett’s more diplomatic leadership, restored Ben-Gurion, and helped move Israel toward France at the moment Paris was fighting **Arab nationalism** in Algeria and confronting Nasser. That relationship eventually delivered **advanced arms** and crucial **nuclear assistance.**

This is the strongest unexpected connection in the investigation. **Operation Susannah** was not a proven master plan to obtain a nuclear weapon. Weiss’s argument is that the scandal changed personnel and alignments in ways that **accelerated** a path Israel likely would have pursued anyway.

The implication is still remarkable. An amateurish series of small fires in Egypt may have helped trigger political consequences that reached from Ben-Gurion’s comeback to the strategic partnership behind **Israel’s nuclear program**. (*[Which it still hasn’t confirmed or denied…](https://imeu.org/resources/important-events/fact-sheet-israel-and-the-atomic-bomb/253)*)

## HONORED FIFTY YEARS LATER

For decades, Israel avoided publicly acknowledging the operation.

Then **[something remarkable happened](https://web.archive.org/web/20131227083535/http://www.ynetnews.com/Ext/Comp/ArticleLayout/CdaArticlePrintPreview/1,2506,L-3065838,00.html)**.

In **March 2005**, Israeli President **Moshe Katsav** presented certificates of appreciation to surviving members of the network and relatives of those who had died. [Reuters described the honorees](https://www.reuters.com/article/world/factbox-a-brief-history-of-israeli-espionage-scandals-idUSTRE61G2RQ/) as Israeli-recruited **agents-provocateurs** and summarized the operation plainly: the bombings were intended to be blamed on local Egyptian insurgents.

But Katsav did not merely acknowledge them.

> “We decided now to express our respect for these heroes.”

**Heroes??**

Israeli military intelligence recruited the network to **attack** **Egyptian**, **American**, and **British-owned civilian sites** while directing suspicion toward the **Muslim Brotherhood, Egyptian communists, local nationalists, or other supposed malcontents**.

## SO WHO ACTUALLY GAVE THE ORDER?

After all of that, we can finally return to the question that poisoned Israeli politics for years.

*Who gave the order?*

There is no surviving signed directive saying, **“Activate Operation Susannah.”** No recording settles the argument. No document has emerged that lets historians stamp *CASE CLOSED* across the file.

But unresolved does not mean every explanation is equally convincing.

From what I can tell, [the surviving evidence points](https://web.archive.org/web/20080823032820/http://www.haaretz.com/hasen/spages/1013203.html) most strongly toward **[Binyamin Gibli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binyamin_Gibli)**, the head of Israeli military intelligence, with **Mordechai “Motke” Ben-Tzur** acting as the officer who transmitted and implemented the decision through **Unit 131**.

The most damaging evidence against Gibli is surprisingly simple.

**The calendar.**

Gibli maintained that Defense Minister **Pinhas Lavon** verbally authorized the Egyptian operation on **July 16, 1954**.

There is a problem with that date.

The network had already attacked Alexandria post boxes on **July 2**.

Then, on **July 14**, it struck American-linked facilities in Cairo and Alexandria.

So by the time Lavon supposedly gave the order on July 16, **Operation Susannah had already been underway for two weeks**.

**To preserve his version,** Gibli had to separate those earlier attacks from the operation Lavon supposedly authorized—to suggest, in effect, that the July 2 and July 14 incidents were something else.

But **the operatives themselves** later identified those attacks as part of the same Israeli campaign…

*Oops.*

So we are left with ***an awkward choice***.

Either the director of Israeli military intelligence somehow **did not know** that his own clandestine network was already carrying out sabotage in Egypt...

...or **Lavon’s supposed July 16 authorization was not the true beginning of the operation.**

The second explanation fits the rest of the evidence much better.

**If this were only a dispute over dates**, we could chalk it up to foggy memories.

But the evidence against Lavon did not merely become questionable.

**Some of it was manufactured.**

Meanwhile, **Avri Elad** admitted that his original testimony to the Olshan-Dori inquiry had also been false. And according to Elad, he had lied because people above him **wanted responsibility pushed toward Lavon.**

*Think about what happened.*

- The man controlling **military intelligence** accused his **civilian superior** of giving a verbal order that conveniently left no paper trail.
- The date associated with that alleged authorization came **after the operation had already begun**. *Whoops.*

At some point, **coincidence** starts carrying quite a load…

### ELAD POINTED THE FINGER AT GIBLI

Elad eventually said the answer was straightforward:

**Gibli gave the order.**

Elad is hardly the witness you would choose if you were building a clean prosecution. His later contacts with Egyptian intelligence, his security conviction, his shifting testimony, and the suspicion that he betrayed the Egyptian network all give us reasons to treat anything he said cautiously.

**He also had an obvious motive.**

If Elad could convince investigators that senior officials had manipulated him, he could recast himself from disgraced intelligence officer into **another victim of the affair**.

Plus**, Elad’s claim** that he had been used to give false testimony **turned out to have substance.** He said the official story had been manipulated….then later investigations found manipulated testimony and altered evidence.

A liar can still tell the truth about the people who taught him to lie.

Ben-Tzur should not disappear into the background here. He appears to have been deeply involved in turning headquarters decisions into operational instructions. ## FAQ: FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

### Was Operation Susannah really an Israeli false-flag operation?

Yes. Israel’s Ministry of Defense archive identifies it as a false flag, states that Israeli military intelligence recruited the network, and says blame was intended to fall on Egyptian groups while Israeli sponsorship remained hidden.

### Did the 1954 bombings kill anyone?

The incendiary attacks themselves caused no reported deaths. The operation nevertheless produced grave consequences: Max Binnet died by suicide after arrest, Egypt executed Moshe Marzouk and Shmuel Azar, and other operatives spent years in prison.

### Did Pinhas Lavon order the operation?

It has never been conclusively established. Gibli said Lavon gave an oral order; Lavon denied it; the Olshan-Dori inquiry could not decide; and later discoveries of forged evidence and perjury undermined the case against Lavon.

### Why were American and British facilities targeted?

The plan sought to make Egypt appear unstable or hostile to Western interests while Britain negotiated its Suez withdrawal and the United States considered closer relations and aid. Planners hoped the resulting mistrust would damage Nasser and preserve the British presence.

### What is the most important unresolved question?

The central unresolved question remains who authorized activation of Unit 131 in July 1954. Related questions—especially whether Avri Elad betrayed the network—remain disputed, but they do not alter the documented Israeli sponsorship or false-flag design.
