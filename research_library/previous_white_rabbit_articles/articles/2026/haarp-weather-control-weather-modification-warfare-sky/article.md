### **Project Cirrus: The Hurricane That Turned Back**

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/c934c760-f96d-47bf-8be4-1b2a394b0ab9_461x248.jpeg)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!antl!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fc934c760-f96d-47bf-8be4-1b2a394b0ab9_461x248.jpeg)

*Project Cirrus crew and scientists pose in front of B-17 used in the hurricane seeding.*

**In 1947**, the U.S. military and General Electric scientists carried out **[Project Cirrus](https://www.aoml.noaa.gov/hurricane_blog/70th-anniversary-of-the-first-hurricane-seeding-experiment/)**, an early attempt to seed a hurricane. The storm was believed to be moving safely out to sea.

After seeding, **it changed course** and struck near Savannah, Georgia.

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/e33d88e5-e911-4993-8c32-9a13111dbbd3_461x369.gif)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!iLRA!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe33d88e5-e911-4993-8c32-9a13111dbbd3_461x369.gif)

Track of 1947 hurricane “King” (Unisys). Does this look natural to you?

**[Dr. Irving Langmuir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving_Langmuir)**, the head of the GE lab that created the materials for the experiment, [issued a statement](https://www.aoml.noaa.gov/hurricane_blog/70th-anniversary-of-the-first-hurricane-seeding-experiment/) that he was “99% sure” the storm had changed course due to the seeding.

There was a lot of public outcry over **Project Cirrus** (people had died) so overt weather modification went quiet for a while.

But the weather didn’t cooperate. In 1954 and 1955, six major hurricanes — Carol, Edna, Hazel, Connie, Diane, and Ione — slammed into the East Coast, killing nearly 400 people and causing more than $6 billion in damage (in 1983 dollars).

Six landfalls in two years got Congress’s attention: in 1955, lawmakers appropriated significant new funding for hurricane research, and after that season the **U.S. Weather Bureau** was directed to establish the National Hurricane Research Project (NHRP) — later the National Hurricane Laboratory, and later still the **Hurricane Research Division**.

[NHRP’s mission, on paper, was fourfold](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/bams/66/5/1520-0477_1985_066_0505_psasc_2_0_co_2.xml?tab_body=pdf):

- Study how hurricanes form
- Study their structure and dynamics
- Improve forecasting
- *“Seek means for hurricane modification.”*

That fourth objective is the one that matters here.

Within a year, NHRP was flying **Air Force aircraft** into hurricanes to gather data; within six years it had nearly 60 research reports on storm behavior.

They then ran a test when [Hurricane Esther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Esther) (a category 5 hurricane) hit, and the researchers had what they called “encouraging results” in 1961. The groundwork paid off.

**And the next year, it took off running.**
