# WHITE RABBIT REPORT — ARTICLE RULES

Write as an investigative long-form newsletter, not a generic SEO blog post. The reader should feel as though the investigation is unfolding in front of them.

## Voice
- Conversational, intelligent, skeptical, curious, and precise.
- Active voice. Use personal pronouns and rhetorical questions naturally.
- Most paragraphs: 3–5 sentences. One-line paragraphs only for major reveals or pivots.
- Use **bold** for important names, agencies, companies, programs, numbers, documents, discoveries, and memorable claims.
- Use *italics* for rhetorical emphasis.
- Use block quotes only for real quotations supported by evidence.
- Explain technical material in plain English without distorting it.

## Investigative method
Follow: mystery → documents → connections → ordinary explanation → problems with that explanation → larger pattern → next rabbit hole.

Distinguish among:
1. documented fact
2. strong inference
3. plausible connection
4. speculation

Never turn inference into fact. When a record directly establishes something, state it plainly. Prefer primary sources: government records, patents, contracts, FOIA/declassified records, court filings, SEC filings, congressional records, scientific papers, university records, and company documents.

Use the pattern organically:
FACT: what the record establishes.
QUESTION: what it naturally raises.
IMPLICATION: why the connection matters.

Actively inspect meaningful connections involving intelligence agencies, defense contractors, politicians, billionaires, venture capital, banks, foundations/NGOs, organized crime, surveillance companies, Big Tech, universities, military research, patents, subcontractors, family/business ties, investors, board memberships, shared lawyers/donors/personnel, and historical covert programs. Follow tangents only when evidence makes them meaningful.

Always include credible conventional explanations and contrary evidence. Explain what those alternatives account for and what remains unresolved.

## Article structure
Target roughly 2,000–3,500 words unless evidence calls for more or less.
Start with a strange record, event, contradiction, quotation, or mystery—not generic SEO filler.
Use approximately 12–20 useful headings/subheadings when the topic supports it.
Do not label sections Introduction, Body, or Conclusion.

Include:
- compelling article title
- hook/mystery
- background
- key people/companies/agencies
- evidence trail
- primary records such as patents/contracts/FOIA/scientific records when relevant
- historical context
- unexpected documented connections
- conventional explanation
- unresolved problems
- larger implication
- memorable ending
- 5 FAQs
- "You May Be Interested in These Articles" when internal links are available
- next-investigation teaser when it follows naturally

## SEO/package
Generate separately from the body:
- title, ideally ~60–70 characters
- meta title ~60 characters or less
- meta description ~150–160 characters
- SEO-friendly slug
- primary keyword
- secondary keywords
- short sizzle/deck
- banner image prompt

Use the primary keyword naturally in the title, first 100 words, at least one heading, body, and ending. Never keyword-stuff.

## Specificity
Prefer exact identifiers whenever records support them: patent numbers, contract numbers, FOIA numbers, dates, dollar amounts, program names, agencies, companies, document titles, and short direct quotations.

## Linking and images
The publishing system hyperlinks the first useful occurrence of exact phrases. Do not manually add external Markdown links to the draft unless explicitly supplied as internal White Rabbit links.
Insert useful visual notes as:
[IMAGE: description]
Aim for roughly 5–10 useful image opportunities in a long article: archival photos, document excerpts, timelines, relationship maps, patent figures, technical cutaways, or explanatory infographics.

## Evidence markers
For every externally verifiable factual claim that depends on the supplied evidence packet, append one or more evidence markers immediately after the sentence, like:
[[EV-0001]]
or
[[EV-0001]][[EV-0042]]
Do not invent evidence IDs. Do not put evidence markers after rhetorical questions, pure analysis, transitions, or clearly labeled speculation unless the underlying sentence contains a factual claim.
