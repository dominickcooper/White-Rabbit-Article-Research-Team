# White Rabbit voice canon

These are operational defaults, not phrases to imitate mechanically.

1. Keep the narrator visibly present as the investigator who finds, tests and connects records.
2. Let documents arrive as discoveries: document → realization → next document → connection → larger realization → payoff. Do not default to claim → qualification → caveat → conclusion.
3. Make the reader feel that they are following the investigation with the author.
4. Natural turns such as “And then I found this,” “Wait,” “Now look who shows up,” “Okay. Maybe. Except...” and “So...that's interesting” illustrate cadence; never repeat them as a formula.
5. Explain intelligence, finance, law, science and bureaucracy in plain English before interpreting them.
6. Most paragraphs should contain roughly three to five sentences. Use one-line paragraphs selectively as punctuation.
7. Let facts create questions, and questions expose implications. Rhetorical questions are welcome when earned.
8. Once converging evidence supports a strong inference, state it clearly. Do not keep apologizing for reaching it.
9. Attribution often supplies the qualification: “DiGennaro told Valentine” ordinarily needs no reflexive “no primary document confirms this” sentence. Add a caveat only when uncertainty or contradiction materially changes interpretation.
10. Write an investigation, not an intelligence white paper or inspector-general report.
11. Prefer people, documents, events, places and movement over abstract institutional summaries.
12. Show important connections before summarizing them. A strong movement often follows FACT → QUESTION → IMPLICATION without labeling those steps.
13. Open sections with a receipt, person, event, contradiction or discovery. End them by changing the reader's question.
14. Conclusions deliver the larger implication or next rabbit hole; they do not merely recap.
15. Published White Rabbit articles are voice canon as well as project canon. Study their
    rhythm, short reactions, questions, humor, sarcasm, theories and rabbit-hole turns;
    never mechanically copy a phrase bank.
16. Accepted testimony may become a downstream premise after useful first attribution.
    Preserve its source type internally without repeatedly retrying credibility in prose.
17. Personal theory is allowed. “My theory,” “I think,” and “From where I'm sitting” can
    mark inference more naturally than sterile institutional hedging.
18. Publication movement is FACT → QUESTION → IMPLICATION → NEXT RECEIPT. Put a genuinely
    story-changing boundary once and keep moving.
