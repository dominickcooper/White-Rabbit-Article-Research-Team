# ZR/RIFLE: The Florida Bill and the Kennedy Assassination Trail

In April 1963, **William King Harvey** ran up a small collection of expenses in South Florida: hotel rooms, long-distance calls, another man's lodging and **$75 for a boat chartered at Islamorada**. He charged them to **QJWIN/ZR/RIFLE**, an account associated with a European criminal recruiter and the CIA's assassination capability. The expense period ran from April 13 to April 21. Whoever was doing the bookkeeping had left us something considerably more interesting than a business trip.

We know about the package through [Mason Cargill's May 30, 1975 review of the project files](https://www.archives.gov/files/research/jfk/releases/2023/157-10003-10490.pdf). Cargill described the receipts, believed QJWIN himself wasn't involved, and suggested asking Harvey to explain the spending. The purpose of the boat trip was missing. So was a satisfactory explanation of the other man's name.

[IMAGE: Crop of Cargill's 1975 file review describing the April 13–21, 1963 hotel, telephone and Islamorada boat expenses; identify this as the review, not the original vouchers | ALT: File-review paragraph listing Florida expenses charged to QJWIN/ZR/RIFLE, including a $75 operational boat charter.]

Now put that beside **E. Howard Hunt's account of a 1963 meeting in a Miami safe house**. In Chapter Eight of *Bond of Secrecy*, his son Saint John recounts Hunt saying that **David Morales** and **Frank Sturgis** approached him about killing President Kennedy. Morales said Harvey had selected him for an off-the-board assignment. In Morales's reported understanding, the authority ran upward through **Cord Meyer** to **Lyndon Johnson**; Meyer was working with **David Atlee Phillips** and **Antonio Veciana**.

A second account describes how the personnel might have been assembled. In *ZR Rifle: The Plot to Kill Kennedy and Castro*, **Claudia Furiati** says Phillips took over ZR/RIFLE after Harvey's removal from the anti-Cuba center. Her interview with Cuban intelligence official **Fabian Escalante** goes further: five sectors of the anti-Castro and criminal world began fusing in late 1962 into a reconstituted Operation 40.

The bill doesn't identify Hunt's meeting, whose exact date remains unspecified. What it gives us is a reason to follow Harvey through the year those accounts describe. Who could recruit the men, arrange their travel, pay their expenses and keep their assignments out of ordinary channels? Before we get back to the boat, we need to meet the people behind its account.

## HARVEY COULD HIRE MORE THAN ONE MAN

Harvey's authority came from men near the top of the CIA. **Richard Bissell**, the Agency's deputy director for plans, assigned him to develop an executive-action capability in early 1961. The [Church Committee's investigation](https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/sites-default-files-94465.pdf) later reconstructed what the euphemism included: assassination. The objective was to have people and methods available when a target was supplied.

On **February 19, 1962**, **Richard Helms** authorized Harvey to retain QJWIN and “such other principal agents and sub-agents as may be required.” QJWIN's salary was **$7,200 a year**, with another $7,500 authorized for expenses through the end of 1962. Further funds could be provided if needed. Harvey could account for spending by general category and his own certification.

Then Helms dispensed with a layer of paperwork: **“This memorandum is to be considered in lieu of project.”** Travel, operational expenses and the other costs of the activity were covered under that authority. The handling instruction was EYES ONLY. A remarkably compact administrative arrangement for a business with such an unpleasant product.

Harvey later denied recruiting an agent specifically for assassination or preparing a target list. The committee nevertheless identified a developed standby capability, while the project papers demanded nonattribution and an operator capable of ruthless work. The authorization also allowed Harvey to reach beyond his principal agent. To understand the sort of access he was buying, follow QJWIN.

## THE RECRUITER WHO WASN'T TOLD THE JOB

QJWIN was **José Marie André Mankel**, a man with European criminal contacts. His work included finding burglars and safecrackers for the CIA. Candidates could be approached with a commercial story about stealing business documents rather than told who really wanted them. It was a recruitment system built around people whose useful skills already required discretion.

The details in Cargill's review are wonderfully unromantic. Two prospective recruits discussed the practicalities of opening a safe: equipment, a lookout, or bringing enough men during working hours to force somebody in the office to open it. Mankel was also considered for art-dealer cover in Germany. The paintings would supply a respectable explanation for the man doing the introductions.

That burglary work was real. It provided contacts, cover and experience arranging jobs outside the law, all within the larger story of Harvey's executive-action capability. But the file takes Mankel somewhere else before settling into those European recruiting assignments. In late 1960, **Justin O'Donnell** recruited him for a rapid trip to **Leopoldville, Congo**.

A **November 2, 1960 dispatch** warned of a “large element of personal risk.” The true, specific requirements were being withheld from Mankel. Later records treated the one-shot assignment as completed, yet the review still couldn't explain what he had originally been sent to do. Even the recruit had entered the arrangement without the whole job description.

While in Leopoldville, Mankel encountered another CIA asset, **WIROGUE**, who was staying at the same hotel. WIROGUE's preparation included surveillance, interception and demolition. According to Mankel's report, WIROGUE offered him **$300 a month** to join an intelligence network and an **“execution squad,”** then added the prospect of bonuses for special jobs. Mankel refused.

There are two assignments here, and the refusal belongs to the second one. It tells us Mankel declined WIROGUE's offer; it leaves O'Donnell's original concealed task unanswered. That question sits inside a country where the CIA was separately plotting against **Patrice Lumumba**. Church investigators couldn't substantiate a QJWIN or WIROGUE role in the assassination plan or attempt, and a later official account denied their involvement in Lumumba's arrest or death.

I keep coming back to the withheld instructions. They show how a man could be recruited, sent abroad and paid for a sensitive assignment while only a few people held the full explanation. That is a useful way to understand the tasking described in the Kennedy accounts. And Harvey's next major assignment brought him into a criminal channel that already had a named president as its target.

## ROSELLI BROUGHT ACCESS

**John Roselli** gave the CIA access to a different underworld. The Castro assassination channel had developed through **Robert Maheu**, Roselli and Mafia figures including **Sam Giancana** and **Santos Trafficante**. Harvey inherited that channel after Bissell's late-1961 assignment. During the 1962 phase, poison pills and arms figured in the operations against Castro.

Harvey also ran **Task Force W**, the CIA component of **Operation MONGOOSE**, the campaign against Cuba. Here the job expanded from maintaining a capability to supporting operations in a live theater. The people who could arrange contacts, move supplies or reach an intermediary on the island had immediate uses. Roselli's value lay in opening those doors.

Furiati's interview with Escalante supplies another name at the junction: **Tony Varona**, whom Escalante identifies as “Mr X,” a Castro-operation intermediary in the CIA, Mafia and Cuban exile field. Somebody had to carry the plan beyond a meeting among American officers and gangsters and toward people with access in Cuba. Poison pills were of limited use to anybody while they remained on the wrong side of the Florida Straits.

The senior CIA accounts disagree about where ZR/RIFLE ended and the Roselli operation began. Bissell told investigators that the activities had folded together and become one. Helms said their lines never crossed. Meanwhile, **Roselli's alias appeared inside the ZR/RIFLE project file**.

Whatever Bissell and Helms called the arrangement, Harvey had the authority and the Roselli contact, and the files recorded both. Follow Roselli and the supposed boundary leads straight into the Castro plots. Furiati's Kennedy argument picks up those people as the anti-Castro apparatus begins to change.

## FIVE SECTORS LOOKING FOR WORK

Furiati places Harvey's removal from the center of anti-Cuba operations in **January 1963**. She then assigns the ZR/RIFLE role to Phillips, describing him as the man able to reunite displaced commando groups through his long-standing relationships. In her account, Phillips knew whom to call when the personnel needed gathering again.

Escalante's explanation, on pages 147–148, describes the ingredients of a new Operation 40. He says the fusion began at the end of 1962, drawing on five sectors:

- **The Bay of Pigs brigade's secret police**, supplying the counterintelligence element.
- **Nino Diaz's detachment**, trained at Pontchartrain.
- **Special-missions personnel trained in Panama**.
- **Exile organizations**, including MIRR, Alpha 66, DRE and the L Commandos.
- **ZR/RIFLE contractors with Mafia connections**, linking Roselli, Trafficante, Marcello and Giancana.

These groups supplied different kinds of access and experience. Escalante names Cuban figures including **Joaquin Sanjenis, Felix Rodriguez, Manuel Artime, Orlando Bosch and Luis Posada**, alongside Americans including Phillips, Hunt, Sturgis and **Gerry Hemmings**. The important feature of his reconstruction is the assembly: men from several existing organizations being brought into a new working arrangement.

Readers of our [Operation 40 investigation](https://thewhiterabbitreport.substack.com/p/operation-gladio-in-america-watergate-operation-40-dea-syndicate) will recognize the personnel world. Here Escalante is making an additional claim about what happened within it. His account gives those recurring names proposed jobs and a period of reorganization. The five sectors are his reconstruction, not a recovered CIA staffing chart.

[IMAGE: Five-sector diagram explicitly labeled “Escalante's reconstruction, as told to Furiati”; distinguish the source's alleged fusion from documented membership | ALT: Five labeled personnel pools converging on Escalante's proposed reconstituted Operation 40.]

Escalante also describes **spring-1963 planning in New Orleans**, naming Roselli, former CIA deputy **Charles Cabell**, Sturgis and Hemmings. He describes two groups controlled by **Jack Ruby** and Sturgis. For this part he expressly draws on *Double Cross*, by Sam and Chuck Giancana. We are following the book through Escalante's interpretation, rather than hearing a second independent witness to that planning.

Furiati assigns Phillips an organizing role, Trafficante a bridge to the Mafia and **Helms** overarching responsibility. Hunt's account puts **Johnson and Meyer** above the chain he describes. Those accounts leave different people at the top, and Harvey's continuing Florida spending complicates any simple handover to Phillips. The files make it worth asking what might have changed hands—personnel, particular tasks, access to contacts—while Harvey still handled an account.

## WHAT HUNT SAID HE WAS ASKED TO DO

Hunt's story becomes more specific in **Eric Hamburg's afterword** to *Bond of Secrecy*. Hamburg reproduces a handwritten memo attributed to Hunt that dates Johnson's recruitment of Meyer to 1962. In its 1963 sequence, Meyer discusses the plot with Phillips, Phillips brings in Harvey and Veciana, and Veciana meets Sturgis and enlists Morales for an intended assassination in Miami. The memo alleges Johnson changed the itinerary to Dallas.

An intended Miami operation would put the alleged planning in the same city where Harvey was incurring expenses that spring. The meeting's date remains missing, so the two events cannot yet be joined. Hunt's named intermediaries give us people whose movements and communications could answer that question.

A second passage reproduced by Hamburg supplies the job Hunt was supposedly offered. Sturgis brings Morales to meet Hunt; after Morales leaves, Sturgis explains that the “Big Event” is killing Kennedy. When Hunt asks why they need him, Sturgis says he could help with the cover-up. Hunt refuses because he wants no part of anything involving Harvey.

The Chapter Eight narrative gives the scene differently, presenting the identification of Kennedy as the target with Morales still there. It also has Hunt claiming he hadn't previously met Morales, while Hamburg's afterword places the two men in earlier Guatemala operations. Was Sturgis introducing a stranger, or bringing together men who already knew one another? Hunt's original papers and the interviews could help untangle those very different descriptions of the meeting.

Saint John's disclosure history is equally specific. He visited his ill father in **December 2003** while pursuing a publishing and film project; his father linked cooperation to financial prospects and sought a **$150,000 advance**. Saint John says Hunt prepared coded names, a diagram and a timeline. During **April 2005 interviews with Hamburg**, however, the son says his father denied what he had previously told him.

Saint John then describes Hamburg changing tactics, asking about the individual men while Hunt avoided claiming firsthand knowledge. The son believed those answers substantiated the earlier disclosure. We have the denial and the son's interpretation of what followed, rather than a single tidy confession. And Hamburg's afterword also quotes Furiati and other writers, so parts of the two books lead back to the same sources.

What makes the cover-up allegation worth pursuing is the function it assigns Hunt. The proposed operation required more than the people who would fire weapons. Somebody also had to manage what other people would be told.

## BISHOP, OSWALD AND THE STORY ABOUT CUBA

**Antonio Veciana**, the founder of Alpha 66, described an intelligence handler he knew as **Maurice Bishop**. Furiati identifies Bishop with Phillips, drawing on the Cuban investigation and the work of investigator **Gaeton Fonzi**. Her account places Veciana at a **September 1963 meeting in Dallas** where Bishop was with **Lee Harvey Oswald**, before Oswald's Mexico trip. Veciana said he did not speak to Oswald, who left shortly after he arrived.

The later encounter arranged by House assassination investigators didn't produce a neat identification: Furiati reports that Phillips and Veciana did not recognize one another. Her identification of Bishop therefore remains disputed. Veciana's account also describes a request from his handler that takes us beyond the question of the man's name and into the proposed use of a Cuban embassy official.

The official was **Guillermo Ruiz**, described as the husband of Veciana's cousin and stationed in Mexico. According to the account Furiati relays, Bishop wanted Veciana to approach Ruiz with a large payment to defect and support a story linking Oswald to Cuba. In Escalante's interview, the request comes several days after the murders of Kennedy and Oswald. Escalante then reasons backward: he interprets the earlier Dallas encounter as deliberate preparation for an operation to blame Cuba.

That last step is Escalante's inference. The reported recruitment pitch is the concrete mechanism underneath it: obtain an apparently authoritative Cuban source to give Oswald's story a particular meaning. In our [Russian Dossier investigation](https://thewhiterabbitreport.substack.com/p/russian-dossier-jfk-assassination-secrets), the patsy question was already central. This account adds a proposed means of supplying the political explanation for the murder.

[IMAGE: Attributed chronology separating Veciana's reported September Dallas encounter, the post-assassination Ruiz pitch and Escalante's inference of advance planning | ALT: Three-part timeline distinguishing reported events from Escalante's interpretation of their relationship.]

Furiati also relays **Marita Lorenz's** account of a September gathering at Bosch's Miami home and a **November 15 trip to Dallas in two cars**. Lorenz named Sturgis, Bosch, Diaz Lanz, Oswald, Hemmings and the Novo Sampol brothers; she described weapons in hotel rooms, a visit by Ruby and her own return to Miami on November 19 or 20. Furiati records that the committee judged her statements inconsistent. That alleged itinerary remains a separate, contested branch with dates and passengers to examine.

I would expect an undertaking like the one these accounts describe to leave traces in travel, contacts and expenses, even if its purpose stayed out of the paperwork. Harvey had something to say about that last part. A memorandum from the year before Kennedy's murder shows him intervening in what the CIA would put on the page.

## HARVEY EDITS THE PAPER TRAIL

On **August 14, 1962**, Harvey objected to assassination language in a written MONGOOSE memorandum. He wanted **“liquidation of leaders”** removed and said the CIA would not discuss assassination in open meetings. The memorandum is preserved in the State Department's *Foreign Relations of the United States* microfiche supplement, document 290. His concern about what would be recorded preceded Kennedy's murder by more than a year.

It is an unusually helpful instruction for anyone trying to understand these files. Harvey had authority to certify sensitive spending, contacts capable of arranging criminal work and a stated preference for keeping assassination out of the written discussion. A later investigator could find the payment mechanism and still struggle to identify the assignment. The paperwork was being managed by somebody who had thought about that problem from the other side.

Harvey's instruction explains a practice, without identifying a hidden Kennedy assignment. It also draws my attention back to the records his operation still produced. Hotel bills, phone charges and aliases might preserve relationships that an operational summary leaves out. Those were exactly the things Cargill found in the Florida package.

## THE OTHER MAN ON THE BILL

Cargill recorded the name as **John A. Wallston**, with an address of **56510 Wilshire Boulevard, Los Angeles**. Harvey had paid for his lodging on April 17, 18 and 19, 1963. The address looks malformed, and the surname presents an immediate problem. Elsewhere in the project files, however, Cargill found a personal envelope containing a note identifying **John Roselli as John Ralston**.

Roselli is therefore a plausible identity for the man on the bill. The spelling and address still need resolving, but the connection has more behind it than two vaguely similar names. Harvey already dealt with Roselli in the Castro operation, and the Ralston alias was recorded inside the same project files used for the expense inquiry.

Read the package again with Roselli in mind: Miami and Perine hotels, long-distance calls, another man's room and a boat chartered at Islamorada for operational purposes. A Cuba assignment is a substantial explanation, and Cargill himself suggested it. The calls' destinations, the other man's identity and the boat's job remain unanswered. They are also the details that could tell us which contacts Harvey was using during the year described by Hunt and Furiati.

I would like to know who answered those calls. An identified number or a charter operator could lead to a person, an itinerary or an assignment instead of another argument about what ZR/RIFLE meant. The original vouchers, hotel folios and Harvey's calendar are worth looking for precisely because their answers could separate the operations that later accounts have joined together.

## THE EXCEPTION HARVEY LEFT BEHIND

Two months after the Florida expenses, Harvey wrote a memorandum that should have ended the trail. On **June 27, 1963**, he described the ZR/RIFLE aspects of the operation as terminated, apart from minor accounting and **one precautionary “lifeline.”** The [wording reproduced in the file review](https://www.archives.gov/files/research/jfk/releases/2023/157-10003-10490.pdf) also names a man fully familiar with those aspects: **Fletcher M. Knight**. Harvey referred to an oral briefing the previous day.

The project could be closed while an exception remained, and somebody knew what the exception meant. Was it a contact, a communications arrangement, an emergency route or access to somebody whose services might still be needed? The memorandum doesn't identify it. It does tell us that the explanation was known inside the organization.

QJWIN's CIA relationship separately continued into **February 1964**. Cover arrangements and termination costs can outlast operational work, so his later retention does not identify him as the lifeline. These are two trails to follow: Knight's knowledge of the exception, and the records explaining Mankel's continued relationship.

Our [TWO-FOLD investigation](https://thewhiterabbitreport.substack.com/p/usaid-and-the-cia-series-part-5-operation-two-fold) encountered a comparable problem with the word *terminated*. An end date could leave particular functions selected for preservation, while people and relationships required their own accounting. Here Harvey put the exception into the closing memorandum himself. We don't have to imagine that he reserved something; we have to find out what he reserved.

## WHAT THE SEVENTY-FIVE DOLLARS OPENS

The seventy-five dollars no longer looks like an isolated curiosity. It came out of an account Harvey could use to support recruited agents, inside files that also recorded his access to Roselli. He knew how to keep sensitive work out of ordinary written discussion. When he closed the ZR/RIFLE aspects of the operation, he expressly left something available.

Hunt describes recruitment and a requested cover-up role. Furiati and Escalante describe a reassembly of existing personnel, with Phillips organizing and criminal intermediaries connecting the parts. Veciana's account introduces a proposed source for a blame-Cuba story. The remaining work is to follow the people and functions between those accounts and the records of what they did.

My reading is that **the durable resource was access**: an officer who could authorize a job, an intermediary who could find the right person, and arrangements for supporting work that had to remain unattributable. Such a capability needn't disappear when a particular file closes. Harvey's exception makes that a question about an actual retained arrangement.

The April bill asks who was there and what they were doing. An original hotel folio, a telephone destination or the boat's charter record could attach names and an assignment to that spending. The June memorandum points toward Knight's files and the briefing in which Harvey explained what would remain. Those are places to look for the particular contacts and support arrangements described in the Kennedy accounts.

Harvey left us the expense claim. He also named somebody who understood the exception. **What, exactly, did Harvey keep open—and what did he tell Fletcher Knight?**

## FAQ

### What was Project ZR/RIFLE?

ZR/RIFLE was a CIA executive-action capability associated with William Harvey. Its files include real criminal recruitment and burglary work alongside assassination planning, tightly restricted knowledge and special financial authority. Harvey could retain QJWIN and additional agents or subagents.

### What did Hunt allege about Kennedy's assassination?

Through his son's account and a memo reproduced by Eric Hamburg, Hunt described a Miami solicitation involving Morales and Sturgis, Harvey's involvement, and claimed authority through Meyer and Johnson. The reproduced memo adds a proposed Miami-to-Dallas shift and a requested cover-up role. The accounts differ, and Saint John also reports a later denial.

### Did QJWIN's refusal settle his Congo assignment?

No. QJWIN refused WIROGUE's reported execution-squad offer after arriving in Congo. His original high-risk assignment had been arranged separately, with its specific requirements withheld. Investigators did not substantiate his participation in the Lumumba plot; the original task remains unresolved.

### Was John A. Wallston actually John Roselli?

Roselli is a plausible identification because his Ralston alias appears in the project files and his operational relationship with Harvey is documented. The receipt name and malformed address still need resolving. The Florida expenses do not identify a Dallas mission.

### What was Harvey's precautionary lifeline?

The June 27, 1963 memorandum leaves it unnamed while identifying Fletcher Knight as fully familiar with the ZR/RIFLE aspects. It might have preserved a contact or support arrangement, but the referent remains unknown. QJWIN's continued CIA relationship is a separate fact.
