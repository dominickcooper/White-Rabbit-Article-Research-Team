# Project ZR/RIFLE: The CIA's Assassination Network

In April 1963, **William King Harvey** ran up a small collection of expenses in South Florida: hotel rooms, long-distance calls, another man's lodging and **$75 for a boat chartered at Islamorada**. He charged them to **QJWIN/ZR/RIFLE**, an account associated with a European criminal recruiter and the CIA's assassination capability. The expense period ran from April 13 to April 21. Whoever was doing the bookkeeping had left us something considerably more interesting than a business trip.

We know about the package through [Mason Cargill's May 30, 1975 review of the project files](https://www.archives.gov/files/research/jfk/releases/2023/157-10003-10490.pdf). Cargill described the receipts, believed QJWIN himself wasn't involved, and suggested asking Harvey to explain the spending. The purpose of the boat trip was missing. So was a satisfactory explanation of the other man's name.

[IMAGE: Crop of Cargill's 1975 file review describing the April 13–21, 1963 hotel, telephone and Islamorada boat expenses; identify this as the review, not the original vouchers | ALT: File-review paragraph listing Florida expenses charged to QJWIN/ZR/RIFLE, including a $75 operational boat charter.]

Now put that beside **E. Howard Hunt's account of a 1963 meeting in a Miami safe house**. In Chapter Eight of *Bond of Secrecy*, his son Saint John recounts Hunt saying that **David Morales** and **Frank Sturgis** approached him about killing President Kennedy. Morales said Harvey had selected him for an off-the-board assignment. In Morales's reported understanding, the authority ran upward through **Cord Meyer** to **Lyndon Johnson**; Meyer was working with **David Atlee Phillips** and **Antonio Veciana**.

A second account describes how the personnel might have been assembled. In *ZR Rifle: The Plot to Kill Kennedy and Castro*, **Claudia Furiati** says Phillips took over ZR/RIFLE after Harvey's removal from the anti-Cuba center. Her interview with Cuban intelligence official **Fabian Escalante** goes further: five sectors of the anti-Castro and criminal world began fusing in late 1962 into a reconstituted Operation 40.

The allegation is that men and resources assembled for foreign operations were turned against Kennedy. Hunt's meeting has no precise date, and the Florida bill names no target. But Harvey appears in both places: paying for an unexplained operation in the files, selecting a participant in the story Morales reportedly told Hunt. **What kind of machinery was available to him?**

## HARVEY COULD HIRE MORE THAN ONE MAN

Harvey's authority came from men near the top of the CIA. **Richard Bissell**, the Agency's deputy director for plans, assigned him to develop an executive-action capability in early 1961. The [Church Committee's investigation](https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/sites-default-files-94465.pdf) later reconstructed what the euphemism included: assassination. The objective was to have people and methods available when a target was supplied.

On **February 19, 1962**, **Richard Helms** authorized Harvey to retain QJWIN and “such other principal agents and sub-agents as may be required.” QJWIN's salary was **$7,200 a year**, with another $7,500 authorized for expenses through the end of 1962. Further funds could be provided if needed. Harvey could account for spending by general category and his own certification.

Then Helms dispensed with a layer of paperwork: **“This memorandum is to be considered in lieu of project.”** Travel, operational expenses and the other costs of the activity were covered under that authority. The handling instruction was EYES ONLY. Harvey could hire the people, authorize the trip and certify the spending; the explanation could stay within a very small circle.

Harvey later denied recruiting an agent specifically for assassination or preparing a target list. The committee nevertheless identified a developed standby capability, while the project papers demanded nonattribution and an operator capable of ruthless work. That combination—people available, authority to spend, knowledge kept narrow—is the machinery behind the cryptonym. QJWIN's career shows how a man could enter it without being told the whole job.

## THE RECRUITER WHO WASN'T TOLD THE JOB

QJWIN was **José Marie André Mankel**, a man with European criminal contacts. His work included finding burglars and safecrackers for the CIA. Candidates could be approached with a commercial story about stealing business documents rather than told who really wanted them. It was a recruitment system built around people whose useful skills already required discretion.

Two prospective recruits in Cargill's review discussed the practicalities of opening a safe: equipment, a lookout, or bringing enough men during working hours to force somebody in the office to open it. Apparently, waiting until the office closed was the inconvenient option. Mankel was also considered for art-dealer cover in Germany. The paintings would supply a respectable explanation for the man doing the introductions.

The burglary work gave Mankel a reason to cultivate criminals and a commercial story to tell them. His earlier assignment was much less clearly explained, even to Mankel himself. In late 1960, **Justin O'Donnell** recruited him for a rapid trip to **Leopoldville, Congo**.

A **November 2, 1960 dispatch** warned of a “large element of personal risk.” The true, specific requirements were being withheld from Mankel. Later records treated the one-shot assignment as completed, yet the review still couldn't explain what he had originally been sent to do. Even the recruit had entered the arrangement without the whole job description.

While in Leopoldville, Mankel encountered another CIA asset, **WIROGUE**, who was staying at the same hotel. WIROGUE's preparation included surveillance, interception and demolition. According to Mankel's report, WIROGUE offered him **$300 a month** to join an intelligence network and an **“execution squad,”** then added the prospect of bonuses for special jobs. Mankel refused.

Mankel had already been sent to Congo on O'Donnell's concealed assignment when WIROGUE tried to recruit him. Refusing the execution-squad offer leaves that original job unexplained. The CIA was separately plotting against **Patrice Lumumba**, but Church investigators couldn't substantiate a QJWIN or WIROGUE role in the assassination plan or attempt; a later official account denied their involvement in Lumumba's arrest or death. None of those accounts supplies the instructions O'Donnell withheld.

Mankel's files describe the recruitment, the travel and a completed assignment while leaving its purpose obscure. Somebody could know the work was finished without the later reviewer being able to say what it was. With Harvey's next major assignment, the target was explicit: **Fidel Castro**. Harvey inherited a channel through which the CIA was already trying to reach him.

## ROSELLI BROUGHT ACCESS

**John Roselli** gave the CIA access to a different underworld. The Castro assassination channel had developed through **Robert Maheu**, Roselli and Mafia figures including **Sam Giancana** and **Santos Trafficante**. Harvey inherited that channel after Bissell's late-1961 assignment. During the 1962 phase, poison pills and arms figured in the operations against Castro.

Harvey also ran **Task Force W**, the CIA component of **Operation MONGOOSE**, the campaign against Cuba. He now had an anti-Castro assignment and a criminal intermediary who could help reach people outside normal Agency channels. The recruitment problem had become a delivery problem: get the means of killing Castro to someone who could get close enough to use them.

Furiati's interview with Escalante supplies another name at the junction: **Tony Varona**, whom Escalante identifies as “Mr X,” a Castro-operation intermediary in the CIA, Mafia and Cuban exile field. Somebody had to carry the plan beyond a meeting among American officers and gangsters and toward people with access in Cuba. Poison pills were of limited use to anybody while they remained on the wrong side of the Florida Straits.

The senior CIA accounts disagree about where ZR/RIFLE ended and the Roselli operation began. Bissell told investigators that the activities had folded together and become one. Helms said their lines never crossed. Meanwhile, **Roselli's alias appeared inside the ZR/RIFLE project file**.

A useful administrative separation, if Helms was right. The same officer still had the executive-action authority and the Roselli contact, and Roselli's alias had found its way into the project file. Furiati's Kennedy allegation concerns the redeployment of this sort of access: officers who knew the intermediaries, intermediaries who knew the men, and men already accustomed to covert work.

## FIVE SECTORS LOOKING FOR WORK

Furiati places Harvey's removal from the center of anti-Cuba operations in **January 1963**. She then assigns the ZR/RIFLE role to Phillips, describing him as the man able to reunite displaced commando groups through his long-standing relationships. In her account, Phillips knew whom to call when the personnel needed gathering again.

Escalante's explanation, on pages 147–148, describes the ingredients of a new Operation 40. He says the fusion began at the end of 1962, drawing on five sectors:

- **The Bay of Pigs brigade's secret police**, supplying the counterintelligence element.
- **Nino Diaz's detachment**, trained at Pontchartrain.
- **Special-missions personnel trained in Panama**.
- **Exile organizations**, including MIRR, Alpha 66, DRE and the L Commandos.
- **ZR/RIFLE contractors with Mafia connections**, linking Roselli, Trafficante, Marcello and Giancana.

Escalante's roster crosses those organizational boundaries. He names Cuban figures including **Joaquin Sanjenis, Felix Rodriguez, Manuel Artime, Orlando Bosch and Luis Posada**, alongside Americans including Phillips, Hunt, Sturgis and **Gerry Hemmings**. In his reconstruction, the organizers could draw on men with different specialties instead of building a new workforce from scratch.

The personnel will be familiar from our [Operation 40 investigation](https://thewhiterabbitreport.substack.com/p/operation-gladio-in-america-watergate-operation-40-dea-syndicate). Escalante describes their late-1962 fusion as the formation of a new working arrangement. Furiati puts Phillips in the organizing role, with Trafficante connecting the Mafia and exile worlds. Their allegation is about who could bring these people together and give their existing skills another use.

[IMAGE: Five-sector diagram explicitly labeled “Escalante's reconstruction, as told to Furiati”; distinguish the source's alleged fusion from documented membership | ALT: Five labeled personnel pools converging on Escalante's proposed reconstituted Operation 40.]

Drawing expressly on *Double Cross*, by Sam and Chuck Giancana, Escalante describes **spring-1963 planning in New Orleans** involving Roselli, former CIA deputy **Charles Cabell**, Sturgis and Hemmings. He reads that account as a plan using two groups controlled by **Jack Ruby** and Sturgis. Roselli appears in the planning group as the Mafia representative; Sturgis appears as an organizer of one of the groups. In Hunt's story, Sturgis also does the recruiting.

The accounts diverge over who was above these men. Furiati gives **Helms** overarching responsibility; Hunt's account, through Morales, points upward to **Johnson and Meyer**. And Furiati's Phillips succession leaves an awkward question in the paperwork: if he had taken over ZR/RIFLE, why was Harvey still charging Florida expenses to its account? A transfer of personnel or particular duties could leave Harvey handling money, but the scope of that supposed handover remains unresolved.

## WHAT HUNT SAID HE WAS ASKED TO DO

**Eric Hamburg's afterword** to *Bond of Secrecy* reproduces a handwritten memo attributed to Hunt that places Phillips at a recruiting junction. The memo dates Johnson's recruitment of Meyer to 1962. In its 1963 sequence, Meyer discusses the plot with Phillips, Phillips brings in Harvey and Veciana, and Veciana meets Sturgis and enlists Morales for an intended assassination in Miami. It then alleges that Johnson changed the itinerary to Dallas.

In that telling, the plan moves through introductions: Meyer to Phillips, Phillips to Harvey and Veciana, then Veciana to Sturgis and Morales. The intended target is Kennedy while the location changes from Miami to Dallas. Hunt supplies no precise date for his Miami meeting; the April expenses cannot date it for him.

Hamburg's next passage brings the recruitment to Hunt's door. Sturgis brings Morales to meet him; after Morales leaves, Sturgis explains that the “Big Event” is killing Kennedy. Hunt asks why they need him, and Sturgis says he could help with the cover-up. Hunt says he refuses because he wants no part of anything involving Harvey.

The Chapter Eight narrative has Morales still there when Kennedy is identified as the target. It also has Hunt claiming he hadn't previously met Morales, while Hamburg's afterword places the two men in earlier Guatemala operations. Was Sturgis introducing a stranger, or bringing together men who already knew one another? Hunt's versions leave different answers to a basic question about the recruitment: how much did these men already know about each other?

Saint John's disclosure history is equally specific. He visited his ill father in **December 2003** while pursuing a publishing and film project; his father linked cooperation to financial prospects and sought a **$150,000 advance**. Saint John says Hunt prepared coded names, a diagram and a timeline. During **April 2005 interviews with Hamburg**, however, the son says his father denied what he had previously told him.

Saint John describes Hamburg changing tactics, asking about the individual men while Hunt avoided claiming firsthand knowledge. The son believed the answers substantiated the earlier disclosure despite that denial. Hamburg also draws on Furiati and other writers in the afterword, so their repeated accounts are part of the same reporting trail.

Sturgis's reported request gives Hunt a job beyond the shooting: help cover it up. That concern with what other people would learn also appears in the account involving Phillips and Veciana. There, the proposed task is to attach a Cuban explanation to Oswald.

## BISHOP, OSWALD AND THE STORY ABOUT CUBA

**Antonio Veciana**, the founder of Alpha 66, described an intelligence handler he knew as **Maurice Bishop**. Furiati identifies Bishop with Phillips, drawing on the Cuban investigation and the work of investigator **Gaeton Fonzi**. Her account places Veciana at a **September 1963 meeting in Dallas** where Bishop was with **Lee Harvey Oswald**, before Oswald's Mexico trip. Veciana said he did not speak to Oswald, who left shortly after he arrived.

Furiati also reports that Phillips and Veciana did not recognize one another at a later encounter arranged by House assassination investigators, leaving the Bishop identification disputed. Veciana's account gives the handler a second role after the assassination. He wanted an introduction to a man inside the Cuban embassy.

The official was **Guillermo Ruiz**, described as the husband of Veciana's cousin and stationed in Mexico. According to the account Furiati relays, Bishop wanted Veciana to approach Ruiz with a large payment to defect and support a story linking Oswald to Cuba. In Escalante's interview, the request comes several days after the murders of Kennedy and Oswald. Escalante then reasons backward: he interprets the earlier Dallas encounter as deliberate preparation for an operation to blame Cuba.

A Cuban embassy official would give that story something an anonymous rumor lacked: a source who appeared to know. Veciana's reported family connection supplied the approach; money supplied the inducement. Our [Russian Dossier investigation](https://thewhiterabbitreport.substack.com/p/russian-dossier-jfk-assassination-secrets) put the patsy question at the center of the Kennedy case. Here the allegation includes an attempt to recruit someone who could tell the world what the patsy's supposed connection to Cuba meant.

[IMAGE: Attributed chronology separating Veciana's reported September Dallas encounter, the post-assassination Ruiz pitch and Escalante's inference of advance planning | ALT: Three-part timeline distinguishing reported events from Escalante's interpretation of their relationship.]

Furiati also relays **Marita Lorenz's** account of a September gathering at Bosch's Miami home and a **November 15 trip to Dallas in two cars**. Lorenz named Sturgis, Bosch, Diaz Lanz, Oswald, Hemmings and the Novo Sampol brothers; she described weapons in hotel rooms, a visit by Ruby and her own return to Miami on November 19 or 20. Her story puts Sturgis among the travelers and Ruby at the hotel, while Furiati records that the committee judged her statements inconsistent.

The disputed Lorenz itinerary supplies a travel story; Hunt supplies a solicitation and proposed cover-up role; Veciana supplies the handler's approach to a prospective Cuban source. The accounts intersect around people performing different jobs, although they never become one agreed command chain. Harvey, meanwhile, left his own instruction about a problem any such operation would face: what to put in writing.

## HARVEY EDITS THE PAPER TRAIL

On **August 14, 1962**, Harvey objected to assassination language in a written MONGOOSE memorandum. He wanted **“liquidation of leaders”** removed and said the CIA would not discuss assassination in open meetings. The memorandum is preserved in the State Department's *Foreign Relations of the United States* microfiche supplement, document 290. His concern about what would be recorded preceded Kennedy's murder by more than a year.

Compare that with Helms's financial authorization. Harvey could certify sensitive expenses while asking that assassination disappear from the written discussion. The CIA still had to pay people, get them from one place to another and settle their bills. A later reviewer could therefore find the cost of an operation without finding its purpose neatly stated beside it.

That is the position Cargill found himself in with the Florida package. Rooms, calls and a boat had been paid for under QJWIN/ZR/RIFLE. The officer who submitted those expenses had already shown how he wanted assassination discussed. Now look at the other man's hotel bill.

## THE OTHER MAN ON THE BILL

Cargill recorded the name as **John A. Wallston**, with an address of **56510 Wilshire Boulevard, Los Angeles**. Harvey had paid for his lodging on April 17, 18 and 19, 1963. The address looks malformed, and the surname presents an immediate problem. Elsewhere in the project files, however, Cargill found a personal envelope containing a note identifying **John Roselli as John Ralston**.

Roselli is therefore a plausible identity for the man on the bill. The spelling and address still need resolving, but the connection has more behind it than two vaguely similar names. Harvey already dealt with Roselli in the Castro operation, and the Ralston alias was recorded inside the same project files used for the expense inquiry.

Read the package again with Roselli in mind: Miami and Perine hotels, long-distance calls, another man's room and a boat chartered at Islamorada for operational purposes. A Cuba assignment is a substantial explanation, and Cargill himself suggested it. The calls' destinations, the other man's identity and the boat's job remain unanswered. They are also the details that could tell us which contacts Harvey was using during the year described by Hunt and Furiati.

If Wallston was Roselli, the bill would place Harvey's Mafia intermediary inside this particular expense trail. A telephone destination or charter record could identify further contacts and the work they supported. That would give the Kennedy allegations something more exact to meet than a shared city and a shared year. As it stands, we have an operation paid for under the account—and, two months later, Harvey's qualified termination of the ZR/RIFLE aspects.

## THE EXCEPTION HARVEY LEFT BEHIND

Two months after the Florida expenses, Harvey wrote a memorandum that should have ended the trail. On **June 27, 1963**, he described the ZR/RIFLE aspects of the operation as terminated, apart from minor accounting and **one precautionary “lifeline.”** The [wording reproduced in the file review](https://www.archives.gov/files/research/jfk/releases/2023/157-10003-10490.pdf) also names a man fully familiar with those aspects: **Fletcher M. Knight**. Harvey referred to an oral briefing the previous day.

The word *terminated* comes with an exception and the name of somebody who understood the arrangement. A contact? An emergency route? A means of reaching someone again? Harvey leaves the lifeline unnamed, but Knight and the oral briefing make clear that the description was shorthand for something understood inside the organization.

QJWIN's CIA relationship separately continued into **February 1964**. Cover arrangements and termination costs can outlast operational work; those later payments do not tell us whether Mankel was the lifeline. Knight's knowledge concerns the retained ZR/RIFLE arrangement, while Mankel's continuing relationship has its own unfinished accounting.

Our [TWO-FOLD investigation](https://thewhiterabbitreport.substack.com/p/usaid-and-the-cia-series-part-5-operation-two-fold) followed people and functions beyond a formal project ending. Harvey's memorandum gives us a reason to do the same here: he explicitly preserved an exception. His financial authority, his criminal contacts and that retained arrangement make the end of a cryptonym a very different question from the end of access to the people behind it.

## A NETWORK BUILT TO BE USED

The Florida bill sits inside a system the CIA deliberately built: **a deniable assassination capability with criminal recruitment and exceptional financial authority**. Harvey could retain agents, certify expenses and keep sensitive knowledge within a small circle. His work intersected directly with the Mafia channel against Castro. He also wanted assassination language removed from the written discussion, which helps explain how the later reviewer could encounter rooms, calls and a boat without their operational purpose.

This is the machinery beneath the Kennedy allegation. Furiati and Escalante describe existing anti-Castro personnel being assembled for another use; Hunt describes a recruitment chain, a Miami plan allegedly shifted to Dallas and a request for help covering it up. Veciana's account adds an approach to someone who could supply an Oswald/Cuba story. Their proposed operation has organizers, intermediaries, recruits and a task beyond the killing: shaping who would be blamed.

**The capability to assemble deniable work was already there.** That is the larger consequence of following Harvey from the authorization to Roselli and back to Florida. Recruiting, paying and supporting people for a lethal operation were problems the Agency had already worked on. The Kennedy accounts allege a redirection of those resources, and their named relationships make that proposition concrete enough to pursue.

I read the preserved lifeline as a warning against assuming that a closed project file closed every relationship. Its function and duration remain unknown; Knight's knowledge could help explain them. The April vouchers could identify whom Harvey was supporting before the termination. Neither record names a Kennedy mission, but both concern the practical arrangements through which the alleged network would have had to act.

At the opening, seventy-five dollars bought us a bookkeeping puzzle. It now points into an assassination apparatus whose officer, criminal contacts and methods recur in specific accounts of a plot against an American president. The unanswered question reaches well beyond the passenger on one boat or the meaning of one word in a closing memorandum: **did the people who could arrange a murder abroad turn that access toward Kennedy—and who gave them the work?**

## FAQ

### What was Project ZR/RIFLE?

ZR/RIFLE was a CIA executive-action capability associated with William Harvey. Its files include real criminal recruitment and burglary work alongside assassination planning, tightly restricted knowledge and special financial authority. Harvey could retain QJWIN and additional agents or subagents.

### What did Hunt allege about Kennedy's assassination?

Through his son's account and a memo reproduced by Eric Hamburg, Hunt described a Miami solicitation involving Morales and Sturgis, Harvey's involvement, and claimed authority through Meyer and Johnson. The reproduced memo adds a proposed Miami-to-Dallas shift and a requested cover-up role. The accounts differ, and Saint John also reports a later denial.

### Did QJWIN's refusal settle his Congo assignment?

No. QJWIN refused WIROGUE's reported execution-squad offer after arriving in Congo. His original high-risk assignment had been arranged separately, with its specific requirements withheld. Investigators did not substantiate his participation in the Lumumba plot; the original task remains unresolved.

### Was John A. Wallston actually John Roselli?

Roselli is a plausible identification because his Ralston alias appears in the project files and his operational relationship with Harvey is documented. The receipt name and malformed address still need resolving. The Florida expenses do not identify a Dallas mission.

### What was Harvey's precautionary lifeline?

The June 27, 1963 memorandum leaves it unnamed while identifying Fletcher Knight as fully familiar with the ZR/RIFLE aspects. It might have preserved a contact or support arrangement, but the referent remains unknown. QJWIN's continued CIA relationship is a separate fact.
