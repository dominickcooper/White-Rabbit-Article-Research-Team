## Narrative and author-voice choices

- The opening identifies the 1975 review as the source for the 1963 expenses; no invented
  boat scene or pretense of having the original receipts.
- Personal narration marks interpretation and curiosity: “I keep coming back to the
  withheld instructions,” “I would like to know who answered those calls,” and “My reading
  is…” No invented archival visit, interview or personal discovery history.
- The five-sector list is the single main explanatory list. It gives readers the claimed
  assembly mechanism before adding further names.
- Paragraphs generally carry several connected sentences. Short reactions remain within
  paragraphs rather than becoming a sequence of theatrical isolated lines.
- Dry humor comes from the paperwork and practical delivery problem, including the
  compact authorization and poison pills remaining on the wrong side of the Straits.
- The final question asks what Harvey retained and told Knight. It does not assume Knight
  himself ran the retained arrangement.
- The ending's inference concerns access and support, while the Kennedy links retain their
  testimonial and unresolved status.
