## Correction to the working premise

The item was **not an attachment** and was **not authored by Hill**. It was a Dropbox link whose filename resolves to:

> Marshall Van Alstyne, “Why Bitcoin Has Value,” *Communications of the ACM* 57(5), 30–32 (May 2014), DOI: 10.1145/2594288.

The original Dropbox URL is unavailable. An author-uploaded copy is indexed by ResearchGate, but automated retrieval was blocked by its security gate, so no local article PDF is represented as preserved. Metadata are confirmed by [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/273609426_Why_Bitcoin_Has_Value) and [Boston University](https://www.bu.edu/questrom/profiles/marshall-van-alstyne/).

## Paper analysis

The essay evaluates Bitcoin's economic value rather than presenting Blockstream policy. Its relevant themes are:

- utility as a medium of exchange;
- scarcity/divisibility and monetary functions;
- network adoption and transactional value;
- volatility, legitimacy, and comparison with government-backed money;
- blockchain as a distributed public transaction ledger.

It is not a central-bank plan, sovereign-currency blueprint, securities memorandum, tax opinion, or AML program. Because the full local copy could not be lawfully retrieved, this characterization is bounded to the published metadata and accessible text/abstract; no quotation beyond the email itself should enter the article without rechecking the paper.

## Causal and policy assessment

- Hill's phrase “Thought you'd be interested” does not say the link followed an island discussion. That connection is **PLAUSIBLE_CONNECTION** only.
- The email combines fundraising progress with investor reading. Routine investor cultivation is the boring and adequate explanation.
- The paper does not prefigure the later sovereign pitches in a documentable way. Any overlap—Bitcoin value, money functions, government backing—is generic economic subject matter.

## Answer

Epstein did more than passively wire money: he received founders' fundraising updates and economic reading, and he forwarded this item to another financier. But the essay was an external CACM article, not Blockstream doctrine, and no substantive reaction is visible. The safe finding is:

> **VERIFIED:** Epstein consumed and circulated economic framing supplied by Hill while considering the Blockstream investment. **NOT SUPPORTED:** that Hill/Back gave him a sovereign-currency policy program or that the paper drove his later government pitches.
