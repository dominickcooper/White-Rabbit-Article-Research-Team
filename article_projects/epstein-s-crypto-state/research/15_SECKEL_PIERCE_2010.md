# Seckel → Pierce, 2010

Research cutoff: 2026-09-23.

## Bottom line

**VERIFIED:** Al Seckel placed Brock Pierce in Epstein's in-person orbit by November 2010 and explicitly pitched Pierce's gaming/virtual-currency experience to Epstein on December 24. This is the earliest located route into a person who later became central to Epstein's crypto deal flow. It is a **virtual-goods/gaming-economy origin point**, not a verified Bitcoin origin point.

## What Pierce was doing in 2010

- Pierce had founded Internet Gaming Entertainment, a real-money market maker for MMORPG currency, accounts, and virtual goods. That business history best explains Seckel's language. It was centralized gaming commerce, not Bitcoin.
- In 2010 Pierce joined/invested around Titan Gaming, a cash- and virtual-currency tournament platform. Titan acquired Xfire in August 2010; contemporary descriptions emphasize skill-based gaming and monetization.
- Seckel did not name IGE, Titan, Xfire, Bitcoin, a blockchain, or a payment protocol. The exact company/project behind his December description therefore remains **OPEN**.

Context: [Wired's IGE history](https://www.wired.com/2008/11/ff-ige/), [contemporary Xfire/Titan report](https://www.engadget.com/2010-08-03-xfire-purchased-by-skill-based-gaming-firm-titan.html), and [EFTA02005667](https://bitcoinprotocol.org/epstein-bitcoin-emails/files/EFTA02005667.pdf).

## Questions resolved

- **What did Seckel say?** Pierce had built a large online-gaming virtual-currency/trading market; Epstein's financial methods might make it more profitable.
- **How was Pierce described?** A friend, “off-scale,” interesting, and responsible for building that market.
- **Was a company named?** No.
- **Did Seckel disclose a financial interest?** No. His profit language describes the possible collaboration, not a documented Seckel stake.
- **Did Epstein answer the pitch?** No substantive reply is visible. The quoted Epstein message concerns weekend logistics.
- **Did Pierce and Epstein later communicate directly?** Yes, by September 2011. The first located direct message refers to prior brainstorming.
- **Was there pre-June-2011 follow-up?** None located in the specified 2010-11-01 through 2011-06-30 search window.
- **Did a meeting occur?** Pierce was on Seckel's November gathering list. Attendance is likely but not independently confirmed; the direct correspondence proving discussion appears in September 2011.

## Boring explanation tested

Seckel was assembling an eclectic salon and pitching a commercially successful gaming entrepreneur to a wealthy financier. That ordinary investor-network explanation fits every located fact and does not require prior Epstein interest in Bitcoin.

## Relationship to the June 2011 outreach

It is **PLAUSIBLE_CONNECTION**, not a proved causal chain, that exposure to Pierce's older virtual-currency world helped prime Epstein to ask for Bitcoin contacts. Six months, no intermediary message, and no Bitcoin reference separate the two events. Calacanis—not Pierce—was the documented source Epstein used in June.

> **Did Brock Pierce enter Epstein's network before Epstein became explicitly interested in Bitcoin?**

**Yes.** Pierce entered the located network by November–December 2010 through gaming virtual currency. Explicit Epstein interest in Bitcoin appears on June 4, 2011. That distinction materially strengthens the article by giving it a conventional, pre-crypto-commercial gateway rather than an origins conspiracy.
