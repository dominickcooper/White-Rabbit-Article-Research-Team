# Epstein’s Crypto Network: Bitcoin, Blockstream & Sovereign Money

## THE EMAIL FROM DUBAI

On July 10, 2017, **Sultan Ahmed bin Sulayem** answered a proposal from Jeffrey Epstein. Epstein wanted Dubai to back a digital currency. Bin Sulayem’s reply, preserved in the Justice Department’s Epstein files, was blunt:

> “[I totally agree I want to do this](https://www.justice.gov/epstein/files/DataSet%2011/EFTA02393663.pdf).”

Then came the practical question: “Can Dpw start this digital currency we are 80% owned by Dubai government.”

There is no evidence that DP World started the currency, that Dubai approved one, or that a coin ever launched. But the exchange puts the **Jeffrey Epstein Bitcoin** story somewhere stranger than a venture-capital portfolio. By 2017, Epstein was talking to a government-linked businessman about state-backed digital money.

*How did he get from virtual gaming currencies to this?*

## BEFORE BITCOIN: THE VIRTUAL-CURRENCY GATEWAY

The earliest route I found begins on Christmas Eve 2010. **Al Seckel** wrote Epstein about his friend **Brock Pierce**, describing an enormous market in online-game currency and suggesting that Pierce’s system might benefit from Epstein’s financial methods. A November gathering list had already placed Pierce in Seckel’s proposed Epstein circle; the [December pitch](https://www.justice.gov/epstein/files/DataSet%209/EFTA00901408.pdf) made the commercial logic explicit.

Pierce had built **Internet Gaming Entertainment**, a marketplace for MMORPG currency, accounts, and virtual goods. He was also involved in the cash-and-virtual-currency tournament world around Titan Gaming and Xfire. This was centralized digital commerce—the sort of business chronicled in [Wired’s history of IGE](https://www.wired.com/2008/11/ff-ige/)—not a documented encounter with Bitcoin, Satoshi, or cryptographic cash.

That distinction gives us a plausible starting point. Digital value could be traded, monetized, and financed before Epstein showed a traceable interest in Bitcoin itself. Six months later, he went looking for the people behind the new thing.

## “THE BIT COIN GUYS”

On June 4, 2011, Epstein emailed tech entrepreneur Jason Calacanis: “[I would like to get in touch with the bit coin guys](https://www.justice.gov/epstein/files/DataSet%209/EFTA01866591.pdf).” Calacanis pointed him toward an interview with **Gavin Andresen** and **Amir Taaki**, two visible representatives of a project whose anonymous creator had largely disappeared from public view.

Epstein then used another access broker. On June 11, **John Brockman** sent Gavin’s details and told Epstein, “[This is the guy: Gavin Andresen](https://www.justice.gov/epstein/files/DataSet%209/EFTA00629471.pdf).” Brockman framed the proposed contact as a business conversation.

It failed. Four days later, after an assistant followed up, Gavin replied: “[No, sorry, I’m busy](https://www.justice.gov/epstein/files/DataSet%209/EFTA00432104.pdf).” That tiny email closes an important door. Epstein sought access to Bitcoin’s lead developer; the record does not show that he got it.

## HOW CLOSE DID THIS GET TO SATOSHI?

Close enough to create tempting chronology. Not close enough to create an origin story.

Satoshi’s [last publicly known email to Gavin](https://riski.wiki/wiki/User:Gavinandresen/Blog/2022-04-26_Eleven_years_ago_today%E2%80%A6) is dated April 26, 2011. Gavin told Satoshi that In-Q-Tel had invited him to a “Mobility of Money” event for the intelligence community. He later said the June 14 event took place at CIA headquarters. Epstein’s request for “the bit coin guys” came ten days before the talk; Brockman’s approach came three days before it; Gavin’s refusal came one day after.

The sequence is a timeline, not a causal chain. No document shows Epstein knew about the event when he contacted Calacanis, and no evidence links CIA or In-Q-Tel to Satoshi’s exit.

**Adam Back** is the stronger later proximity. Satoshi contacted Back before Bitcoin’s public release about Hashcash, as the [preserved pre-publication correspondence](https://github.com/lugaxker/nakamoto-archive) shows. In April 2014, travel records placed Back in a St. Thomas group with Blockstream co-founder Austin Hill; Epstein told Taaki he had “Andy Back” on his island. The overlap arrived more than five years after Bitcoin’s launch and says nothing about information moving backward through time.

**The documents do not put Epstein inside Bitcoin’s creation story. They show that once Bitcoin existed, he moved toward people unusually close to it.**

## THE 2008 COINCIDENCE

Readers will reasonably ask about the emails from October 2008. Twenty-four days before Satoshi announced the white paper, Epstein wrote Peter Mandelson about “[A new system that usess the internet to distribute value](https://www.justice.gov/epstein/files/DataSet%209/EFTA00694620.pdf).” Roughly 41 hours before the announcement, he described the “birth of a new system” and a computer-linked “Financial Organism” to a recipient whose name is redacted.

I followed that coincidence through duplicate, thread, phrase, and date searches. It did not produce a cryptographer, a copy of the white paper, a Satoshi correspondent, or an identifiable recipient with a digital-cash connection. The concrete mechanism in the October 30 email was a government crediting phone accounts—central administration, not Bitcoin’s peer-to-peer architecture.

The timing is real. The bridge is not.

## FOLLOW THE MONEY

By 2014, the records stop being merely social. Epstein invested approximately **$3 million in Coinbase** through IGO LLC during the company’s Series C period, according to DOJ asset and closing records reviewed by the [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/02/03/epstein-files-coinbase-silicon-valley/). Brock Pierce was part of the deal-flow route. The available packet does not establish which Coinbase principals knew IGO’s beneficial owner, so “Coinbase partnered with Epstein” would say more than the documents do.

The Blockstream trail is harder and cleaner. A wire record shows **Southern Financial LLC** sending “[500,001.00](https://www.justice.gov/epstein/files/DataSet%209/EFTA00080250.pdf)” to Joi Ito for a Kyara Investments III subscription to fund a Blockstream investment. Kyara’s [operating agreement](https://www.justice.gov/epstein/files/DataSet%209/EFTA00584696.pdf) lists Southern Financial with 500 of 1,000 membership interests and Ito with the other 500. Ito managed the vehicle; Epstein signed for Southern Financial.

The unequal contributions matter. Southern supplied $500,001; Ito’s listed contribution was $2,000. The equal membership count does **not** mean Epstein owned half of Blockstream. It means Southern held half of a special-purpose investment vehicle whose sole business was Blockstream equity.

Epstein’s money did not fund Bitcoin’s creation. **It financed a company building infrastructure around Bitcoin after the network already existed.** Coinbase reached the exchange layer; Blockstream reached deeper into the technical and financial infrastructure beneath it.

## WHO WAS “REID”?

One Blockstream allocation email said only “Reid.” The surrounding record strongly indicates **Reid Hoffman**: a later conflict thread explicitly copied Hoffman, Ito, Epstein, and Hill, and another message tied the same “Reid bitcoin position” to Reid’s travel. Context makes the identification a strong inference, not a signed cap table.

The distinction becomes sharper when money enters the picture. No located Hoffman message mentions Southern Financial, Kyara’s beneficial ownership, or the $500,001 wire. The record places Hoffman in the allocation conversation; it does not show that he knowingly routed Epstein’s money.

## WHAT EPSTEIN WAS READING

After the St. Thomas trip, Austin Hill emailed Epstein and copied Back. He shared a Dropbox link to “[Why Bitcoin Has Value](https://doi.org/10.1145/2594288),” a *Communications of the ACM* essay by Marshall Van Alstyne. It was not Hill’s paper, not an attachment, and not Blockstream doctrine.

Epstein forwarded Hill’s message to real-estate financier Andrew Farkas within minutes. No substantive reply from Epstein appears in the thread, and no forwarding to Treasury or a foreign government surfaced. Still, the episode adds a small but useful correction: Epstein was not only wiring money. He was receiving and circulating economic arguments about Bitcoin while evaluating the company.

## REGULATORS ENTER THE NETWORK

The regulatory trail begins with **Donald Norman**, the Bitcoin Consultancy and Intersango co-founder introduced by Amir Taaki. Norman met Epstein, discussed KYC and legal exposure, then reported “very positive and detailed feedback from the FSA in the UK.” The claim lives inside [Norman’s email thread](https://www.justice.gov/epstein/files/DataSet%209/EFTA00656005.pdf). No named FSA official or regulator-side document has been found.

The U.S. Treasury connection is direct. In August 2014, **Anne Shere Wallwork**, then a senior counselor in Treasury’s Office of Terrorist Financing and Financial Crimes, asked Joi Ito and Epstein for their thinking on virtual-currency models, risks, adoption, barriers, and MIT’s Bitcoin Project. Ito wrote that Wallwork had recently met Epstein. An October calendar record shows a scheduled call covering decentralized virtual currency, regulation, developing economies, and remittances.

The thread proves access, not policy influence. It is a different category of access from a startup pitch: a convicted sex offender and private financier had become a requested participant in a federal conversation about the structure and risks of virtual currency.

## TETHER, NOBLE AND THE MIT BRIDGE

Brock Pierce kept opening commercial doors. In October 2014, he told Epstein he wanted to get Larry Summers involved with “[Noble Markets … and Tether](https://www.justice.gov/epstein/files/DataSet%209/EFTA00664424.pdf).” Tether was barely out of the gate. The document shows Pierce trying to use Epstein’s network around the ventures; it does not show Epstein investing in Tether.

Joi Ito connected a different set of layers. MIT’s Digital Currency Initiative became an institutional home for Bitcoin Core developers including Gavin Andresen, Wladimir van der Laan, and Cory Fields. In an email to Epstein, Ito wrote, “[Used gift funds to underwrite this which allowed us to move quickly and win this round](https://bitcoinprotocol.org/epstein-bitcoin-emails/files/EFTA00680068.pdf). Thanks.”

MIT received Epstein money, and Ito associated gift funds with the rapid launch of DCI support. The available accounting does not trace particular Epstein dollars into particular developers’ pay. The accurate story is institutional: Epstein’s donor relationship sat beside MIT’s decision to support critical Bitcoin work, without evidence that he directed code or personnel.

## FROM BITCOIN TO STATE MONEY

And this is where Bitcoin stops being the whole story.

By 2014 Epstein was pitching a “[sophisticated Russian version of bitcoin](https://www.justice.gov/epstein/files/DataSet%209/EFTA00981054.pdf)” through former Norwegian prime minister **Thorbjørn Jagland**, apparently for Vladimir Putin. There is no proof Jagland forwarded the idea or that Putin saw it. But by 2016 and 2017 the same conceptual move appeared again: borrow blockchain mechanics, add sovereign backing, wrap the product in local politics or religion, and route it through someone close to power.

Epstein’s complaint about Bitcoin was revealing. A digital asset without a sovereign guarantee was not, in his framing, a “real” currency. The state should supply the trust that Bitcoin’s architecture had been designed to route around.

## RUSSIA, SAUDI ARABIA AND QATAR

Russia received the shortest pitch. In January 2014, Epstein urged Jagland to discuss a Russian Bitcoin analogue with Putin; in October 2017, he tried again: “talk to putin re digital currencies . the future.” No outcome appears.

The Saudi-linked version added religion and smart contracts. In October 2016, Epstein proposed both a physical regional “[sharia currency](https://www.justice.gov/epstein/files/DataSet%2011/EFTA02446989.pdf)” and a Bitcoin-like digital version. His claim that he had spoken to some of Bitcoin’s founders is uncorroborated. A later letter routed through **Aziza Alahmadi** appears intended for Mohammed bin Salman, but no forward or receipt proves that the crown prince received it.

Qatar produced the deepest exchange. **Fawzi Siam**, introduced through Sheikh Jabor Al Thani, asked [questions about blockchain architecture, legislation, sovereign guarantees, and Sharia review](https://www.justice.gov/epstein/files/DataSet%209/EFTA01054971.pdf). Calls and a possible scholar meeting were discussed. No final proposal, central-bank mandate, or implementation followed in the located record.

Was Epstein packaging essentially the same digital-money architecture for different governments? By this point, that becomes the strongest inference—not because one email says “strategy,” but because the components recur.

## “GENGHIS COIN”

Mongolia makes the concept plain. After Khaltmaagiin Battulga won the July 2017 runoff, intermediary **Ganbat Chuluunkhuu** relayed thanks for Epstein’s “support.” The thread never explains what that support meant, so it cannot be converted into a claim of election aid or financing.

Epstein quickly proposed a Mongolian digital currency and floated the branding “GENGHIS COIN.” His instruction was sharper than the joke: “[not bit coin, you need your own](https://www.justice.gov/epstein/files/DataSet%209/EFTA01040718.pdf),” using the “same architecture ie block chain” with sovereign backing.

That is the conceptual hinge. Epstein was not proposing that Mongolia adopt Bitcoin. He was proposing that a state copy selected machinery while replacing Bitcoin’s missing sovereign with a government.

## DUBAI SAYS YES TO THE CONVERSATION

Epstein carried the Mongolia idea to Sultan Ahmed bin Sulayem almost immediately. He argued that Dubai should back the currency, beat the rest of the world, and make it real. Bin Sulayem agreed and asked whether **DP World** could start it because of the Dubai government’s ownership.

Now the opening email has context. It was not an isolated brainstorm. It was one iteration of a concept that had already moved through Russia, Saudi-linked contacts, Qatar, and Mongolia.

Still, the record ends before execution. No engineering team, charter, budget, pilot, or currency launch appears. Dubai said yes to the conversation. The documents do not show Dubai buying the plan.

## ABU DHABI AND THE FOUR-DAY TRAP

In October 2017, Epstein asked David Stern to seek a digital-currency meeting with “MBZ.” Stern replied: “[Digital currency: MBZ handed to Khaldoon, speaking to him](https://www.justice.gov/epstein/files/DataSet%209/EFTA00994138.pdf).” The context strongly indicates Mohammed bin Zayed and Khaldoon Al Mubarak, though the email itself uses only initials and a first name.

The same update separately asked about Leon Black meeting **Ahmed Ali Al Sayegh**, then chairman of Abu Dhabi Global Market. Four days later, ADGM published [ICO and virtual-currency guidance](https://www.adgm.com/media/announcements/abu-dhabi-global-market-sets-out-guidance-on-initial-coin-offerings-and-virtual-currencies).

The timing is interesting. The causal link is absent. The email treats the currency update and the Al Sayegh question as separate numbered items, and guidance of that scope would have been developed before October 5.

## A PITCH, NOT A PROGRAM

Across **Russia, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Mongolia, Dubai, and Abu Dhabi**, the proposal changed costumes while keeping its frame:

- blockchain or Bitcoin-like architecture;
- sovereign backing and elite sponsorship;
- local branding, including Sharia compliance or “Genghis Coin”;
- a politically connected intermediary who might reach the decision-maker.

The repetition supports a strong inference that Epstein had a reusable pitch. The missing pieces define its limit. No located record shows a central-bank mandate, organized technical team, final legal charter, funded pilot, launch, or state adoption.

**Epstein appears to have had a pitch. What the documents do not show is a state buying it.**

## STABLECOINS AND A CRYPTO BANK

The interest did not end with the sovereign pitches. In October 2018, MIT-linked cryptographer **Madars Virza** explained two stablecoin models to Epstein: hard-pegged IOUs such as Tether and Gemini Dollar, and soft-pegged algorithmic systems. Epstein’s reply was basic and commercial: “[i dont understand the model. how does the exchange make money](https://cdck-file-uploads-global.s3.dualstack.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/zcash/original/3X/f/0/f0e7ab8d3064f947d11c557362bcd849ef2cd348.pdf).”

In May 2019, seven weeks before his arrest, Epstein received a pitch for **Medici Bank**, a Puerto Rico institution aimed at fintech, blockchain, and crypto clients. The [pitch summary](https://www.justice.gov/epstein/files/DataSet%209/EFTA00495372.pdf) traced its founders through an earlier Brock Pierce-backed payments company and described a bank built for digital-native firms that ordinary banks considered risky.

Epstein questioned the staffing, capitalization, licensing, and how investors would get paid. He ultimately rejected the proposal. This is continuity, but not control: by 2019 he was still evaluating the banking problems around crypto, and the located record shows him saying no.

## THE FILES STILL OUTSIDE THE REVIEW

The documentary record is still moving. In [**Phang v. Blanche**](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/district-of-columbia/dcdce/1%3A2026cv01417/291779/43/), Judge Emmet Sullivan wrote that DOJ had told Congress it did not review or produce foreign-language materials because first-level reviewers could not practically determine responsiveness. On September 16, Sullivan ordered the government to begin review and production and give notice by September 24.

Then DOJ sought a stay. On [September 22](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/73246595/phang-v-blanche/), Sullivan denied the broader partial-stay motion but **temporarily stayed the foreign-language requirement** while the government seeks relief from the D.C. Circuit in case **26-5299**. As of September 23, the appellate court had not resolved that request, and the docket did not show that foreign-language review or production had begun.

Epstein’s crypto activity was international. Separately, DOJ excluded foreign-language material from its initial review. Whether those categories overlap is unknown. Nothing in the current court record says the untranslated files concern Bitcoin, sovereign currency, or cryptocurrency at all.

## WHAT THE DOCUMENTS ACTUALLY SHOW

The documented record is already substantial: a gaming-currency gateway; deliberate outreach to Bitcoin figures; investments in Coinbase and a Blockstream vehicle; contact with an entrepreneur claiming FSA feedback; direct Treasury access; Tether and Noble discussions; a donor relationship beside MIT’s Bitcoin work; repeated sovereign-currency proposals; and later questions about stablecoins and crypto banking.

The strongest inference is that Epstein deliberately positioned himself across several layers of the emerging digital-money ecosystem. A second strong inference is that his sovereign proposals were localized versions of a common idea.

What remains unestablished is just as clear. Epstein did not appear in the located creation record. No evidence shows that he knew or financed Satoshi, that an intelligence agency created Bitcoin, that Hoffman knowingly facilitated his funding, that he controlled Coinbase, Blockstream, or MIT, that a government adopted his proposals, or that the foreign-language files contain a crypto revelation.

[IMAGE: Epstein’s Crypto Network infographic | ALT: Network infographic separating documented money, direct contact, introductions, reported access, and strong inferences across commercial, academic, regulatory, and sovereign branches]

Bitcoin was built around removing the trusted intermediary. Epstein’s crypto story is almost entirely about intermediaries: the person who could make an introduction, the academic who could reach Treasury, the financier who could open an allocation, the political contact who could reach a royal court.

His value was not inventing the technology. It was placing himself between networks that normally did not touch.

## FAQ

### Did Jeffrey Epstein help create Bitcoin?

No evidence located in the released record connects Epstein to Bitcoin’s design, white paper, code, or pre-publication development. His traceable Bitcoin activity begins after launch.

### Did Jeffrey Epstein invest in Coinbase?

Yes. DOJ-derived asset and closing records support an approximately $3 million 2014 investment through IGO LLC. The available evidence does not establish that Coinbase’s founders knew Epstein was the beneficial owner.

### Did Epstein invest in Blockstream?

Yes, indirectly. Southern Financial supplied $500,001 to Kyara Investments III, an Ito-managed vehicle created to hold Blockstream equity. That did not give Epstein half of Blockstream.

### Was Jeffrey Epstein connected to Satoshi Nakamoto?

No direct connection has been found. Epstein later approached Gavin Andresen and entered Adam Back’s orbit, but Gavin declined the contact and the Back overlap began years after Bitcoin launched.

### Did any government adopt Epstein’s digital-currency proposals?

No adoption has been found. Qatar asked detailed questions, Dubai responded enthusiastically, and Abu Dhabi reportedly delegated a conversation, but no mandate, pilot, launch, or state-issued coin linked to Epstein appears in the located record.
