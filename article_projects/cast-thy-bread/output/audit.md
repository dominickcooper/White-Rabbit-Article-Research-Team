## Bottom line

The article’s central claim is adequately supported: CTB’s existence, command traffic, and multiple planned or attempted operations rest on the Morris-Kedar archival reconstruction and identifiable IDFA files. The revision now weighs competing explanations and assigns responsibility without converting inference into documented fact. It avoids turning the CIA’s preservation of a U.S. Embassy report into CIA confirmation, treats Acre’s epidemiology separately from the attack evidence, and distinguishes institutional lineage from proof of a continuous offensive program.

## Dossier-to-article comparison

- **Preserved:** Yadin command chain; Ben-Gurion inference; Keynan/HEMED B structure; Katchalsky acquisition; Acre’s dual causal pathways; Gaza’s three evidentiary layers; Negev and ʿAylabun operations; canceled Cairo plan; reconnaissance in Lebanon/Syria; archival deletion; IIBR lineage; modern-mechanism distinction.
- **Intentionally compressed:** extensive tactical background of the Jerusalem road campaign; every Negev pipeline sabotage; all modern Gaza water statistics. These did not materially strengthen the central CTB finding and risked conflating distinct mechanisms.
- **Not elevated:** cholera claims concerning Egypt and Syria in 1947–48. They remain unverified and appear only as rejected/unsupported allegations.
- **Potential evidence loss:** exact footnote-level IDFA citations for every brigade daily report are in the dossier/source paper rather than linked individually in the article. The article retains the principal archive series and status distinctions.

## Claim-level corrections applied

1. Replaced categorical “Acre outbreak was caused by CTB” with a four-part assessment separating contamination, outbreak causation, possible worsening, and military effect.
2. Added the British medical paper’s March 17 chlorine exhaustion, April 13 fecal contamination, April 9–13 likely infection window, and failure to isolate salmonella from water.
3. Described Pundaq evidence as later testimony aligned with the contemporaneous program, not as a contemporaneous attack report.
4. Treated the Egyptian account as an allegation at the time; later Israeli records provide confirmation of mission intent.
5. Stated that the CIA archive preserved a U.S. Embassy press review and did not independently confirm the bacteria.
6. Separated direct evidence of Yadin’s control from inferential evidence of Ben-Gurion’s authorization.
7. Removed any implication that all HEMED members prepared or deployed pathogens.
8. Distinguished Nakam personnel/scientific overlap from operational continuity.
9. Distinguished HEMED-to-IIBR institutional lineage from a continuous offensive program.
10. Defined modern “indirect biological warfare” as the State of Palestine’s characterization of disease-producing conditions, not direct pathogen placement.
